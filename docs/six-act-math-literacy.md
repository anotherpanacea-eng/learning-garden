Read-only design audit, 2026-10-21. Sources inspected: complete pinned Marble topic records and dependency records in `curriculum/marble/`; `curriculum/atlas.json`; current `docs/curriculum-spirals.md`, `docs/learning-atlas.md`, and broad Number Garden runtime question/model scope. 503 Mathematics records and 286 English records exist. ## Verdict and scope

**No: the six-act story outline did not yet account properly for all intermediate instruction.** It named purposes and destinations. Several foundational instructional strands have no sufficient current activity: formal multi-digit arithmetic, an ordered decoding/spelling progression, fluent transcription, fraction-number-line-to-decimal bridges, ratios, signed arithmetic, probability, and independent developed writing. An open-ended project is not a substitute for the small lesson that makes a needed representation or operation intelligible.

Keep six story acts as a narrative framework. Treat the following strand sequences as curriculum, offering the required small lessons within and beside adventures. Acts are invitations, not age or skill gates. A learner may use advanced ideas with arithmetic or reading support, and revisit a foundation without losing narrative progress. Development is uneven across strands.

Source age bands are reference metadata. The roughly-thirteen horizon needs an explicit **core destination versus stretch** choice: `mt_KB_Czd7RQH` trigonometry is 13–15; quadratic graphs and simultaneous equations mostly13–14. They can be authored stretch paths without making the finale conditional on every upper topic. A thirteen-year-old horizon does not imply a universal attainment target or complete school curriculum.

Every numbered item below is a small instructional transition, generally requiring worked/guided examples, contrasting examples, practice in changed forms, and an invitation to use it elsewhere. It is not one screen or one isolated question. Adjacent source records may be satisfied by the same well-designed sequence; broad records may need several sequences.

Act shorthand: I Recovery; II A Town of Our Own; III Beyond Willow Bridge; IV The Town Has Two Stories; V A Question of Your Own; VI Opening of the Ways. Timing is proposed, not mandated.

## Mathematics

### M1-number. Number, place value and calculation choices — mostly I–III, continued IV–V

Current foothold: bundles/three-digit tasks and within20 add/subtract; not a full sequence or full place-value/column-method coverage.

1. Check cardinality, counting-on, conserved quantity, zero and place-value foundations remain available as optional doors; do not infer these from age.
2. Make ten/ten tens/hundred and exchange both ways without changing quantity; connect objects, unit names, numerals, expanded form and number lines.
3. Extend thousands and larger places; distinguish digit from value, and a zero placeholder from no quantity at all.
4. Compare/order numbers using the highest differing place; locate approximate positions between labelled endpoints.
5. Addition as joining/change and subtraction as removing/difference; unknown can be start, change or result. Choose an operation from meaning rather than a keyword.
6. Mental partitioning, compensation, number-line/counting-up approaches; compare strategies and check an inverse.
7. Column addition with exchange; subtraction with exchange, including across internal zeros. Physically explain what the recorded exchange means before compact notation.
8. Extend to larger whole numbers without making digit count the only source of challenge. Explain equivalence of two methods; diagnose an erroneous method.
9. Rounding to a named place using midpoint/bounds; estimate before calculation and distinguish approximate from exact answers.
10. Base-ten places continue right of one: tenths, hundredths, thousandths; connect fraction strips/grids, number lines and decimal notation. 0.4=0.40, but 0.4 is not less than0.35 because4<35.
11. Add/subtract decimals by shared unit/place, including unequal-length notation and contexts besides money. Money alone hides sub-cent values and other decimal uses.
12. Multiply/divide by powers of ten through changing each digit's value; do not teach a movable decimal point or append-zero rule as the explanation.
13. Select mental, written, calculator or estimate appropriately; carry units; choose appropriate precision and discuss rounded inputs/error intervals later.

Homes: storehouse bundling bench, café till, recipe bench, courier time/length records. Roman numerals can be a history-linked notation contrast, not a required gateway to decimal calculation.

### M2-groups. Multiplication, division and arithmetic structure — I–III, then revisited

Current foothold: full ordered12×12 practice, arrays/decomposition, same-quantities/different-unknown benches. This supplies neither written algorithms nor all multiplication/division meanings.

1. Equal groups versus merely a total; what each factor names; repeated addition and rectangular arrays.
2. Commutativity changes the array orientation while story units/roles remain named; associative regrouping and identity/zero cases.
3. Derive unknown facts from known facts by doubling/halving, ×10±groups and distributive splits. Fluent retrieval may grow; speed is not a gate.
4. Contrast sharing(total and groups given) with grouping(total and group size given); connect each to unknown-factor equations.
5. Additive versus multiplicative comparison: three more than versus three times as many. Include the unknown reference amount, not just unknown result.
6. Multiply by tens/hundreds from base-ten units; area/partial-products models to two/three-digit×one-digit and then multi-digit×two-digit compact methods.
7. Short/long division as partitioning place-value quantities; estimate quotient digits and use multiply-back checks.
8. Interpret remainders: leave extras, round number of vehicles up, discard partial full packages, or express equal shares as fractions/decimals. Same calculation, different answer form according to purpose.
9. Factors/multiples/common factors/common multiples; prime/composite and1; prime factorisation later. Connect common denominators and shared delivery cycles.
10. Squares/cubes and meaning of powers; roots as inverses later. Distinguish3² from3×2.
11. Brackets/order of operations and numerical expressions; retain alternative expressions that represent the same process.
12. Make and critique a multistep plan with unnecessary/missing information; evaluate whether the answer is plausible in context.

Homes: café packing station, delivery manifest, Bramble rule challenge, modular workshop. Need explicit zero, one and division-by-zero exclusions; fact selection limited to1–12 does not teach them.

### M3-fractions. Fractions, decimals and percentages — II–IV, revisited V–VI

Current foothold: Sharing Counter halves/quarters/same whole and set-of12, early Number Garden fractions, measuring in50mL units. A substantial bridge remains.

1. Define the whole/unit; equal amount rather than merely equal number of pieces; equal shares may look different.
2. Unit fraction as one equal part; non-unit fraction as iterations of that unit, first area/length then discrete sets. Name denominator as unit size and numerator as count.
3. Fractions as lengths/numbers: partition0–1 intervals and iterate beyond1. A number-line fraction is not a shaded picture category.
4. Compare same numerator/same denominator; benchmarks0,1/2,1; always retain a common referent. Same fraction of different wholes can give different amounts.
5. Equivalence by repartitioning and locating the same number; generate families by multiplicative scaling; simplify using common factors.
6. Add/subtract same units/denominators; regroup across wholes; distinguish a number from its mixed/improper notation.
7. Common units for unlike denominators, first related denominators then general cases. Do not add denominators. Estimate with benchmarks.
8. Fraction of an amount as sharing then iterating; fraction as division a÷b; connect remainders to fractional results and missing-whole problems.
9. Tenths/hundredths/thousandths as fraction units; equivalent decimals; compare unequal decimal lengths; convert selected fractions by division, distinguishing terminating/repeating results later.
10. Percent as per hundred, linked to fractions/decimals, including percentages above100 and of changing-sized wholes.
11. Known percentage landmarks→1%/10% strategies→general percentage of an amount. Compare percentage and absolute differences.
12. Multiplication by a fraction as scaling: less-than1 shrinks positive quantity, more-than1 grows it. Fraction×whole, whole×fraction, fraction×fraction using length/area/number-line reasoning.
13. Division meanings with fractional quantities: unit fraction÷whole versus whole÷unit fraction, then fraction÷fraction through measuring/how-many and inverse multiplication. Explain reciprocal method rather than make it the first meaning.
14. Four operations with decimals/fractions/mixed numbers; select exact or approximate representation for the purpose; negative rational extension separately.
15. Percentage increase/decrease via original amount; repeated change not additive, inverse percentage change/recovering original later. Fixed simple-interest assumptions only on an explicit optional finance path.

Homes: café portions→recipes→supply comparisons, Garden area/seed rates, post baggage proportions. Need models with thirds/fifths/eighths/etc., not only halves/quarters and money.

### M3-ratio. Ratio, rate and proportional reasoning — III–V

1. Distinguish part:part from part:whole; keep the referent and order visible.
2. Equivalent ratios by scaling both quantities; additive versus multiplicative continuation contrasted.
3. Ratio notation alongside bar models, ratio tables and double number lines; noninteger scale factors deliberately included.
4. Unit rate as “for one” with units; compare prices for unequal package sizes and travel distances over unequal times.
5. Unequal sharing by ratio: find total parts, one part, then each share; connect2:3 with2/5 and3/5 of the whole.
6. Direct proportion preserves ratio and passes through origin. Contrast with a fixed setup fee plus per-item price; a straight line need not be proportional.
7. Scale diagrams and similar shapes; length scales byk, area byk², volume byk³. Larger drawing and larger measurement unit must not be conflated.
8. Compound measures: speed and later density with explicit units; average speed across whole journey not unweighted average of segment speeds.
9. Inverse proportion under a stated fixed total; contrast with direct proportion and a merely decreasing relationship. No assumption that all work can be divided perfectly among workers.
10. Express relationship algebraically/graphically and choose an appropriate representation. Test whether a model's assumptions fit rather than assuming every context is proportional.

Homes: supplier counter, Cartographer's Table and journey planner. Not built by merely scaling number of café guests.

### M4-algebra. Early algebra to symbolic relationships — II–V, optional VI stretch

1. Equality as same value, including8=5+3 and balanced unknowns; not an instruction to write an answer on the right.
2. Describe a pattern; term-to-term rule versus position-to-term rule; several continuations can fit a finite beginning without a supplied generating rule.
3. Unknown placeholder→named variable; letters can mean a fixed unknown or a varying quantity in different tasks. Translate situation↔diagram↔expression.
4. Expression versus equation versus inequality; simplify versus evaluate versus solve. Notational conventions for multiplication, coefficients, powers and brackets.
5. Substitute values, including zero/fractions/negatives once those are taught; carry units and order of operations.
6. Collect like terms and distribute over brackets by maintaining equivalence; subtraction/negative multipliers require signed-number lessons.
7. Solve one-step then two-step equations using inverse operations/equivalent transformations; unknown both sides and brackets later; verify by substitution.
8. List possible pairs satisfying one condition; show why one equation in two variables need not determine a unique pair.
9. Table→ordered pairs→graph with axes/scale; first quadrant then all four; distinguish plotted samples from justified continuous relationship.
10. Linear sequence nth term and linear relationship y=mx+c; distinguish term number, input, gradient/rate and intercept/initial value.
11. Rearrange simple formulae to make a different quantity the subject; factor common terms as reverse distributivity.
12. Solve inequalities and display solution sets on a number line; distinguish a set from one equation solution; reversing sign on negative multiply/divide needs an explanation.
13. Two constraints and simultaneous linear equations: find candidate pairs/graph intersection, then algebraic methods if included in agreed horizon.
14. Stretch: square-area growth versus constant-rate growth, double-bracket area model→quadratic expression/graph and approximate graphical solutions. No claim this is necessary for every thirteen-year-old finale.

Homes: Bramble becomes a rule tester; café pricing and package constraints, delivery schedules, garden designs. Each symbolic transition needs its own teachable representation; a game that lets the child guess a rule is not full algebra instruction.

### M1-signed. Signed numbers, powers and numerical generalisation — III–V

1. Negative positions/quantities relative to a chosen zero; order them on a number line.
2. Distance between signed positions versus signed change; crossing zero; absolute magnitude versus numerical order.
3. Addition/subtraction as directed changes, including subtracting a negative. Use more than temperature metaphors and connect to structure/patterns.
4. Multiplication/division sign relationships explained by consistent patterns/distributivity, not a chant alone; extend to rational numbers.
5. Integer powers, squares/cubes and roots; exact roots versus approximations. Optional scientific notation, significant figures and rational/irrational-number investigations for project needs.

Source gap to audit: sign-rule node has hard prerequisite positive/negative-number node whose description is contextual quantities, not complete signed addition/subtraction. Graph reachability does not prove pedagogical sufficiency.

### M5-space. Geometry, measurement, space and time — I–V, optional VI stretch

1. Attribute and unit: length versus area versus capacity/volume versus mass; direct comparison, repeated nonstandard then standard units; no gaps/overlaps.
2. Align ruler zero and read intervals, including shifted/nonzero starts; estimate then measure, choose tool/unit, state limits of supplied digital model versus actual measurement.
3. Read scales with different step sizes; precision/readout versus actual accuracy. Convert related linear/mass/capacity/time units through unit relationships.
4. Money values/equivalent coins→totals/change; clocks nearest minute,12/24hour, calendars and elapsed time across hour/day. Time is not base ten.
5. Classify2D/3D shapes by properties, including nonprototypical orientations; square is a rectangle; face/edge/vertex, nets and cross-sections later.
6. Perimeter as boundary and area as covering; unit squares→rectangular area→decomposition/distributivity; same perimeter/different areas and converse.
7. Volume with unit cubes→layers/base area×height→composite prisms; distinguish volume from surface area. Area/volume conversion factors differ from length factors.
8. Angles as turn, independent of arm length; right/acute/obtuse/reflex; use protractor and bearings/compass directions where introduced.
9. Coordinates as ordered pair, axes and origin; transformations translation/reflection/rotation with unchanged shape; symmetry and congruence.
10. Triangle/quadrilateral/polygon angle sums; parallel-line relationships; derive unknown angles through argument, not appearance.
11. Triangle/parallelogram/trapezium area by rearranging familiar areas; perpendicular height versus sloped side. Circle radius/diameter→circumference/area withπ and approximations.
12. Similarity/enlargement and scale; congruence criteria and constructions as optional more formal paths. Physical ruler/compass work not established by tapping completed pictures.
13. Stretch: Pythagoras from square areas and right-angle condition; unknown long or short side; square roots; coordinate distance. Then trig requires similar right triangles, side-role reference to a selected angle, ratios and inverse functions, not just formula substitution.

Homes: Cartographer's Table, Workshop, garden layout, stage design. Current small shape/measurement checks should remain narrow footholds.

### M6-data. Data, statistics and probability — II–V

1. Form a question; choose what to observe/count, define category/unit, and distinguish sample from population and missing from zero.
2. Tally/table/pictogram/bar chart with one unit then chosen scales; frequency and individual measurement are different quantities.
3. Chart construction: relevant rows, labels, scale, source fidelity and question answer are separable; misleading scales and omitted categories later.
4. Repeated measurements/variation; dot/line plots including fractional units; time-series line graphs versus categorical bars, gaps versus changes.
5. Mean as equal redistribution, then calculation; median/mode/range; outliers and choice of summary; distributions can have same average and different spread.
6. Sampling and survey-question effects; unfair comparisons, sample size and representativeness. Explain uncertainty without a universal confidence score.
7. Paired measurements→scatterplot; correlation direction/strength, outliers, reasonable best fit; prediction within data versus extrapolation; correlation does not establish cause.
8. Chance language→equally likely versus unequal outcomes; sample space; probability as fraction and0–1 measure; decimals/percent links.
9. Complementary and mutually exclusive/exhaustive outcomes; probabilities sum1. “Two possible outcomes” does not imply half each.
10. Repeated experiments→relative frequency versus theoretical probability; short-run surprises and large-run tendencies. More trials do not guarantee every successive estimate is closer.
11. Systematic enumeration/product rule, tables, sets/Venn diagrams, then simple tree diagrams/combined events; independence and replacement must be specified before multiplication shortcuts.

Homes: Field Notebook becomes genuine investigation notebook, chance stall at market, courier reliability models. Current fixed-scale chart source matching is only a subset of steps2–3.

### M7-practices. Reasoning across content — I–VI

1. Say what quantities, units and representations refer to; distinguish an answer from a reason.
2. Move between objects, diagrams, words and equations while preserving their meaning.
3. Choose a strategy, estimate, check and explain a revision.
4. Use repeated reasoning to propose a generalisation and test a counterexample.
5. Compare arguments, assumptions and representation limits; justify a claim.
6. Select useful models in a new situation and explain what the result means.

These recurrent META targets also need content-specific lessons. A fraction-related M7 record must be considered alongside M3, not lost when filtering domains. See the [original bridge checklist](six-act-teaching-bridges.md).

## English / literacy

### E1-code. Decoding, spelling and word structure — independent foundation door I–III; complex vocabulary later

Current Word Garden morphology/novel word play is valuable; it is not systematic phonics, encoding or conventional-spelling instruction. All source English phonics nodes start beforeage8, so age-only filtering would erase an entire potentially needed strand.

1. Optional print/letter-name/phoneme awareness, oral blending/segmenting and letter-sound doors based on observed need.
2. Reviewed sequence of grapheme–phoneme correspondences: single consonants/short vowels→consonant digraphs→long-vowel/split digraphs→vowel teams/r-controlled/other alternative patterns. Actual sequence must be designed by a literacy specialist, accounting for dialect and pronunciations.
3. Blend through an unfamiliar word; segment spoken word and choose/write graphemes; word chains change one sound; distinguish sound count from letter count.
4. Teach irregular portions of high-frequency words alongside regular mappings; don't ask children to guess words from pictures/context in place of decoding.
5. Read connected text controlled to taught patterns, then reread for accuracy/meaning/prosody; separate first decoding attempt from familiar reread, narrated or jointly read conditions.
6. Syllable segmentation and multisyllabic decoding; inflectional endings, productive roots/affixes, pronunciation shifts; use morphology to support rather than replace sound–spelling knowledge.
7. Encoding pattern choice, suffix rules(drop-e/change-y/doubling), plurals/possessives/contractions, homophones chosen by meaning, less-common word origins and subject vocabulary.
8. Dictated words/sentences and independent application; allow review/correction and record support separately. A correct multiple-choice spelling is not spelling from memory.
9. Dictionary/spellcheck use and verification; distinguish inventive story words, plausible spelling attempts, and conventional published spelling. Preserve discovery play, offer conventional form when useful.

Homes: Word Workshop separate short lessons, library with matched tiny stories, Post Office words needed in an actual letter. Needs reviewed human-quality phoneme audio; ordinary full-text TTS is insufficient for clean isolated sound instruction. No speech-recognition reading-level claims.

### E2-vocabulary. Vocabulary, semantics and register — I–V

1. Word meaning in concrete context, categories/attributes, precise definitions and multiple meanings.
2. Contrast related words and intensity; select for intended meaning rather than simply longer words.
3. Meaning-bearing roots/affixes, word families, Greek/Latin and other origins; inference from parts/context and verification with a reference.
4. Literal/figurative uses, idioms, simile/metaphor; later allusion/irony/pun/connotation. Unfamiliar cultural references need contextual teaching, not a trap.
5. Domain-specific vocabulary intentionally reused in observing, reading, explaining and writing; academic connecting terms such as despite, proportional and evidence need teaching too.
6. Register/dialect/audience choices; everyday language varieties are not mistakes. Teach formal English as an additional contextual resource.
7. Thesaurus/dictionary choices verified against sentence meaning; discuss uncertain/partial knowledge and refine it.

### E2-grammar. Grammar, sentence construction and punctuation — II–V

1. Complete thought/main clause, subject/verb, sentence boundaries; intentional fragment in art versus accidental unclear fragment.
2. Word classes in actual sentences and how changing noun/verb/adjective/adverb affects meaning; determiners and prepositions.
3. Coordination contrasts additive/adversative/alternative; subordination expresses cause,time,condition; do not equate longer sentence with better sentence.
4. Tense/agreement/pronoun reference for meaning across sentences; ambiguous referents and perspective, avoiding treatment of a child's dialect as defective.
5. Expand/reduce/combine sentences; noun phrases, adverbials, relative clauses and placement; modifiers attach clearly.
6. Direct speech and reporting clauses, comma/quotation conventions; apostrophes contraction/singular/plural possession contrasted.
7. Paragraph cohesion through reference, repetition/synonyms and logical links; paragraph breaks organize an idea rather than count sentences.
8. Modal language marks certainty/possibility/obligation; consistent/purposeful tense shifts, active/passive focus, conditional/hypothetical forms.
9. Punctuation choices express relationships: commas, brackets/dashes, colon/semicolon and lists; proofread clarity versus revise substance.
10. Deliberate sentence rhythm, grammar/register/voice for effect; compare alternatives in narrative/report/argument rather than vocabulary-label quizzes alone.

Home: a sentence laboratory inside Post Office/Story Loft, reader-specific revision, Archive reporting. Current supplied-card reader challenges are a start at reference/audience, not full sentence construction.

### E3-reading. Reading comprehension and literature — I–V

1. Maintain separate pathways for listening comprehension and independent word reading; provide rich read-aloud material while decoding develops.
2. Retrieve explicit details and track who/what/where/time; sequence events and causal versus merely temporal connections.
3. Notice comprehension failure, reread, resolve referent/vocabulary gap, ask a question; an incorrect inference can reflect missing vocabulary rather than reasoning difficulty.
4. Infer feelings/motives/unstated links and cite words that support interpretation; distinguish said, inferred and assumed; two interpretations may be supportable.
5. Main idea versus interesting detail; paragraph summaries then whole-text summaries, retaining central meaning rather than copying phrases.
6. Genre/structure across story, poem, drama and informational text; illustration/table/chart contribution versus decorative appearance; rehearse performance as interpretation.
7. Character/motive/conflict/setting and causal plot; theme develops across events, distinct from a forced moral or topic word.
8. Compare narrators(first/third, limited/omniscient), perspective versus author opinion, later unreliable narration; contradictory accounts can arise from location/knowledge/time as well as dishonesty.
9. Word choice, imagery, sound, figurative techniques and structural choices shape tone/effect; quote accurately and explain the mechanism.
10. Compare works across genres/cultures/periods and differing treatments of a theme, including whole books and substantial texts. A library of tiny fragments cannot supply wide sustained reading.
11. Research reading: find information efficiently, distinguish claim/reason/evidence and assess whether evidence addresses the exact claim; evaluate sufficiency, provenance, date and purpose.
12. Synthesize multiple accounts without flattening disagreement; distinguish factual disagreement, interpretation and value choice. Treat bias as something to investigate, not a label for every source with a viewpoint.

Homes: English Paris, town library, Archive, Story Loft plays. Need substantial commissioned/licensed/original texts and optional external reading guides; narrative length, vocabulary load, background knowledge and inferential demand vary independently.

### E4-writing. Composition and revision — I–V

1. Oral rehearsal, drawing/dictating/writing routes; sentence-level idea to purposeful brief text. Input method is distinct from quality of ideas.
2. Audience/purpose and reader knowledge; compose instructions/recount/message/opinion/explanation, preserving author's intention.
3. Narrative sequence with a beginning/problem/actions/consequence/ending; character desire and motive rather than merely extending to12 scenes.
4. Plan related ideas, group paragraphs/headings, draft connected account; model an explanation of how/why as distinct from a chronological recount.
5. Develop factual text with relevant definitions/examples/details; distinguish personal opinion, supported explanation and research-based claim.
6. Revise from real reader questions; compare before/after and explain what changed. Revision of meaning/organization and proofreading spelling/punctuation need separate passes.
7. Develop narrative context, point of view, dialogue/action, pacing and setting effects; models/mentors from read texts, not plot ingredients alone.
8. Opinion→organized reasons→specific evidence→counterclaim and answer; represent another position accurately, revise if evidence warrants, allow unresolved value tradeoffs.
9. Research question→search/source selection→notes→paraphrase/quotation→organized synthesis→source list. Teach plagiarism/attribution explicitly and distinguish a quotation from one's own account.
10. Longer writing coherent across sections, transitions and structure; choose form/tone/format suited to audience, including scripts/poetry/reports/letters/presentations.
11. Independent revision with intended effect, justified craft decisions and human response; publish locally/perform with privacy controls. There is no reliable creative-quality autograder implied by text entry.

Homes: Post Office to Story Loft to newspaper/Archive; town debates and learner-owned project publication. Automatic feedback can test explicit constraints and supplied alternatives; it cannot certify open argument/artistic success.

### E1-transcription. Transcription, handwriting and keyboard fluency — independent pathway II–V

1. Optional comfortable tool use/legible letter and digit formation/spacing; paper or stylus requires adult observation, not pretend measurement from mouse taps.
2. Sustained fluent transcription, useful letter joining only where appropriate; dictated known material isolates spelling/transcription load from composition.
3. Keyboard navigation, editing, selecting/cursor moves, punctuation, paragraphs and accessible input including speech dictation; distinguish composition evidence from transcription support.
4. Build stamina gradually; planning/drafting/revision remain possible with adult scribe or narration. Handwriting style must not gate story progress.

The pinned taxonomy has only five handwriting/transcription nodes, largely early formation/joining; keyboarding and older-child transcription stamina need explicit original extensions or reviewed external pathways.

### E5-speaking. Speaking, listening, discussion and performance — I–VI

1. Ask/clarify, listen/take turns and retell what another said; practice with a real partner where possible.
2. Give organized descriptions/instructions/explanations and monitor listener understanding; reader-response puppet is a narrow simulation, not evidence of interpersonal listening.
3. Prepare from notes; paraphrase accurately and identify a speaker's reasons/evidence.
4. Build on another contribution, ask a relevant follow-up, disagree respectfully and change a position for a reason.
5. Plan/rehearse/present for an audience using useful visuals; choose register, pace, intonation and silence for purpose, not compulsory performance style.
6. Structured discussion/debate with counterclaims and summaries; separate forceful delivery from sound reasoning.
7. Interpret/perform scripts and poems, compare productions and explain performance choices.

Screen-selectable dialogue can model choices; observed oral communication needs human dialogue/observation. No requirement for microphone recording or public sharing.

7. Coverage needs topic-to-unit-to-activity-with-limits records and explicit skipped/deferred/manual/offscreen entries.
