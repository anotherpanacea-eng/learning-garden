## The *ustedes* choice

This is acceptable as written. *Ustedes* is correct everywhere and is the plural in most of the Spanish-speaking world. In Spain it sounds polite rather than wrong. The one place it shows is *¿Cuándo vuelven?*, a postcard question to the crew. Suggested framing for the grown-up notes: "The Spanish uses *ustedes* for 'you all'. In Spain, friends usually say *vosotros*; *ustedes* is also correct, and it is what most Spanish speakers use."

## Questions for the fluent reader

1. *Más arriba en el valle* is natural. *Valle arriba* is also natural but harder for a beginner to parse. Keep it.
2. *El embarcadero* is the more exact word for a small landing, but *el muelle* is common for any jetty and is shorter. Keep *el muelle*.
3. *Barca* is right for a small river boat. *La Garza* is called a barge in English, which strictly would be a *barcaza*, but *barca* is fine. *Amarrarse* is right; *amarrar bien las barcas* would also work.
4. Use *la previsión del tiempo* (problem 3). *El pronóstico* is understood in Spain but is less usual there for a daily forecast.
5. The order is right: peel and cut, fry slowly, beat the eggs, mix, then cook both sides. *Mézclalo todo* is natural home-cooking Spanish. The short version's "add the eggs at the end" is a fair simplification.
6. Teo's and Marisol's lines sound like real people, and the nudges' Spanish quotations are exact.
7. *¿Cuándo vuelven?* to one addressee works, because it asks about the whole crew. No pairing of tiles is awkward.

**Longer-note load.** It is right for a beginner who reads well in English, provided the sentence glosses stay one tap away. The weather and recipe longer notes are the densest, and the support tools carry them.
