## Independent response and action probes

A temporary oracle enumerated all **642 valid denomination vectors** under count≤25 and value≤50¢. Across six cases, five relation inputs and four return-choice inputs, it examined **77,040 combinations**. The fitting amount-vector counts are exactly **1, 0, 2, 1, 4, 2** as listed above. Assessment of the relation depends solely on fixed tender versus price; assessment of the return depends on its separate response choice and vector. In particular, the oracle permits false/true results and never makes a return judgement conditional on a correct relation answer.

Concrete counterexamples establish the intended distinctions:

- Start ribbon-exact with blank relation, null return choice and an empty vector. Keeping gives null/null, even though the vector's mathematical total is zero. Explicitly choosing Return no coins (0¢), or Use these coins on that empty mat, produces a definite fitting return. A correct exact relation then yields true/true.
- In ribbon-dime, selecting “Less than the price” and returning one nickel yields false/true. Selecting “More than the price” with an unsure return yields true/null. Neither response erases the other.
- In leaf-short, explicit amount zero yields changeMatch=false; short yields true. A kept short response may retain a nickel in its inactive vector without asserting that the nickel should go back. Showing 6+0=5 as a true allocation, showing −1¢ in returned coins, or depicting a seller keeping 6¢ from the 5¢ tender would all be wrong.
- In label-quarter, entering a dime plus three pennies, then choosing short or unsure, preserves that four-coin/13¢ idea as inactive work. Use these coins restores it as the active amount. The dedicated zero button instead deliberately clears it and proposes zero; this is a disclosed edit rather than incidental loss during another response choice.
- Successfully removing the final coin sets amount with zero value, because that was an actual edit. Refused removal from an empty mat leaves null/short/unsure unchanged. Merely opening Return or pressing Next never turns an empty mat into an answer.

A separate enumeration covered **3,852 exchange/vector combinations** for the six existing rules: 1,832 enabled transformations, 1,962 missing-input refusals and 58 cap refusals. Every enabled transformation conserves cents; some change physical coin count. For example, 24 pennies + one nickel is a valid 25-coin/29¢ vector, but trading its nickel into five pennies would exceed the count cap and must fail atomically. Exchanging a quarter into two dimes plus a nickel changes one coin to three while preserving 25¢. These are checks of the specified arithmetic and action contract, not tests of a future implementation.

**Scope/overlap — clean; blocking=false.** A/B are alternative payments equal to the same price. Market Change instead fixes price and tender and asks which part returns to the customer. That is a new part–whole/inverse demand rather than another equivalent-payment task. A new optional root and small local modules preserve old records' meaning without a generic editor or economy engine. Prices remain fictional, and Café stock, meals, budgets, taxes, till scarcity and rewards stay outside the slice.

**Project commitments — clean; blocking=false.** The greatest evidence risk is calling a supported fitting return an independent strategy or money-handling success. **Mechanical validatability — clean; blocking=false.** All case arithmetic, equivalent vectors, independent assessments, exchange conservation/refusals, response activation, safe allocation and relevance are deterministic. Mathematical source identities are load-bearing: version1 penny/nickel/dime/quarter must remain 1/5/10/25 even when labels or art descriptors differ. Historical recorded count/value/outcome fields are deliberately bounded-shape admitted, not recalculated; the spec honestly distinguishes readable records from verified old truth.
