# Text review: words, letters, stories, post office, loft

Scope: `dist/` post-office-*, post-rival*, message-*, letter-*, letter-trail-*, word-garden-*, story-*, story-order-ui, loft-*. Read-only review of child- and grown-up-facing strings in the main snapshot. Line numbers refer to the minified `dist/*.mjs` files, where one line often holds a whole screen.

## Top issues

1. **British spelling in the message and letter games, with the rest of the game in American.** `message-content.mjs:5,14` "Two labelled baskets", "a labelled garden bed"; `message-content.mjs:25,27,28,29` and `letter-content.mjs:55,59,71,78,81` "neighbours"/"neighbour"; `loft-ui.mjs:7` "An earlier theatre notebook is kept". The Post Office, Word Garden and Loft all say "neighbor", so the same word changes spelling between screens. Fix: "labeled", "neighbor(s)", "theater". The title `letter-content.mjs:71` "A new neighbour’s first trip" is also a heading a child reads.

2. **Spec and research vocabulary leaking into child screens.** Examples a child (or a parent reading aloud) meets mid-play:
   - `message-ui.mjs:36` "Choose a supplied sentence", "Try this supplied sentence", "The puppet will run this supplied sentence". "Supplied sentence" shows up about ten times. Rewrite: "Pick a note card", "Try this card", "The puppet will follow this card."
   - `letter-trail-ui.mjs:16` "Familiar notes return as rereading; their words are not generated by AI." and "Familiar or earlier exposure uncertain". Move both to the grown-up panel.
   - `letter-trail-ui.mjs:18` "Earlier exposure is uncertain; treat this as familiar practice." Rewrite: "You may have seen this letter before."
   - `letter-trail-ui.mjs:17` "Keep a supplied version if you want this choice to inform the next suggestion." / "it won’t count again toward a new suggestion." Rewrite: "Keep this card if you like it. It helps pick your next letter."
   - `post-office-ui.mjs:109` reading buttons "Reading not observed / Read together / Reported read independently" and "This report does not measure decoding or fluency." These are grown-up record options inside the child's delivery page. Move them under a "For grown-ups" summary and relabel: "Not sure / We read it together / I read it myself".
   - `post-office-ui.mjs:59` "This explanation is support. It does not count as completing your delivery." It reads as a warning about record-keeping. Rewrite: "This was Pip's practice trip. Your own parcel is still waiting."

3. **Inconsistent names for the same thing in the Post Office.** The counter is "the sorting counter" (`post-office-core.mjs:100,108`, `post-office-content.mjs:21,25`, `post-office-ui.mjs:109`) and "the parcel counter" (`post-office-ui.mjs:51` heading "Pack: at the parcel counter"). The plan is "flight plan", "flight instructions", "route", "your words" and "Your message" (`post-office-core.mjs:110` "Your message has ended."). Pick "sorting counter" and "flight plan" and use them everywhere; for line 110 write "Your flight plan is finished, but the parcel is still with you. Add a Give card."

4. **Instructions that name a button that isn't there.** `post-office-ui.mjs:103` "Press Fly once, then check where your words took the courier." The button says "Fly the next instruction". `post-office-core.mjs:100` "Let’s check the picture together, or try another count." There is no "check the picture" control; the help button says "Pack together". Rewrites: "Press Fly the next instruction. Then look where the courier went." and "Count the picture again, or press Pack together."

5. **Em-dashes on child-visible text.** `letter-trail-ui.mjs:16` "what this reader already knows—and needs to know." becomes "what this reader knows, and what they need to know." `word-garden-ui.mjs:21` "+ cutting — a letter experiment" becomes "+ cutting: a word experiment". `post-office-ui.mjs:114` notebook buttons "Title — waiting" become "Title: waiting" or "Title (waiting)".

6. **Post Office welcome and step text pack too many steps into one block.** `post-office-ui.mjs:16` (the narrated welcome) has six sentences and five tasks: "Read an order, pack what is needed, and write a flight plan. We will fly one instruction at a time. You can change your plan, get help, or take a break." The on-screen list at `:114` already does this well. Trim the audio to: "The storm scattered the post. Neighbors are waiting. Pack a parcel. Plan the flight. Fly one step at a time." Also `post-office-ui.mjs:55` "6 instruction spaces used. Changing a line brings the courier back to the start; your packed parcel stays ready." becomes "3 of 6 cards used. If you change a card, the courier flies home. Your parcel stays packed."

7. **The free-text message box has no pretend-names guidance.** `message-ui.mjs:34` "My message for a person to read" (600 characters, saved and included in downloads) and the reason/reply boxes at `message-ui.mjs:40` don't tell anyone to keep real names out. The Loft already has a good line at `loft-ui.mjs:17`: "Use pretend names if you plan to share a download." Add the same line under each message textarea, and drop the adult storage sentence at `:34` ("The puppet’s automatic action uses only a supplied sentence. Writing stays on this device under your save settings...") or move it to the grown-up panel.

8. **Answer length gives away the right choice (an AGENTS.md rule).** `story-content.mjs:67` the correct choice "Luma opened its wings in the bright patch and closed them in shade" is almost twice as long as the others. The same happens at `story-content.mjs:124` ("The card under the folded roof had fewer wet marks in this test"). Balance them: "Luma opened its wings in sun and closed them in shade" / "Every creature closes its wings in the shade of a leaf" / "Luma can never open its wings when it is shady."

## Instruction clarity

- `post-office-content.mjs:21` "Reserve 2 for Post Office repairs, then deliver the remaining ribbons to Ember." and `:25` "Reserve 5 ... deliver the remaining stars". "Reserve" and "remaining" are hard words for a beginning reader. Rewrite: "Keep 2 ribbons for repairs. Take the rest to Ember."
- `post-rival-generator.mjs:50` and `post-rival.mjs:13` "Reserve 3 for the gate bow. How many ribbons remain?" Rewrite: "Bramble uses 3 for a bow. How many are left?"
- `post-rival.mjs:12-14` "Try a smaller ribbon table". A child who just picked "Multiplication table" will read "table" as a times table. Rewrite: "Try a smaller ribbon puzzle."
- `post-rival-generator.mjs:61` "A different worked example." is teacher jargon. Rewrite: "Here is a practice puzzle."
- `post-office-core.mjs:118` "Revise the give instruction; the parcel stays with you." Rewrite: "This parcel is for Clover. Change the Give card. You still have the parcel."
- `post-office-core.mjs:12` "This saved delivery cannot safely continue with this version. Its exact work is kept; choose a fresh delivery to try again." Rewrite: "This old delivery can't open now. We kept it. Pick a new delivery."
- `post-office-ui.mjs:75` "These choices change the next puzzle. The one already open stays exactly as it is." Fine for grown-ups; for a child, "Your choice starts with the next puzzle."
- `post-office-ui.mjs:94` "This encounter is kept in your notebook. Another challenge can use the same delivery, even after its parcel arrives." Unclear. Rewrite: "Saved in your notebook. You can meet Bramble again any time."
- `message-ui.mjs:33` "You can change your mind. A clue is separate from a guess about the note." The second sentence is abstract. Rewrite: "You can pick a different clue."
- `message-content.mjs:9` clue feedback "This explains why the note is unclear, but it does not tell the reader which basket Rabbit needs." The clue feedback lines are long and two-clause throughout. Rewrite: "Yes, there are two baskets. Which one does Rabbit want?"
- `letter-content.mjs:76` "without expecting shared local knowledge" and `:81` "A named place and a landmark can replace knowledge that old friends share." Rewrite: "Dot is new. Name the place and something near it."
- `word-garden-content.mjs:9` "-ness: the quality or state it describes" and `:52` "We added two meaning-bearing parts." Both are too abstract. Rewrite: "-ness turns a word like kind into a thing: kindness." / "We added two parts, and each one changed the meaning."
- `word-garden-content.mjs:51` the prompt asks "How might each painter handle a full paint pot?" but the choices are "Talk about a careful painter." etc. The choices don't answer the question. Retitle the prompt "Which painter shall we talk about?" or make the choices answers.
- `word-garden-content.mjs:55` "Choose a plant and cutting you have not tried together. What could their combination mean in your story?" Rewrite: "Pick a word and a part you haven't tried. What could the new word mean?"
- `story-content.mjs:69` hint "Choose the sentence about this creature and the events Pip actually watched." Rewrite: "Pick the sentence about what Pip saw Luma do."
- `story-content.mjs:79,113,149` "Optional off-screen idea:" opens each project. It's planning vocabulary. Rewrite: "Want to make one on paper?"
- Naming drift in the puppet area: "Story Loft" (`loft-ui.mjs:25`), "my puppet show" (`message-ui.mjs:28`), "the reader’s stage" and "the little stage". The grown-up area is "Grown-up notes", "the local notebook" (`message-ui.mjs:42`), "the private notebook" (`loft-ui.mjs:25`) and "notes download". Pick one name for each.

## Descriptive voice

- `message-content.mjs:14` "The map shows a labelled garden bed and a labelled footbridge. The garden bed has empty planting spaces. The footbridge has a cleared path." Reads like a spec. Try: "Pip holds one seed packet. The garden bed has empty rows waiting. The footbridge path is swept clean after the storm."
- `story-content.mjs:120` the shelter test ("They used equal-sized pieces of the same paper and followed the same steps, leaving one roof flat and folding the other.") reads like a lab report. Keep the fair-test facts in shorter beats: "Pip and Dot made two paper roofs the same size. One was flat. One was folded. Ten raindrops fell on each."
- Character replies in the letter and message cases often sound robotic: `letter-content.mjs:13` "The object and the place are clear to me.", `:57` "The object and recipient are clear.", `message-content.mjs:19` "The destination and purpose are both clear to me." Give the reader puppets voices: "An umbrella, to the café door. Off I go!"
- `letter-content.mjs:78` "I do not need to know your old habits." sounds a little sharp. Try: "Now I can find it, even on my first day."
- `post-office-ui.mjs:103` "A neighbor’s parcel has arrived." is flat for the payoff moment. Name them: "Clover has her buns. The welcome-back table is ready."
- `loft-ui.mjs:25` "Now our neighbors are ready for a little show. Make a little show, ..." says "a little show" twice. Try: "The storm scattered the library puppets. Gather them up and make a little show."
- `word-garden-content.mjs:21` "The neighbors rebuild the storm-damaged bridge." could be livelier: "Plank by plank, the neighbors rebuild the bridge."

## Accessibility

- **Double narration.** Several pictures repeat the visible caption as their aria-label, so a screen reader reads the same text twice: `loft-art.mjs:28` stage `aria-label` equals the caption right below it (`loft-ui.mjs:25`). `post-office-ui.mjs:39` map `aria-label` and `<figcaption>` hold the same string. `message-ui.mjs:30` scene `aria-label` and `.msg-scene-description` hold the same string. Give the picture a short label ("Scene picture") or use `aria-labelledby` pointing to the caption.
- `post-office-ui.mjs:38` map alt "Flying witch (she/her) at Willow Post Office. ... Dotted lines are possible flights, not required stops." Reading pronouns in parentheses aloud is clunky, and the last sentence is a "not X" construction. Rewrite: "The flying witch is at Willow Post Office. The parcel goes to Lantern Hill. Dotted lines show places you can fly."
- `post-office-ui.mjs:87` region `aria-label="Build the actual multiplication"` doesn't match the visible heading "Build this puzzle in parts". Use `aria-labelledby="post-rival-build-title"`.
- `post-office-ui.mjs:69` (rivalPicture with splitAt): the "Separate 2 bundles from the other 3" step marks the split with blue and gold cells only, so the cue depends on color. Add a gap or a "First 2 bundles / Other 3" label row.
- `word-garden-ui.mjs:26` part buttons render "-er" plus a span "end" with no separator, so a screen reader hears "dash er end". Add a visually hidden comma, or set `aria-label="-er, goes at the end"`.
- `word-garden-content.mjs:24` "the word LIBRARY" may be spelled out letter by letter in narration. Write "the word Library" and let CSS add capitals if the sign art needs them.
- `post-office-ui.mjs:109` the delivery page stacks four nested `<details>` (neighbors, optional ideas, prediction, reading). That's a heavy reading load next to the task. Keep "Where the neighbors are" and move the rest to grown-ups.

## Typos, spelling and style

- `loft-content.mjs:5` "a Moon crunch bite": the capital M on Moon looks like a typo. Use "a moon-crunch bite".
- `word-garden-content.mjs:41` "carer ... especially common in British English." This line is accurate, but in an American game "caregiver" may confuse a beginning reader less. Optional: "People say carer in some places. Many Americans say caregiver."
- "Shall" questions in letter replies (`letter-content.mjs:14,36,80,92,104` "Shall we tell a different story or revise?") read as British and formal. Use "Want to tell a different story, or change the note?"
- `post-office-ui.mjs:109` "An optional idea, not a test." is a "not X" construction. Use "Just for fun. You can change your mind."
- "Welcome" reassurance is overused enough to feel hollow: "Every combination is welcome" (`word-garden-ui.mjs:26`), "Leaving it blank is welcome" and "A short show is welcome too" (`loft-ui.mjs:17`), "Every scene is welcome" (`loft-ui.mjs:21`), "Every surprising response is welcome; the game does not judge a story" (`message-ui.mjs:40`), "Help and another try are always welcome" (`post-office-ui.mjs:103`). Keep one per screen at most.
- Preachy lines: `post-office-ui.mjs:93` Bramble's "A courier who asks for help is still a clever courier." The word "still" implies asking for help is a step down. Try "You found a way! My gate is free." `word-garden-content.mjs:25` "It would be unkind to laugh at a neighbor who is finding a job difficult." Try "Dot is wobbly on the ladder. Laughing would be unkind, so Pip holds it steady." `word-garden-content.mjs:44` "A word can describe one situation, rather than everything about a person." is an adult aside; it can go.
- `word-garden-content.mjs:39` "A less common real word!" The exclamation reads as forced. Use "This is a real word, but people rarely use it."
- `story-content.mjs:63` "Pip watches Luma in the story." "In the story" breaks the spell. Use "Pip watches Luma."
- `post-office-ui.mjs:55` label "A person to receive it" when the list holds a bunny, dragon, kitten and bird. Use "Who gets the parcel?"

## Lines worth keeping (voice reference)

- `story-content.mjs:63` "A beetle-sized notebook is mostly margin."
- `story-content.mjs:28` "Dot volunteers to be postmaster. The hat is still under discussion."
- `story-content.mjs:147` "Dot asks for a window with a view of something other than Pip’s ankle."
- `word-garden-content.mjs:12` "Pip unpacks the basket. The tools are dry, and so is his sandwich."
- `word-garden-content.mjs:29` "Standing on the map is unhelpful. The duck steps aside."
- `post-office-ui.mjs:114` "Parcels to pack. A broom to borrow. Neighbors to find."
- `loft-ui.mjs:25` "A small stage. A whole world to imagine."
- `word-garden-ui.mjs:26` "The storm scattered the library signs. Behind the library, words are growing again."

These lines share a few traits: short declaratives, one small comic or tender detail, and a sense of scale at a creature's height. The flat scene text in the message and letter cases would gain the most from that voice.
