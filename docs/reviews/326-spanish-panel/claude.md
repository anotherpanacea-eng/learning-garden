# Spec 326 Spanish phrases: Claude

Reviewer: Claude (Anthropic), Opus 5.5, in a cloud Claude Code session, 2026-10-31. Written before the other reports came back, and committed only after them.

1. **«¡Pan, cestas, miel!»** (chapter 1, Longer), glossed "Bread, baskets, honey." This is a natural market cry in Spain, correct as written: the opening ¡, plural *cestas*, and no articles, as street calls usually drop them. The gloss is exact. Confidence: high. Keep it.

2. **«Carrizo» ... «Reed.»** (chapter 2, Longer). *Carrizo* is the everyday Spain name for the common reed (*Phragmites australis*). It grows along riverbanks and wet ground, and it is used for baskets, matting and roofs (*cañizo*). It fits the setting and the gloss exactly. Confidence: high. Keep it.

3. **Cross-text finding: *junco* in the River Market text.** The 117 companion calls the same plant *el junco* and glosses it "reed". In Spain *junco* is the rush (*Juncus*), a different and much smaller plant that is no use for roofs. The gloss misleads, and the two texts disagree about one plant. Fix:
   - «...y una caja pequeña de semillas de carrizo.»
   - «El carrizo crece bien en la tierra mojada.»
   - Word list: *el carrizo* reed.
   - Phrasebook: *el carrizo* in place of *el junco*.
   - Sound note: "*J* is a breathy sound from the back of the throat: *jardín*." This keeps the J example on a word the errand already teaches.

   Confidence: medium-high. Reed seed is a minor point. Reeds spread mostly by their roots, but carrying seed in a box is fine for the story.

**Verdict: usable as written** for the two 326 phrases, with **the listed fix** to the River Market text.
