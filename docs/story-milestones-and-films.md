# Story milestones and film production plan

Planning brief begun 2026-10-21; joined course/media inventory updated 2026-10-25. **Spec 120 owns the current story names, contribution rules and reveals. The older generic closure table below is a dramatic reference, not an alternative runtime trigger contract.** The [six-act programme](specs/49-six-act-curriculum.md) owns the dramatic arc; individual activity specifications own their actual completion rules. This document identifies useful endings and commissionable films. The [current roadmap timing table](roadmap.md#films-and-songs-when-each-is-needed) owns commissioning order; this dated brief’s older “commission now” labels do not override that queue. Proposed triggers below are design requirements for future slices, **not implemented achievements**.

A story win means something was made, shared, tried or deliberately kept. It does not mean a subject was mastered. Help, narration, a smaller supplied plan and working together can reach the same ending. Every destination stays open. A film is optional presentation: its still picture and short text must deliver the complete ending when a file is unavailable, skipped, or unsuitable for reduced motion. No video asset is a prerequisite for playing or finishing.

## What to commission first

1. **The first celebration: A Town of Our Own.** A 35–50 second Act II anchor. This is the nearest new story horizon, after the town has recovered. Build the film around several contributions coming together, rather than a second repair montage. It must accommodate a small supported contribution as well as a large project.
2. **A tray for the gathering.** A 12–20 second optional chapter film for the whole-batch recipe slice. A prepared tray travels to a waiting table; another neighbor brings a different contribution. A completed-looking picture alone must not imply that the learner solved the arithmetic. No particular guest count or recipe appears in the film.
3. **Both ways across Willow Bridge.** A 40–55 second Act III anchor: an outward parcel and a return gift establish reciprocal exchange. Produce concept art now; lock the partner community and route only after its activity pack is reviewed.
4. **The map we changed**, **A question of our own**, and **The opening of the ways** are later act anchors. Storyboard them now, but defer final shots of maps, investigations and apparatus until their models and stories are specified. This avoids commissioning attractive scenes that teach the wrong mechanism or assume a single moral answer.

The existing storm, Paris, café and Bramble films already cover the strongest immediate Act I moments. Replacing those is lower priority than supplying the next act's ending. No new film is needed for each multiplication fact, correct answer, ingredient, chart or letter.

## Film briefs a producer can use

All titles and shot suggestions are original proposals. Existing character art is in `dist/`; use the actual game designs rather than an unrelated visual franchise. The world grows from recovery into belonging, reciprocity, disagreement, authorship and shared agency.

### A Town of Our Own — commission now

35–50 seconds. Open in a healthy, inhabited town on an ordinary evening. A prepared tray arrives, someone hangs a sign, another neighbor tends a small garden, and a puppet curtain opens. Use three or four contributions, not a checklist of all subjects. Bramble helps without a redemption test. The final wide shot shows people enjoying a shared place. A visiting courier leaves an invitation at the bridge.

Possible final line: “There is more than one way to make a place your own.” End with space for a local title/still. Do not claim “you mastered,” “you saved everyone,” or that every child chose a particular project. Keep the learner outside the fixed film; all avatar/guide choices remain valid.

### A tray for the gathering — commission now, optional

12–20 seconds. A neighbor carries two separate pretend-ingredient preparation jars on a tray to a gathering table. Another neighbor adds something different. Another pair of places is set at the table. No counters, written equation, exact guest count, disappearing stock, pouring into a real recipe or instant serving of the active café order.

The interactive batch model teaches the maths; this short scene makes its purpose visible. Possible final line: “One contribution can help make a gathering.” A small still offers the same ending until the film exists.

### Both Ways Across Willow Bridge — concept now, finish after Act III pack review

40–55 seconds. Show preparation in both communities, an outward journey, a meaningful exchange and a return parcel. Neither place is merely the other's lesson or rescue project. An unusual object or observation gives the next act a question. Keep route geometry, partner identity and trade quantities unspecified until the underlying pack is approved.

### The Map We Changed — storyboard later

35–50 seconds. Two sincere accounts lead to two possible plans. Someone notices a useful detail; a confident guide pauses and listens. A map or proposal is revised while a second reasonable possibility remains visible. Close on people taking a first reversible step together, with an unanswered question still on the noticeboard. No villain exposed by a quiz, moral ranking, or single mandatory preference.

### A Question of Our Own — storyboard later

35–50 seconds. A question leads to a small first version, a surprising response, and a deliberate revision or smaller design. Use a neutral fictional example that does not pretend to be the learner's saved project. Show consultation as capable work. Close on a contribution shared with a co-present audience; never imply online publication, a personal-data upload or applause as a writing-quality score.

### The Opening of the Ways — finale concept now, production later

50–75 seconds. Several communities prepare an exchange: provisions, a route, a careful message, a considered habitat choice and a signal. These are contributions rather than compulsory branches. The first reciprocal journey succeeds on a modest scale. At the end a new apprentice arrives with an unfamiliar parcel. The familiar guide stands alongside an open place at the table. The learner is invited to help someone else begin.

Possible final line: “There will always be another question. This time, someone new has come to ask it with you.” The town is thriving, not wrecked again. Leave room for continued partnerships and investigations after the credits.

## Delivery and integration contract

- Supply an editable/source version, a silent-playable picture version, optional narration/music stems, captions/transcript and a representative poster still. A self-contained HTML film can reuse today's player contract; a conventional video file will need a separately checked media adapter. Do not assume an MP4 can simply replace an existing HTML iframe.
- For an HTML delivery, use `dist/bramble-film.html` as the reference: same-origin self-contained page; `?autoplay=1&embedded=1`; silent picture autoplay; manual poster/still under reduced motion; explicit Sound joining at the current picture with the optional `audioSession` playback hint; Pause/Play/Replay and Space controls and stop on `pagehide`. It has no captions or transcript (the picture is a single labelled canvas), so a new film must supply them as the first bullet requires. Send `postMessage('garden-film-finished', location.origin)` at the end. Declare any teaching-exposure message per film; do not send the Bramble-specific example message for unrelated scenes. The host dialog owns Skip/Escape and focus return. No runtime network fetch. MP4/stem integration waits for a separately reviewed adapter.
- No story or teaching information may depend on hearing the soundtrack or finishing the film. Sound starts only by explicit action, joins at the current picture and never restarts it. Pause/stop/skip/replay, keyboard focus and a complete reduced-motion still/text route must work.
- The current player records **seen when opened**, not watched to completion. New integrations must distinguish offered/opened/skipped/replayed from the underlying story action. None is evidence of reading, recall, scientific understanding or mastery.
- Use fixed fictional people, places and artifacts. No real child names, photos, voices, responses, progress, dialogue or exported notebooks go to production. A locally shown learner artifact stays outside the fixed asset and requires its own explicit activity integration.
- Avoid showing the answer to an active task. If a film deliberately displays a numerical example or source evidence, the activity must account for that exact exposure before later describing a task as unfamiliar. Prefer answer-free cinematic shots.
- The existing films are local generated visuals/audio with no service dependency. New files need documented rights and credits; no copied film/game characters, third-party music, remote trackers or runtime generation. Keep file size and decode cost modest, avoid rapid flashing and test real Apple sound behavior before claiming a device fix.
- No asset is commissioned as a substitute for a missing lesson, a scientific model review, phonics audio review or a learner-controlled explanatory model. Explanatory animations and celebratory films have different jobs.
