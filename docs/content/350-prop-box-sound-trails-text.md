Art ids are fox350, fog350, quit350, mix350, quiz350; kit348 reuses the selected tool-set sense and original artwork if practical. A kit is not a young animal here. The quiz has no questions to answer in this activity. The mixing picture does not blend the beads into one new color. No real ingredients or physical props are required.

Vowels in fox/fog vary by region and need not sound identical in the two words. This pair contrasts counts, not a promised one-phoneme substitution. No speech correction, required mouth position or audible-release test. The selected words do not demonstrate x in exam/xylophone, qu in other borrowed words, or u's usual vowel value. q is usually followed by u in English spelling; do not tell the child that no exception exists.

## Five manual model frames for each word

Each row below gives frame0 (whole word), frames1/2/3 (the three mapped groups in table order), and frame4 (whole mapping/summary). Pictures and meanings are supplied throughout. The adult's reference pronunciation is folded separately; the child may point, join in or listen.

### fox
0. Read fox together. Your partner says the whole word.
1. Point to f. Your partner says the first sound.
2. Point to o. Your partner says the vowel sound.
3. Point to x. Listen for two sounds at the end.
4. Three letters, four sounds. In fox, x helps write two sounds.

### fog
0. Read fog together. Your partner says the whole word.
1. Point to f. Your partner says the first sound.
2. Point to o. Your partner says the vowel sound.
3. Point to g. Your partner says the last sound.
4. Three letters, three sounds in our model of fog.

### kit
0. Read kit together. Your partner says the whole word.
1. Point to k. Your partner says the first sound.
2. Point to i. Your partner says the vowel sound.
3. Point to t. Your partner says the last sound.
4. Three letters, three sounds in our model of kit.

### quit
0. Read quit together. Your partner says the whole word.
1. Point to q and u. Your partner says the first two sounds.
2. Point to i. Your partner says the vowel sound.
3. Point to t. Your partner says the last sound.
4. Four letters, four sounds. In quit, q and u work together at the start.

### mix
0. Read mix together. Your partner says the whole word.
1. Point to m. Your partner says the first sound.
2. Point to i. Your partner says the vowel sound.
3. Point to x. Listen for two sounds at the end.
4. Three letters, four sounds. In mix, x helps write two sounds.

### quiz
0. Read quiz together. Your partner says the whole word.
1. Point to q and u. Your partner says the first two sounds.
2. Point to i. Your partner says the vowel sound.
3. Point to z. Your partner says the last sound.
4. Four letters, four sounds. In quiz, q and u work together at the start.

- Heading **kit and quit**. Text: **Our model has three sounds in kit and four in quit. Listen with your partner. The start of quit has two sounds.**

## Folded adult guidance

Shared: **Say the word naturally, then model its sounds without adding uh. Sound dots show speech sounds, not syllables or measured timing. The printed word and picture are supplied. A count of counters does not show how a child read or spoke. They may point, join in or listen. This page does not listen, record or grade speech. Counters and model steps last only for this visit and are not added to Grown-up notes. Other words can use these letters differently.**

Context guidance, supplied with the current word:
- fox/mix: **At the end, x represents /k/ followed by /s/ in this word. Model both without adding a vowel. These are two phonemes, not one sound called the letter name.**
- fog: **Use the local natural vowel in fog. It may differ from fox. Model three sounds without demanding a released final g.**
- kit: **Use the tool-set meaning. Model three sounds. The child may have seen this printed word before.**
- quit/quiz: **In this word, qu writes /k/ followed by /w/. Keep two sound dots even though the two letters are highlighted together. This u is not the middle vowel sound in cup. Say the whole word naturally rather than the letter names.**

The reference notation is adult-only. No accepted phonics recording, synthesis model or oral answer is supplied.
