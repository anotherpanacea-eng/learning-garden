**About this model:** “The pole keeps its length. The push unit follows its end sideways, always pushing straight down. The pivot stays still while each end follows an arc. We imagine very slow, controlled changes and ignore acceleration, pole weight, bending and friction. A brake holds each checkpoint. Our force numbers come from balance calculations, not a real force meter. Real lifting needs suitable equipment and trained people.”

**Why a longer push arm helps:** “A 12 N load at the middle setting needs 12 N through 1 m downwards. At the nearer-load setting, it needs 6 N through 2 m downwards. Both raise the load 1 m and put in 12 J of work. The longer push arm trades less force for more vertical movement.”

**Why not measure the curved path?** “Our push points straight down, so only the downward part of its movement counts in that push's work. The end moves sideways too. Multiplying this vertical force by the whole curved path would give the wrong answer.”

Grown-ups: “This is a supplied quasi-static model, not a dynamic speed simulation, physical experiment or assessment. The pole is treated as rigid and weightless. We balance turning effects at each position and imagine a controlled transition, rather than claiming balanced torques start motion by themselves. The stationary pivot does no work. The ideal input work equals the load–Earth potential energy gain. Real machines have additional losses and weight. No actions, help requests, reading or hearing results are recorded.”
