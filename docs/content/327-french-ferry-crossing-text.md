# 327 · Le bac: finite text

Companion to [spec 327](../specs/327-french-ferry-crossing.md). Proposed runtime text, not shipped. Every French line has the English beside it. The French is bound to the [327 panel](../reviews/327-french-panel/resolution.md), as resolved there, and any changed line needs a new pass. Shipped French puts a no-break space inside « » and a narrow no-break space before ? and !. Speech gets the French without the guillemets.

The ferry keeper, Madame Roux, speaks to the explorer and Dot together, so she uses *vous*. The explorer's answers are short and need no verb forms beyond *nous sommes*.

## Question words (the teaching card)

Shown before the first crossing, and from its link at any time. Each French line has a **Hear it** button. The *Où ?* and *D'où ?* buttons speak the whole questions, *Où allez-vous ?* and *D'où venez-vous ?*.

| French | English | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Où ? | Where? | Here, *Où allez-vous ?* asks where you are going. In this game, the place answer starts with *À*: *À la gare.* (To the station.) |
| D'où ? | Where from? | Here, *d'* is a short form of *de*, meaning "from". *D'où venez-vous ?* asks where you are coming from. In this game, the place answer starts with *De*: *De la gare.* (From the station.) |
| Combien ? | How many? / How much? | For people or things, it asks how many, and the answer gives a number: *Nous sommes quatre.* (There are four of us.) In *Combien coûte… ?*, it asks how much something costs. |
| À quelle heure ? | At what time? | The answer has a time: *À huit heures.* (At eight o'clock.) |

**Listen for the difference.** *Où allez-vous ?* asks where you are going. *D'où venez-vous ?* asks where you are coming from. One small sound, *d'*, changes the question.

**Dot shows you first.** Madame Roux: « Combien de colis avez-vous ? » How many parcels do you have? Dot: « Trois colis. » Three parcels. Dot: "She asked *combien*, so I gave her a number."

## Numbers that sound alike (the sounds card)

Each phrase has its own **Hear it** button.

| French | English |

| deux heures | two o'clock |
| dix heures | ten o'clock |
| douze heures | twelve o'clock |
| trois euros | three euros |
| treize euros | thirteen euros |
| trente euros | thirty euros |

**Listen for the differences.** Hear these phrases: *deux heures, dix heures, douze heures*; *trois euros, treize euros, trente euros*. Each group has different vowel sounds. Listen to the whole phrase, too: *trente euros* links with a "t" sound before *euros*, where *trois euros* and *treize euros* link with a "z" sound. Hear them again and compare.

**Step 5, your turn to ask.** Prompt: Ask Madame Roux when the ferry leaves. Say: « À quelle heure part le bac ? » (What time does the ferry leave?) Then **Hear her answer**: « Le bac part à dix heures. » (The ferry leaves at ten o'clock.) Choices, as clock faces with text: 2 o'clock · 10 o'clock · 12 o'clock. Fitting: 10 o'clock.

**Ending:** Madame Roux: « Merci. Montez, s'il vous plaît. » Thank you. Come aboard, please.

## Grown-up notes

**Line:** The ferry crossing (French): {Le bac du matin / Le bac de l'après-midi} {crossed / started / not started}{, words shown / , no French voice}; French speech started with her words hidden on {n} of 5; said aloud {n} of 5 (the explorer's own choice of button).

**Details:** This is listening and speaking practice in a supplied situation, not a French assessment. The game hears nothing: there is no microphone and no speech recognition. **I said it** records only that the explorer chose that button. The notes can say that French speech started while Madame Roux's words were hidden. They can't show that anyone heard, understood or finished listening. The voice is the device's own French speech, not a recording of a French speaker. It can't show how well anyone speaks or understands French, and it doesn't change the French reading activities.
