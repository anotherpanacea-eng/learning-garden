Original project-owned additions. Existing base-case scene/request/question strings in `message-content.mjs` are reused, not rewritten. Freeze the selected purpose and these additions with each elected record. Braces below refer only to the explicitly chosen reader/reference; never interpolate unescaped authored HTML.

## Entry and plan

- Entry: **My own message**.
- Intro: **A message can help someone know what to do. Choose a reader, then tell or write it.**
- Readers: basket = **A helper at the Post Office who can see two baskets**; seed-packet = **Pip, who needs to know where to take the seeds**; flags = **Dot, who needs to know what to do with the flags**.

- Modes: **Tell it or use paper** / **Write on this device**.
- Oral/paper: **Tell your message to someone, or write it on paper. Your spoken or paper words are not recorded.**
- Typed choice: **Your words stay in this explorer's notebook under your save settings. Notes downloads include them. Use pretend names.**
- Existing prose: **Your earlier words are waiting. Opening this turn does not replace them.**
- Plan action: **I'm ready to write**. Oral equivalent: **I'm ready to tell it**.
- Next: **Ask the reader**. Back: **Back to my message**. Choose: **Choose another reader**.

## Teach and contrast

Teaching title: **A word worth checking**.
Teaching: **Say your message first. Pick a word you want to check. Look at a clue or ask someone to find it in a dictionary. Compare the letters, then read your message again.**
Contrast title: **Clear words and careful spelling**.
Contrast: **“Bring the basket” can be spelled correctly and still leave a question. Which basket? A reader needs useful details too.**
Worked example, explicitly a different note:
1. **“Bring my coat.” Two coats are hanging by the door.**
2. **The reader asks, “Which coat?”**
3. **“Bring my raincoat with the star patch.” The detail helps the reader choose.**
4. **Now the writer can check a spelling too. Both jobs can help.**
Example controls: **Show the next step** / **Previous step**.
Oral checking plan: **If a spelling feels uncertain, tell someone how you could check it. Then try that plan together.**

## Human reader and revision

Heading: **What does the reader need?**
Always reuse the selected base-case question as an authored question, visibly labeled **A question to try together**. Questions remain the basket helper's “Which basket should I bring: the blankets or the seed packets?”, Pip's “Where should I take the seed packet: the garden bed or the footbridge?”, Dot's “Should I hang the flags or fold them at the footbridge?” They are supplied questions, not responses generated from the child's writing.

Second question: **Is there a word you want to check? Show me how you could check it.**
Human-reader note: **A person reads or hears your message. The game does not decide what your own words mean. You can keep an unexpected idea.**
Revision prompt: **Which words would you keep? Which would you change for this reader?**
Oral revision prompt: **Tell it another way, or change it on your paper.**

## Grown-up notes

Heading: **Spelling for a reader**.
Meaning: **The child chose a purpose and could compose, consult a spelling reference and revise for a reader. These are exact elected versions, not marks or levels. A copied word does not show spelling from memory.**
Support: **The reader question was supplied. Opened teaching, displayed references and read-aloud are recorded where readable. Human help, dictionary use, paper work and device spelling suggestions are not tracked. Missing help flags do not mean independent work.**
History: **The first and latest explicitly kept versions are shown. Intermediate drafts and spoken or paper responses are not recorded. The current typed draft is separate. Original words are included in Notes downloads.**

## Turn and reference navigation labels

The four turn labels are **Plan**, **Write or tell**, **Read together**, **Revise**, with **Back**, **Next** and a literal position **{n} of 4** (1–4). Other headings/navigation: **My message**, **Revise for this reader**, **My kept versions**, **Back to the note cards**, **Look at the scene and note**. A reload without a pending reference says **This pending clue is no longer here. Your word-study work stays.** The reader's existing purpose/title stays visible; no generated reader response is implied.
