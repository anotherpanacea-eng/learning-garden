# Supper-Table Talk: all child-facing text

Companion to [spec 253](../specs/253-supper-table-talk.md). Use as written, after review. `{guide}` is never gendered. Pronouns: Pip, Hob and the baker he/him; Dot, Bramble and the orchard keeper she/her; the librarian and Hollis they/them; Ember, Puff and the new apprentice take no pronouns. Sources: [audit](../source-audits/253-supper-table-talk.md). Nothing is scored, timed or locked.

**Speaking rule:** every talk card is spoken with a grown-up, a sibling or a friend. Nothing is recorded or stored, and there is no microphone. Cards are about the town, the story, things on the table or made-up situations, never about the child.

**Labels used throughout:**
- **A story from Willowbank** marks the town, its people and its suppers (fiction).
- **A true thing about language** marks how talk works.
- **A made-up model** is not used in this pack.

## Arriving

**Place title:** Supper-Table Talk

**First visit** (A story from Willowbank):
> In the corner of the café, a tin jar sits on the long table. It is full of folded cards.
>
> "Talk cards," says Puff. "Pull one out. Then talk about it with someone."
>
> "What if I just eat?" says Pip.
>
> "Then you'll miss the best part of supper," says Dot.

**Every talk card (always shown):** Say it out loud with a grown-up, a sibling or a friend. Nothing is recorded.

## Story scenes

### Scene 1. Pip describes the soup (Act I, the welcome-back supper)

**Hook (A story from Willowbank):** Six tables, six lanterns, one very hungry fox. Pip can smell the soup before he can see it.

**Short:**
> At the welcome-back supper, Pip describes the soup so well that everyone wants some. Dot says where a lantern should go, and why.

**Longer:**
> The café is full. Six lanterns glow over six tables.
>
> "What's for supper?" asks Dot.
>
> Pip shuts his eyes and sniffs. "Orange soup. Thick. Little green bits on top. It smells of carrots and pepper, and it's steaming."
>
> Puff peers into the pot. "It is orange. It does have green bits."
>
> "Pip," says {guide}, "I can see that soup with my eyes shut."
>
> "I've been thinking about it all day," says Pip.
>
> Dot moves the blue lantern to the long table. "This one goes here," she says, "because it's the brightest, and the long table has the most people."
>
> Nobody argues. Pip is already on his second bowl.

### Scene 2. Two cakes and a talking spoon (Act II ch. 3, Ember's birthday at the café, in the Harvest Supper season)

**Hook:** It's Ember's birthday. The baker has made two cakes. Everyone has an opinion, and everyone wants to say it at once.

**Short:**
> At Ember's birthday, everyone talks at once about the cakes. Ember holds up a spoon: whoever holds it talks. Now everyone gets a turn.

**Longer:**
> The baker sets down two cakes. "Plum," he says, "dark and crumbly. And lemon, pale and sharp."
>
> "Plum!" "Lemon!" "Both!" Everyone talks at once. Nobody hears anything.
>
> Ember holds up a wooden spoon. "Whoever holds the spoon talks. Everyone else listens. Then pass it on."
>
> Pip gets it first. "Plum, because it's sweet." He passes it.
>
> Dot takes it. "Lemon, because it's fresh. And Pip's right that plum is sweet."
>
> The door bangs. Bramble sweeps in with her magpie and a fistful of ribbons. She takes the spoon. "Lemon, because it's the best."
>
> "That's the same thing twice," says Ember.
>
> Bramble thinks. "Lemon, because my magpie likes yellow things."
>
> "That's a reason," says Ember. "A strange one. But a reason."
>
> The baker cuts both.

**Keeper's note (A true thing about language):**
> Many classrooms agree on talk rules before a discussion: one speaker at a time, listen to the end, and give a reason. A spoon, a shell or a ball can show whose turn it is.

### Scene 3. Hob's long fence story (Harvest Supper, any year)

**Hook:** Hob Pennick has a story about his fence. It is a long story. It has a goat in it, eventually.

**Short:**
> Hob's fence story goes on and on. Pip folds a napkin boat. Hob notices, skips to the goat, and now everyone listens.

**Longer:**
> "So," says Hob, "the first post was oak. The second post was also oak. The third post..."
>
> Pip folds his napkin into a boat. Puff watches the window.
>
> Hob stops. He looks at the napkin boat. He looks at Puff.
>
> "I've lost you," he says. "Shall I skip to the goat?"
>
> "There's a goat?" says Pip.
>
> "Standing on top of the fence. Every morning. Eating my hat."
>
> Now everyone is listening. Puff turns from the window. Pip's boat sinks slowly into the gravy.
>
> "Why didn't you start with the goat?" asks Dot.
>
> Hob scratches his head. "I suppose I should have."

### Scene 4. Supper with Hollis (Act VI ch. 4; shown only once this chapter is reached)

**Hook:** Hollis, the old Keeper, has come home and stays for supper. "Tell me what happened," they say. Everyone starts at once.

**Short:**
> Hollis asks what happened while they were away. Pip starts in the middle. Dot tells it in order, from the storm to the long rain.

**Longer:**
> Hollis folds their long gray legs under the table. "Tell me what happened while I was away."
>
> "The soup was orange!" says Pip. "And the magpie took a spoon, and then there was a storm, and..."
>
> "Then?" asks Hollis. "Or first?"
>
> Dot sets down her cup. "First, the storm. Then everyone helped mend the town, and we had a welcome-back supper. After that, the market came back. Then letters came from up and down the river. At last, the long rain came, and our plan held."
>
> Hollis nods slowly. "In order. Like a good ledger."
>
> "I was getting to the storm," says Pip.

### After Act VI's ending: Someone new at the table (shown only after that ending)

**Hook:** The new apprentice sits at the long table with an unfamiliar parcel. Dot wants to tell the apprentice about the café. Help her plan a short talk.

**Short:**
> Dot remembers being new. She plans a short talk about the café: a start, three facts, and an ending. You help her choose.

## Teach cards

Each card is 30 words or fewer; worked-example steps appear one tap at a time. Every card is **A true thing about language**.

**Card 1. Paint a picture with words**
> To describe something, say what your listener would notice: its size, shape and color, what it's made of, and any sound or smell.

Worked example: *Ember's plum cake.*
1. Size and shape: tall and round.
2. Color: dark purple, with green sugar leaves.
3. Smell: warm plums.

**Card 2. Describe in an order**
> Describe things in an order, like top to bottom. Your listener builds the picture one piece at a time.

Worked example: *Dot's hat, top to bottom.*
1. Top: a long feather.
2. Middle: a red crown.
3. Bottom: a wide, floppy brim.

**Card 3. Wonder out loud**
> A wondering tries out an idea before you are sure. Start with "I wonder…", "What if…" or "Maybe it's because…". A wondering can change.

Worked example: *The lanterns flicker, but no window is open.*
1. I wonder why they flicker.
2. Maybe it's because the door keeps opening.
3. Let's watch the door and see.

**Card 4. Try a new word**
> When you hear a new word in a story, try it out in talk. Using a word helps you keep it.

Worked example: *"Crumbly" means it breaks into small bits.*
1. The baker said the plum cake was crumbly.
2. What else breaks into bits? A dry roll.
3. Try it: "This roll is crumbly."

**Card 5. An opinion and a reason**
> An opinion is what you think. A reason says why. People can think different things. Say it like this: "I think… because…"

Worked example: *Pip at Ember's birthday.*
1. Opinion: plum cake is best.
2. Reason: it's sweet.
3. Together: "I think plum is best, because it's sweet."

**Card 6. A reason has to fit**
> A reason has to help your choice. Saying your choice again isn't a reason. A point for the other choice doesn't help. A point against it can.

Worked example: *Bramble picks lemon cake, not plum cake.*
1. "Because it's the best" just says lemon again.
2. "Because it's fresh" is a point for lemon. It fits.
3. "Because the plum cake is stale" is a point against plum. That fits too.

**Card 7. Take turns**
> One person talks at a time. Listen to the end. Then take your turn. A talking spoon shows whose turn it is.

Worked example: *Ember's spoon.*
1. Ember holds the spoon, so Ember talks.
2. Pip has an idea. He waits.
3. Ember passes the spoon. Now Pip talks.

**Card 8. Link your turn**
> Stay on the topic. Link your turn to the last one: "Yes, and…", "I agree, because…" or "I think something different, because…".

Worked example: *Talking about the café sign.*
1. Dot: "A teapot."
2. Pip links: "Yes, and soup coming out of the spout."
3. Same topic, one new idea.

**Card 9. Keep your listener with you**
> Start with something interesting. Look at your listener. If they drift off, ask them a question or skip to the good part.

Worked example: *Hob's fence story.*
1. Hob starts with posts. Everyone drifts off.
2. Hob notices the napkin boat.
3. He skips to the goat. Everyone listens.

**Card 10. Every view gets heard**
> When someone thinks differently, say their reason back first. Then give yours: "I see why you think that. I think something different, because…"

Worked example: *Where should the supper be?*
1. Bramble: "At the gate. There's more room."
2. Dot: "More room is a good point."
3. Dot: "I'd still pick the café, because it's warm."

**Card 11. Retell it in order**
> A retelling has a beginning, a middle and an end. Time words like first, then, after that and at last keep your listener on track.

Worked example: *Ember's birthday, in order.*
1. First, the baker brought two cakes.
2. Then everyone talked at once, so Ember held up a spoon.
3. At last, the baker cut both cakes.

**Card 12. A short report (Keeper's Desk)**
> A report starts by naming its topic, gives facts about that topic in a sensible order, and ends by wrapping up. Pause after each fact.

Worked example: *A talk about the café.*
1. Opening: "Let me tell you about the café."
2. Three café facts, one at a time.
3. Closing: "So come in and find a table."

## Benches

### (a) Describe It

**Header:** Pick a card. Describe it so your partner can picture it.

**The noticing hand (one finger each):** How big? · What shape? · What color? · What is it made of? · Any sound or smell?

**Talk cards:**
1. Describe Ember's plum cake.
2. Describe Dot's hat, from the top down.
3. Describe the café lantern. Start at the top.
4. Describe the miller's cat, from nose to tail.
5. Describe the baker's loaf. What sound does it make, broken open?
6. Make up a teapot. Describe it so your partner could draw it.

**Swap:** Now your partner describes a card. You guess which one.

**Guess the Thing (on screen, three rounds):** Read the three clues. Tap the picture they describe.
- Round 1 clues: It has a wide brim. It is red. It has a feather. Pictures: Bramble's pointy black hat · Dot's hat ✔ · the café lantern
- Round 2 clues: It is soft. It has whiskers. It sleeps by the mill wheel. Pictures: the magpie · Hob's lamb · the miller's cat ✔
- Round 3 clues: It is tall. It is purple. It has sugar leaves. Pictures: the plum cake ✔ · the lemon cake · the café lantern

**Guess feedback (any wrong picture):** That fits some clues, not all. Check every clue.

### (b) I Wonder

**Header:** Wonder out loud. Try an idea before you are sure.

**Starters:** I wonder why… · What if… · Maybe it's because… · I'm not sure yet, but…

**Talk cards:**
1. The lanterns flicker, but no window is open. Wonder why.
2. What if Willow Brook ran uphill?
3. Puff's cup was full. Now it's empty. Who drank it, do you think?
4. What if the café floated on a boat?
5. What if Pip's tail could talk? What would it say?
6. Try a new word: say a sentence with "crumbly". Then "steaming". Then "flicker".

**New-word cards (from the scenes):** crumbly (breaks into small bits) · steaming (so hot it gives off steam) · flicker (shine on and off, unsteadily)

### (c) Because

**Header:** Say what you think. Then say why. People can think different things.

**Frame card:** I think… because…

**Talk cards:**
1. Plum cake or lemon cake for Ember? Say why.
2. Which lantern should go on the long table? Say why.
3. Should the Harvest Supper be inside or outside? Say why.
4. What should the café paint on its new sign? Say why.
5. Which is the best seat in the café? Say why.
6. Should Pip get a third bowl of soup? Say why.

**After each card:** Your partner's turn. Listen for "because".

**On screen:** Which reason fits? (Set C below.)

### (d) Pass the Spoon

**Header:** Hold the spoon to talk. Pass it on when you're done.

**Talk rules card:** One talker at a time. · Listen to the end. · Link your turn to the last one. · Pass the spoon.

**Linking starters:** Yes, and… · I agree, because… · I think something different, because… · That makes me wonder…

**Topic cards (keep going for three turns or more):**
1. What should the café paint on its new sign?
2. Plan a picnic for Ember. What should we bring?
3. Make up a new game for Harvest Supper.
4. What could Hob do about the goat on his fence?

**Two of you?** Pass the spoon back and forth.

### (e) Keep Them Listening

**Header:** A good talker watches the listener.

**Drifting-off cards (Willowbank pictures):** Pip folds a napkin boat. · Puff looks out the window. · Ember yawns. · Bramble says, "Wait, what?"

**Bring-them-back cards:** Ask a question. · Skip to the good part. · Change your voice: slower, quieter, or excited. · Check in: "Shall I go on?"

**Note:** Sometimes a yawn just means someone is sleepy. Check in and see.

**Talk cards:**
1. Tell Hob's goat story. Start with the most surprising part.
2. Tell your partner about the plum cake. Ask them a question halfway.
3. Bramble wants supper at the gate. Say her reason, then yours.

### (f) Tell It in Order

**Header:** Tell it so your listener can follow: beginning, middle, end.

**Time words:** first · next · then · after that · at last · so

**Market-day cards (A story from Willowbank; Puff missed market day):**
- At dawn, the baker's cart rolled to the bridge.
- On the bridge, a wheel came off.
- Then everyone carried the loaves by hand.
- So the market opened an hour late.
- At the end of the day, Pip ate the squashed loaf.

**Talk cards:**
1. Tell Puff about market day, with three time words.
2. Retell Ember's birthday, from two cakes to two slices.
3. Retell Hob's goat story, in order.

**Act VI card (after Act VI ch. 4 only):** Tell Hollis what happened, in order: storm, supper, market, letters, rain.

**A Short Talk About the Café (Keeper's Desk):** Help Dot plan a talk for someone new.
- Plan: pick an opening, three facts and a closing.
- Then: give the talk to a grown-up.
- Openings: Um. So. The café. · Let me tell you about the café. ✔ · Did you know the brook has fish?
- Fact cards: Six lanterns hang over the tables. ✔ · Willow Brook runs past the mill. · Ember and Puff come most days. ✔ · Bramble's gate has ribbons. · Guests are served at their tables. ✔
- Closings: That's it. Bye. · Also, the mill has a wheel. · So come in and find a table. ✔
- Off-topic pick feedback: Is it about the café? Keep the talk on its topic.
- **Pace tip:** Pause after each fact. Can your grown-up say it back?

**Act VI version:** the same plan, for the new apprentice. **Standalone version:** the same plan, "for someone new in town".

## Problem sets

Every item has the "I'm stuck" ladder. Choices appear in the order written; ✔ marks the fitting one. Tags in backticks name the misconception a non-fitting choice tests. Opinion items key only whether a reason fits, using the card's facts; no opinion is keyed.

### Set A. Describe it (Sprout, Seedling)

**A1 (Sprout, guided).** Steps shown first: 1. Picture the plum cake. 2. Which words show what you'd see? 3. Pick the one that paints a picture.
- Prompt: Which words help you picture Ember's plum cake?
- Choices: "A cake that is on the table by Ember." (`no-picture-words`) · "Tall and purple, with sugar leaves." ✔ · "I like that cake more than bread." (`opinion-not-description`)
- Feedback: By Ember: That says where it is. What does it look like? · More than bread: That's an opinion. What would your eyes see?
- Hint: Close your eyes. What color is it?
- Explanation: Size, color and shape words paint a picture.

**A2 (Sprout).**
- Card: Dot holds up her hat. Pip says, "Dot's hat is red."
- Prompt: What could Pip add to help you picture it?
- Choices: "It is red. It is really, really red." (`repeat-not-detail`) · "Bramble has a pointy black hat too." (`off-topic`) · "It has a wide brim and a feather." ✔
- Feedback: Really red: That says red again. Add something new. · Bramble: That's Bramble's hat. Stay with Dot's.
- Hint: Look at the hat's shape. What else is on it?
- Explanation: A new detail, like a brim or a feather, shows your listener more.

**A3 (Seedling).**
- Card: Three clues. It is round. It is warm. It smells of seeds and crust.
- Prompt: Which thing on the table do the clues describe?
- Choices: The baker's loaf ✔ · The café lantern (`not-every-clue`) · Puff's teapot (`not-every-clue`)
- Feedback: Lantern: Round and warm, maybe. Does a lantern smell of crust? · Teapot: Round and warm, yes. Check the smell clue.
- Hint: Use all three clues, not just one.
- Explanation: Only the loaf fits every clue.

**A4 (Seedling).**
- Card: Pip describes the café lantern for Puff.
- Prompt: Which description goes in an order that is easy to follow?
- Choices: "Top: a brass ring. Middle: blue glass. Bottom: a candle." ✔ · "Blue glass, then a candle, then a brass ring, then blue glass again." (`jumbled-order`) · "It's a lantern with a candle. A candle lantern. It's a nice lantern." (`repeat-not-detail`)
- Feedback: Blue glass again: It jumps around and repeats. Try top to bottom. · Nice lantern: That says lantern and candle again. Where are the parts?
- Hint: Start at the top and work down.
- Explanation: Top to bottom builds the picture one piece at a time.

### Set B. I wonder (Sprout, Seedling)

**B1 (Sprout, guided).** Steps shown first: 1. A telling says what is so. 2. A wondering tries an idea. 3. Listen for "I wonder".
- Prompt: Which one is Pip wondering out loud?
- Choices: "The soup is hot and full of carrots." (`telling-not-wondering`) · "I wonder who made the soup." ✔ · "I ate three rolls and half of Dot's." (`telling-not-wondering`)
- Feedback: Carrots: That tells what is so. It doesn't try an idea. · Three rolls: That tells what happened. Listen for "I wonder".
- Hint: Which one sounds like a question in Pip's head?
- Explanation: "I wonder" starts a wondering. It tries an idea out loud.

**B2 (Seedling).**
- Card: Puff's cup was full when Puff went out. Now the cup is empty. The miller's cat has wet whiskers.
- Prompt: Which "maybe" uses the clues?
- Choices: "Maybe Puff drank it before going out." (`ignores-clue`) · "Maybe the cat lapped it up." ✔ · "Maybe the cup was never full at all." (`ignores-clue`)
- Feedback: Before going out: The card says the cup was full when Puff left. · Never full: The card says it was full. Use that clue.
- Hint: Who has wet whiskers?
- Explanation: The cat fits every clue. It's a good maybe, not a sure thing.

**B3 (Seedling).**
- Card: The baker says the plum cake is "crumbly". It breaks into small bits.
- Prompt: Which sentence uses "crumbly" the way the baker did?
- Choices: "The brook is crumbly and cold today, too." (`word-wrong-sense`) · "Crumbly, crumbly! I just like saying crumbly." (`word-no-meaning`) · "My roll is crumbly. Bits everywhere." ✔
- Feedback: Brook: Water doesn't break into small bits. · Just saying it: Fun to say! Now use it to tell about something.
- Hint: What breaks into small bits at supper?
- Explanation: A new word sticks when you use it to tell about something.

**B4 (Seedling).**
- Prompt: Dot asks, "What if the café had no chairs?" Which reply explores it?
- Choices: "Maybe we'd sit on cushions, like a picnic." ✔ · "It has chairs, Dot. I'm sitting on one right now." (`shuts-down-idea`) · "Can I please have more soup, and another roll?" (`change-subject`)
- Feedback: Sitting on one: True! But "what if" asks you to imagine. · More soup: That changes the subject. Try imagining.
- Hint: "What if" asks you to pretend. What might happen?
- Explanation: "Maybe…" explores a "what if". Any idea can be tried out.

### Set C. Because (Sprout, Seedling, Sapling)

**C1 (Sprout, guided).** Steps shown first: 1. What does Dot think? 2. Find a fact about her choice. 3. Does it say why?
- Card: Dot thinks the blue lantern should go on the long table. Facts: The blue lantern is the brightest. The red lantern has a new candle.
- Prompt: Which reason fits Dot's opinion?
- Choices: "because it is the brightest" ✔ · "because the red one has a new candle" (`other-side-reason`) · "because I just think it should" (`no-reason`)
- Feedback: New candle: That's a reason for the red lantern. Dot picked blue. · Just think so: That doesn't say why. What about the blue lantern?
- Hint: Find a fact about the blue lantern.
- Explanation: A reason that fits tells why Dot's choice makes sense.

**C2 (Seedling).**
- Card: Pip thinks soup is the best supper. Facts: Soup comes with rolls. Salad is crunchy. Pip likes crunchy food.
- Prompt: Which reason fits Pip's opinion?
- Choices: "because it is the best supper" (`says-it-again`) · "because salad is crunchy" (`other-side-reason`) · "because it comes with rolls" ✔
- Feedback: Best supper: That says his opinion again. Why is it best? · Crunchy: Pip likes crunchy food, so that's a point for salad.
- Hint: Which fact is a point for soup?
- Explanation: "Because it comes with rolls" is a point for soup. It says why.

**C3 (Seedling).**
- Card: Dot thinks lemon cake is best, because it's fresh. Pip thinks plum cake is best, because it's sweet.
- Prompt: Pip and Dot disagree. What is true?
- Choices: "Only one of them can be right about cake." (`one-right-opinion`) · "Both gave an opinion and a reason." ✔ · "Neither of them gave any reason at all." (`missed-reason`)
- Feedback: One right: They like different cakes. A liking isn't right or wrong. · No reason: Listen again. Each one said "because".
- Hint: Did each one say "because"?
- Explanation: Two opinions can both stand. Each has its own reason.

**C4 (Seedling).**
- Card: Bramble says, "The ribbon gate is the best gate, because it's the best gate." Facts: The ribbon gate has bells. It never squeaks.
- Prompt: Which reason could Bramble use instead?
- Choices: "because it really, truly is the best" (`says-it-again`) · "because it never squeaks" ✔ · "because I said so, that's why" (`no-reason`)
- Feedback: Truly best: That says "best" again. Why is it best? · Said so: That isn't a reason. Use a fact about the gate.
- Hint: Find a fact about the ribbon gate.
- Explanation: A reason adds something new. "It never squeaks" says why.

**C5 (Sapling).**
- Card: The orchard keeper thinks the Harvest Supper should be outside. Facts: The sky is clear. The café holds twenty guests. Forty guests are coming.
- Prompt: Which reason uses a fact from the card?
- Choices: "because eating outside is always more fun" (`unchecked-reason`) · "because it might snow later on tonight" (`other-side-reason`) · "because forty guests won't fit inside" ✔
- Feedback: More fun: Maybe! But that's a liking, not a fact from the card. · Snow: The card doesn't say that, and it points the other way.
- Hint: Read the numbers on the card.
- Explanation: Twenty fit inside; forty are coming. A fact makes a reason easy to check.

### Set D. Pass the spoon (Sprout, Seedling, Sapling)

**D1 (Sprout, guided).** Steps shown first: 1. Who holds the spoon? 2. That one talks. 3. What do the others do?
- Card: Ember holds the talking spoon and is talking about the cake.
- Prompt: Pip has an idea. What should he do?
- Choices: "Say it right now, as loudly as he can." (`interrupt`) · "Reach over and take the spoon from Ember." (`grab-turn`) · "Listen, then ask for the spoon." ✔
- Feedback: Loudly: Ember is still talking. Wait for the end. · Take it: The spoon passes when Ember is done.
- Hint: Who is holding the spoon?
- Explanation: One talker at a time. Listen to the end, then take a turn.

**D2 (Seedling).**
- Card: Puff says, "The cake needs candles. Or Ember could make one big flame!"
- Prompt: Which reply builds on what Puff said?
- Choices: "Yes, and Ember could light them with one puff!" ✔ · "Did you know Hob's goat eats his hat every morning?" (`change-subject`) · "The cake needs candles. The cake needs candles." (`repeat-not-build`)
- Feedback: Goat: That changes the subject. Stay with the cake. · Candles twice: That repeats Puff. Add something new.
- Hint: Which reply uses Puff's idea and adds to it?
- Explanation: "Yes, and…" keeps Puff's idea and adds a new piece.

**D3 (Seedling).**
- Card: The table is talking about the café's new sign. Dot: "A teapot." Pip: "A teapot with soup in it."
- Prompt: Whose next turn stays on the topic?
- Choices: Hob: "My fence fell over again last week." (`change-subject`) · Bramble: "Add my magpie on the spout." ✔ · Ember: "Is there any more of that cake?" (`change-subject`)
- Feedback: Fence: Interesting, but the topic is the sign. · More cake: That's a new topic. What about the sign?
- Hint: What is everyone talking about?
- Explanation: Bramble's turn links to the teapot and adds to it.

**D4 (Sapling).**
- Card: The baker says, "I'm baking a new bread for winter. It has a surprise inside."
- Prompt: Which question builds on what the baker said?
- Choices: "What color is the café's front door?" (`change-subject`) · "Is the new thing you're baking bread?" (`already-told`) · "Is the surprise sweet or salty?" ✔
- Feedback: Front door: That's a new topic. Ask about the bread. · Is it bread: The baker already said so. Ask something new.
- Hint: What did the baker keep a mystery?
- Explanation: A good question picks up a speaker's point and asks for more.

### Set E. Keep them listening (Sapling)

**E1 (Sapling, guided).** Steps shown first: 1. Hob wants to tell his fence story. 2. Which start makes people curious? 3. A question or a surprise works.
- Prompt: Which start would make the table want to hear Hob's story?
- Choices: "This is about a fence. Fences have posts. Posts are wood." (`dull-start`) · "Guess what was standing on my fence this morning?" ✔ · "Um. So. Well. Um, a fence." (`no-start`)
- Feedback: Posts: True, but it doesn't make anyone curious. · Um: Take a breath. Then start with something interesting.
- Hint: Which start makes you ask, "What?"
- Explanation: A question or a surprise at the start pulls listeners in.

**E2 (Sapling).**
- Card: Hob has talked about fence posts for a long time. Pip folds a napkin boat. Puff looks out the window. The funny goat part comes last.
- Prompt: What could Hob do now?
- Choices: "Keep going. There are nine more posts." (`ignore-listener`) · "Talk louder, so they have to listen." (`louder-not-better`) · "Skip ahead to the funny goat part." ✔
- Feedback: Nine more: Pip and Puff have drifted off. Notice that. · Louder: Louder isn't more interesting. Change what you say.
- Hint: What are Pip and Puff doing?
- Explanation: Watch your listeners. If they drift, change something.

**E3 (Sapling).**
- Card: Bramble says, "Have the supper at the ribbon gate. There's more room there." Dot would keep it at the café.
- Prompt: Which reply from Dot values Bramble's view?
- Choices: "Good point about room. But the café is warm." ✔ · "That's a silly idea, Bramble, and you know it." (`dismiss-view`) · "Fine, have it at the gate. I don't care at all." (`drop-own-view`)
- Feedback: Silly: That brushes Bramble off. Name her reason first. · Don't care: Dot can listen and still keep her own view.
- Hint: Which reply names Bramble's reason?
- Explanation: Name their reason, then give yours. Both views get heard.

**E4 (Sapling).**
- Card: The librarian says, "Let's read a story after supper. Everyone can listen over pie."
- Prompt: Which reply agrees and adds a new idea?
- Choices: "Yes, and Puff could do the voices." ✔ · "I agree. I agree with what you said." (`agree-no-add`) · "No, because the pie would get cold." (`disagree-not-asked`)
- Feedback: Agree twice: That agrees, but adds nothing new. · Pie cold: A fair disagreement! This one asks you to agree and add.
- Hint: Look for "yes, and".
- Explanation: Agree, then add something. The idea grows.

### Set F. Tell it in order (Sapling, Keeper's Desk)

**F1 (Sapling, order).** Put Puff's market-day cards in order.
- Cards (shown in this order): So the market opened an hour late. · On the bridge, a wheel came off. · At the end of the day, Pip ate the squashed loaf. · At dawn, the baker's cart rolled to the bridge. · Then everyone carried the loaves by hand.
- Answer: At dawn, the cart rolled to the bridge; on the bridge, a wheel came off; then everyone carried the loaves; so the market opened late; at the end of the day, Pip ate the squashed loaf.
- Hint: Find the time words: dawn, then, so, end of the day.
- Explanation: Time words and causes put a retelling in order.

**F2 (Sapling).**
- Prompt: Which retelling for Puff keeps the order?
- Choices: "The market opened late, and then a wheel came off." (`jumbled-order`) · "A wheel came off, so the market opened late." ✔ · "Pip ate the squashed loaf, then the cart rolled out." (`jumbled-order`)
- Feedback: Then a wheel: The wheel came off first. That's why it was late. · Then the cart: The cart left at dawn. Pip ate at the day's end.
- Hint: Which came first, the wheel or the late opening?
- Explanation: "So" shows the cause came first.

**F3 (Keeper's Desk).**
- Card: Dot is planning a short talk called "About the café".
- Prompt: Which fact does not belong in a talk about the café?
- Choices: "Six lanterns hang over the tables." (`drop-on-topic-fact`) · "Ember and Puff come most days." (`drop-on-topic-fact`) · "Willow Brook runs past the mill." ✔
- Feedback: Lanterns: The lanterns are in the café. Keep it. · Ember and Puff: The regulars are part of the café. Keep it.
- Hint: Is it about the café?
- Explanation: Every fact in a report should be about its topic.

**F4 (Keeper's Desk).**
- Prompt: Which closing line fits Dot's talk about the café?
- Choices: "So come in and say hello to Puff." ✔ · "And that's it, I think. The end. Bye." (`abrupt-end`) · "Also, Willow Brook runs past the mill." (`new-topic-at-end`)
- Feedback: Bye: That stops suddenly. Wrap up the café. · Brook: A closing doesn't start a new topic.
- Hint: A closing wraps up the topic.
- Explanation: A good closing ties back to the café.

**F5 (Keeper's Desk, spoken; never graded, nothing stored).**
- Prompt: Give Dot's café talk to a grown-up: opening, three facts, closing.
- Grown-up guide: Listen for an opening that names the café, three café facts in a sensible order, short pauses, and a closing. Say back one fact you heard.

## "I'm stuck" ladder

**Step 1, hint:** the item's own hint.

**Step 2, an easier case and how to start:**
- Describe items: "Try this one first: a red ball. Round, red, bouncy. Now look at yours."
- Wonder items: "Try this one first: wet paw prints by the door. Maybe someone came in from the rain. Now use your clues."
- Because items: "Try this one first: 'I think the hat is good, because it keeps rain off.' Find the because."
- Spoon items: "Try this one first: Dot says, 'Let's have soup.' 'Yes, and rolls!' adds to it. Now find yours."
- Listener items: "Try this one first: a story about socks that goes on and on. What would wake it up?"
- Order items: "Try this one first: Pip woke up. Pip ate. Pip fell asleep. Now find your first card."

**Step 3:** Skip for now. You can come back any time.

## Key terms (glossary)

- **Describe:** say what something is like, so a listener can picture it.
- **Detail:** one small piece of a description, like a feather on a hat.
- **Wonder:** try out an idea before you are sure.
- **Opinion:** what someone thinks about something.
- **Reason:** why someone thinks it.
- **Topic:** what everyone is talking about.
- **Turn:** your time to talk.
- **Build on:** keep someone's idea and add to it.
- **Listener:** the person you are talking to.
- **View:** the way someone sees something; their opinion.
- **Retell:** tell what happened again, in order.
- **Report:** a short talk that gives facts about one topic.
- **Opening and closing:** the first and last lines of a talk.
- **Crumbly:** breaks into small bits.
- **Steaming:** so hot it gives off steam.
- **Flicker:** shine on and off, unsteadily.

## Grown-up panel

**What this teaches:** describing with detail and in an order; wondering out loud and trying new words; giving an opinion with a reason that fits; taking turns, staying on topic and building on what someone said; keeping a listener interested and hearing another view; retelling in order and, at Keeper's Desk, a short report with an opening, three facts and a closing.

**How the talk cards work:** you are the partner. Pull a card, take turns, and talk. A sibling or friend can be the partner too. Nothing anyone says is recorded or stored, the game never asks for a microphone, and it never knows whether a card was used.

**Why the cards keep to Willowbank:** many talk activities ask children to describe their home or family or tell about their day. These cards never do. A child's home, family, feelings and whereabouts are private, and some children find those questions hard. Ember's cake and Hob's goat teach the same skills.

**About opinions:** no opinion is marked right or wrong here. The on-screen questions only ask whether a reason fits an opinion, using facts the card states. When your child gives an opinion, ask "Why?" once, kindly, and take a turn yourself.

**What it does not claim:** the talk on screen is supplied choices, not your child's own speech. Spoken talk is something you hear, not something the game measures. Register (formal and informal talk) is taught at the Post Office corner, and listening to a speaker's reasons at the meeting corner.
