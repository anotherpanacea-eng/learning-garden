# The Prop Box · proposed original interface copy

Status: draft for review; original brief55 word meanings and shared miniatures are reused separately. This is adult-read exploration, with no recorded phonics audio.

- Title: **Word Workshop**. Room: **The Prop Box**.
- Opening: “Dot found the theater's props and little pictures after the storm. Their labels washed away. Can we make some new ones?”
- Entry note: “Read together with a grown-up. Say each word, point to its three sounds, then put the sounds together.”
- Open choice: “Choose a prop or a little scene. They are all open.”
- Suggested first pair: “Try mat, then sat. What changes at the start?”
- Steps: **Meet the prop / Make a label / Read together**.
- Page actions: **Back / Next / Choose another prop / Choose a little scene**.
- Persistent target: “Our prop: {word}.”
- Tile instruction: “Choose a place, then tap a letter.”
- Place labels: **First letter / Middle letter / Last letter**; filled name: “{First|Middle|Last} letter: {letter}; choose this place.” Empty: “{First|Middle|Last} letter: empty; choose this place.”
- Actions: **Clear this place / Clear label / Show the word / Hide the word / Try this label**.
- Incomplete: “The {first|middle|last} place is empty. Choose a letter for it.”
- Matching label: “{word}. This label matches our card. Say the word together.” Action: **Put the label with this picture**.
- Another known label: “You made {word}. We have a picture for that label too. Keep it, or keep working on {target}.” Actions: **Keep this other label / Back to my prop**.
- Other combination: “{letters}. We don't have an open picture for this label here. You can change a letter or try another card.” Actions: **Change a letter / Back to my prop**. Never say “not a word.”
- Unavailable matching card: “An earlier card has this label, but we can't open that card here. The other pictures are still open.”
- Gallery: **Our labels**. Note: “Labels we have put with props. We can visit any one again.”
- Placed: “The {word} label is with its picture.” Repeated: “That label is already in our box. We can use it again.”
- Pointing: **Point through the word**. Instruction: “Ask your grown-up to say the sounds while you point from left to right. Then say the word together.” Controls: **Previous place / Next place / The whole word / Back to my label**. Positions: **First sound / Middle sound / Last sound / Whole word**.
- Separate example: **Show a different example**. Label: “A different label: {example word}. Your label is waiting.” First frame: “{meaning} Say {word} together.” Next frames: “Point to the {first|middle|last} sound. Your grown-up says it. Put {letter} in the {first|middle|last} place.” Final frame also says: “Say the sounds, then the whole word together.” Actions: **Previous example part / Next example part / Back to my label**. Earlier collection without another readable word: “This prop box has no different example to show. We can still make our label together.”
- Contrast group: **Compare a word**. Each qualifying sibling has a native button: **Compare {word} and {other}**. Heading: “{word} and {other}.” Invitation: “Point to the first letter in each word. Ask your grown-up to say both words clearly. What sound changes?” Caption: “The last two letters match. The first letters are different.” Return: **Back to my label**.
- Shared reading: **A little scene**. Key: “Underlined words are our prop words. Your grown-up supplies the other words.” The key does not make the targets an independent reading test.
- Collection ready: “The prop box has all its labels. What scene shall we make?” Actions: **Read a little scene / Make another scene / Visit a label again**. Keep all activities open.
- Partly unreadable collection: “An earlier prop card cannot open here, so we cannot call this whole box finished. The other props and scenes are still open.”
- Story Loft link: **Make a scene in Story Loft**. Back link: **Word Garden**.
- Missing draft: “This card has no label draft yet.” Action: **Start this label**.
- Unavailable picture alternative: “Picture unavailable. Read the card's meaning.”
- Unavailable linked text: “That earlier scene cannot open here. Choose another scene or return to your label.”
- Chooser: **Choose a card or a scene**.

## Twelve exact card texts

Meaning is a complete sentence, not a noun phrase inserted after an article. Alt describes the authored drawing; guidance appears only in the grown-up panel. No phoneme transcription is frozen into the runtime collection.

| Word | Meaning sentence | Picture alternative | Grown-up guidance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| cat | A cat waits by the prop box. | One cat sits by a wooden box. | Say cat naturally, then model its three sounds while pointing. The letter c represents the k sound in this word; that is not a rule for every c. |
| mat | A mat lies on the floor. | A small rectangular floor mat has a woven edge. | Say mat, then its three sounds from left to right. Let the first sound lead into the vowel. Show a floor mat, not a blanket. |
| sat | The puppet sat down. | Before: a puppet stands. After: it sits on a low stool. | Read both pictures together to show the earlier action. Model sat and its three sounds. Compare mat and sat only after both words have been said; their first sounds differ. |
| bat | This bat is a toy for hitting a ball. | A toy sporting bat lies next to a ball. | Establish the sporting-prop meaning. The animal is another legitimate meaning, not a reading mistake. Model the three sounds without turning the first into a separate buh syllable. |
| pin | A badge pin is a pretend theater prop. | A round badge has a pin fitting shown beside its back. | Establish the badge-pin meaning from the picture. Say pin and model its three sounds. This is a picture activity, not a request to handle a pin. |
| tin | This tin is a small metal box. | A small silver-colored metal container has a fitted lid. | Explain this meaning if tin is unfamiliar. Say tin naturally, then model its three sounds; letter names are a different activity. |
| fin | The toy fish has a fin. | An outlined fin on the back of a toy fish has a pointing arrow. | Indicate the fin rather than the whole fish. Say fin, model its three sounds, then put them together. The picture explains meaning; it does not prove the label was decoded. |
| bin | The props go in a storage bin. | An open storage bin holds a ball and a folded cloth. | Establish a storage bin, rather than assuming a waste-bin meaning. Say bin naturally and model its three sounds without adding a separate buh syllable. |
| hop | The puppet makes a little hop. | Before: a puppet stands on the floor. After: it is just above the floor. | Model hop in context, letting its beginning lead into the vowel. Do not demand an isolated breath sound or physical hopping. Keep all three printed positions visible. |
| mop | This mop cleans a pretend stage. | A cleaning mop has a long handle and bundled strands. | Say mop naturally, model the three sounds, and blend again. A sustained beginning does not mean every consonant can be prolonged in the same way. |
| top | This toy is a spinning top. | A spinning toy has a broad conical body, short stem and pointed base. | Establish the spinning-toy meaning rather than the highest point. Model top and its three sounds without adding a separate tuh syllable. |
| pop | A bubble goes pop. | Before: one whole bubble. After: its outline breaks into short marks. | Say the word pop, not a bubble sound effect. Its first and last sound occupy different positions even though the same letter appears twice. Do not add a separate puh syllable. |

## Recovery and Notes copy

- Recovery offer: **Start a fresh prop box**, relabeled **Open the newer prop box** only for a known lower revision. Confirmation actions: **Cancel / Open a fresh prop box**.
- Confirmation: “Start with this version's prop box? Its drafts and labels will start fresh. Your earlier box will be kept in the Grown-up notes download.”
- Refusal: “We couldn't keep this change. Your earlier work is still here. Download Grown-up notes to keep a copy.”
- Unknown root: “This earlier prop box cannot open here. It is kept in Grown-up notes. The rest of Word Garden is open.”
- Notes heading: **Word Workshop: Prop Box**.
- Notes boundary: “These are supported label encounters, not independent reading, spelling or comprehension results.”
- Notes empty: “No readable labels have been placed.”
- Notes list: “Labels placed: {retained readable labels}.”
- Notes draft: “Waiting label: {target}. Letters: {three places, with empty for a null place}.”
- Notes unavailable: “Earlier material is kept in the download but cannot be shown here.”
- Notes recovery: “Earlier prop boxes are kept unchanged in this download.”
- Notes link: **Open Word Workshop**, to `#words/props`.
- Word Garden entry link, in both legacy branches: **Visit the Prop Box**.
- **Make another scene** and **Make a scene in Story Loft** both link to `#loft`, without writing or transferring work there.

For grown-ups: “This room has no recorded phonics demonstrations. Model the word and its three sounds yourself. Letter names and sounds are different. Each word here uses three letters for three sound positions; that is not a rule for every English word. Family endings are not single sounds or suffixes. The shared texts include words we have not taught here. Help, pictures and copied labels are welcome; the saved labels do not establish independent reading, spelling or comprehension.”

Sense notes and picture requirements are those in brief55, including the sporting bat, badge pin, metal tin, toy fish's fin, storage bin, spinning top, sat before/after, puppet hop and bubble pop. No action requires handling a pin or physically hopping. All target pronunciations remain adult-provided; do not run these labels or the shared texts through browser speech as a phonics demonstration.

Recovery/refusal copy follows the existing Parcel Labels pattern, using ordinary language: earlier work is kept, the affected card cannot be opened here, other choices remain available, and Notes can be downloaded. No “unsupported schema”, “exportable”, “provenance” or “recorded as support” appears in child instructions.
