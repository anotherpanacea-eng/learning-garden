# The Prop Box · proposed original interface copy

Status: draft for review; original brief55 word meanings and shared miniatures are reused separately. This is adult-read exploration, with no recorded phonics audio.

- Title: **Word Workshop**. Room: **The Prop Box**.
- Opening: “Dot found the theatre's props after the storm. Their labels washed away. Can we make some new ones?”
- Entry note: “Read together with a grown-up. Say each word, point to its three sounds, then put the sounds together.”
- Open choice: “Choose a prop or a little scene. They are all open.”
- Suggested first pair: “Try mat, then sat. What changes at the start?”
- Steps: **Meet the prop / Make a label / Read together**.
- Page actions: **Back / Next / Choose another prop / Choose a little scene**.
- Persistent target: “Our prop: {word}.”
- Tile instruction: “Choose a place, then tap a letter.”
- Place labels: **First letter / Middle letter / Last letter**; filled name: “{First|Middle|Last} letter: {letter}; choose this place.” Empty: “{First|Middle|Last} letter: empty; choose this place.”
- Actions: **Clear this place / Clear label / Show the word / Hide the word / Try this label**.
- Incomplete: “The {first|middle|last} place is empty. Choose a letter for it.”
- Matching label: “{word}. A label for our {sense}. Say it together.” Action: **Put the label with this prop**.
- Another known label: “You made {word}. We have {sense} too. Keep this label, or keep working on {target}.” Actions: **Keep this other label / Back to my prop**.
- Other combination: “{letters} is outside this prop box. Could it name a prop in a story you invent?” Actions: **Change a letter / Back to my prop**. Never say “not a word.”
- Gallery: **Our labels**. Note: “Labels we have put with props. We can visit any one again.”
- Placed: “The {word} label is beside its prop.” Repeated: “That label is already in our box. We can use it again.”
- Pointing: **Point through the word**. Instruction: “Ask your grown-up to say the sounds while you point from left to right. Then say the word together.” Controls: **Previous place / Next place / The whole word / Back to my label**. Positions: **First sound / Middle sound / Last sound / Whole word**.
- Separate example: **Show a different example**. Label: “A different label: {example word}. Your label is waiting.” First frame: “This is {meaning}. Say {word} together.” Next frames: “Point to the {first|middle|last} sound. Your grown-up says it. Put {letter} in the {first|middle|last} place.” Final frame also says: “Say the sounds, then the whole word together.” Actions: **Previous example part / Next example part / Back to my label**. Earlier collection without another readable word: “This prop box has no different example to show. We can still make our label together.”
- Shared reading: **A little scene**. Key: “Underlined words are our prop words. Your grown-up supplies the other words.” The key does not make the targets an independent reading test.
- Collection ready: “The prop box has all its labels. What scene shall we make?” Actions: **Read a little scene / Make another scene / Visit a label again**. Keep all activities open.
- Partly unreadable collection: “An earlier prop card cannot open here, so we cannot call this whole box finished. The other props and scenes are still open.”
- Story Loft link: **Make a scene in Story Loft**. Back link: **Word Garden**.

For grown-ups: “This room has no recorded phonics demonstrations. Model the word and its three sounds yourself. Letter names and sounds are different. Each word here uses three letters for three sound positions; that is not a rule for every English word. Family endings are not single sounds or suffixes. The shared texts include words we have not taught here. Help, pictures and copied labels are welcome; the saved labels do not establish independent reading, spelling or comprehension.”

Sense notes and picture requirements are those in brief55, including the sporting bat, badge pin, metal tin, toy fish's fin, storage bin, spinning top, sat before/after, puppet hop and bubble pop. No action requires handling a pin or physically hopping. All target pronunciations remain adult-provided; do not run these labels or the shared texts through browser speech as a phonics demonstration.

Recovery/refusal copy follows the existing Parcel Labels pattern, using ordinary language: earlier work is kept, the affected card cannot be opened here, other choices remain available, and Notes can be downloaded. No “unsupported schema”, “exportable”, “provenance” or “recorded as support” appears in child instructions.
