# The Puppet's Sentence Cards: all child-facing text

Companion to [spec 255](../specs/255-puppets-sentence-cards.md). Use as written, after review. `{guide}` is never gendered. Pronouns: Pip he/him; Dot, Bramble she/her; the librarian they/them; Puff takes no pronouns. The library puppets are things and take "it". Sources: [audit](../source-audits/255-puppets-sentence-cards.md). Nothing is scored, timed or locked.

**Reading rule for build:** every Sprout line, card and prompt has read-aloud, and every Sprout item has a picture. Picture notes in *italics* say what the art must show, because some items depend on it; the same words are the art's text equivalent.

**Labels used throughout:**
- **A story from Willowbank** marks the town, its people, the puppets and the show (fiction).
- **A true thing about language** marks real facts about English.

## Arriving

**Place title:** The Puppet's Sentence Cards

**First visit** (A story from Willowbank):
> A row of puppets hangs in the Story Loft. Beside them sits a box of picture cards.
>
> "Every card is a word," says Dot. "Put them together and the puppets have lines."

**What you already know (recall card, links to `#loft/printer`):** You moved word tickets and chose an end mark. Here you make the words for the tickets.

**Every bench screen (always shown):** Any line you build is a real try. Pip will read it just as it stands.

## Story scenes

### Scene 1. The scattered puppets (Act I, ch. 4, mend the village)

**Hook (A story from Willowbank):** The storm blew the Story Loft's puppets everywhere. Pip and Dot are putting them back. They can't agree which puppet is which.

**Short:**
> Pip and Dot tidy the puppets. Pip says "this" for one near him. Dot says "those" for two far away.

**Longer:**
> The storm blew the puppets all over the Story Loft. One hangs from a beam. Two hide under the far stool.
>
> Pip picks up a rabbit puppet. "This one is soggy," he says.
>
> Dot points across the loft. "That one is soggier."
>
> "Which one?" says Pip.
>
> "That one, on the beam. And those two, under the stool."
>
> Pip looks at the puppet in his paws. Then he looks at the stool, far away. "This is close. That is far. Those are far, and there are two."
>
> {guide} holds up two kitten puppets. "And these?"
>
> "These are close," says Dot. "And dry. Hooray."

**Keeper's note (A true thing about language):**
> This and these usually point to things close to the speaker. That and those usually point to things farther away. This and that are for one thing; these and those are for more than one.

### Scene 2. Dot's first note (Act I, ch. 2, deliver and explain)

**Hook (A story from Willowbank):** Dot is new in town. She pins a note to the post office door to say hello. It says "Dot" a lot.

**Short:**
> Dot's note says "Dot" six times. Pip laughs. {guide} shows her how "she" and "her" can stand in for her name.

**Longer:**
> Dot pins her note to the post office door.
>
> *Dot is new. Dot is the postmaster. Dot likes Dot's hat. Dot will bring your letters. Please wave at Dot.*
>
> Pip reads it out loud. "Dot, Dot, Dot, Dot, Dot. That's a lot of Dot."
>
> "It's my note," says Dot. "Who else would it be about?"
>
> "Nobody else," says {guide}. "So after the first Dot, you can say she. Or her."
>
> Dot tries it. "Dot is new. She is the postmaster. She likes her hat."
>
> "Shorter," says Pip. "And I still know it's you."
>
> Dot keeps the first "Dot." "So everyone knows who she is," she says.

### Scene 3. The picture cards (Act II, ch. 4, the first show)

**Hook (A story from Willowbank):** The Story Loft's first show is coming. The puppets' lines are on picture cards, so even the smallest helpers can read them.

**Short:**
> Pip puts a dragon card next to a sleeping card. "A dragon sleeps!" A naming word and a doing word make a puppet's line.

**Longer:**
> The picture cards are spread across the loft floor. One shows a dragon. One shows a puppet fast asleep. One shows a kitten. One shows a hop.
>
> Pip puts the dragon card down, then the sleeping card. "A dragon sleeps," he reads.
>
> "That's a line," says Dot. "Who is it about?"
>
> "The dragon. That's the naming word."
>
> "And what does it do?"
>
> "Sleeps. That's the doing word." Pip yawns. "Like me."
>
> {guide} adds a waking card. "Now the next line."
>
> "The dragon wakes," reads Pip. "Not a dragon this time. The dragon. We already met it."
>
> The dragon puppet opens one stitched eye.

### Scene 4. The printed program (Act II, ch. 4)

**Hook (A story from Willowbank):** Dot prints a program for the audience. Bramble reads it at the door. Two lines catch her eye.

**Short:**
> The program says "Two puppets bows." Bramble spots it. One puppet bows; two puppets bow. Dot fixes the program.

**Longer:**
> Dot hands out the printed programs. Bramble reads hers at the door.
>
> *Two puppets bows. The dragon sing a song.*
>
> "Your program has a wobble," says Bramble. Her magpie taps the paper, as if it agrees.
>
> Dot reads it again. "Oh. Two puppets bow. The dragon sings a song."
>
> "People say it lots of ways," says {guide}. "All of them are real English. A printed program uses the way most books do."
>
> "One dragon sings," says Pip. "Two dragons sing. The s hops from one word to the other."
>
> "We have one dragon," says Dot. "So the dragon sings. I'll print a new one."
>
> Bramble folds her old program into a fan. "I'll keep this one too."

**Keeper's note (A true thing about language):**
> English is spoken in many places, in many ways. In some kinds of English, people say "she sing" or "I sings." Those follow their own patterns. Most printed books use "she sings" and "I sing."

### Scene 5. The show poster (Summer, Market Fair and the Show)

**Hook (A story from Willowbank):** Pip paints a poster for the summer show. Dot reads it aloud, and stops twice.

**Short:**
> Pip's poster says "AN UNICORN" and "A HOUR." Say them aloud: a or an goes by the first sound you hear, not the letter.

**Longer:**
> Pip's poster is still wet. It says: *AN UNICORN PUPPET! A HOUR OF FUN!*
>
> Dot reads it aloud. "An yoo-nicorn. A our." She frowns. "My mouth doesn't like that."
>
> "Unicorn starts with a U," says Pip. "U is a vowel. So it's an."
>
> "Say it slowly," says {guide}. "What sound comes first?"
>
> "Yoo," says Pip. "Oh. That's like yes. That's not a vowel sound."
>
> "And hour?" says Dot. "The h is silent. It starts with an ow sound."
>
> Pip paints over the poster: *A UNICORN PUPPET! AN HOUR OF FUN!*
>
> Then he adds two more words. *A SMALL GREEN DRAGON!*
>
> "Who's that?" says Dot.
>
> "The dragon," says Pip. "Now everyone can picture it."

**Keeper's note (A true thing about language):**
> A few h-words change from speaker to speaker. Some people say "an historic day," and some say "a historic day." Each person picks the one that fits the sound they say.

## Teach cards

Each card is 30 words or fewer; worked-example steps appear one tap at a time. Every card is **A true thing about language**. Cards 1, 2, 4 and 5 are read aloud with pictures.

**Card 1. Naming words (Sprout)**
> A naming word names a person, an animal, a place or a thing. Rabbit, Pip, loft and drum are naming words. They are called nouns.

Worked example: *The rabbit hops.*
1. Who is the line about?
2. The rabbit.
3. Rabbit is the naming word.

**Card 2. Doing words (Sprout)**
> A doing word tells what someone or something does. Hops, sings and sleeps are doing words. They are called verbs.

Worked example: *The kitten sings.*
1. Who is it about? The kitten.
2. What does the kitten do?
3. Sings. That is the doing word.

**Card 3. One word, two jobs (Sapling)**
> A word's job depends on its line. "Pip waves": waves is a verb. "Pip gives a wave": wave is a noun, the thing Pip gives.

Worked example: *Pip gives a wave.*
1. Find the doing word: gives.
2. What does Pip give? A wave.
3. Here, wave names a thing: a noun.

**Card 4. A and the (Sprout)**
> Say "a" for any one, or one we haven't met yet. Say "the" for one we already know.

Worked example: *A dragon comes in. The dragon waves.*
1. First line: we haven't met this dragon. A dragon.
2. Second line: now we know which dragon.
3. The dragon.

**Card 5. This, that, these, those (Sprout)**
> This is one thing close to you. That is one thing far away. These are more than one, close by. Those are more than one, far away.

Worked example: *Pip holds a hat. Dot's hat hangs across the loft.*
1. Pip's hat is close to him: this hat.
2. Dot's hat is far from Pip: that hat.
3. Two hats far away: those hats.

**Card 6. A or an? Listen first (Sapling)**
> Say the next word aloud. Does it start with a vowel sound, like apple, egg or owl? Use an. Any other sound? Use a.

Worked example: *___ owl puppet.*
1. Say owl aloud.
2. It starts with an open-mouth ow sound. That is a vowel sound.
3. An owl puppet.

**Card 7. The sound decides, not the letter (Sapling)**
> Some words trick your eyes. Hour starts with h, but the h is silent: an hour. Unicorn starts with u, but sounds like "yoo": a unicorn.

Worked example: *___ umbrella and ___ unicorn.*
1. Say umbrella. It starts with "uh," a vowel sound: an umbrella.
2. Say unicorn. It starts with "yoo," like yes.
3. A unicorn.

**Card 8. A or an fits the very next word (Sapling)**
> A or an goes by the word right after it. That might be a describing word, not the noun. An apple, but a big apple.

Worked example: *___ old drum.*
1. The next word is old.
2. Old starts with a vowel sound.
3. An old drum.

**Card 9. Pronouns stand in for names (Seedling)**
> After you say a name once, a pronoun can stand in for it. Pip waves. He bows. He stands in for Pip.

Worked example: *Dot is new. Dot is the postmaster.*
1. Dot is named once already.
2. Dot uses she and her.
3. Dot is new. She is the postmaster.

**Card 10. Use the pronouns each person uses (Seedling)**
> Pip uses he and his. Dot uses she and her. The librarian uses they and their. Puff uses no pronouns, so we just say Puff.

Worked example: *Puff sat in Puff's seat.*
1. Puff uses no pronouns.
2. So we keep the name.
3. Puff sat in Puff's seat. That's right as it is.

**Card 11. I or me? (Sapling)**
> In print, I does the action: I bowed. Me gets the action: Dot waved at me. With two people, cover the other name and listen.

Worked example: *Dot and ___ found a hat.*
1. Cover "Dot and."
2. "I found a hat" or "me found a hat"?
3. I found a hat. So: Dot and I found a hat.

**Card 12. Words for anyone and everyone (Sapling)**
> Some pronouns don't name one particular person or thing. Anyone, everyone, nobody, something and everything are like that.

Worked example: *The loft is empty. Not one person is there.*
1. Not one person: nobody.
2. Every person: everyone.
3. Nobody is here.

**Card 13. He hops, we hop (Seedling)**
> In most printed books, a doing word happening now gets an s with he, she, it, or one puppet. With I, you, we, they, or many puppets: no s.

Worked example: *The rabbit hops. Two rabbits hop.*
1. One rabbit: the rabbit hops.
2. Two rabbits: two rabbits hop.
3. With two rabbits, the doing word has no s.

**Card 14. Growing a noun (Seedling)**
> A noun and the words that tell more about it make a noun phrase. A dragon. A small dragon. A small green dragon.

Worked example: *the dragon*
1. Add how big: the small dragon.
2. Add what color: the small green dragon.
3. The noun is still dragon. The rest tells more.

**Card 15. What job does a sentence do? (Seedling)**
> A statement tells. A question asks. A command tells someone to do something. Here we use a strict rule: an exclamation starts with What or How.

Worked example: *Four lines from the show.*
1. The drum is loud. A statement.
2. Is the drum loud? A question.
3. Bang the drum. A command. What a loud drum it is! An exclamation.

**Card 16. Words about words (Sapling)**
> An adjective tells more about a noun: sleepy dragon. An adverb can tell how something happens: hops slowly. Tense tells when: hops is present, hopped is past.

Worked example: *The sleepy dragon hopped slowly.*
1. Sleepy tells about the dragon: an adjective.
2. Slowly tells how it hopped: an adverb.
3. Hopped happened before now: past tense.

## Benches

### (a) The Line Builder (Sprout, Seedling)

**Header:** Pick a card for each box. Pip will read your line.

**Card tray:**
- Who (naming cards, with pictures): rabbit · kitten · dragon · owl · unicorn
- Does (doing cards, with pictures): hops · sings · waves · sleeps · spins
- Little words (Seedling only): a · an · the

**Sprout:** the little word is printed on each naming card (the rabbit, the owl). The child picks who and what it does. All 25 lines are fine, including the surprising ones.

**Seedling:** the child also picks a, an or the.

**Pip reads it:** Pip reads the line exactly as built, then the puppet acts it out.

**Feedback (Seedling, shown under the line):**
- a owl: Say it aloud. Owl starts with a vowel sound. Try an.
- an rabbit, an kitten, an dragon or an unicorn: Say the puppet's name. Listen to its first sound. Try a.

**Naming or doing?** After the line is read: Tap the naming word. Now tap the doing word.
- Tapped the doing word first: That tells what it does. Which word names who?
- Tapped a, an or the: That little word points to the puppet. Tap the puppet's name.

**Two-line scene (Seedling):** Make two lines about one puppet. The first meets it; the second already knows it.
- Second line with a: We met this puppet already. Try the.

### (b) This or That? (Sprout, Seedling)

**Header:** Pip is talking. Is the puppet close to Pip, or far away? One, or more than one?

**Grid (picture cards):** *close, one: Pip holds one puppet* → this · *far, one: one puppet on a far beam* → that · *close, many: two puppets in Pip's paws* → these · *far, many: two puppets under the far stool* → those

**Feedback:**
- this for a far puppet: That puppet is across the loft from Pip. Far away is that.
- that for a close puppet: Pip is holding it. Close is this.
- this or that for two puppets: There are two. For more than one, use these or those.

### (c) Say It First (Sapling)

**Header (A true thing about language):** Say each word aloud. Listen to the very first sound. Then choose a or an.

**Word cards (each with a voiced recording):** apple · egg · owl · umbrella · orange · hat · drum · hour · unicorn · uniform · big apple · old drum

**Step 1:** Is the first sound a vowel sound or a consonant sound?

**Step 2:** Choose a or an.

**Feedback:**
- a apple, a egg, a owl or a orange: Say it. It starts with a vowel sound. Use an.
- an drum: Say drum. You hear d first. That is a consonant sound.
- an hat: Say hat. You hear h first. That is a consonant sound.
- a hour: Say hour. The h is silent. It starts with a vowel sound.
- an unicorn or an uniform: Say it. It starts with "yoo," like yes. Use a.
- a umbrella: Say it. It starts with "uh." That is a vowel sound.
- an big apple: The next word is big. Big starts with b. Use a.
- a old drum: The next word is old. Old starts with a vowel sound.

**Fix the poster:** Tap a word on Pip's first poster to change it. *AN UNICORN PUPPET! A HOUR OF FUN!* becomes *A UNICORN PUPPET! AN HOUR OF FUN!*

### (d) The Program (Seedling, Sapling)

**Header:** Get the show program ready to print. Most printed books are written this way.

**Grown-up line on the bench (always shown):** People speak English in many ways, and all are real English. This bench practices the printed-book way. It never marks anyone's own speech.

**Name swap:** Tap a name that comes back again. Then pick a word to stand in for it.
- Program text: *Pip is the fox. Pip bows. Dot is the postmaster. Dot waves Dot's hat. The librarian pulls the curtain. The librarian holds the librarian's script. Puff sits in Puff's seat.*
- Pronoun cards: he · she · they · his · her · their
- Accepted swaps: Pip bows → He bows · Dot waves Dot's hat → She waves her hat · The librarian holds the librarian's script → They hold their script · Puff sits in Puff's seat → no swap.

**Name swap feedback:**
- she or her for Pip: Pip uses he and his.
- he or his for Dot: Dot uses she and her.
- he, she, his or her for the librarian: The librarian uses they and their.
- Any card for Puff: Puff uses no pronouns. Keep Puff's name.
- Swapping the first name: Keep the first name, so readers know who it is.
- They holds: Many people say it that way. In print, they hold.

**He hops, we hop:** Choose the doing word for each line of the program.
- Lines: The rabbit ___ (hop / hops) · Two rabbits ___ (hop / hops) · I ___ (hop / hops) · We ___ (hop / hops) · The librarian ___ (wave / waves) · They ___ (wave / waves) · Everyone ___ (clap / claps)
- Book-way answers: hops · hop · hop · hop · waves · wave · claps

**He hops, we hop feedback:**
- hop with one rabbit: Many people say it that way. In printed books, one rabbit hops.
- hops with two rabbits: Many people say it that way. In printed books, two rabbits hop.
- hops with I or we: Many people say it that way. In printed books, I hop and we hop.
- wave with the librarian: Many people say it that way. In printed books, the librarian waves.
- waves with they: Many people say it that way. In print, they takes no s, even for one person.
- clap with everyone: Many people say it that way. In printed books, everyone takes the s: everyone claps.

### (e) Grow the Noun (Seedling, Sapling)

**Header:** Grow the noun so the audience can picture it.

**Poster nouns:** the dragon · the drum · the hat

**Describing cards:** How big? small · big. What color? red · green · blue.

**How it works:** the size card goes in the first box, the color card in the second. The picture changes to match. The noun stays at the end.

**Card after growing:** Your noun phrase: the small green dragon. The noun is dragon. Small and green tell more about it.

**Which one?** Item F2 (its card, choices and feedback) shown as a bench.

### (f) The Word Box (Seedling, Sapling)

**Header:** These are the words people use to talk about sentences.

**Label the line:** *The tiny kitten sang loudly.* Tap a word, then tap its name: noun, verb, adjective or adverb.
- Answers: tiny, adjective · kitten, noun · sang, verb · loudly, adverb · The is left unlabeled (a pointing word, shown grayed).

**Label feedback:**
- loudly as adjective: Loudly tells how the kitten sang. It tells about the verb: an adverb.
- tiny as adverb: Tiny tells what the kitten is like: an adjective.

**Sentence jobs:** Sort four lines into statement, question, command and exclamation.
- The show starts soon. (statement) · Is the drum ready? (question) · Pass me the drum. (command) · How loud that drum is! (exclamation)

**Sentence jobs feedback:**
- Pass me the drum in statement: It tells someone to do something. That is a command.
- How loud that drum is! in question: It starts with How and doesn't ask. An exclamation.
- Pass me the drum in exclamation: Here, exclamations start with What or How. This one is a command.

**Then or now?** Toggle the line between *The dragon hops.* (present tense) and *The dragon hopped.* (past tense).

## Problem sets

Every item has the "I'm stuck" ladder. Choices appear in the order written; ✔ marks the right one. Tags in backticks name the misconception a wrong choice tests. Sprout items are read aloud and show the picture described in italics.

### Set A. Naming and doing (Sprout, Seedling, Sapling)

**A1 (Sprout, guided, tap, read aloud).** Steps shown first: 1. Look at the picture. 2. Who is in it? 3. Find that word.
- Picture: *a rabbit puppet hopping*
- Line: The rabbit hops.
- Prompt: Tap the naming word.
- Answer: rabbit
- Feedback: Tapped hops: Hops tells what it does. Which word names who? · Tapped The: The points to the rabbit. Tap its name.
- Hint: Who is the line about?
- Explanation: Rabbit names the puppet. It is the naming word, a noun.

**A2 (Sprout, read aloud).**
- Picture: *a kitten puppet singing*
- Line: The kitten sings.
- Prompt: Which word tells what the kitten does?
- Choices: The (`article-is-noun`) · kitten (`noun-for-verb`) · sings ✔
- Feedback: The: The points to the kitten. It doesn't tell what it does. · kitten: Kitten names who. What does it do?
- Hint: Look at the picture. What is the kitten doing?
- Explanation: Sings tells what the kitten does. It is the doing word, a verb.

**A3 (Sapling).**
- Line: Pip takes a nap.
- Prompt: In this line, what job does nap do?
- Choices: A noun: it names what Pip takes ✔ · A verb: napping is an action (`action-means-verb`) · An adjective: it tells about Pip (`any-word-describes`)
- Feedback: Verb: The doing word here is takes. Nap is the thing Pip takes. · Adjective: Nap doesn't tell what Pip is like.
- Hint: Find the doing word first. Then ask: what does Pip take?
- Explanation: Nap names the thing Pip takes. Here, nap is a noun.

### Set B. A, the, this and that (Sprout, Seedling)

**B1 (Sprout, guided, read aloud).** Steps shown first: 1. Is the hat close to Pip? 2. Is there one hat? 3. Close and one.
- Picture: *Pip holding one hat in his paw*
- Prompt: Pip says, "___ hat is mine." Which word fits?
- Choices: This ✔ · That (`near-far`) · These (`one-many`)
- Feedback: That: Pip is holding it. Close is this. · These: There is only one hat.
- Hint: Is the hat close to Pip or far away?
- Explanation: One thing close to you is this. This hat is mine.

**B2 (Sprout, read aloud).**
- Picture: *Dot pointing at two drums across the loft*
- Prompt: Dot says, "___ drums are loud." Which word fits?
- Choices: This (`one-many`) · These (`near-far`) · Those ✔
- Feedback: This: There are two drums. This is for one. · These: The drums are far from Dot. These is for close.
- Hint: How many drums? Are they close or far?
- Explanation: More than one, far away, is those. Those drums are loud.

**B3 (Seedling).**
- Card shown: Pip wants to play a drum. Any drum will do.
- Prompt: What does Pip say?
- Choices: "Can I have a drum?" ✔ · "Can I have the drum?" (`the-for-any`) · "Can I have drum?" (`no-article`)
- Feedback: The drum: The means one we both know. Any drum will do. · Have drum: Most lines need a little word before drum.
- Hint: Does Pip want one special drum, or any drum?
- Explanation: A is for any one. Can I have a drum?

**B4 (Seedling).**
- Line: A dragon comes in. ___ dragon waves.
- Prompt: Which word fits the second line?
- Choices: A (`a-after-known`) · The ✔ · Those (`one-many`)
- Feedback: A: We already met this dragon. Use the. · Those: There is only one dragon.
- Hint: Have we met this dragon yet?
- Explanation: First we meet a dragon. After that, it's the dragon.

### Set D. Pronouns (Seedling, Sapling)

**D1 (Seedling, guided).** Steps shown first: 1. Dot is already named. 2. Dot uses she and her. 3. Which card fits?
- Line: Dot is new. ___ is the postmaster.
- Prompt: The missing word means Dot. Dot uses she and her. Which word fits?
- Choices: She ✔ · They (`ignores-stated-pronoun`) · It (`it-for-person`)
- Feedback: They: Dot uses she and her. · It: It is for things. Dot is someone, not a thing.
- Hint: What does Dot use?
- Explanation: She stands in for Dot. Dot is new. She is the postmaster.

**D2 (Seedling).**
- Line: Dot loves ___ hat.
- Prompt: The hat is Dot's own. Which word fits?
- Choices: his (`ignores-stated-pronoun`) · her ✔ · its (`it-for-person`)
- Feedback: His: Dot uses she and her. · Its: Its is for things. Dot is someone, not a thing.
- Hint: Dot uses she and her. Which one shows the hat is hers?
- Explanation: Her shows the hat belongs to Dot. Dot loves her hat.

**D3 (Sapling, guided).** Steps shown first: 1. Cover "Pip and." 2. Say "I took a bow." 3. Say "me took a bow."
- Line: Pip and ___ took a bow.
- Prompt: For the printed program, which word fits?
- Choices: me (`me-as-subject`) · I ✔ · myself (`myself-for-I`)
- Feedback: Me: Many people say it that way. In print, cover Pip: I took a bow. · Myself: Some people say it that way. In print, cover Pip: I took a bow.
- Hint: Cover "Pip and." What sounds right on its own?
- Explanation: I does the action. In print: Pip and I took a bow.

**D4 (Sapling).**

## Key terms (glossary)

- **Naming word:** a word that names a person, animal, place or thing. Also called a noun.
- **Noun:** a naming word, like rabbit, Pip, loft or drum.
- **Doing word:** a word that tells what someone does. Also called a verb.
- **Verb:** a doing word, like hops or sings. Is and has are verbs too.
- **Vowel sound:** a sound you make with your mouth open, like the start of apple, egg, owl or up.
- **Consonant sound:** a sound where you block or squeeze the air, like b, h or the "y" in yes.
- **Pronoun:** a word that stands in for a noun, like he, she, they, it, I or me.
- **Noun phrase:** a noun and the words that tell more about it, like the small green dragon.
- **Adjective:** a word that tells more about a noun, like sleepy or green.
- **Adverb:** a word that tells how, when or where something happens, like slowly.
- **Statement:** a sentence that tells something.
- **Question:** a sentence that asks something.
- **Command:** a sentence that tells someone to do something.
- **Exclamation:** in this pack, a sentence that starts with What or How, like What a loud drum that is! Many people call any line with ! an exclamation.
- **Tense:** the form of a verb that shows when. Hops is present tense; hopped is past tense.
- **Compound:** a word made of two words joined, like sunflower.
- **Suffix:** a word part added to the end of a word, like -ed in jumped.
- **Apostrophe:** a mark like this ' that can show who owns something, as in Dot's hat.
- **Comma:** a mark like this , that separates words or parts of a sentence.
- **Program:** a printed sheet that tells the audience about a show.

## Grown-up panel

**What this teaches:** nouns and verbs in simple sentences; the determiners a, an, the, this, that, these and those; choosing a or an by the next word's first sound; personal, possessive and indefinite pronouns, and using each person's own pronouns; subject-verb agreement in printed Standard English; expanded noun phrases; and England's Year 2 grammar terms.

**About everyday speech:** English has many varieties, and some use different verb patterns ("she sing," "I sings," "we was"). These follow their own rules and are not mistakes. This pack teaches the forms most printed books use, as a choice for a printed program, and it never checks or marks how a child speaks. Please don't correct your child's own speech during these benches; talk about the program instead.

**Terms:** England's national curriculum introduces a or an in Year 3 and the listed terms in Year 2; the child text uses American spelling. The pack calls my, his, her and their possessive pronouns, as England's grammar glossary and the Common Core do; many grammar books call them possessive determiners when they come before a noun. No item asks for the term. "Naming word" and "doing word" are starting points only: some verbs (is, has, likes) name no action, and a word like wave can be a noun or a verb, so older items test a word's job in its line. Exclamations: England's grammar glossary gives *What a good friend you are!* as its example, and England's key stage 1 grammar test framework (the Standards and Testing Agency's) narrows the term to sentences that begin with What or How. Everyday usage, and the Common Core's "exclamatory sentences," also count lines like *Sit down now!* G2, G3 and the sentence-jobs sort use the narrow rule and say so on screen: a command ending in ! is filed as a command, and the feedback adds that many people would call it an exclamation.

**What it does not claim:** pictures and lines are simplified. Every puppet line, the program and the poster are invented. Some h-words (historic, and herb, which most Americans say without the h) vary between speakers, so the pack uses only hour, where the h is silent everywhere. Longer noun phrases, adjective order and clear pronouns come in a later pack; tense, apostrophes, commas, compounds and suffixes are taught in full on other benches. Nothing is scored; saved work keeps only codes for the child's choices and their outcomes.
