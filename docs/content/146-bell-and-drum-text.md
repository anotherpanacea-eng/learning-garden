## Story scenes

### Act II: Dot's bell (opens activity a)

**Short:**
> Dot's post office bell can't be heard at the end of the lane. "Should I ring it harder?" she asks. "Will that make it louder, or higher, or both?"

**Longer:**
> Dot stands on the post office step with her bell in one hand and her hat in the other.
>
> "Puff at the end of the lane never hears my bell," Dot says. "So I rang it harder. Pip says it sounded higher. I think it just sounded louder."
>
> "I was eating at the time," says Pip. "Everything sounds higher when you're eating."
>
> {guide} laughs. "There's a bench in the studio for exactly this. A drum, a ruler and a string. We can change one thing at a time and watch what happens."
>
> Dot puts her hat back on, which means she has decided. "Then let's find out properly. If I'm going to ring harder, I want to know what I'm getting."

**Keeper's note:**
> Louder and higher are two different things. How hard you ring changes how big the bell's vibration is. The size and shape of the bell set how fast it vibrates. You'll test both on the bench.

### Act III: Bramble's magpie (opens activity c)

**Short:**
> Bramble says her magpie can hear the mill bell all the way from Vadoalto. The miller doubts it. You can check on the valley map.

**Longer:**
> Bramble sits on the ribbon gate, swinging her boots. Her magpie sits on her shoulder, looking pleased with itself.
>
> "My magpie flew to Vadoalto on Tuesday," Bramble says, "and it heard the mill bell from there. It came straight back."
>
> "It came back because I was baking," says the baker.
>
> The miller wipes flour off her hands. "Vadoalto is eighteen kilometers away by the brook. I've rung that bell for years, and nobody past the ribbon gate has ever said they heard it."
>
> "Maybe they weren't listening," says Bramble.
>
> {guide} unrolls the valley map on the bench. "We can't ask the magpie. But we can work out how long the sound would take, and how quiet it would be by then."
>
> The magpie says nothing, which is very magpie of it.

**Keeper's note:**
> In the valley model, sound always travels at 343 meters each second, near or far. What changes with distance is how loud it is. The model can't tell us what a real magpie heard. It can tell us whether Bramble's claim fits the rules.

**Longer:**

## Activity a: Make it louder, make it higher

**Model label (every screen):** Simplified picture: the trace shows swing size and swing speed. It is not a measurement. Any sound you play is a simple computer tone, not a recording.

**Audio line (by Play):** Start with your device volume low. Stop whenever you want. You can do all of this without sound.

**Makers and dials:**

| Maker | How hard dial | Pitch dial | Action button |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Drum | Gentle · Medium · Hard | Skin: Loose · Medium · Tight | Strike |
| Ruler | Gentle · Medium · Hard | Over the edge: Long · Medium · Short | Twang |
| String | Gentle · Medium · Hard | String: Loose · Medium · Tight | Pluck |

**Trace caption:** Swing size: {small / medium / big}. Swings in this window: {2 / 3 / 4}.
**Rice caption (drum):** The rice jumped {a little / higher / very high}.
**Compare heading:** Your last two tries
**Two changed note:** Two things changed between these tries. Which one made the difference? · **Change one back** · Keep comparing

### Teach cards

**T1. What makes a sound** (worked example, one step per tap)
> Every sound starts with something moving back and forth fast. That's a **vibration**.
1. Strike the drum.
2. Look at the rice. It jumps.
3. The skin is moving up and down fast. That makes the sound.

**T2. Bigger swing, louder sound**
> Hit harder and the skin swings farther. A bigger swing makes a louder sound. Scientists call the swing's size **amplitude**.
1. Strike gently. The rice jumps a little.
2. Strike hard. Same skin. The rice jumps higher.
3. Swing size went up. The sound got louder. The note stayed about the same.

**T3. Faster swing, higher sound**
> How fast something swings sets its **pitch**. Faster swings make a higher sound. A short ruler swings faster than a long one.
1. Twang the ruler with a long piece over the edge. Count the swings: 2.
2. Slide it in. Short piece, same twang. Swings: 4.
3. Faster swings. Higher sound. Swing size stayed the same.

**T4. Two different things** (the key contrast)
> Louder and higher are not the same. One dial changes swing size. The other changes swing speed. You can have loud and low, or quiet and high.

**T5. Volume means loudness**
> In science, **volume** here means how loud a sound is. It is a different meaning from how much space something takes up.

**T6. Sound makes things move too**
> Sound can make other things vibrate. Watch Pip shout beside the drum. He doesn't touch it.
1. Pip shouts from the side.
2. The rice jumps.
3. His voice's vibrations reached the skin and shook it.

### Spot the vibration (tap to reveal)

| Card | Reveal |
| --- | --- |
| Drum | The skin vibrates. You can see the rice jump. |
| Plucked string | The string vibrates. It blurs as it swings. |
| Wooden block, tapped | The wood vibrates, too fast and small to see. |
| Two stones clinked | The stones vibrate for a moment after they touch. |
| A whistle | The air inside the whistle vibrates. |
| A bell | The metal vibrates. Touch a ringing bell and it stops. |

**After all six:** Every sound came from something vibrating. Some vibrations hide.

### Guided first item (a)

**Prompt:** Dot strikes the drum harder. What happens to the swing size?
**Steps shown first:** 1. Same drum, same skin. 2. Only How hard changed. 3. Look at the rice.
**Choices:** It gets bigger · It stays the same · It gets smaller
**Answer:** It gets bigger.
- *It stays the same* (`M-object-volume`): Look at the rice. It jumped higher, so the skin swung farther.
- *It gets smaller*: Hitting harder pushes the skin farther. Watch the rice again.
- *Right:* Yes. A harder hit, a bigger swing, a louder sound.

### Practice (a)

1. **Prompt:** Change only one dial. Make the string sound higher.
   **Answer:** Tighten the string (Pitch dial to Tight). Feedback if How hard changed instead: "That made it louder. Which dial changes swing speed?"
2. **Prompt:** Make the ruler louder, but keep the same note.
   **Answer:** Turn How hard up; leave the overhang alone. Feedback if overhang changed: "The note changed too. Put the ruler back and try the other dial."
3. **Prompt:** Make a quiet, high drum sound.
   **Answer:** Gentle, Tight. Feedback: "Quiet and high at once. Two dials, two different things."

**Hint (all practice):** One dial is for swing size. One is for swing speed.
**I'm stuck worked example:** "To make the ruler higher: keep How hard the same, slide the ruler in so less hangs over. Now try your one: which dial is for speed?"

## Activity b: Through what?

### Teach card

**T7. Sound needs something to travel through**
> A vibration has to be passed on. Air, water, wood and string can all pass it on. Empty space has nothing to pass it.
1. Pip taps the table.
2. The wood vibrates and passes it along.
3. Dot, with her ear on the table, hears the tap.

## Activity c: Bell down the valley

**Model line (every result):** This is a made-up model. Real hearing also depends on wind, hills, trees, buildings and other noise.

**About the map (Details):** A story from Willowbank. The valley, its places and its distances are made up. Les Saules and Vadoalto use the distances along the brook from the miller's flood-bell notes.

**About loudness steps (Details):** Made-up model. The bell starts with some loudness steps. Every time the distance doubles, the sound spreads over more space and loses one step. At 0 steps, you can't hear it. In a storm, rain covers anything under 3 steps.

### Teach card

**T8. Sound takes time**
> Sound travels about 343 meters every second in air. Farther away, it takes longer to arrive. It never slows down on the way.
1. The bakery is 200 meters away.
2. Time = distance ÷ speed.
3. 200 ÷ 343 = about 0.6 seconds.

**T9. Sound spreads out**
> Sound spreads in every direction, like ripples. Farther away, the same sound is spread thinner. So it's quieter. It isn't slower.

### Guided first item (c)

**Prompt:** The library is 400 meters away. How long does the bell take?
**Steps shown first:** 1. Sound goes 343 meters each second. 2. Time = 400 ÷ 343. 3. Round to one decimal place.
**Answer:** About 1.2 seconds.
**If 0.9 (343 ÷ 400):** Upside down. How many 343s fit in 400? A bit more than one.
**If 400 or 137,200:** That's far too long. Sound covers 343 meters in just one second.

### Practice (c)

1. **Prompt:** The miller rings harder. Does the sound reach the library sooner?
   **Choices:** Yes, louder sounds travel faster · It arrives later · **No, same time, just louder**
   - *Faster* (`M-loud-faster`): Loudness doesn't change speed. It still takes 1.2 seconds.
   - *Later*: Speed stays 343 meters a second. Only the loudness changed.
2. **Prompt:** Tap Storm. Which places can still hear the bell?
   **Answer:** The bakery, the library and Hob's farm. Feedback: "Rain noise covers the quieter places. The sound still travels at the same speed."
3. **Prompt:** Dot rings her hand bell hard. Where can it be heard?
   **Answer:** Only at the bakery, just (1 step). Feedback: "A smaller bell starts with fewer steps, so it fades out sooner."

**Hint (all practice):** Look at the step meter, not the clock.
**I'm stuck worked example:** "The bakery is 200 meters. 200 ÷ 343 is about 0.6 seconds. Now try 400 meters the same way."

## Activity d: Thunder counter

**Line on every screen:** This estimates distance. It is not a safety tool. In a real storm, follow official weather advice.

### Teach card

**T10. Count from flash to bang**
> Light reaches you almost at once. Thunder's sound takes about 3 seconds for each kilometer. Count the seconds, then divide by 3.
1. Flash. Start counting.
2. Bang at 6 seconds.
3. 6 ÷ 3 = 2. About 2 kilometers away.

**Miles card (Details):** Using miles? Sound takes about 5 seconds for each mile. Divide by 5.

### Guided first item (d)

**Prompt:** The librarian counts 12 seconds. About how far away is the lightning?
**Steps shown first:** 1. About 3 seconds for each kilometer. 2. 12 ÷ 3 = ?
**Answer:** About 4 kilometers.
**If 12:** That's the count, in seconds. Divide by 3 to change it to kilometers.
**If 36:** Too far. Sound covers a kilometer in about 3 seconds, so divide.

### Storm row (older)

**Counts across the evening:** 12 s · 9 s · 6 s · 9 s
**Prompt:** What is the storm doing?
**Answer:** Coming closer (4, 3, 2 km), then moving away (3 km).
**Feedback:** Shorter counts mean closer lightning. When counts grow, it's moving away.

## Problem sets

Each item lists: depth, format, prompt, choices with the answer in bold, misconception tags and feedback, hint, explanation, and an I'm stuck worked example where useful. Feedback is 20 words or fewer.

### Set A. Louder or higher?

**A1** · Sprout · 3-choice
- **Prompt:** What makes the drum's sound?
- **Choices:** The wood the drum is made of · **The skin shaking back and forth** · The stick being so hard
- *Wood*: The wood is part of it, but the sound comes from the skin vibrating.
- *Stick being so hard* (`M-visible-only`): The stick starts it. The skin's vibration makes the sound.
- **Hint:** Watch the rice when the drum sounds.
- **Explanation:** Sounds come from vibrations. The rice jumps because the skin is vibrating.

**A2** · Sprout · 3-choice
- **Prompt:** You clink two stones together. What vibrates?
- **Choices:** Nothing, stones can't move · Only the air around your hands · **The stones**
- *Nothing* (`M-visible-only`): Stones do vibrate, just too small and fast to see.
- *Only the air*: The air carries the sound, but the stones vibrate first.
- **Hint:** Every sound starts with something vibrating. What touched?
- **Explanation:** Hidden vibrations still make sound. The stones shake for a moment after they touch.

**A3** · Sprout · 3-choice
- **Prompt:** Pip shouts beside the drum without touching it. Why does the rice jump?
- **Choices:** **His voice made the skin vibrate** · The rice jumps by itself · His breath blew the rice
- *By itself*: Rice stays still until something shakes it.
- *Breath*: Pip shouted from the side, so his breath went past. The sound shook the skin.
- **Tag on the item:** `M-reverse-missing` (sound cannot make things vibrate).
- **Hint:** Sound is a vibration. What could it shake?
- **Explanation:** Sound can make other things vibrate. Pip's voice reached the skin and shook it.

**A4** · Seedling · 3-choice
- **Prompt:** Dot rings her bell harder. What changes?
- **Choices:** It sounds higher · **It sounds louder, same note** · It sounds louder and higher
- *Higher* (`M-harder-higher`): Harder makes a bigger swing. That's louder. The note stays about the same.
- *Louder and higher* (`M-loud-high`): Louder, yes. Higher needs faster swings, and the same bell swings at about the same speed.
- **Hint:** Ringing harder changes the size of the swing.
- **Explanation:** A harder ring makes a bigger vibration, so a louder sound. The bell's size sets its note.

**A5** · Seedling · Order the cards (two rows)
- **Prompt:** Put each row in order.
- **Row 1 cards:** Louder sound · Hard hit · Big swing → **Hard hit, Big swing, Louder sound**
- **Row 2 cards:** Small swing · Quiet sound · Gentle tap → **Gentle tap, Small swing, Quiet sound**
- **Feedback if swing and sound swap:** The swing comes first. The sound comes from the swing.
- **Hint:** What do you do first? What moves next?
- **Explanation:** The pattern works both ways: harder, bigger, louder; gentler, smaller, quieter.

**A6** · Seedling · 3-choice
- **Prompt:** You slide the ruler in. Less hangs over. What changes?
- **Choices:** It sounds lower · It sounds louder · **It sounds higher**
- *Lower* (`M-big-higher`, reversed): A shorter piece swings faster. Faster swings sound higher.
- *Louder* (`M-loud-high`): Swing size didn't change. Swing speed did. That changes pitch.
- **Hint:** Short things swing fast or slow?
- **Explanation:** A shorter ruler vibrates faster, so its pitch is higher.

**A7** · Seedling · 3-choice
- **Prompt:** Which change makes the string sound higher?
- **Choices:** Pluck it harder · **Tighten it** · Loosen it
- *Pluck harder* (`M-harder-higher`): That makes it louder. The note stays about the same.
- *Loosen* (reversed pattern): A looser string swings slower. That's lower.
- **Hint:** One change makes swings faster.
- **Explanation:** A tighter string vibrates faster, so it sounds higher.

**A8** · Sapling · Match
- **Prompt:** Match each trace to how it sounds.
- **Trace A:** 2 swings, big size. **Trace B:** 4 swings, small size.
- **Labels:** Loud and low · Quiet and high
- **Answer:** A = **Loud and low**; B = **Quiet and high**.
- **Feedback if A = quiet and high** (`M-loud-high`): Big swings mean loud. Few swings mean low. A is loud and low.
- **Hint:** Size tells loudness. Count of swings tells pitch.
- **Explanation:** The two qualities are separate. A sound can be loud and low.
- **I'm stuck:** "A trace with 3 small swings: small size means quiet; 3 swings is middle pitch. Now look at A's size first."

**A9** · Sapling · 3-choice
- **Prompt:** The mill bell and Dot's bell are the same shape. Which sounds lower?
- **Choices:** Dot's small bell · They match, both are bells · **The big mill bell**
- *Dot's small bell* (`M-big-higher`): Bigger bells vibrate more slowly. Slower means lower.
- *They match*: Size matters. Same shape and metal, but the bigger one vibrates slower.
- **Hint:** Think of the long ruler and the short ruler.
- **Explanation:** For bells of the same shape and metal, a bigger bell vibrates more slowly and sounds lower.

**A10** · Sapling · Say why (a grown-up listens; not graded)
- **Prompt:** Bramble says loud and high are the same. What would you show her?
- **A good answer might:** use the bench: turn How hard up with the pitch dial fixed (louder, same note), then change only the pitch dial (higher, same swing size).
- **Hint:** Use one dial, then the other.

### Set B. Through what, and how fast?

**Teach card T11. Different speeds**
> Sound goes about 343 meters a second in air, about 1,480 in water, and about 5,900 in steel. Solids are often fastest.
1. Air: 343 meters in one second.
2. Water: more than four times as far.
3. Steel: about seventeen times as far.

**B1** · Seedling · 3-choice
- **Prompt:** Can sound travel through water?
- **Choices:** **Yes, and faster than through air** · No, only through air · Yes, but slower than through air
- *No* (`M-air-only`): Water carries sound well. Whales call to each other through it.
- *Slower* (`M-gases-fastest`): Surprising, but water carries sound about four times faster than air.
- **Hint:** Think of Dot's spoons in the bowl.
- **Explanation:** Sound travels through liquids. In water it is about four times faster than in air.

**B2** · Seedling · 3-choice
- **Prompt:** Why can you hear Pip through the wall?
- **Choices:** The sound floats round it like smoke · **The wall vibrates and passes it on** · You only hear him through gaps
- *Like smoke* (sound as a substance): Sound isn't stuff. It's a vibration passed through the wall.
- *Gaps* (`M-wall-blocks`): Gaps let sound through too. The solid wall passes it on as well.
- **Hint:** Solids can carry vibrations.
- **Explanation:** The wall passes the vibration on. It comes through quieter, but it comes through.

**B3** · Seedling · 3-choice
- **Prompt:** Why can't astronauts in space hear each other shout?
- **Choices:** Space is too cold · Space is so big the sound fades · **There's nothing to carry the sound**
- *Too cold*: Cold air still carries sound. Space has no air at all.
- *Fades* (`M-space-sound`): Even right beside each other, there's nothing to pass it on.
- **Hint:** What does sound need to travel through?
- **Explanation:** Sound needs a material. Space is almost empty, so astronauts use radios.

**B4** · Sapling · Order the cards
- **Prompt:** Order from fastest sound to slowest.
- **Cards:** Air · Steel · Water → **Steel, Water, Air**
- **Feedback if Air first** (`M-gases-fastest`): Lots of space between air particles slows it. Close-packed steel passes it fastest.
- **Hint:** Use the teach card numbers.
- **Explanation:** Steel about 5,900, water about 1,480, air about 343 meters per second.

**B5** · Sapling · Number
- **Prompt:** Sound in water goes 1,480 meters a second. How far in 2 seconds?
- **Answer:** **2,960 meters.** Steps: 1,480 × 2 = 2,960.
- **If 740:** You divided. In 2 seconds it goes twice as far.
- **Hint:** Distance = speed × time.
- **Explanation:** Every second adds another 1,480 meters.

**B6** · Sapling · Number (two parts)
- **Prompt:** Bramble taps a 59 meter steel rail. Which sound reaches you first?
- **Follow-up prompt:** How much sooner does it arrive?
- **Steps (guided):** 1. Steel: 59 ÷ 5,900 = 0.01 s. 2. Air: 59 ÷ 343 = about 0.17 s. 3. Subtract.
- **Answer:** **Through the steel, about 0.16 seconds sooner.**
- **If "through the air"** (`M-gases-fastest`): Steel is the faster path. Check the two times.
- **Hint:** Work out each time, then compare.
- **Explanation:** You could hear two taps: one through the rail, then one through the air.

**B7** · Sapling · 3-choice
- **Prompt:** You shout at a cliff 170 meters away. When does the echo return?
- **Choices:** About 0.5 seconds · **About 1 second** · About 2 seconds
- *0.5 s* (`M-echo-one-way`): That's one way. The echo goes there and back: 340 meters.
- *2 s*: Too long. 340 meters at 343 a second is about 1 second.
- **Hint:** An echo travels to the cliff and back.
- **Explanation:** 170 × 2 = 340 meters. 340 ÷ 343 is about 1 second.
- **I'm stuck:** "A wall 343 meters away: there and back is 686 meters, which takes 2 seconds. Now double 170."

**B8** · Keeper's Desk · Number
- **Prompt:** Your echo comes back after 3 seconds. How far is the cliff?
- **Answer:** **About 515 meters** (514.5). Steps: 3 × 343 = 1,029 meters there and back; 1,029 ÷ 2 = 514.5.
- **If about 1,029** (`M-echo-one-way`): That's there and back. Halve it for the distance to the cliff.
- **Hint:** First find how far the sound went in all.
- **Explanation:** Echo timing is how ships measure depth with sound, using water's speed instead.

**B9** · Keeper's Desk · 3-choice
- **Prompt:** Why does sound travel faster in steel than in air?
- **Choices:** **Steel's particles are close and tightly held** · Sound squeezes through big gaps faster · Steel is heavy, so it pushes harder
- *Gaps* (`M-gases-fastest`): Gaps slow it. Close, linked particles pass the vibration on quickly.
- *Heavy*: Weight alone isn't the reason. How tightly particles are held together matters.
- **Hint:** Think about passing a push along a line of people holding hands.
- **Explanation:** Close, tightly linked particles hand on the vibration fast. Speeds still vary between materials.

**Keeper's note (after B9):**
> In air, a vibrating object squashes the air next to it, then lets it spread, again and again. Squashed parts are called compressions; spread parts are rarefactions. They travel outward. The air itself only jiggles in place.

### Set C. Down the valley

**C1** · Seedling · Number (guided)
- **Prompt:** Hob's farm is 800 meters from the bell. How long does the sound take?
- **Steps:** 1. Sound goes 343 meters each second. 2. 800 ÷ 343. 3. Round to one decimal place.
- **Answer:** **About 2.3 seconds.**
- **If 0.4:** Upside down. How many 343s fit in 800? More than two.
- **Hint:** Time = distance ÷ speed.
- **Explanation:** 800 ÷ 343 is about 2.3. Farther places take longer.

**C2** · Seedling · 3-choice
- **Prompt:** Why is the bell quieter at the orchard than at the bakery?
- **Choices:** The sound slows down on the way · **The sound spreads out over more space** · The sound gets used up
- *Slows down* (`M-fade-slows`): Speed stays 343 meters a second. It spreads thinner instead.
- *Used up* (runs-out idea): Spread is the reason. The same sound covers more space far away.
- **Hint:** Picture ripples in a pond.
- **Explanation:** Sound spreads in all directions, so less of it reaches a faraway ear.

**C3** · Sapling · 3-choice
- **Prompt:** In the model, could the magpie hear the mill bell from Vadoalto?
- **Choices:** Yes, on a still day · We can't tell anything without going there · **No, it fades out long before**
- *Still day* (overclaim): Even on a still day the model's bell fades to nothing by 6,400 meters.
- *Can't tell* (underclaim): The model can't prove what a magpie heard. It does show the bell fading out.
- **Hint:** Look at the steps at the ribbon gate, 3,200 meters.
- **Explanation:** The bell drops to 0 steps at 6,400 meters. Vadoalto is about 18 kilometers away.

**C4** · Sapling · Number
- **Prompt:** If the sound could reach Vadoalto, 18,000 meters away, how long would it take?
- **Answer:** **About 52 seconds**, under a minute (accept 52 to 53). Steps: 18,000 ÷ 343 = 52.5.
- **If about 6 hours:** That's the floodwater. Sound is far faster than water.
- **Hint:** Same rule: distance ÷ 343.
- **Explanation:** Time isn't the problem. Loudness is: the sound would arrive too faint to hear.

**C5** · Sapling · Match
- **Prompt:** Both sound equally quiet. Match each to its reason.
- **A:** The miller rings gently. You're at the bakery. **B:** The miller rings hard. You're at Hob's farm.
- **Reasons:** The bell swung less · The sound spread out over distance
- **Answer:** A = **The bell swung less**; B = **The sound spread out over distance**.
- **Feedback if swapped** (`M-object-volume`): At the bakery you're close, so the gentle ring is why. At Hob's, distance is why.
- **Hint:** Which one changed the bell? Which one changed where you stood?
- **Explanation:** Both arrive with 3 steps. One is quiet at the bell; the other gets quiet on the way.

**C6** · Seedling · 3-choice
- **Prompt:** The librarian counts 9 seconds after a flash. How far is the lightning?
- **Choices:** About 27 kilometers · About 9 kilometers · **About 3 kilometers**
- *27* (multiplied): Divide, don't multiply. 3 seconds for each kilometer.
- *9* (count taken as distance): Nine is the seconds. Divide by 3 to get kilometers.
- **Tag on the item:** `M-light-instant-sound-instant` if the child says thunder and lightning arrive together (grown-up prompt).
- **Hint:** About 3 seconds for every kilometer.
- **Explanation:** 9 ÷ 3 = 3. The light came almost at once; the sound took 9 seconds.

**C7** · Sapling · 3-choice
- **Prompt:** Sound takes 2.9 seconds per kilometer. Is "divide by 3" a bit high or low?
- **Choices:** **A little too low** · A little too high · Exactly right
- *Too high*: Dividing by 3 gives 3 km for 9 s. The exact answer is 3.09 km.
- *Exactly right* (`M-rule-exact`): Close, but 3 is a rounded number. It's out by about 3 in every 100.
- **Hint:** Try 9 seconds both ways: 9 ÷ 3, and 9 × 0.343.
- **Explanation:** The rule gives a slightly short distance. It's a handy estimate, not an exact one.

**C9** · Keeper's Desk · Sort
- **Prompt:** A fair test: which bell carries farther? Sort each card.
- **Bins:** Keeps the test fair · Makes the test unfair
- **Cards:** Same listening spots for both bells (**fair**) · Same ringer, same strength (**fair**) · Ring both on the same still day (**fair**) · Listeners who don't know which bell rang (**fair**) · Repeat each bell several times (**fair**) · Ring the new bell on a still day, the old one in rain (**unfair**) · Bramble listens for one, Pip for the other (**unfair**)
- **Feedback on an unfair card placed as fair:** That changes two things at once. Then you can't tell what made the difference.
- **Hint:** Only the bell should change.
- **Explanation:** A fair test changes one thing. Everything else, weather, spots, ringer and listeners, stays the same.

**C10** · Keeper's Desk · 3-choice
- **Prompt:** The ribbon gate is twice as far as the top field. What happens?
- **Choices:** **The sound spreads over four times the area** · The sound spreads over twice the area · The sound spreads over the same area
- *Twice* (common slip): Spreading goes up and sideways, so doubling the distance gives four times the area.
- *Same* (`M-one-way`): Farther away, the sound covers more space, so less reaches each ear.
- **Hint:** Picture a square 1 step wide, then 2 steps wide.
- **Explanation:** In open air, each doubling spreads sound over four times the area. The model drops one step.

## Key terms

| Term | Child-friendly definition |
| --- | --- |
| vibration | Moving back and forth very fast. |
| amplitude | How big a vibration's swing is. Bigger swing, louder sound. |
| volume | How loud a sound is. |
| pitch | How high or low a sound is. Faster vibrations, higher pitch. |
| material | The stuff something is made of, like air, water or wood. |
| echo | Sound that bounced back to you off something. |
| speed of sound | How far sound travels in one second. |
| model | A simple picture of how something works, with some things left out. |
| eardrum | A thin skin inside the ear that vibrates when sound arrives. |
| fair test | A test where only one thing changes. |

## How you hear (looking only, no questions)

**Card:** A true thing about sound
> Sound goes into the ear and makes the eardrum vibrate. Three tiny bones pass it on. Deep inside, tiny hairs turn it into messages for the brain.

## Grown-up panel

**What this teaches:** sounds come from vibrations; louder means a bigger vibration and higher means a faster one, and these are separate; sound needs a material and cannot cross empty space; it travels at a speed set by the material (about 343 m/s in air at 20 °C, about 1,480 m/s in fresh water, about 5,900 m/s in steel); it fades because it spreads out; echoes are reflections; light arrives almost at once, so flash-to-bang counts estimate distance.

**What it does not claim:** the bench traces are schematics, not measurements. Any sound played is a simple computer tone at fixed digital levels, not a recording or a decibel level. The valley map, its distances and the loudness steps are a made-up model; real hearing depends on wind, terrain, buildings and background noise. The thunder counter estimates distance and is not storm-safety guidance; follow official weather services. The ear card is for looking only; this activity asks nothing about anyone's hearing.

**Sources in brief:** speed-of-sound values from physics teaching and ultrasonic-testing references (conditions in the source audit); the divide-by-5 (miles) rule from the US National Weather Service and the divide-by-3 (kilometers) rule as reported from the UK Met Office; the ear card from the NIDCD; teaching approach informed by Marble's teaching research (paraphrased). The National Weather Service advises being in a safe place while you count.

**Try it together (adult-led, off-screen):**
- *Rice on a drum:* sprinkle a little dry rice on a drum or a tight plastic-wrap cover over a bowl. Tap gently, then harder. Watch the rice. Then bang a metal tray close by without touching the drum (results vary; a loud, close sound works best).
- *Ruler twang:* hold a ruler firmly on a table edge and twang it. Change only how far it hangs over, then only how hard you twang. Keep fingers clear of the swinging end.
- *String telephone:* two paper cups and a long string. Pull it tight, then let it go slack.
- *Louder far away?* Somewhere open and safe, away from traffic, one person stands still and claps the same way each time; the other walks away in steps. Talk about what changes and what doesn't.
- Keep sounds at a comfortable level. Never shout or clap close to anyone's ear.
