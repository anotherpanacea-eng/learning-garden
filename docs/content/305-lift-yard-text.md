**The Lift Yard** · Help restock Willowbank's upper storeroom. Choose a ramp, then pull the delivery to the top mark. A longer ramp can need less pull, but more rope.

**Plan / Pull** · **Yard / Numbers** · **Other deliveries** · **Return to current job**. Jobs wait while you look around this yard. Leaving the yard or reloading starts a fresh shift. Nothing here changes your saved learning notes.

**About this model**: “The drum slides; it does not roll. The rope pulls parallel to the board. We imagine steady movement with no friction or winch losses, and ignore starting and stopping. A holding brake keeps each checkpoint still. Real lifting takes suitable equipment and trained people.”

**Why does the work stay the same?** “For this model, required pull = load weight × height ÷ ramp length. A 12 N load rising 1 m needs 12 J of rope work. On a 2 m ramp it needs 6 N for 2 m; on a 4 m ramp, 3 N for 4 m. The rope's positive work increases gravitational potential energy of the load and Earth. This is not net work from all forces. The ramp supports the drum and gravity also acts; with steady motion, all forces on the drum together do zero net work: rope +{F*L} J, gravity −{w*h} J, ramp support 0 J. Its speed stays constant.”
