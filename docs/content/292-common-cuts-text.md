Compact readout: Still {a}/{d}; {count} of {denominator} equal pieces colored. Each piece is 1/{denominator} of the whole.
Full optional explanation: Now {count} of {denominator} equal pieces are colored: {count}/{denominator}. The colored end is still at {a}/{d}.
Unit: Each current piece is 1/{denominator} of this same-sized whole.
Split: Each old piece became {factor} smaller equal pieces. Both counts changed; the amount did not. Dashed marks are the cuts just added.
Undo: The last cut marks were removed. The colored end stayed still. The earlier marks still show the larger pieces.
No split: These are the starting equal pieces. Count spaces, not dividing marks.
Bound: This table shows at most 12 equal pieces in a whole. You can undo a cut and try another way.

## Supplied comparison templates

Matched: Both ribbons now use {d} equal pieces for the same-sized whole. A has {nA} of those pieces colored; B has {nB}. {nA}/{d} {symbol} {nB}/{d}. {A reaches farther / B reaches farther / The colored ends match}. The amounts did not change when the pieces got smaller.
Unmatched: A uses {dA} pieces in its whole; B uses {dB}. Those pieces have different sizes. Comparing just the colored-piece counts will not settle the amount. Try matching the piece sizes, or compare the fixed ends.
Talk: How did the labels change while the colored ends stayed still? Why can you compare the counts now?
Grown-up note: All labels and explanations here are supplied model readouts. Button use and narration do not establish independent understanding. This explores a common-unit method; endpoints and benchmarks are useful ways to compare too.
