## The story scene: the wall in the archive

Adapter to spec 120, Act I (after the welcome-back supper). Fiction throughout; the dates on the Long Line are real.

**Hook (30 words max):**
> The librarian has a long paper strip and a box of pins. Pip has a biscuit. Bramble has opinions.

**Short (30 words max):**
> The librarian and Pip pin a long line on the archive wall. Bramble says her grandmother's ribbons are older than everything. The librarian keeps unrolling the line.

**Longer (150 words max):**
> The librarian pins one end of a paper strip by the archive door. Pip holds the roll.
>
> "Real dates go on this one," says the librarian. "The Moon landing near this end. The Paris flood a little before."
>
> "And the supper?" asks Pip. "That was the best thing that ever happened."
>
> "The supper goes up there." The librarian points to a short bunting strip. "Willowbank's own story."
>
> Bramble ties a red ribbon to the far left of the bunting strip. "My grandmother's ribbons. Older than everything."
>
> "Older than everything on this strip," says the librarian. "We can't say when the first one was tied. Now hold this." They hand her the roll and walk backward. Past the shelves. Past the window. Past the door.
>
> "Where are you going?"
>
> "To the pyramids," calls the librarian.
>
> Bramble looks at her ribbon for a long moment. "Fine. Older than the bakery."

**Keeper's note (80 words max):**
> Hollis's pages carry dates, counted from the town's own events: the year of the fine harvest, then five years to the first big flood. Real people did this too. Egyptians dated by the year of the king's reign; Romans sometimes counted from the city's legendary founding. A town's own count can't go on the Long Line until it is tied to a real year, and Willowbank's is a story.

**Later scene, when Hollis's pages dry (Short, 30 words max):**
> Hollis wrote "the year of the fine harvest" and "five years later, the first big flood." The librarian pins both on the bunting strip, five steps apart.

## Teach cards

Each card: one idea (30 words max), then a worked example revealed one step per tap.

### T1. Order first

> Order means what came first, next and last. You can put story events in order even without dates.

Worked example: *Order three Willowbank cards.*
1. The storm came in the night.
2. The next morning, Pip and Dot found the parcel.
3. Then the pages went on the bakery windowsill to dry.

### T2. Order or scale?

> Cards in a row show order. A scaled line shows how long. On it, more distance means more time.

Worked example: *Galileo 1610, Paris flood 1910, Moon landing 1969.*
1. In a row, the three cards sit the same distance apart.
2. Spaced by time, Galileo to Paris is 300 years: 3 boxes.
3. Paris to the Moon is 59 years: just over half a box.
4. Same order. Very different gaps.

### T3. BCE counts down

> BCE years count down toward the line between BCE and CE. A bigger BCE number is longer ago.

Worked example: *Which came first, 2550 BCE or 753 BCE?*
1. Both are BCE, so they count down toward the line.
2. 2550 is farther from the line than 753.
3. So 2550 BCE came first, about 1,800 years earlier.

### T4. No year zero

> The calendar has no year 0. 1 BCE is followed straight away by 1 CE. That is one year.

Worked example: *From 30 BCE to 50 CE.*
1. From 30 BCE back to the line: 30 years.
2. From the line on to 50 CE: 50 years.
3. 30 + 50 = 80, but that counts a year 0 that never happened.
4. Take away one: 79 years.

**Contrast card (shown beside T4):**
> On a thermometer, counting down goes 2, 1, 0, minus 1. On the calendar, it goes 2 CE, 1 CE, 1 BCE. No zero.

### T5. Decades and centuries

> A decade is ten years. The first century was years 1 to 100. So the 1400s are the 15th century.

Worked example: *1455.*
1. 1455 is in the 1450s, a decade.
2. Its hundreds are 1400s: fourteen hundreds.
3. Add one for the century: the 15th century.

### T6. Placing on a scaled line

> To place a date, count from a mark you know. Count the spaces, not the marks.

Worked example: *Place 1086 at 1 box = 100 years.*
1. Start at the line between BCE and CE.
2. 1000 years is 10 boxes to the right.
3. 86 more years is most of one more box.
4. 1086 sits just before the 11-box mark.

### T7. About and ranges

> "About" or "c." means close to that year. A range, like 447-432 BCE, means from one year to another.

Worked example: *The Gutenberg Bible, about 1455.*
1. Experts agree it was printed in Mainz.
2. Records point to 1454 or 1455.
3. So we write "about 1455", or "c. 1455".

### T8. Deep time needs a new scale

> Dinosaurs lived millions of years ago. That is too far for the history line. Deep time needs a bigger scale.

Worked example: *The end of the dinosaurs, about 66 million years ago.*
1. On the Long Line, 1 box = 100 years.
2. 66 million years would be 660,000 boxes.
3. On paper at 1 cm a box, that is 6.6 kilometers.
4. So we zoom out: 1 box = 1 million years.

## Problem sets

Format for each item: prompt (15 words max), choices or response, answer, misconception tags, hint, per-choice feedback (20 words max), explanation (1-2 sentences). Stuck worked examples follow each set.

### Set A. First, order (Sprout, Seedling)

**A1 (Sprout, order the cards).**
- **Prompt:** Put these Willowbank cards in order.
- **Cards:** Pip and Dot find the parcel · the storm · the supper, page 1 comes free.
- **Answer:** the storm, the parcel, the supper.
- **Hint:** What had to happen before there was a parcel to find?
- **Feedback, correct:** Yes. Storm, then parcel, then supper.
- **Feedback, other orders:** Look again. The parcel came the morning after the storm.
- **Explanation:** The storm brought the parcel, so it came first. Page 1 came free later, at the supper.

**A2 (Sprout, 3-choice).**
- **Prompt:** Which happened longest ago?
- **Choices:** a. People walk on the Moon · b. The Great Pyramid is built · c. The Paris flood
- **Answer:** b.
- **Misconceptions:** a tests "famous means old"; c tests "old photos mean very long ago".
- **Hint:** Look at the years. Which card says BCE?
- **Feedback a:** The Moon landing was in 1969, quite near now on the line.
- **Feedback b:** Yes. About 2550 BCE, more than 4,500 years ago.
- **Feedback c:** The Paris flood was in 1910. The pyramid is far older.
- **Explanation:** The pyramid is on the far left of the Long Line. The Paris flood and the Moon landing are both near now.

**A3 (Seedling, 3-choice, order vs scale).**
- **Prompt:** Which gap is longer in time?
- **Shown:** the "in a row" view with Galileo 1610, Paris flood 1910, Moon landing 1969, evenly spaced.
- **Choices:** a. Galileo to the Paris flood · b. The Paris flood to the Moon landing · c. They are the same
- **Answer:** a (300 years against 59).
- **Misconceptions:** c tests "evenly spaced cards mean equal time"; b tests "the newest gap feels biggest".
- **Hint:** Spaces in a row don't show time. Use the years.
- **Feedback a:** Yes. 300 years against 59. The row hid that.
- **Feedback b:** That gap is 59 years. Galileo to Paris is 300.
- **Feedback c:** They look the same in a row. Try "spaced by time".
- **Explanation:** In a row, cards keep order only. Spaced by time, 1610 to 1910 is three boxes and 1910 to 1969 is just over half a box.

**A4 (Seedling, 3-choice, BCE order).**
- **Prompt:** Which came first?
- **Choices:** a. The Parthenon, 447 BCE · b. Rome's legend date, 753 BCE · c. Egypt united, about 3100 BCE
- **Answer:** c.
- **Misconceptions:** a tests "a smaller BCE number is earlier".
- **Hint:** BCE counts down. Which number is farthest from the line?
- **Feedback a:** 447 is closest to the line, so it is the latest of the three.
- **Feedback b:** 753 BCE is earlier than 447 BCE, but 3100 BCE is earlier still.
- **Feedback c:** Yes. 3100 is farthest from the line, so longest ago.
- **Explanation:** BCE numbers get smaller as time moves toward the BCE/CE line. The biggest BCE number is the earliest.

**A5 (Seedling, true or not from this card).**
- **Card shown:** *A story people told.* Roman tradition gave 753 BCE as the year Romulus founded Rome.
- **Prompt:** Does this card say archaeologists found Romulus's house?
- **Choices:** a. Yes, the card says so · b. No, the card does not say that · c. The card says Romulus never lived
- **Answer:** b.
- **Misconceptions:** a tests "a legend date is evidence"; c tests overreading in the other direction.
- **Hint:** Read the card's words only. What does it tell you?
- **Feedback a:** Look again. The card tells a date Romans gave, not a find.
- **Feedback b:** Yes. It gives a tradition's date. It reports no dig.
- **Feedback c:** The card doesn't say that either. It only reports the tradition.
- **Explanation:** A legend card tells us what people said, and when they said it happened. Evidence from the ground has its own card.

**A6 (Sprout, which strip).**
- **Prompt:** Where does "Pip finds the parcel" go?
- **Choices:** a. The Willowbank strip · b. The Long Line · c. Both strips
- **Answer:** a.
- **Misconceptions:** c tests "story events can be history too"; b tests mixing story and history.
- **Hint:** Is Pip in Willowbank's story, or in real history?
- **Feedback a:** Yes. Pip's parcel is a story from Willowbank.
- **Feedback b:** The Long Line is for true things from history. Pip is in our story.
- **Feedback c:** Each card goes on one strip only. This one is a story.
- **Explanation:** Willowbank is a made-up town. Its events go on the bunting strip and never on the Long Line.

**Stuck worked examples, Set A:**
- *For A2 and A4:* "Which came first, 1910 or 1969? 1910 is a smaller CE number, so it is earlier. To start yours: find any BCE card first."
- *For A3:* "Two gaps: 1900 to 2000 and 2000 to 2010. In a row they look equal. Subtract: 100 and 10. To start yours: subtract each pair of years."

### Set B. Counting years (Seedling, Sapling)

**B1 (Seedling, number, guided).**
- **Prompt:** How many years from the Domesday survey, 1086, to the Paris flood, 1910?
- **Steps:** 1. Both are CE. 2. Subtract: 1910 − 1086.
- **Answer:** 824.
- **Misconception tag (common wrong answers):** 1086 + 1910 tests "always add"; 924 tests a borrowing slip.
- **Hint:** Both years are CE, so take the smaller from the larger.
- **Explanation:** Two CE years: subtract. 1910 − 1086 = 824.

**B2 (Seedling, number).**
- **Prompt:** About how many years from Mansa Musa's journey, 1324, to Gutenberg's Bible?
- **Card date:** about 1455.
- **Answer:** about 131.
- **Hint:** Use 1455 for "about 1455". Then subtract.
- **Explanation:** 1455 − 1324 = 131. The answer is "about" because the Bible's date is about 1455.

**B3 (Seedling, 3-choice).**
- **Prompt:** How many years from 1 BCE to 1 CE?
- **Choices:** a. 2 · b. 0 · c. 1
- **Answer:** c.
- **Misconceptions:** a tests "there is a year 0 between them"; b tests "1 minus 1".
- **Hint:** There is no year 0. What comes right after 1 BCE?
- **Feedback a:** That counts a year 0. The calendar skips straight to 1 CE.
- **Feedback b:** They are different years, one after the other. That's one year.
- **Feedback c:** Yes. 1 CE comes straight after 1 BCE.
- **Explanation:** The calendar goes from 1 BCE straight to 1 CE, so the gap is one year.

**B4 (Sapling, 3-choice, guided steps available).**
- **Prompt:** How many years from 30 BCE to 50 CE?
- **Choices:** a. 79 · b. 20 · c. 80
- **Answer:** a.
- **Misconceptions:** b tests "subtract across the line" (50 − 30); c tests "there is a year 0".
- **Hint:** Count back to the line, then on. Then remember no year 0.
- **Feedback a:** Yes. 30 + 50 − 1 = 79.
- **Feedback b:** Subtracting works for two CE years. These are on both sides of the line.
- **Feedback c:** Close. That counts a year 0. Take one away.
- **Explanation:** Across the line, add the two numbers and take away one. 30 + 50 − 1 = 79.

**B5 (Sapling, number).**
- **Prompt:** How many years from Rome's legend date, 753 BCE, to 1086 CE?
- **Answer:** 1,838.
- **Misconception tags:** 1,839 (no "minus one"); 333 (subtracting across the line).
- **Hint:** One BCE year, one CE year. Add, then take one away.
- **Explanation:** 753 + 1086 − 1 = 1,838.

**B6 (Sapling, number).**
- **Prompt:** How long from Egypt united, about 3100 BCE, to 30 BCE?
- **Answer:** about 3,070 years.
- **Misconception tags:** 3,130 or 3,129 (adding two BCE years as if across the line).
- **Hint:** Both are BCE. Same side of the line: subtract.
- **Explanation:** 3100 − 30 = 3,070. Ancient Egypt lasted more than 3,000 years.

**B7 (Sapling, 3-choice, compare).**
- **Prompt:** From 30 BCE to the Moon landing in 1969: longer or shorter than ancient Egypt?
- **Choices:** a. Longer · b. About the same · c. Shorter
- **Answer:** c (1,998 years against about 3,070).
- **Misconceptions:** a tests "recent history feels longer because more is known".
- **Hint:** Work out 30 BCE to 1969 first. Remember: no year 0.
- **Feedback a:** 30 + 1969 − 1 = 1,998. Egypt's span was about 3,070.
- **Feedback b:** One is about 2,000 years, the other about 3,000. Not close.
- **Feedback c:** Yes. Ancient Egypt lasted longer than all the time since.
- **Explanation:** 30 BCE to 1969 is 1,998 years. Ancient Egypt's span was about 3,070 years, longer by more than 1,000.

**B8 (Sapling, number).**
- **Prompt:** About how many years from the Parthenon's start, 447 BCE, to the Pont du Gard?
- **Card date:** around 50 CE.
- **Answer:** about 496.
- **Hint:** BCE to CE: add, then take one away.
- **Explanation:** 447 + 50 − 1 = 496. "About", because the Pont du Gard's date is about.

**Stuck worked examples, Set B:**
- *For B1, B2, B6:* "From 1900 to 1950? Same side of the line, so subtract: 50. To start yours: check both years are on the same side."
- *For B3, B4, B5, B7, B8:* "From 2 BCE to 2 CE? 2 back, 2 on: 4. No year 0: 3. Check: 2 BCE, 1 BCE, 1 CE, 2 CE is three steps. To start yours: count back to the line first."

### Set C. Centuries and scale (Sapling)

**C1 (3-choice).**
- **Prompt:** Gutenberg's Bible, about 1455, is from which century?
- **Choices:** a. The 14th century · b. The 15th century · c. The 16th century
- **Answer:** b.
- **Misconceptions:** a tests "the century is the first two digits".
- **Hint:** The 1400s: fourteen hundreds. Then add one.
- **Feedback a:** The first two digits are 14, but centuries count from 1. Add one.
- **Feedback b:** Yes. The 1400s are the 15th century.
- **Feedback c:** The 16th century is the 1500s. 1455 comes before that.
- **Explanation:** The 1st century was years 1 to 100, so each century's number is one more than its hundreds.

**C2 (3-choice).**
- **Prompt:** The Moon landing, 1969: which decade and century?
- **Choices:** a. The 1960s, 19th century · b. The 1970s, 20th century · c. The 1960s, 20th century
- **Answer:** c.
- **Misconceptions:** a tests "first two digits name the century"; b tests rounding the year instead of naming its decade.
- **Hint:** The decade uses the tens. The century adds one to the hundreds.
- **Feedback a:** The decade is right. For the century, add one to nineteen hundreds.
- **Feedback b:** 1969 rounds to 1970, but its decade is 1960 to 1969.
- **Feedback c:** Yes. The 1960s, in the 20th century.
- **Explanation:** 1969 is in the ten years from 1960 to 1969. Its hundreds are the 1900s, so it is in the 20th century.

**C3 (3-choice).**
- **Prompt:** The Parthenon, 447 BCE, is from which century?
- **Choices:** a. The 5th century BCE · b. The 4th century BCE · c. The 5th century CE
- **Answer:** a.
- **Misconceptions:** b tests "the century is the first digit"; c tests dropping BCE.
- **Hint:** BCE centuries work the same way. 447 is in the 400s.
- **Feedback a:** Yes. The 400s BCE are the 5th century BCE.
- **Feedback b:** The 4th century BCE is the 300s BCE. Add one to four hundreds.
- **Feedback c:** The number is right, but keep the BCE. It's before the line.
- **Explanation:** Counting back from the line, the 1st century BCE is 1 to 100 BCE. So 401 to 500 BCE is the 5th.

**C4 (3-choice, paper scale).**
- **Prompt:** On paper, 1 cm = 100 years. Where does 1086 go?
- **Choices:** a. About 1.1 cm right of the line · b. About 108.6 cm right of the line · c. About 10.9 cm right of the line
- **Answer:** c.
- **Misconceptions:** a tests dividing by 1,000; b tests dividing by 10.
- **Hint:** How many hundreds are in 1086?
- **Feedback a:** That is 110 years. 1086 has nearly 11 hundreds.
- **Feedback b:** That would be 1 cm for every 10 years. Here it's 100.
- **Feedback c:** Yes. 1086 ÷ 100 is about 10.9 cm.
- **Explanation:** Each centimeter is 100 years, so divide by 100. 1086 ÷ 100 = 10.86, about 10.9 cm.

**C5 (place on the line).**
- **Prompt:** Place Rome's legend date, 753 BCE, on the Long Line.
- **Answer:** about 7.5 boxes left of the BCE/CE line (accepted within half a box of 7.53).
- **Misconception tags:** placing at 7.5 boxes right (dropping BCE); placing near 753 counting marks from the left end.
- **Hint:** BCE goes left of the line. Count the spaces.
- **Feedback, on the spot:** Yes. Seven and a half boxes back from the line.
- **Feedback, right side:** That is 753 CE. BCE dates go to the left.
- **Feedback, other:** Start at the BCE/CE line and count boxes to the left.
- **Explanation:** 753 BCE is 7.53 hundreds of years before the line, so about seven and a half boxes to the left.

**C6 (number, paper scale).**
- **Prompt:** At 1 cm = 100 years, how long is Egypt united to the Moon landing?
- **Answer:** about 51 cm (5,068 years).
- **Steps (stuck help):** 1. 3100 + 1969 − 1 = 5,068 years. 2. ÷ 100 = 50.68. 3. About 51 cm.
- **Hint:** First find the years. Then divide by 100.
- **Explanation:** 5,068 years at 100 years a centimeter is about 50.7 cm, about half a meter.

**C7 (say why, read by a grown-up).**
- **Prompt:** At this scale, why can't you see the missing year 0?
- **Look-for:** one year is a tenth of a millimeter (0.01 cm), too thin to see; the "minus one" changes the count but not the picture.
- **Hint:** How long is one year at 1 cm = 100 years?
- **Explanation:** One year is 0.01 cm. The missing year matters when you count, but it is far too small to see on this line.

**Stuck worked examples, Set C:**
- *For C1, C2, C3:* "1250? Hundreds: 1200s, twelve hundreds. Add one: 13th century. To start yours: find the hundreds."
- *For C4, C5, C6:* "At 1 cm = 100 years, where is 500 CE? 500 ÷ 100 = 5 cm right. To start yours: divide the years by 100."

### Set D. About, ranges and deep time (Keeper's Desk)

**D1 (3-choice, with an underclaim).**
- **Card shown:** Khufu's reign: about 2589-2566 BCE in some lists; 2551-2528 BCE in The Met's records.
- **Prompt:** Which is the most honest way to date the Great Pyramid?
- **Choices:** a. Nobody can say, because the lists disagree · b. About 2550 BCE; the lists differ by decades · c. In exactly 2550 BCE, with no doubt at all
- **Answer:** b.
- **Misconceptions:** c overclaims ("a number on a card is exact"); a underclaims ("if lists differ, nothing is known").
- **Hint:** The lists disagree a little. Do they disagree a lot?
- **Feedback a:** The lists agree on the century. That's a lot to know.
- **Feedback b:** Yes. Close agreement, honestly marked with "about".
- **Feedback c:** The lists differ, so "exactly" says more than the evidence does.
- **Explanation:** Both lists put Khufu in the same few decades. "About 2550 BCE" keeps what we know and admits what we don't.

**D2 (number).**
- **Prompt:** What range covers both lists? How many years wide is it?
- **Answer:** 2589-2528 BCE, 61 years wide.
- **Hint:** Take the earliest start and the latest end. Both are BCE.
- **Explanation:** The earliest year is 2589 BCE and the latest is 2528 BCE. Both BCE, so subtract: 61 years.

**D3 (number, then say why).**
- **Card shown:** The Pont du Gard. Older books: about 19 BCE. The site today: around 50 CE.
- **Prompt:** How many years apart are the two dates?
- **Answer:** about 68 years (19 + 50 − 1).
- **Say why (grown-up reads):** Why might a date change?
- **Look-for:** new digs found new evidence; an older idea was based on who people thought built it.
- **Hint:** One date is BCE and one is CE.
- **Explanation:** 19 + 50 − 1 = 68. Newer excavations pointed to the middle of the 1st century CE, so the date moved.

**D4 (3-choice).**
- **Card shown:** Library catalog: Gutenberg Bible, "between 1454 and 1455?"
- **Prompt:** What does the question mark tell you?
- **Choices:** a. The catalog thinks the Bible is fake · b. The catalog isn't certain of the exact year · c. The year is unknown, maybe any century
- **Answer:** b.
- **Misconceptions:** a tests "uncertain date means a false object"; c underclaims.
- **Hint:** Is the doubt about the object, or about its year?
- **Feedback a:** The doubt is about the year, not whether the Bible is real.
- **Feedback b:** Yes. Two possible years, honestly marked.
- **Feedback c:** The range is only two years wide. That's quite certain.
- **Explanation:** A question mark or "about" in a catalog flags a small doubt about the year. Here the range is 1454 to 1455.

**D5 (sort into two groups).**
- **Prompt:** Sort each card: a story people told, or evidence from the ground.
- **Cards:** Romulus founded Rome in 753 BCE (tradition) · remains of huts on the Palatine Hill, about 1000-700 BCE · a Roman scholar, Varro, worked out the 753 BCE date centuries later.
- **Answer:** story: the Romulus card. Evidence from the ground: the huts. The Varro card is evidence about the *story* (who worked out its date, and when).
- **Misconceptions:** putting the Romulus card under evidence tests "a precise date makes a legend history".
- **Hint:** Which card describes something dug up?
- **Explanation:** Archaeologists found traces of huts. The 753 BCE date was worked out by Varro in the 1st century BCE, centuries after the founding it describes.

**D6 (3-choice, deep time).**
- **Prompt:** At 1 cm = 100 years, how far back is 66 million years ago?
- **Choices:** a. 66 cm · b. 660 m · c. 6.6 km
- **Answer:** c.
- **Misconceptions:** a tests "dinosaurs fit on the history line"; b tests a slip of ten in converting.
- **Hint:** 66 million ÷ 100 = how many centimeters? Then 100 cm = 1 m.
- **Feedback a:** 66 cm is 6,600 years. That's before Egypt was united, nowhere near the dinosaurs.
- **Feedback b:** Check the last step: 660,000 cm is 6,600 m.
- **Feedback c:** Yes. 660,000 cm is 6,600 m, or 6.6 km.
- **Explanation:** 66,000,000 ÷ 100 = 660,000 cm = 6,600 m = 6.6 km. Deep time needs a different scale.

**D7 (3-choice, deep-time lens).**
- **Shown:** the lens at 1 box = 1 million years: *Stegosaurus* at about 150, the end of the dinosaurs at about 66.
- **Prompt:** Which gap is longer?
- **Choices:** a. The end of the dinosaurs to now · b. They are about the same · c. *Stegosaurus* to the end of the dinosaurs
- **Answer:** c (about 84 million years against about 66 million).
- **Misconceptions:** a tests "all dinosaurs lived at about the same time"; b tests reading the lens as evenly spaced.
- **Hint:** Subtract 66 from 150. Compare with 66.
- **Feedback a:** That gap is about 66 million years. The other is about 84 million.
- **Feedback b:** Close, but 84 million is a lot more than 66 million.
- **Feedback c:** Yes. *Stegosaurus* was gone long before the last dinosaurs.
- **Explanation:** 150 − 66 = 84 million years. The last dinosaurs were closer in time to us than to *Stegosaurus*.

**D8 (3-choice, strict centuries).**
- **Prompt:** Strictly, the year 1500 belongs to which century?
- **Choices:** a. The 15th century · b. The 16th century · c. Neither; it's between
- **Answer:** a.
- **Misconceptions:** b tests "a new hundred starts a new century"; c tests "boundary years belong nowhere".
- **Hint:** The 1st century ended with year 100, not 99.
- **Feedback a:** Yes. Strictly, the 15th century is 1401 to 1500.
- **Feedback b:** The 1500s start then in everyday talk. Strictly, 1500 ends the 15th.
- **Feedback c:** Every year belongs to one century. Count from year 1.
- **Explanation:** With no year 0, the 1st century was 1 to 100, so the 15th is 1401 to 1500. Everyday talk often uses 1400 to 1499.

## Key terms (glossary)

- **Timeline:** a line that shows events in time order.
- **Order:** which came first, next and last.
- **Scale:** how much time each space on the line stands for.
- **BCE:** Before Common Era. Years before year 1, counting down.
- **CE:** Common Era. Years from year 1, counting up. BC and AD use the same numbers.
- **Year 0:** a year that isn't on the calendar. 1 BCE comes right before 1 CE.
- **Decade:** ten years, like the 1960s.
- **Century:** a hundred years. The 1400s are the 15th century.
- **About, c.:** close to that year. "c." is short for *circa*, Latin for "around".
- **Range:** from one year to another, like 447-432 BCE.
- **Legend:** a story people told about the past. It may hold some truth, but it isn't evidence by itself.
- **Archaeologist:** someone who studies the past from things people left behind.
- **Deep time:** the very long history of Earth, counted in millions of years.

## Grown-up panel

**What this teaches:** putting events in order; the difference between order and a scaled timeline; BCE and CE, and counting across them with no year 0; decades and centuries; what "about", "c." and date ranges mean; why a legend's date differs from evidence; and why Earth's deep time needs a different scale from human history.
