# Act II. A Town of Our Own

**Carried question:** Where did the Sponge go?

**Hook:** The worst is over. Now Willowbank gets to decide what kind of town it wants to be. And Pip's old map shows a marsh that isn't there anymore.

**Opening** (Continue the story after the supper, or a later visit if paused):

**Short:** The market is coming back, the garden is changing, and it's nearly Ember's birthday. Pip keeps looking at the map.

**Longer:** The storm is something people talk about now, instead of something they're in. The market is coming back, one stall at a time. The garden is changing with the weather. Someone has heard that it's Ember's birthday soon, and Ember has heard them hearing it. Pip has the map in his satchel. Every so often he takes it out and puts his paw on the green patch marked *the Sponge*, where the orchard is now. "Maps can be wrong," says Dot. "This one looks very sure of itself," says Pip.

**Ending opens when:** the explorer has made a contribution in any three of the four chapters.

## Chapter 1. The weekly market

**Short:** The market is back. The orchard keeper's apples are the reddest in Willowbank, and she has never heard of a marsh.

**Longer:** Every week a few more stalls come back to the green: bread, honey, string, seeds, a man who sells only spoons. The orchard keeper's stall has the reddest apples in Willowbank. Pip buys one, then shows her the map while he eats it. She turns it round twice. "Marsh?" she says. "Under my trees? My grandfather planted the first ones. He never said anything about a marsh." She hands the map back, slowly. "He did say the low corner was always wet," she adds. "I thought he meant it rained a lot."

**Keeper's note:** A family's memory and a map can disagree. Neither is automatically right. The map was drawn in Hollis's second year of keeping; the orchard keeper's grandfather planted his trees later. Which came first matters.

## Chapter 2. A garden across the seasons

**Short:** "If we'd written down what the weather did, we might have seen the storm coming." Willowbank starts keeping records.

**Longer:** The librarian has an idea, and when the librarian has an idea, they call a meeting about it. "If we'd written down what the weather did," they say, "we might have seen the storm coming. Or at least known how unusual it was. From now on, Willowbank keeps records." Everyone agrees, which is unusual. Dot has already started: there is a page in the almanac with today's weather on it, in her best handwriting. The garden beds are going to change with the seasons, and now someone will be watching.

**Keeper's note:** Weather is what the sky does today. Climate is what it usually does in a place, over many years. You can only tell them apart if someone keeps records for a long time *(check: matches Marble's Weather vs Climate topic)*.

## Chapter 3. Ember's birthday

**Short:** It's Ember's birthday. The café is cooking for everyone. Bramble brings ribbons, and a secret.

**Longer:** Ember has mentioned her birthday eleven times this week, always by accident. The café is cooking for the whole village, which means trays, and trays mean counting. Bramble arrives early with a basket of ribbons and starts tying them on everything: chairs, lanterns, Pip. {guide} asks where she learned to tie knots like that. Bramble goes a bit pink. "My grandmother," she says. "She tied them on the old paths, so nobody got lost. Blue for one way, gold for another." She ties a bow on the parcel. "I just like the colors." A pause. "Mostly I like the colors."

**Keeper's note:** There are faded ribbons on some old fence posts outside Willowbank, along paths nobody uses much now. Nobody has connected them to Bramble's grandmother. Yet.

## Chapter 4. The first show

**Short:** Willowbank puts on its first show since the storm. At the curtain call, Dot is asked a question.

**Longer:** The Story Loft is putting on a show for the whole village: the first since the storm. There are puppets, a painted backdrop of the footbridge, and a part for a heron that nobody can quite explain. "It just felt like the story needed one," says the librarian. At the curtain call, everyone claps, including the ducks, who weren't invited. Then the baker stands up and asks Dot, in front of everyone, whether she's staying in Willowbank for good. Dot looks at Pip. Pip looks at the parcel. "I already unpacked," says Dot. "Twice."

## Ending: A Town of Our Own

**Short:** Lanterns, music, a show and too much cake. The second page comes free. And a letter arrives from upstream.

**Longer:**

> Willowbank has a celebration, and this time it isn't about mending anything. It's just about being here. There are lanterns and music, a show, Ember's cake and a lot of dancing that the ducks join uninvited. Somewhere in the middle of it, the baker comes over with something in his hand. "Second page," he says. "Came off the windowsill by itself."

Then the page 2 reveal (companion text, section 3).

> As the evening ends, {courier} comes up the bridge road with a letter. The stamp is from a town called Vadoalto, up the river. The storm went through there too.
