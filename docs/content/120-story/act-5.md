# Act V. A Question of Your Own

**Carried question:** What will I write on the blank pages?

**Hook:** The last pages are blank, headed *For the next Keeper*. What do you want to find out?

**Opening** (Continue the story after the Act IV ending, or a later visit if paused): page 5 comes free (companion text, section 3), then:

**Short:** The last pages are empty. Across the top Hollis wrote *For the next Keeper*. "So," says {guide}. "What do you want to find out?"

**Longer:** The rest of the pages come apart all at once, the way a stack of pancakes does when you lift the top one. They're blank. Every single one. Pip turns them over, in case Hollis wrote on the backs. Hollis didn't. Across the top of the first page is a heading in the long thin handwriting, and nothing else: *For the next Keeper.* "I think that might be you," says Dot. "Or all of us. But mostly you." {guide} sits down on the bakery step. "So," they say. "What do you want to find out?"

**Ending opens when:** one project is kept with a stated limit, in any path below.

## How every project path works

Each path follows spec 49's project steps. The story gives each step a scene line and a job; the activity named in each job does the work.

| Step | What the explorer does | Shared line |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Choose** | Picks a path, or a smaller version of it. | {guide}: "Big or small? Small is a whole project too." |
| **Ask** | Says what they don't know yet. | {guide}: "What's the one thing we'd need to find out first?" |
| **Make** | Makes a first version, using any job below. | {guide}: "First versions are supposed to be a bit wobbly. That's what they're for." |
| **Show** | Someone sees it, uses it, or says something about it. | (the path's own response line) |
| **Change** | Changes one thing, or decides to keep it as it is and says why. | {guide}: "Would you change anything? Keeping it the same is a choice too." |
| **Keep** | Writes one thing the project can't tell us yet. | {guide}: "What can't it tell us yet? Hollis always wrote that bit down." |

**[new] The project page.** Each kept project becomes a page in the Keeper's Pages with its first version, its changed version (or "kept as it was, because..."), and its limit line. Sprout limits are chosen from three picture cards per path; Seedling chooses or writes a sentence; Sapling and the desk write their own.

A child may change paths, shrink a path, or start a second one. Nothing is lost when they switch.

## Path 1. A night garden

**Invitation:** "Some flowers only open in the dark. Who visits them?"

**Short:** The orchard keeper says some flowers in her hedge open only at night. Who comes to visit them, when everyone is asleep?

**Longer:** "There's a flower in my hedge," says the orchard keeper, "that's shut tight all day. Then at dusk it opens like a little white trumpet, and in the morning it's shut again." Nobody in Willowbank has ever stayed up to see who visits it. Pip volunteers immediately. Pip falls asleep at a quarter past eight. "We'll need a plan," says {guide}, "and probably a blanket."

**Keeper's note:** Some flowers open in the evening and are visited by moths and other night insects *(check: pick a real example and its visitors from Marble's plant topics before naming one)*. Watching at night needs a dim red light, patience and a notebook.

**Jobs**

**Sprout · [Living Garden habitat trail]** {guide}: "Let's look at the hedge by day first. Where are the flowers that are closed?"
Kept: "Found them. Three closed flowers, marked with a stick each, so we can find them in the dark."

**Seedling · [Field Notebook]** The orchard keeper: "Keep notes on the flowers at dusk for a week. Open or shut? Any visitors?"
Kept: "A week of notes. Most nights, something came. Twice, nothing did, which also counts."

**Sapling · [Plant investigation]** {guide}: "Plan a fair way to find out whether the flowers open because it's dark or because it's cooler."
Kept: "A fair plan, and an honest note on what you couldn't control. The orchard keeper says she'll help on the cold nights."

**Keeper's Desk:** design a night survey for the hedge: what to count, when, for how many nights, and what would change your mind.

**Show:** The orchard keeper reads the notes by lantern-light. "I've lived next to that hedge all my life," she says, "and I never knew it had company."

**Limit cards (Sprout):** "We only watched one hedge." · "We fell asleep some nights." · "We couldn't see the smallest visitors."

## Path 2. The Willowbank Gazette

**Invitation:** "Willowbank has a lot of news and no proper newspaper."

**Short:** The librarian made the Gazette for the meeting. Now everyone wants another one. Who will write it?

**Longer:** Since the meeting, people keep stopping the librarian in the street to ask when the next Gazette is coming out. "I only made it for the meeting," says the librarian, who is secretly delighted. "I can't write a whole newspaper by myself." Dot offers to deliver it. Pip offers to draw the pictures. Bramble offers to write a column called *Bramble Was Right*. Nobody has offered to find out what's actually happening, which is the main thing.

**Keeper's note:** A good report says what happened, where, when and who saw it. It keeps what people saw separate from what they think about it. An opinion piece is allowed to argue, and it says so at the top.

**Jobs**

**Sprout · [Puppet Notice Printer]** The librarian: "Pick a picture and a headline for the front page."
Kept: "Front page done. Pip says the picture is the best part. Pip drew the picture."

**Seedling · [Messages for a Reader]** The librarian: "Write three sentences about the storm: what happened, where, and when."
Kept: "Three sentences kept. The librarian asks a reader to check the report before putting it on page one."

**Sapling · [Water Evidence Shelf / Source Trail]** The librarian: "Read the neighbors' notes about the new wet corner. Write a short report that keeps what people saw separate from what they think."
Kept: "Your report goes on page two. Two neighbors write in to say it was fair to them. That has never happened before."

**Keeper's Desk:** write the Gazette's opinion piece about the low fields. Give the best argument against your own view, and answer it. A grown-up reads it; nothing grades it.

**Show:** Dot delivers the Gazette to every door. By lunchtime there are three letters to the editor, one of them from the magpie.

**Limit cards (Sprout):** "We only asked a few people." · "Some news happened after we printed." · "Bramble's column is not checked."

## Path 3. A sky-watching night

**Invitation:** "The library has a telescope nobody has used in years."

**Short:** There's a telescope in the library attic. It's dusty. The miller's cat is sitting on it. What could we see?

**Longer:** Up in the library attic, under a sheet, under a box, under the miller's cat, there is a telescope. It has brass legs and a lens cap with a heron drawn on it. "Hollis's," says the librarian, quietly. "They used to watch the moon from the mill roof." Nobody has used it since. Pip wants to see the moon. Dot wants to see a planet. Ember wants to see a dragon-shaped star and is very sure there is one.

**Keeper's note:** The Moon looks different from night to night because we see different amounts of its sunlit side *(check: matches Marble's Moon phases topic)*. A planet looks like a steady bright star that moves slowly against the others over many nights.

**Jobs**

**Sprout · [Field Notebook: draw the Moon]** Pip: "Draw the Moon tonight, the shape it really is. Then draw it again in a few days."
Kept: "Two Moons in the notebook, different shapes. Pip says the Moon is showing off."

**Seedling · [Courier Clock Walk / Preparation Clock]** The librarian: "Sunset is at 7 and it's dark enough an hour later. If we need half an hour to carry the telescope up, when do we start?"
Kept: "On the mill roof in time. The telescope arrives with all its legs."

**Sapling · [My Count Collection]** {guide}: "Keep a chart of the Moon's shape every clear night for a month. What pattern do you see?"
Kept: "A month of Moons on one chart. It goes round, like the seasons, only faster."

**Keeper's Desk:** write a sky guide for Willowbank: what can be seen from the mill roof this month, at what time, and what can't be seen from here and why.

**Show:** On the sky-watching night half the village climbs the mill roof. The miller holds the ladder and does not look down. Ember sees a star that is, if you squint, slightly dragon-shaped.

**Limit cards (Sprout):** "Some nights were cloudy." · "We only watched from one roof." · "The telescope is old and a bit blurry."

## Path 4. A travelling show

**Invitation:** "Vadoalto has never seen a Willowbank show."

**Short:** Nuria says Vadoalto has never seen a puppet show. Could the Story Loft take one up the river?

**Longer:** In her last letter, Nuria mentioned, very casually, that Vadoalto has never had a puppet show. Not once. The librarian read that line out loud three times. A travelling show means a show that fits in a boat, puppets that survive a river trip, a story people upstream will understand, and at least a few words in Spanish. "And a heron," says the librarian. "It still needs a heron."

**Keeper's note:** A show for a new audience has to make sense to people who don't know Willowbank. Telling a story to strangers is a good way to find out which bits only made sense to you.

**Jobs**

**Sprout · [Story Loft]** The librarian: "Make a short scene for the show. It has to fit in one box."
Kept: "Your scene is packed. The box closes, just, if Pip sits on it."

**Seedling · [Packing parcels / Equal Groups]** Dot: "Eighteen puppets, three boxes. The same in each, so no box is too heavy."
Kept: "Packed evenly. The boat sits level in the water."

**Sapling · [Sentence Bridges / Bell Studio]** The librarian: "Write the welcome for the start of the show, in English with a few Spanish words. Nuria will help with the Spanish."
Kept: "Your welcome is learned by heart. Nuria fixes one word and says the rest is *perfecto*."

**Keeper's Desk:** adapt one Willowbank story for an audience who has never been here. What do you cut, what do you explain, and what do you change?

**Show:** The boat reaches Vadoalto at dusk. The show plays on the landing. At the end, the Vadoalto baker stands up and asks, loudly, whether there will be another one.

**Limit cards (Sprout):** "Some jokes only work in Willowbank." · "We didn't know all the Spanish words." · "Only one town has seen it so far."

## Path 5. A new courier route

**Invitation:** "There must be a quicker way down to Les Saules."

**Short:** Letters to Léon take three days. {courier} thinks there's a quicker way. Where could it go?

**Longer:** Letters to Léon take three days, because they go by the long valley road and wait at every village for the post bag. {courier} is sure there's a quicker way. Dot is sure there isn't. Bramble says her grandmother's ribbons used to go somewhere downstream, and digs out a scrap of old map from her hat. It shows a path nobody uses, along the brook, then up over a little hill. "It might be quicker," says {guide}. "Or it might be a goat track. Let's find out."

**Keeper's note:** A quicker route is only better if it's safe in every season you need it. Planning a route means knowing the distance, the time, the ground, and what to do if a way is closed.

**Jobs**

**Sprout · [Map Reader Dispatcher]** {courier}: "Help me follow Bramble's old path on the map, one turn at a time."
Kept: "Followed to the end. It does reach Les Saules. It also goes past a very surprised goat."

**Seedling · [Courier Clock Walk]** Dot: "The new path takes 4 hours each way. The old road takes 9. If {courier} leaves at 7, when are they back each way?"
Kept: "Written in the post office book, both ways. Dot admits the new path is quicker, in small handwriting."

**Sapling · [Same Route, Different Scale / Cartographer's Table]** The librarian: "Draw the new route at the post office map's scale, and mark where it might be closed in spring floods."
Kept: "The new route is on the wall, with a blue warning where the brook path floods."

**Keeper's Desk:** write a route card for couriers: the way, the time, the season it's safe, and the other way to take when it isn't.

**Show:** The first letter by the new path reaches Léon the same afternoon. He writes back at once: *Already? Is this a trick?*

**Limit cards (Sprout):** "We only walked it in one season." · "The goat may move." · "We don't know if it floods in spring."

## Path 6. The Sponge, again

**Invitation:** "Somebody should keep watching the low corner."

**Short:** The reeds at the bottom of the orchard are spreading. Is the Sponge coming back? Somebody should keep watching.

**Longer:** The wet corner at the bottom of the orchard is not quite the same as it was at the meeting. The reeds have spread. There's a frog that wasn't there before, or possibly the same frog, moving around a lot. The orchard keeper asks whether someone could keep proper notes, "so that whatever we decide next, we decide it knowing something." Nuria sends a letter with tips from Vadoalto, and a drawing of her own post ([letters.md](letters.md), letter 5).

**Keeper's note:** A survey that repeats the same count in the same place over time can show change. One visit is a snapshot; many visits make a record. Hollis's ledger was a survey of one post, kept for forty years.

**Jobs**

**Sprout · [Living Garden habitat trail]** {guide}: "Count the frogs and herons at the low corner today. Then again next week."
Kept: "Two counts in the almanac. One frog, then three. Or the same frog, three times."

**Seedling · [My Count Collection]** The orchard keeper: "Mark how far the reeds reach each month, by the fence posts."
Kept: "A chart of the reeds, post by post. The orchard keeper looks at it for a long time."

**Sapling · [Fair Test / Field Notebook]** The miller: "Design a better flood post for the low corner, so we can read how wet it is after every rain."
Kept: "Your post goes in at the low corner, with a mark at every 10 centimeters. The first rain fills it to the second mark."

**Keeper's Desk:** write a guide to the Sponge for visitors: what it was, what's coming back, what we know and what we don't, with your survey as evidence.

**Show:** Nuria visits, reads the survey, and says Vadoalto has nothing like it. She asks to copy it.

**Limit cards (Sprout):** "We only counted in daytime." · "It's only been a few months." · "Frogs hide."

## Ending: A Question of Our Own

**Short:** Your project goes into the Keeper's Pages. On the last line is one thing it can't tell us yet. Hollis would have liked that line.

**Longer:**
> {guide} helps you bind your project into the Keeper's Pages, right behind *For the next Keeper*. Your first version is there, wobbly in places, and the version after it. On the last line you've written the thing your project can't tell us yet.
>
> Pip reads that line twice. "Why put the bit you don't know?"
>
> "Because the next Keeper will want to know where to start," says {guide}.
>
> The baker, passing with a tray, looks over your shoulder. "Hollis always wrote that bit," he says. "Every page. Drove the miller mad."
