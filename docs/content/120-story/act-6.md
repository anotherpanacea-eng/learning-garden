# Act VI. The Opening of the Ways

**Carried question:** Is the town ready, and who is the Keeper?

**Hook:** Willowbank and Vadoalto want to keep the Ways open for good. Then the rain starts, and it doesn't stop.

**Opening** (Continue the story after the Act V ending, or a later visit if paused): Nuria's letter ([letters.md](letters.md), letter 6), then:

**Short:** Nuria writes: let's trade every week, for good. Goods down the river, news and seeds up. Who carries what, and which way?

**Longer:** Nuria's letter is the longest yet, and it has a list. Vadoalto's bakers want apples. Willowbank's basket-makers want reed. Both towns want news, seeds and, for some reason, Pip's drawings. "Let's do it properly," she writes. "Every week, for good. Not just when somebody remembers." Dot reads the letter out at the post office and then stands very straight, because a weekly exchange is a post office matter. "We'll need to decide who carries what," she says, "and which Way, and what happens when a Way is closed."

**Ending opens when:** an exchange plan is kept (chapter 2), the long rain has played (chapter 3), and the first journey is made (chapter 4). Chapter 1 is a scene with optional jobs.

## Chapter 1. Choose a partner and a purpose

**Short:** Vadoalto wants apples. Willowbank wants reed. Everybody wants honey cake. What should go each way?

**Longer:** Nuria comes down the river path with a list and a jar of honey, and sits at the café with Dot, the orchard keeper and the baker. "Our bakers want your apples," she says. "Your basket-makers want our reed. Everybody wants your café's honey cake, which seems unfair, since the honey is ours." The baker says the cake is mostly the baking. Nuria says the cake is mostly the honey. They agree to call it a joint cake. By the end of the afternoon there's a list of what goes down the river, what goes up, and a note at the bottom in Dot's writing: *letters both ways, always.*

**Keeper's note:** A fair exchange means both sides get something they need. It doesn't mean both sides send the same amount of the same thing.

## Chapter 2. Compare routes and keep a plan

**Short:** Three Ways go to Vadoalto. Each one is good for something and bad for something. Which way, and how often?

**Longer:** Dot unrolls the post office map on the café table and weighs the corners down with honey jars. "Three Ways," she says, tapping each one. "The river path is fastest, but it floods. The bridge road is slow, but it's always dry. The high ford is short, and only safe in summer." Bramble leans over. "My grandmother used to say the ford is for people in a hurry who like wet feet." "So which do we use?" says Pip. "Depends," says Dot. "On the season. And the parcel. And whether it's raining. Which it usually is."

**Keeper's note:** A good plan says what happens when something goes wrong. A plan that only works on a sunny day is half a plan.

**Jobs**

**Sprout · [new: route planner, picture choice]** Dot: "This parcel must stay dry. Which Way should it take: the river path, the bridge road, or the high ford?"
Kept: "The bridge road. Slow, dry and very boring, which is what a dry parcel wants."

**Seedling · [new: hours clock]** Dot: "{courier} leaves at 9 with only letters. The river path takes 3 hours each way. When will they be back?"
Kept: "Back by mid-afternoon, as planned. Dot writes it in the timetable."

**Keeper's Desk:** write the exchange plan: what goes each way, how often, by which Way in each season, and what happens if a Way is closed.

## Chapter 3. The long rain

Starts with **Continue the story** after the exchange plan is kept, or on a later visit if the explorer chooses to pause. No real-day wait is required.

### The rain starts

**Short:** It rains for three days. Upstream, Nuria watches Vadoalto's flood post climb. She writes one line, and Vadoalto's runner sets off.

**Longer:**
> It rains for three days. Not storm rain: steady, grey, patient rain that doesn't stop for lunch. Upstream, Nuria watches Vadoalto's flood post climb past the third mark, then the fourth. She does what Hollis taught her. She writes one line on a card, and Vadoalto's fastest runner sets off down the river path.
>
> **The brook is rising at Vadoalto. You have until evening.**
>
> In Willowbank, the bell rings. Everyone knows what to do, because they've talked about nothing else for months.

**Keeper's note:** Water that rises upstream reaches the towns below some hours later. A post that is read upstream gives the towns downstream time. That's what a warning is: time.

### Getting ready (preparation jobs)

Each is a whole contribution to this chapter.

**Sprout · [Gather the Cupboard / Bundle Exchange]** The baker: "Help me carry the flour up to the attic. Bags on the top shelf, jars on the middle."
Kept: "The flour is up high and dry. The baker sits on the top step to get his breath back."

**Seedling · [new: hours clock]** The miller: "It's 2 o'clock. The water should be here by 6. The boards take an hour to put in. When must we start, at the latest?"
Kept: "The boards go in with time to spare. The miller's cat supervises from the window."

**Sapling · [Instructions for a Reader]** The librarian: "Write the notice for the bridge: when to stop crossing, and where to go instead."
Kept: "Your notice is on the bridge. People read it, then go round by the high road, as it says."

**Keeper's Desk:** read the flood post, the rain and the warning card together. How sure are we about the time? What would change it? Write the bell-ringer's note.

### Passing the warning on

**Short:** Dot remembers Les Saules. "If it's coming here, it's going there next." Someone has to tell Léon, in French.

**Longer:** In the middle of the carrying and the boards and the bell, Dot stops with a sack of letters in her arms. "Les Saules," she says. "If it's coming here, it's going there next. Somebody has to tell Léon." She looks at {courier}. {courier} is already pulling on their boots. "In French," says Dot. "So everybody there can read it, not just Léon."

**[new] The warning card** (all depths; Sprout taps **Send**, Seedling chooses the French sentence that matches the English from three, Sapling and the desk build it from word cards):

> **The brook is rising. It will reach you tomorrow morning. From Willowbank.**
> **Le ruisseau monte. Il arrivera chez vous demain matin. De la part de Willowbank.**

Seedling's two other choices (each plausible, each wrong in one way):
> *Le ruisseau descend. Il arrivera chez vous demain matin.* (The brook is going down.)
> *Le ruisseau monte. Il est arrivé chez nous hier.* (It reached us yesterday.)

**Word cards (Sapling):** *Le ruisseau* · *monte* · *descend* · *Il arrivera* · *chez vous* · *demain matin* · *ce soir* · *De la part de Willowbank*

**After sending:** {courier} is already halfway down the river path.

### The water arrives

One **authored fictional outcome** chosen by the plan kept in Act IV; not a calculated model result or advice for a real flood. It describes this particular invented rain, never a general guarantee for a wall or marsh. A missing/unreadable prior plan requires the reviewed fallback in spec 120 before this scene can ship.

**The Wall:**
> **Short:** The water climbs the wall and stops. The orchard stays dry. Downstream, past the wall, it spreads over Léon's meadow.
>
> **Longer:** The water climbs the new wall and stops. Behind it, the orchard is dry and the mill boards hold. Downstream, past the end of the wall, the water spreads wide over the meadow by Léon's ferry, and the ferry can't run for two days. In this story rain the wall held here, while the ferry meadow flooded. That scene alone cannot tell us how another wall or another storm would behave.

**The Sponge:**
> **Short:** The water spreads into the low fields and slows. The mill stays dry. Three apple trees stand in water to their knees.
>
> **Longer:** The water reaches the low fields and spreads out. The reeds bend under it. It slows. The miller watches the post; in this story rain the water stays below the boards. Nobody claims a measured time saving from the tray model. At the low corner, three apple trees stand up to their knees in water. The orchard keeper sighs, and goes to check on the reeds.

**Some of Each:**
> **Short:** The wall turns the water into the wet meadow. The mill boards hold, just. Nobody got everything. Nobody lost everything.
>
> **Longer:** The wall above the mill turns the water toward the wet meadow, which fills like a bath. The mill boards hold, just. Some of the orchard gets its feet wet. Nobody got everything they wanted. Nobody lost everything, either.

**Every version ends:**
> The bridge holds. The mill holds. Everything you mended holds. The miller scratches a new mark on the flood post, and writes the date beside it, and then, after a moment, writes *Warned in time.*

## Chapter 4. The first journey, and someone new

**Short:** The rain passes. The first weekly parcel goes up the river under your plan. On the way back, someone tall and grey is walking up the path.

**Longer:**
> The rain passes. The brook goes back to being a brook. The first regular parcel goes up the river under your plan: apples, two of Pip's drawings and a honey-cake recipe that Vadoalto says it will improve. {courier} comes back the next evening with reed bundles and a note: *Same time next week?*

## Ending: The Opening of the Ways

**Short:** Bramble ties a ribbon on each Way. Someone new is at the bridge with a parcel. Dot gets there first. Then she looks at you.

**Longer:**
> Bramble ties a ribbon on each Way: blue for the river path, gold for the bridge road, red for the high ford. "So nobody gets lost," she says. "And because I like the colors."
>
> Hollis watches her tie the knots. "Your grandmother's," they say. Bramble goes pink and ties another one, faster.
>
> Down at the bridge, someone new is standing with an unfamiliar parcel, looking at the label like it might bite. Dot gets there first.
>
> "I was new once," she says. "Come and see the post. {guide} and I will show you. Well. Mostly the Keeper will."
>
> She looks at you.

**Season line:**

| Season | Line |
| --- | --- |
| Spring | Blossom on every ribbon post, and the brook high, loud and staying where it should. |
| Summer | The high ford is open, and Bramble's red ribbon flaps in the warm wind. |
| Autumn | The first weekly parcel up the river is all apples. The one coming down is all reed. |
| Winter | Snow on the ribbons, lanterns on the bridge, and the Ways open all the same. |

**Almanac line:** *Act VI: The Opening of the Ways. The Ways are open. Hollis came home.*

**After the ending, the village line becomes:** *Willowbank, by Willow Brook. The Ways are open.*

**Play continues.** Festivals, weekly exchanges, project paths and the apprentice's questions carry on. Hollis visits on some festival days (festivals-and-storm-day.md) and is never needed for anything.
