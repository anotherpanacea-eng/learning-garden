# Festivals and Storm Day

The seasons follow the device calendar (spec 33). Each season has a festival. Its line shows under the village title all season, beside spec 33's season line. Its job rotates by **story year**: year A until the first Storm Day, B in the second story year, C in the third, then A again.

Festival jobs are never required. Each counts once. In Acts II to VI, a kept festival job also counts for the first chapter in its **Feeds** list that belongs to the current act and has no contribution yet; if none fits, it fills its own seasonal job only. Festival jobs never feed Act I.

Each yearly job has two depths. Either is a whole contribution.

---

## Spring: Flood Boards and Planting

**Festival line:** The brook is high and the mill boards are going in. Seeds are going in too. Pip is supervising both, badly.

**Short:** Spring! The brook is high. The miller puts the flood boards in. Everyone else plants seeds. Pip helps with both and finishes neither.

**Longer:** Every spring, when the snow melts in the hills above Vadoalto, Willow Brook comes down fat and fast. The miller slides the wooden flood boards into their slots by the millrace, one on top of another, and reads the post out loud so everyone can hear. Meanwhile, the garden beds are turned over and the seeds go in, row by row. Pip carries one board, plants one seed, and then sits down to supervise. "It's a very important job," he says. "Someone has to watch."

## Summer: Market Fair and the Show

**Festival line:** The market is twice its usual size and there's a show tonight. Ember has bought a front-row seat for every night.

**Short:** Summer! The market fair fills the whole green, and tonight there's a show. Ember is in the front row. Ember is always in the front row.

**Longer:** In summer the weekly market grows until it fills the whole green and spills onto the bridge. If the boats from upstream are running, Vadoalto has stalls too. There are stalls for bread, honey, string, seeds, spoons, and one that sells only hats, which Dot visits every day and buys nothing. In the evening the Story Loft puts on its summer show, with lanterns hung from the willows. Ember has bought a front-row seat for every night. There are only two nights.

## Autumn: Harvest Supper

**Festival line:** Apples in every basket. The café is planning the biggest supper of the year.

**Short:** Autumn! The orchard is full of apples, and the café is cooking the biggest supper of the year. Everyone's invited. Even the ducks, this time.

**Longer:** By autumn the orchard is heavy with apples, and the orchard keeper's family brings them in by the cartload. The café plans a harvest supper for everyone in Willowbank, and this year, after a long discussion, the ducks are officially invited. Tables go out on the green. The cook makes a list, loses it, and makes another. Bramble's magpie steals the second list. "We'll manage," says the cook, which is what the cook always says right before needing a lot of help.

## Winter: Lantern Night

**Festival line:** Lanterns on the bridge, and the almanac read aloud by the fire. Everyone gets to hear what we noticed this year.

**Short:** Winter! Lanterns on the footbridge, a fire on the green, and the almanac read out loud. Everyone gets to hear what we noticed this year.

**Longer:** On the longest night of the year, Willowbank hangs lanterns all along the footbridge and lights a fire in the middle of the green. Then the librarian opens the almanac and reads out what the town noticed that year: the weather, the floods, the birthdays, the things that grew and the things that didn't. Everyone listens. Some people listen for their own names. Ember listens for hers and falls asleep before it comes.

## Storm Day

The local date of the explorer's first story visit, or, for a later arrival, the day they arrived. A lantern is lit on the footbridge. It stays lit until the explorer's next visit, so Storm Day is never missed. It asks for nothing.

**Village line:** A lantern is lit on the footbridge. It's been {years} since the storm. (*a year* in the first year; *two years*, *three years* and so on after.)

### First Storm Day

**Short:** A year since the storm. Pip lights a lantern on the footbridge. "Same day as the storm," he says. "Better weather. Better everything, really."

**Longer:** It's a year since the storm. Pip lights a lantern on the footbridge, because somebody should, and sits down beside it with his satchel. People come by one at a time. The miller says the mill wheel hasn't stuck once. The librarian says every book is back on its shelf, and some of them are even in the right place. Dot says she's been here a whole year, which means she isn't new anymore, which is strange. "Same day as the storm," says Pip. "Better weather. Better everything, really."

### Second Storm Day

**Short:** Two years since the storm. Pip lights the lantern again. "Last year I thought this was a sad day," he says. "Now it's just our day."

**Longer:** Two years since the storm. Pip lights the lantern on the footbridge again. This time Dot brings a second lantern, and Bramble ties a ribbon on the rail. "Last year I thought this was a sad day," says Pip. "Now it's just our day." The miller comes past and checks the flood post on her way, out of habit. The newest mark isn't the storm anymore, or it is. Either way, she reads it out loud.

### Third Storm Day and after

**Short:** Another year since the storm. More lanterns this time. Some of them are from people who weren't even here for the storm.

**Longer:** Another year since the storm. There are more lanterns on the footbridge now, and some of them belong to people who weren't even here for the storm and just like lanterns. Pip lights the first one, as always. Then he reads the almanac's Storm Day page out loud to whoever is sitting nearby, which, this year, includes a duck.

### Later arrival

For an explorer who arrived after the mending or with the letter. Storm Day counts from the arrival day.

**Short:** A year since you came to Willowbank. Pip lights a lantern on the footbridge. "You weren't here for the storm," he says. "But you're here for this."

**Longer:** It's a year since you arrived in Willowbank. Pip lights a lantern on the footbridge and makes you hold it. "The others have a storm day," he says. "You should have one too. Yours is the day you came." Dot nods. "Mine's the day I unpacked," she says. "The first time."

### The almanac page

**Heading:** *A year since the storm.* (Later arrivals: *A year since you came.* After the second, *{years} since...*)

**Body** (filled from records; a line with no record is left out):
> A year ago, {first line}.
> Since then: {places mended} places mended, {pages dried} pages dried.
> This year's weather: mostly {most common weather}. Last year's: mostly {last year's most common weather}.

**First line** by arrival: *the footbridge was down and the mill wheel was stuck* (storm) · *you came to Willowbank just after the welcome-back supper* (mended) · *you walked down the river path with a letter* (letter).

**Pip's line** stays the same every year: "Same day as the storm. Better weather. Better everything, really." For a later arrival: "Same day you came. Better ever since, really."
