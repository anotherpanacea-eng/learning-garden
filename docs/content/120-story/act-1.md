# Act I. After the Storm

**Carried question:** What happened, and who is the parcel for?

**Hook:** The storm has passed. Pip found a wet parcel for someone called the Keeper of the Ways. Nobody knows who that is.

**Opening:** the parcel scene in the companion text, section 1, shown after the storm film.

**Ending opens when:** six tables are served at the welcome-back supper, as built. The four chapters below are open from the start and never required. They give the parcel a reason to travel, and they give the village its repairs.

While Act I is open, the first visit Pip makes to each place with the parcel shows one "Are you the Keeper?" line (companion text, section 2).

---

## Chapter 1. Find and feed

**Short:** The café wants to open, but the cupboard is empty. Neighbors are hungry. Pip is hungriest.

**Longer:** The café survived the storm, mostly. The tables are dry. The kettle works. The cupboard, however, contains one lemon and a note from the cook that says *Back soon, gone looking for flour.* Neighbors keep putting their heads round the door to ask when supper is. Pip puts the parcel on the counter so he has both paws free for carrying things. Then he picks it up again, just in case. "We need food from the orchard," says Dot, "and things from the garden, and someone has to count the plates." Pip says he'll count the plates. Dot says he'll eat off them first.

## Chapter 2. Deliver and explain

**Short:** Letters blew all over the village. Some need delivering. Some need explaining.

**Longer:** The post office roof held, but the window didn't. Letters lie on the floor, on the shelves and in one case on the cat. Dot has volunteered to sort them, which is how the hat discussion started. Some letters have smudged addresses. Some arrived for people who moved away years ago. Pip checks each one in case it says *Keeper of the Ways*. None of them do. "Somebody in this village must get letters for the Keeper," he says. "Somebody must have, once," says {guide}.

**Keeper's note:** A letter with no living recipient usually goes back to the sender. The parcel has no return address, only a long thin H on the string. Pip has not noticed it yet.

**Jobs**

**Sprout · [First Morning: Open the mysterious note]** Dot: "This one isn't a letter. It's a note on a twig. Dot insists it is not a shopping list."
Kept: "A note read, and a postbox found. Dot is wearing the hat now, provisionally."

**Seedling · [Willow Post Office]** The miller: "My sacks of seed need to reach the bakery and the garden. The path's still muddy."
Kept: "Delivered. The miller says you have a courier's feet."

**Sapling · [Instructions for a Reader]** The librarian: "Someone new is coming to help at the library, and they don't know the way. Could you write them instructions a stranger could follow?"
Kept: "The librarian follows your instructions with their eyes closed. Mostly closed. They arrive."

## Chapter 3. Investigate and plant

**Short:** Where did all the water go? And what can grow now that it's gone?

**Longer:** The puddles are shrinking. Some are vanishing into the ground; some are running downhill; some are just sitting there, looking smug. The garden beds are soft and dark. {guide} kneels by one. "The storm took some things and left others," they say. "Let's find out what's living here now, and what wants to grow." Pip asks whether the parcel could be planted. Dot explains, kindly, that it could not.

**Keeper's note:** Rain that lands on soil can soak in, run off or sit on top. Which one happens depends on the ground and how much rain comes at once *(check: matches the Water Lab's USGS-based framing)*. Willowbank will need this idea later.

**Jobs**

**Sprout · [Living Garden habitat trail]** {guide}: "Something's living under the big leaves. Let's look carefully, without moving anything."
Kept: "You found where it lives. It would like you to keep the secret."

**Seedling · [Water Lab]** The miller: "My millrace is either far too full or far too empty. Where does the water go?"
Kept: "Now we know a bit more about where water goes. The miller writes it on the back of a flour sack."

**Sapling · [Storm Workshop]** The post office: "The storm broke the parcel ramp. We need a new one that gets parcels down safely, and we need to know it works."
Kept: "A ramp tested, and the notes kept. Dot sends a parcel down it. Then another, for science."

**Keeper's Desk:** none in this chapter.

## Chapter 4. Mend the village

**Short:** The footbridge, the mill, the bakery, the library, the bell tower and the post office all need mending.

**Longer:** Willowbank lost more than its garden. The footbridge is missing planks. The mill wheel is stuck in mud. The library's books are everywhere, and the bell stopped at a quarter past three. Every finished piece of work, anywhere, can be sent to mend something (spec 33). Pip takes the parcel to every place, asking. At the mill, the miller stops scraping mud and looks at the label for a long time. "There was an old heron who used to read the flood post here," she says. "Grey. Tall. Patient with the cat. They'd have known what to do with a storm like that."

**Keeper's note:** The flood post is a wooden post by the millrace with marks cut into it, one for each flood. The newest mark is fresh: the storm, scratched in by the miller this week. It is higher than all the others.

**Jobs:** the repair jobs in [spec 33's text](../../specs/33-town-content.md), unchanged. Three of them, one per depth, carry an extra line here:

**Sprout · [spec 33: Footbridge, measured planks]** Pip: "Twelve planks. I'll stand on the ones that are done."
Kept: "The planks are in. Pip tests every one. Twice."

**Seedling · [spec 33: Footbridge, rope bundles]** Dot: "The handrail needs rope in equal coils, so it's the same strength all the way."
Kept: "The handrail is up. Dot leans on it, very officially."

**Sapling · [spec 33: Footbridge, a crossing notice]** The miller: "Someone should say when it's safe to cross, and what to do if the brook is high."
Kept: "Your notice is nailed to the bridge. The ducks ignore it, but everyone else reads it."

**Keeper's Desk · [spec 118: Bridge or ford?]** Which crossing should Willowbank rebuild first?

## Ending: the welcome-back supper

The existing supper ending plays first: six tables served, the café full, *Welcome Back, Neighbors*. Then:

**Short:** While everyone eats, the first page comes free. It's a map, and there's a marsh on it where the orchard is now.

**Longer:** the page 1 reveal in the companion text, section 3, then:

> The supper goes on around you. The miller is arguing with the baker about bread. Ember is asleep under a table. Pip holds the map up to the lantern and looks at the green patch for a long time.
>
> "The worst is over," says {guide}. "But I think Willowbank has a question now."

**Season line** (one, by the season the supper is reached in):

| Season | Line |
| --- | --- |
| Spring | Outside, the brook is high and loud, and the first blossom is out on the footbridge rail. |
| Summer | It's still light when supper ends. Nobody wants to go home. |
| Autumn | Leaves blow in every time the door opens. Pip calls it decorating. |
| Winter | Snow starts as everyone leaves. The footbridge has its first lanterns. |

**Almanac line:** *Act I: After the Storm. The welcome-back supper is eaten. One page is dry.*

The village's repairs (chapter 4) carry on after the supper, at the explorer's pace. Later acts never say a place is mended unless it is.

**Between acts** (shown on the village scene until Act II opens on a later visit): *The map is in Pip's satchel. He keeps taking it out.*
