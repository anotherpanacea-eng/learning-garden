# Letters up and down the river

Proposed new correspondence packs using the letter-and-reader interaction pattern; these multilingual sources and sender/return adapters are not present in the current runtime. The explorer chooses English, the other language, or both side by side. **Short** has read-aloud in both languages; **Longer** is for confident readers.

Nuria writes from Vadoalto in Spanish and English. Léon writes from Les Saules in French and English. Spanish addresses Willowbank as *ustedes*; French uses *vous*.

**Every Spanish and French line needs a fluent reader before build.** Notes for the reader follow some letters.

## 1. Nuria: the storm came here too

**English, short:**
> Hello, Willowbank! The storm came here too. Our brook rose very high. Can we compare notes? Write back!
> Nuria, Vadoalto

**Spanish, short:**
> ¡Hola, Willowbank! La tormenta también pasó por aquí. Nuestro arroyo subió muchísimo. ¿Comparamos notas? ¡Escríbanme!
> Nuria, Vadoalto

**English, longer:**
> Hello, Willowbank!
> The storm came through Vadoalto too. Our flood post says the brook rose higher than anyone here can remember. Did yours? I am learning to keep our post, and I would like to compare notes with whoever keeps yours. Will you write back?
> Nuria, apprentice at the flood post, Vadoalto

**Spanish, longer:**
> ¡Hola, Willowbank!
> La tormenta también pasó por Vadoalto. Nuestro poste de crecidas dice que el arroyo subió más alto de lo que nadie aquí recuerda. ¿Y el de ustedes? Estoy aprendiendo a cuidar nuestro poste y me gustaría comparar notas con quien cuide el suyo. ¿Me escriben?
> Nuria, aprendiz del poste de crecidas, Vadoalto

*For the reader:* is *poste de crecidas* natural for a flood-marker post, or would *escala* or *poste de marcas* read better to a child?

## 2. Léon: the ferry is running again

**English, short:**
> Hello, Willowbank! I'm Léon. I keep the ferry at Les Saules, downstream. It's running again. Send news!

**French, short:**
> Bonjour, Willowbank ! Je m'appelle Léon. Je garde le bac aux Saules, en aval. Il fonctionne de nouveau. Donnez-nous des nouvelles !

**English, longer:**
> Hello, Willowbank!
> My name is Léon. I keep the ferry at Les Saules, where your brook joins the big river. After the storm, the water came over our landing and the ferry couldn't cross for a week. It's running again now. Nuria from Vadoalto says you are keeping records. Will you send us yours? We'll send ours.
> Léon

**French, longer:**
> Bonjour, Willowbank !
> Je m'appelle Léon. Je garde le bac aux Saules, là où votre ruisseau rejoint la grande rivière. Après la tempête, l'eau est montée sur notre embarcadère et le bac n'a pas pu traverser pendant une semaine. Maintenant, il fonctionne de nouveau. Nuria, de Vadoalto, dit que vous tenez des registres. Vous nous envoyez les vôtres ? Nous vous enverrons les nôtres.
> Léon

*For the reader:* *aux Saules* (at Les Saules) and *en aval* (downstream) are the words a young learner meets first; check they read naturally.

## 3. Nuria: how high our post reached

Sent in answer to the explorer's question in Act III, chapter 4 (any depth may receive it).

**English, short:**
> In the storm, our brook came almost to the fifth mark. There is a mark every 50 centimeters. That's about 240! Higher than yours? Nuria

**Spanish, short:**
> En la tormenta, nuestro arroyo llegó casi a la quinta marca. Hay una marca cada 50 centímetros. ¡Son unos 240! ¿Más alto que el de ustedes? Nuria

**English, longer:**
> Dear Willowbank,
> Thank you for asking so exactly. Here is the exact answer. Our post has a mark every 50 centimeters. In the storm, the brook came up past the fourth mark and almost to the fifth: about 240 centimeters. I have drawn it for you. Our valley is narrow and steep, so the water climbs fast here. Yours is wider. Does that make a difference? I think it might.
> Nuria

**Spanish, longer:**
> Querido Willowbank:
> Gracias por preguntar con tanta precisión. Aquí va la respuesta precisa. Nuestro poste tiene una marca cada 50 centímetros. En la tormenta, el arroyo pasó la cuarta marca y llegó casi a la quinta: unos 240 centímetros. Se lo he dibujado. Nuestro valle es estrecho y empinado, así que aquí el agua sube rápido. El de ustedes es más ancho. ¿Eso cambia algo? Creo que sí.
> Nuria

*Consistency:* Willowbank's storm mark is 160 cm. In the long rain (Act VI), Vadoalto's post climbs past the third and fourth marks (150 to 200 cm), lower than the storm. Keep these numbers together.

*For the reader:* *Se lo he dibujado* addresses the town with *ustedes*; check it reads clearly to a child, or use *Les he hecho un dibujo*.

## 4. Nuria: the rain gauge

**English, short:**
> We have a rain gauge! Here are the big rains, ten years at a time. Some years were wetter. I hope it helps. Nuria

**Spanish, short:**
> ¡Tenemos un pluviómetro! Aquí están las lluvias grandes, de diez en diez años. Algunos años fueron más lluviosos. Espero que les ayude. Nuria

**English, longer:**
> Dear Willowbank,
> You asked whether our rain gauge goes back far enough. It does! Our grandparents started it. I have copied out the big rains for you, ten years at a time. Some of the years were wetter. Some were not. I don't think it settles your argument, but I think it helps.
> Nuria

**Spanish, longer:**
> Querido Willowbank:
> Me preguntaron si nuestro pluviómetro es lo bastante antiguo. ¡Sí lo es! Lo empezaron nuestros abuelos. Les he copiado las lluvias grandes, de diez en diez años. Algunos años fueron más lluviosos. Otros, no. No creo que esto resuelva su discusión, pero creo que ayuda.
> Nuria

**The copied table** (fictional; aligned to Hollis's years of keeping):

| Years of Hollis's keeping | Big rains at Vadoalto |
| --- | --- |
| 1 to 10 | 6 |
| 11 to 20 | 5 |
| 21 to 30 | 6 |
| 31 to 40 | 8 |

This table counts events above an unspecified fictional threshold; it does not report rainfall totals, intensity, duration or ground conditions. Similar event counts can accompany very different rainfall. It leaves the causal question open: a reader may propose either explanation, both, or another, while naming the missing evidence. No reply endorses "probably both" as the conclusion a careful reader must reach. A future source pack that treats these as calibrated measurements must define the threshold and record provenance. The separately reviewed standalone case in [spec246](../../specs/246-ledger-gauge.md) instead uses an explicitly incomplete English copied extract: its absent threshold is named as missing evidence. It does not invent a value, claim comparable rainfall, deliver the full bilingual letter or change story availability.

## 5. Nuria: how to watch a reed bed

**English, short:**
> Count in the same place every time, in spring and in autumn. And paint your post marks, so you can read them from the bank! Nuria

**Spanish, short:**
> Cuenten siempre en el mismo sitio, en primavera y en otoño. ¡Y pinten las marcas del poste, para leerlas desde la orilla! Nuria

**English, longer:**
> Dear Willowbank,
> We have a reed bed by our river too, so here are our tips. Count in the same place every time, by the same post, in spring and in autumn. Write down the date, even if you see nothing. Nothing is a result! And paint the marks on your post, so you can read them from the bank without getting wet. I have drawn ours. The frogs here are very shy. I hope yours are braver.
> Nuria

**Spanish, longer:**
> Querido Willowbank:
> Nosotros también tenemos un carrizal junto al río, así que aquí van nuestros consejos. Cuenten siempre en el mismo sitio, junto al mismo poste, en primavera y en otoño. Apunten la fecha, aunque no vean nada. ¡No ver nada también es un resultado! Y pinten las marcas del poste, para leerlas desde la orilla sin mojarse. Les he dibujado el nuestro. Aquí las ranas son muy tímidas. Espero que las de ustedes sean más valientes.
> Nuria

## 6. Nuria: let's do it properly

**English, short:**
> Let's trade every week, for good! Apples up the river, reed down. Letters both ways. Who carries what? Nuria

**Spanish, short:**
> ¡Hagamos un intercambio cada semana, para siempre! Manzanas río arriba, carrizo río abajo. Cartas en las dos direcciones. ¿Quién lleva qué? Nuria

**English, longer:**
> Dear Willowbank,
> We have been talking, here in Vadoalto. Our bakers want your apples. Your basket-makers want our reed. Everybody wants your café's honey cake, which seems unfair, since the honey is ours. So let's do it properly: every week, for good, not just when somebody remembers. We will need to decide who carries what, and which Way, and what to do when a Way is closed. I am coming down on Thursday with a list.
> Nuria

**Spanish, longer:**
> Querido Willowbank:
> Aquí en Vadoalto hemos estado hablando. Nuestros panaderos quieren sus manzanas. Sus cesteros quieren nuestro carrizo. Y todo el mundo quiere el pastel de miel de su café, lo cual no parece justo, porque la miel es nuestra. Así que hagámoslo bien: cada semana, para siempre, y no solo cuando alguien se acuerde. Tendremos que decidir quién lleva qué, por qué Camino, y qué hacer cuando un Camino esté cerrado. El jueves bajo con una lista.
> Nuria

*For the reader:* the three *Ways* become *Caminos* with a capital C; check this reads as a name and not a typo.

## 7. Léon: your warning came in time

Arrives on the explorer's next visit after the long rain.

**English, short:**
> Thank you! Your warning came in time. We tied up the ferry and moved everything up the hill. Next time it rains, we'll pass it on too. Léon

**French, short:**
> Merci ! Votre message est arrivé à temps. Nous avons attaché le bac et tout monté sur la colline. La prochaine fois qu'il pleuvra, nous ferons passer le message, nous aussi. Léon

**English, longer:**
> Dear Willowbank,
> Your courier reached us last night, soaking wet and very proud. We read your card out loud in the square. Then we tied up the ferry, carried the boats and the sacks up the hill, and sent word to the farms below us. In the morning the water came up high, but nobody lost anything that mattered. Next time it rains, we will pass the message on too, all the way to the big town.
> Léon

**French, longer:**
> Chère Willowbank,
> Votre coursier est arrivé hier soir, trempé et très fier. Nous avons lu votre carte à voix haute sur la place. Puis nous avons attaché le bac, monté les barques et les sacs sur la colline, et prévenu les fermes en aval. Le matin, l'eau est montée haut, mais personne n'a rien perdu d'important. La prochaine fois qu'il pleuvra, nous ferons passer le message, nous aussi, jusqu'à la grande ville.
> Léon

**Added line if the explorer kept the Wall:**
> **English:** The meadow by the ferry was under water for two days, so the ferry had a rest.
> **French:** Le pré près du bac est resté sous l'eau pendant deux jours, alors le bac s'est reposé.

*For the reader:* *Chère Willowbank* treats the town as feminine (*la ville*); *Cher Willowbank* is also possible. *Votre coursier* is masculine; if the explorer's courier is she or they, the build should choose *Votre coursière* or a neutral wording such as *La personne qui porte votre courrier*. Please pick the neutral form you prefer.

## 8. Léon: a heron on the coast boat

Arrives the evening of the first journey.

**English, short:**
> A tall grey heron came off the coast boat today. They asked about the brook, then walked up the river path. I think they're coming to you! Léon

**French, short:**
> Un grand héron gris est descendu du bateau de la côte aujourd'hui. Ce héron a posé des questions sur le ruisseau, puis a pris le chemin de la rivière vers l'amont. Je crois que c'est pour vous ! Léon

*For the reader:* Hollis is they/them. The French avoids a pronoun for Hollis on purpose. *Un grand héron* is grammatically masculine as a noun, which is normal French; please check whether *iel* should appear anywhere, or whether avoiding the pronoun is the better choice for young learners.
