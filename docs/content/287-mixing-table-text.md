# The Mixing Table · finite English copy

Companion to spec287. Original wording adapted from proposal201's first Shade Table; toy colors/recipes and all construction situations are invented. Freeze each invitation’s model, prompt, explanation and outcome wording with its source. Shared navigation/control labels are interface wording, not new evidence or a new answer key. Braces substitute only frozen labels or bounded counts; no child-authored text or generated question.

Each goal, recipe and any required counts stay visible. Exact counts in goals are constraints to investigate, not locks on either ingredient control.

Every explanation in the table is a supplied example of a goal-fitting pot, never a claim that the child constructed it. Viewing it follows Paint feedback; any changed subsequent Paint has feedback available. No optional-help flag is invented for automatically supplied feedback.

On guided-heron always show **Guided example** and these three lines:

1. **6 blue is two groups of 3 blue.**
2. **Each group goes with 2 white.**
3. **Try 4 white with the 6 blue, then paint a stripe.**

Do not claim that this guided construction was an unsupported solution, even if no optional help was requested.

## Complete stripe feedback

After every successful Paint, keep the play surface compact: actual ordered stripe counts, **Same proportions** or **Different proportions**, and **Fits this invitation** or **Keep changing the pot**, decoded from the recorded comparison/goal. Put the longer frozen feedback and supplied goal example in a closed **What these amounts show** fold beside the stripe. Opening it does not change the pot or record another Paint; afterFeedback means feedback was available, not that this fold was opened or understood. Separately label the table's explanation **Supplied goal example**, preceded by **Here is one pot that fits this invitation. It is an example, not a description of your pot.** Then show the optional ungraded human reason prompt. The supplied example never replaces or describes the child's actual counts. The four frozen outcome codes use these exact templates:

| Code | Comparison sentence | Goal sentence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| same-fit | **Same proportions as the sample. The pot can hold a different total and still keep the recipe.** | **Your pot fits this invitation's amounts and comparison.** |
| same-miss | **Same proportions as the sample. The pot can hold a different total and still keep the recipe.** | **The proportions match, but this invitation still asks for different amounts. Keep its goal in view while you change the scoops.** |
| different-fit | **Different proportions from the sample. The ingredient groups have changed.** | **Your pot fits this invitation's amounts and comparison.** |
| different-miss | **Different proportions from the sample. The ingredient groups have changed.** | **This pot does not fit the invitation yet. Keep its goal in view while you change the scoops.** |

The goal sentence depends on the goal, not just whether the stripe matches the sample. For a different-ratio goal, matching the sample does not fit even if all other counts did; same-miss then uses **This invitation asks for different proportions. Keep its goal in view while you change the scoops.** instead of the amount-only sentence. For a same-ratio goal with wrong ratio but requested counts satisfied, different-miss still uses its stated generic sentence. No color-derived correctness or automatic replacement.

Revision invitation: **Your pot stays as you made it. Add or take back a scoop, then paint again. Your first stripe stays in your notes.**

Same-input no-op: **This is the stripe already shown. Change the pot or use another idea before painting a new version.** Do not count a new Paint or call it newly independent.

## Grown-up model and evidence note

**This is an invented mathematical display toy. Each scoop is one exact model unit. This screen rule does not predict real paint behavior, and taking a scoop back edits a picture rather than unmixing paint. There are no real volume, mass or pigment-strength measurements here.**

**The toy display averages the ingredient colors by scoop count. Same proportions give the same screen color under that rule. The converse does not hold: rounding can make different proportions look the same. Matching uses the ordered ingredient amounts, not pixel color. Counts, patterns and comparison text remain available.**

**For the unequal 2 white : 3 blue recipe, adding the same positive number to both changes the proportions. Equal 1 : 1 recipes are an exception: adding equally keeps them equal. Adding zero changes nothing. Equivalent recipes can be connected by a fractional multiplier too; 4 : 6 to 6 : 9 is times 1.5.**

**Whole Batches already practices complete copies versus an unequal added portion. This table provides a different editable representation: arbitrary two-part compositions, matching relative amounts across different totals, and repairing a near miss. It does not teach the other four benches from the larger proposal, real paint chemistry, unit conversion, graphs or complete ratio mastery.**

**Guided starting materials, optional help, familiar invitations and feedback remain visible in the record. A matching construction is a task response using the supplied recipe, not proof of a mental method, independent explanation, physical measuring skill or mastery. Listen to the child's reasons without asking the game to grade speech.**

**Only supplied source data, pot counts, first and latest painted observations, paint counts and support reports are stored locally for this explorer. There is no typed answer, recording, upload or automatic sharing. Intermediate stripes are not all retained. Existing notes download, transfer and confirmed reset apply.**
