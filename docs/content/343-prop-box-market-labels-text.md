- Story: “Dot is setting up the toy market for our show. Help make labels so the puppets can find their props.”
- Teaching invitation: “Meet letter sounds”.

## Literal word sources

Each has three lowercase letters, three broad reference phonemes and an original picture with no embedded print. Use one frozen word source per stated sense. Guidance below is for an adult, without a phonemic-transcription prompt to the child.

| Word | Adult reference | Meaning / pictured prop | Guidance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| pan | /pæn/ | A shallow pretend cooking pan for the toy market. | Say pan naturally. Model three sounds without adding uh after p. |
| pen | /pɛn/ | An ink pen used to write the market labels. | Say pen, the writing tool. Here e is the short vowel heard in hen. |
| cap | /kæp/ | The costume cap with a short visor from342. | Use the hat sense, not a bottle top. |
| cup | /kʌp/ | An empty drinking cup with a handle for the pretend café. | Say cup naturally. Here u is the short vowel heard in bud. |
| bed | /bɛd/ | A little bed and pillow for a puppet to sleep in. | Say bed. Here d gives its final sound; a natural final stop need not have a loud release. |
| bud | /bʌd/ | A closed flower bud on a small model plant. | Say bud, meaning a new plant growth, not a seed. Point out the pictured closed flower. |
| bag | /bæɡ/ | A cloth bag used to carry the toy props. | Say bag naturally. The last sound is hard g here. Different regional vowel qualities are not errors. |
| bug | /bʌɡ/ | A toy beetle with six legs for the show. | Say bug in the ordinary broad sense. A beetle is not necessarily a true bug in scientific naming. |
| hen | /hɛn/ | A toy hen, meaning a grown female chicken. | Say hen. Let h flow into the vowel rather than adding a separate uh. |
| cub | /kʌb/ | A small bear cub puppet, meaning a young bear. | Say cub. Compare its end with cup if the child wants help. |
| mat | /mæt/ | The small woven mat from342 for the toy market floor. | Existing sense; appears in the supported note only. |

## Shared note

Literal text:

> A bug is on a bud. A pen is in a cup. A cub is on a mat.

Target tokens (case-sensitive exact occurrences in the lowercase words): `bug`, `bud`, `pen`, `cup`, `cub`, `mat`. Every other lexical token, including initial `A`, lowercase `a`, `is`, `on`, `in`, is **adult-supplied**. Punctuation/spacing is retained, not a separate lexical token. The app does not infer reading from pointing or correct position in an illustration. The initial A is not counted as uppercase recognition.

Illustration: a toy beetle resting on a closed model flower bud; an ink pen sticking out of an empty handled cup; a bear cub puppet sitting on a woven mat. This is one invented stage arrangement, not a field observation or a real instruction to place animals in containers.

Grown-up addition: “This packet offers e, u, d and g in selected words. We keep the letters you arrange and the help opened here. Earlier teaching, pictures or other games may already have made a word familiar. This does not show how a child read or spelled it. The note is shared reading; your partner supplies the other words.”
