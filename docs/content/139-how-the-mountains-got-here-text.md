## 2. Story scenes

### Scene 1. The coin on the high path *(Act I, chapter 3)*

**Hook:** The storm washed mud down the high path. Under it, Hob Pennick found a stone with a coin inside.

**Short:**
> Hob holds up a gray stone. A little round coin sits in it. "Is it money?" asks Pip. "Can I buy a bun with it?"

**Longer:**
> Hob Pennick has been clearing the high path since sunrise. He sets a gray stone on the bakery wall. Pressed into it is a small round shape, flat as a coin, with a faint spiral inside.
>
> "Found it where the mud slid off the bank," says Hob. "Is it money?" asks Pip. "Old money? Enough for a bun?"
>
> "Nobody makes money inside a rock," says Bramble, arriving with her magpie. "It's just a stone. The mountains have always been here, and so has that."
>
> {guide} turns the stone in the light. "If it's not money, what is it? And how did it get up a mountain?"
>
> Hob shrugs. "Ask the bench in the Workshop. I left you a note."
>
> Pip looks at the bakery window. "I'll think better after a bun."

**Keeper's note:** The "coin" is a nummulite: the shell of a sea creature with only one cell. Its name comes from a Latin word for a little coin. Nummulites lived in warm, shallow seas around 50 million years ago. So why is one high in the Pyrenees?

### Scene 2. The miller's millstone *(Act I, chapter 4)*

**Hook:** The storm cracked the millstone. The miller needs a new one, and not just any rock will do.

**Short:**
> The millstone has a crack. "It must be granite again," says the miller. "Speckled, hard, all crystals. Which stone in town is like that?"

**Longer:**
> The miller runs a finger along the crack in her millstone. Her cat sits on the good half.
>
> "It's granite," she says. "Speckled, white and gray and pink, all little crystals locked together. It came from melted rock that cooled slowly, deep underground."
>
> "Hob's coin stone is hard," says Pip. "Is that granite?"
>
> "Hard isn't the clue," says the miller. "Look at what it's made of. Crystals, or grains, or layers, or shells."
>
> Bramble picks up a slate from a broken roof. "And this? It splits into sheets."
>
> "Then it has its own story," says the miller. "Every rock does. Bring them to the bench."

**Keeper's note:** Geologists sort rocks into three families by how they formed: from melted rock, from layers of pieces, or from rock changed by heat and pressure. Hardness alone does not tell you the family.

### Scene 3. Two cliffs by the high ford *(Act III, chapter 3)*

**Hook:** Where the courier's path climbs to the ford, the storm cut the bank open. Stripes of rock show, like a cake sliced.

**Short:**
> One cliff has flat stripes. The one across the brook has stripes bent like a folded blanket. "Same stripes," says {guide}. "Different story."

**Longer:**
> The path to the high ford climbs beside a new cut in the bank. Hob calls it Hob's Cut, because he is the one who has to shovel it. Flat stripes of rock run along it: gray, sandy, dark, pebbly, and soil on top.
>
> "That gray stripe at the bottom," says {courier}. "Look. More coins."
>
> Across the brook is an older cliff. The same stripes are there, but they curve up and over, like a blanket folded on a bed.
>
> Bramble frowns. "So which stripe is oldest? The bottom one, obviously."
>
> "On this side, yes," says {guide}. "On that side, I'm not sure."
>
> Bramble's magpie lands on the bend and tilts its head, as if it has an opinion.

**Keeper's note:** In layers that nobody has disturbed, the lowest formed first. Mountain building can fold layers so far that part of the stack is upside down. Then you follow one layer round the bend.

### Scene 4. Bramble changes her mind, partly *(any act, after the coin stone card)*

**Short:**
> "Fine," says Bramble. "The mountains weren't always here. But they're older than anyone. Older than anyone's grandmother's grandmother." She's right about that.

**Longer:**

>
> "So this was the floor of a sea," she says. "And then the land pushed together and lifted it all the way up here."
>

>
> "Then I was wrong about always," says Bramble. "But I was right that they're old. Older than anyone. Older than my grandmother's grandmother's grandmother."
>

>

## 3. Teach cards

Each card: one idea (30 words or fewer), then a worked example revealed one step per tap. All cards are "A true thing from science."

**Card 1. The ground is rock**
> Dig down through grass and soil, and you reach solid rock. Soil is a thin skin. A pebble is a little piece of the Earth's rock.

Worked example: *What is under the garden path?*
1. On top: gravel and soil.
2. Dig deeper: the soil ends.
3. Underneath: hard rock, called bedrock. It goes on far deeper than any spade can dig.

**Card 2. What a volcano is**
> A volcano is an opening in the ground where melted rock comes out. Melted rock on the surface is lava. It glows because it is hot. It is not fire.

Worked example: *What happens to lava when it cools?*
1. Lava pours out, glowing orange.
2. It cools in the air.
3. It hardens into dark, solid rock. Lava was rock all along.

**Card 3. Three rock families**
> Rocks are named for how they formed. Igneous: melted rock that cooled. Sedimentary: layers of bits pressed together. Metamorphic: rock changed by heat and squeezing, without melting.

Worked example: *Which family is the millstone?*
1. Look: crystals locked together, no layers, no shells.
2. How did it form? Melted rock cooled slowly underground.
3. So it is igneous. Its name is granite.

**Card 4. Break, carry, drop**
> Weathering breaks rock where it is. Erosion carries the pieces away. Deposition drops them somewhere new. Soil is tiny rock pieces mixed with rotted leaves and roots.

Worked example: *How does mountain rock reach the brook bed?*
1. Frost cracks a rock high up. That is weathering.
2. Rain and the brook carry the pieces down. That is erosion.
3. The brook slows and drops them as pebbles and sand. That is deposition.

**Card 5. Reading layers**
> Layers pile up one on top of another. In layers nobody has disturbed, the bottom layer formed first and the top layer formed last.

Worked example: *Three layers: sand on top, mud in the middle, shells at the bottom.*
1. Which went down first? The bottom one: shells.
2. Then the mud on top of them.
3. Last, the sand. So oldest to youngest: shells, mud, sand.

**Card 6. Inside the Earth**
> The Earth has layers too. A thin rocky crust, like the skin on an apple. A thick, hot mantle. A core of metal at the center.

Worked example: *Is the mantle a sea of lava?*
1. The mantle is very hot rock.
2. Most of it is solid, but it can flow very, very slowly.
3. So: no sea of lava. Hot, slow rock.

**Card 7. Plates and the Pyrenees**
> The Earth's outer shell is cracked into huge plates. They move a few centimeters a year, about as fast as fingernails grow. Where two plates push, mountains can rise.

Worked example: *How long does a plate take to move 1 meter at 4 cm a year?*
1. 1 meter is 100 cm.
2. Each year: 4 cm.
3. 100 ÷ 4 = 25. About 25 years.

**Card 8. The coin stone**
> Hob's coin is a nummulite, the shell of a tiny sea creature. Rock full of sea shells formed under a sea. Mountain building later lifted it high.

Worked example: *How did sea rock get into the Pyrenees?*
1. Long ago, a shallow sea lay between Spain's land and France's land.
2. Shells piled up on the sea floor and became limestone.
3. The two plates pushed together. The sea closed, and the rock was lifted into mountains.

**Card 9. Fast and slow changes**
> Some changes happen in moments: an earthquake, a landslide. Some take so long you can't watch them: a pebble rounding, a mountain rising. Both change the land.

Worked example: *Is a smooth pebble a change?*
1. It started as a sharp chip of rock.
2. Many floods rolled and rubbed it.
3. Now it is smooth. That is a real change, just a slow one.

**Card 10. Earthquakes**
> An earthquake happens when rock underground suddenly slips or breaks. The ground itself shakes. Most earthquakes are tiny. A few are strong.

Worked example: *Does the Pyrenees have earthquakes?*
1. Yes, many small ones every year.
2. Most are too small for anyone to feel.
3. Now and then, a moderate one shakes houses.

**Card 11. Active, dormant, extinct**
> Active: erupting, or could soon. Dormant: quiet a long time, but could erupt again. Extinct: not expected to erupt again. You decide from history, not looks.

Worked example: *The Garrotxa volcanoes near Olot, in Catalonia.*
1. Last eruption: more than 10,000 years ago.
2. That is long for people, short for volcanoes.
3. Scientists don't call them extinct. They are very quiet. "Dormant" fits best.

## 4. True-thing cards (archive and science)

Each card shows one panel at a time (30 words or fewer); tap for the next.

**The Pyrenees** *(A true thing from science)*
> The Pyrenees rose where the plate carrying Spain and Portugal pushed into the plate carrying France. Most of the rising happened about 55 to 25 million years ago.
>
> Scientists still argue about exactly when it started. The highest peak, Aneto, is 3,404 meters tall.

**The coin stone** *(A true thing from science)*
> Nummulites were sea creatures with one cell and a flat, coiled shell. Their name means "little coin." They were common about 50 million years ago.
>
> Limestone full of them is found across the Pyrenees. It shows that this rock formed under a sea.
>
> In 2024, a park ranger found a fossil starfish in rock of about the same age, high in the Ordesa and Monte Perdido National Park.

**Garrotxa** *(A true thing from science)*
> Near the town of Olot, in Catalonia, about forty small volcanoes sit in green hills. The last one to erupt was Croscat.
>
> Older measurements said about 11,000 years ago; newer ones say 13,000 to 15,000. Different methods, different answers. Either way: more than 10,000 years.

**Earthquakes in the Pyrenees** *(A true thing from science)*
> Instruments record hundreds of small earthquakes in the Pyrenees every year. Only about ten a year are big enough for people nearby to feel. Strong ones are rare.

**Pompeii** *(A true thing from history; older depth, Travelers' Archive)*
> In 79 CE, the volcano Vesuvius in Italy erupted. Ash and light stones called pumice fell on the Roman town of Pompeii and covered it several meters deep.
>
> Many people fled; many others died. Pompeii never became a busy town again.
>
> The ash kept the town as it was: streets, walls, painted rooms, ovens and pots. People began digging it out in 1748, and they are still learning from it.
>
> Vesuvius last erupted in 1944. Scientists watch it every day and night.

**Earthquake safety** *(General safety guidance from official sources)*
> If the ground shakes: **Drop** to your hands and knees. **Cover** your head and neck, under a sturdy table if one is close. **Hold on** until the shaking stops.
>
> If you're inside, stay inside. This comes from official safety advice (US CDC). A grown-up can check your area's advice. The benches here give no safety advice.

### 5.3 Pebble Tumble

**Every screen:** A simplified picture. Real pebbles take many floods and long distances, and the numbers here are made up.

**Buttons:** Quiet day · Flood
**Counters:** Floods: {n} · Sharp corners: {c} · Size: {s}
**Quiet day:** The brook runs gently. The pebble stays put. Nothing changes today.
**Flood, corners left:** The flood rolls the pebble. One sharp corner wears away.
**Flood, round:** The pebble is round now. It rubs a little smaller, very slowly.
**At size 4:** This pebble has a long way left to go. Sand comes later.

**Rules (Details):** The pebble starts with 6 sharp corners and size 10. Each flood wears off 1 corner. When the corners are gone, every 4th flood makes it 1 size smaller. Quiet days change nothing.

- **Mountain building:** The ribbon grows a hair's width each tap. You'd tap for millions of years. Slow.
- *Sapling view adds:* Mountains like the Pyrenees rose about a millimeter a year, or less.

**Sort prompt:** Fast or slow? Tap one for each card.

## 6. Problem sets

Positions of the right answer are given as (1), (2) or (3). Hints nudge; explanations are 1 to 2 sentences.

### Set 1. Rock Families

**A1** · Sprout · 3-choice
**Prompt:** Dig deep under the garden. What do you reach?
1. More soil, all the way down. *(misconception: the Earth is soil)*
2. Solid rock. **(right)**
3. A big empty space. *(misconception: the Earth is hollow)*
**Feedback 1:** Soil is a thin skin. Keep digging and the soil ends.
**Feedback 2:** Yes. Under the soil is bedrock, and it goes on far down.
**Feedback 3:** The Earth isn't hollow. It's rock and metal all the way down.
**Hint:** Think of Hob's spade hitting something hard.
**Explanation:** Soil covers the ground thinly. Underneath almost everywhere is solid rock.

**A2** · Seedling · 3-choice · **guided**
**Steps shown:** 1. Look at the clue: crystals locked together. 2. Which family forms from melted rock?
**Prompt:** The millstone is speckled crystals. Which family is it?
1. Sedimentary *(misconception: every rock is layers of bits)*
2. Metamorphic
3. Igneous **(right)**
**Feedback 1:** Sedimentary rock has grains or layers. This one has crystals from melted rock.
**Feedback 2:** Close. But granite crystals grew as melted rock cooled.
**Feedback 3:** Yes. Granite formed when melted rock cooled slowly underground.
**Hint:** Crystals like these grow as melted rock cools.
**Explanation:** Granite is igneous. Its crystals grew slowly as melted rock cooled deep underground.

**A3** · Seedling · 3-choice
**Prompt:** Hob's coin stone is hard and full of shells. Which family?
1. Igneous, because it's very hard *(misconception: all hard rocks are igneous)*
2. Sedimentary **(right)**
3. Metamorphic, because it's very old *(misconception: old rock must have changed)*
**Feedback 1:** Hardness isn't the clue. Shells are. Melted rock would destroy them.
**Feedback 2:** Yes. Shells piled on a sea floor and were pressed into limestone.
**Feedback 3:** Being old doesn't change a rock's family. Look at the shells.
**Hint:** What could keep a shell in one piece?
**Explanation:** Limestone like this is sedimentary. It formed from shells and mud on a sea floor.

**A4** · Seedling · 3-choice
**Prompt:** Slate is mud rock, squeezed and heated without melting. Which family?
1. Metamorphic **(right)**
2. Igneous, because it was heated *(misconception: heated means melted)*
3. Sedimentary, because it splits in sheets
**Feedback 1:** Yes. Heat and squeezing changed the mud rock without melting it.
**Feedback 2:** Heated isn't melted. It never melted, so it isn't igneous.
**Feedback 3:** It started as mud rock. Heat and squeezing changed it into something new.
**Hint:** Did it melt, or only change?
**Explanation:** Slate is metamorphic. Heat and pressure changed mudstone without melting it.

**A5** · Sapling · 3-choice
**Prompt:** Why do you find fossils in limestone but not in granite?
1. Granite is far too hard for shells to get inside. *(misconception: hardness decides)*
2. Fossils are only ever found near rivers and seas. *(misconception: place decides)*
3. Granite was once melted, and that destroys shells. **(right)**
**Feedback 1:** People can cut granite. The real reason is how it formed.
**Feedback 2:** Fossils turn up on mountains and under deserts too. Think about melting.
**Feedback 3:** Yes. Gentle layers can bury shells. Melted rock destroys them.
**Hint:** Think about how each rock formed.
**Explanation:** Sedimentary layers can bury remains gently. Rock that was melted keeps no shells.

**A6** · Sapling · order the cards, then 3-choice
**Prompt (a):** Put the granite's journey in order.
Cards (shown shuffled): Granite sits in the mountain · Frost cracks it into grains · The brook carries the grains away · The grains settle on a sea floor · They are pressed into sandstone
**Answer:** in the order listed.
**Prompt (b):** Frost cracking the granite is called what?
1. Erosion *(misconception: weathering and erosion are the same)*
2. Weathering **(right)**
3. Deposition
**Feedback 1:** Erosion carries pieces away. Breaking them where they sit is weathering.
**Feedback 2:** Yes. The rock breaks where it is. Moving comes next.
**Feedback 3:** Deposition is the dropping at the end. This is the breaking at the start.
**Hint:** Is the rock moving yet, or only breaking?
**Explanation:** Weathering breaks rock in place. Erosion carries it. Deposition drops it. Granite can become sandstone this way.

**A7** · Keeper's Desk · 3-choice
**Prompt:** The marble step has shiny crystals. Which family, and why?
1. Igneous: crystals mean it cooled from melted rock. *(misconception: crystals mean igneous)*
2. Still limestone: rocks never change family. *(misconception: rocks are permanent)*
3. Metamorphic: heat and pressure changed limestone. **(right)**
**Feedback 1:** Crystals can also grow when heat and pressure change rock. Marble never melted.
**Feedback 2:** Rocks can change family, very slowly. Heat and pressure changed this limestone.
**Feedback 3:** Yes. Marble's crystals grew without melting. It began as limestone.

**B5** · Sapling · 3-choice

**B6** · Sapling · 3-choice

**B7** · Sapling · 3-choice
**Prompt:** Crust, mantle and core are layers. Is the deepest one oldest?

**Feedback 1:** The core wasn't laid down like mud. The rule doesn't fit here.
**Feedback 2:** Yes. Rock layers record time. Earth's layers sort materials and heat.
**Feedback 3:** That rule is only for layers laid down one on another.
**Hint:** Were the crust and core laid down like mud in a brook?
**Explanation:** The bottom-is-oldest rule is for piled-up layers. Earth's layers are a different kind.

## 7. "I'm stuck" worked examples

One per item shape. Each shows an easier case, then how to start this one.

- **Which family (A2 to A4, A7):** *Easier:* Black glassy rock from a cooled lava flow. It cooled from melted rock, so it's igneous. *Start this one:* Read the "how it formed" note. Melted, pressed, or changed?
- **Why fossils (A5):** *Easier:* Drop a shell in wet sand and it stays. Drop it in melted wax and it's gone. *Start this one:* Which rock was once melted?
- **Weathering or erosion (A6):** *Easier:* A sandcastle cracks in the sun (weathering). A wave carries the sand off (erosion). *Start this one:* Is the granite moving yet?
- **Layer order (B1 to B3):** *Easier:* Three books dropped on a table, one at a time. The bottom one was dropped first. *Start this one:* Find the lowest layer.
- **The crack (B5):** *Easier:* A scratch on a table and a cloth over it. The scratch came first. *Start this one:* Which layers are scratched, and which lie over it?
- **The fold (B6):** *Easier:* Fold a striped blanket over. The stripe that was on the bottom is now on top. *Start this one:* Follow the coin layer.
- **Earth's layers (B7):** *Easier:* A peach has skin, flesh and a stone. They weren't piled up one by one. *Start this one:* Were crust and core laid down like mud?
- **Cross-section (B8):** *Easier:* A cake: layers baked, then iced, then a knife cut through. The cut came last. *Start this one:* Which things are cut, and which do the cutting?
- **Fast or slow (C1):** *Easier:* A balloon pops (fast). A tree grows (slow). *Start this one:* Could you watch it in one day?
- **Pebble model (C2, C4):** *Easier:* 3 corners, 1 off per flood. After 2 floods: 1 left. *Start this one:* Count floods until the corners are gone.
- **Rates (C5, C7, C8):** *Easier:* 1 cm a year for 100 years is 100 cm, which is 1 m. *Start this one:* Multiply, then change units.
- **Aneto (C6):** *Easier:* You fill a bucket while it leaks. It never gets as full as the tap alone would make it. *Start this one:* What empties the mountain?
- **Earthquakes (D1, D2):** *Easier:* Push two bricks together hard until one jerks sideways. *Start this one:* What is moving underground?
- **Volcano words (D3, D4):** *Easier:* A cat asleep all afternoon is still a cat that can wake. *Start this one:* How long is long, for a volcano?
- **Ribbon (D7):** *Easier:* A 1 m ribbon for 1,000 years: each year is 1 mm. *Start this one:* Split the 1,000 mm ribbon into that many equal parts.

## 8. Key terms

| Term | Child definition (15 words or fewer) |
| --- | --- |
| bedrock | The solid rock under the soil. It goes down very deep. |
| igneous | Rock made when melted rock cooled and hardened. |
| sedimentary | Rock made from layers of sand, mud or shells pressed together. |
| metamorphic | Rock changed by heat and squeezing, without melting. |
| sediment | Loose bits of rock, sand, mud or shell. Not rock yet. |
| fossil | Remains or traces of a living thing, kept in rock. |
| nummulite | A tiny sea creature with a coin-shaped shell. Now a fossil. |
| weathering | Rock breaking into pieces where it is. |
| erosion | Broken rock being carried away by water, wind or ice. |
| deposition | Carried pieces being dropped in a new place. |
| soil | Tiny rock pieces mixed with rotted leaves and roots. |
| layer | One flat band of rock laid down at one time. |
| fold | Layers bent by squeezing. Sometimes they turn right over. |
| crust | Earth's thin, rocky outside, like apple skin. |
| mantle | The thick, hot layer under the crust. Mostly solid, flows very slowly. |
| core | The metal center of the Earth. Very, very hot. |
| plate | A huge piece of Earth's cracked outer shell. Plates move slowly. |
| lava | Melted rock that has come out on the surface. |
| volcano | An opening where melted rock comes out of the ground. |
| earthquake | Sudden shaking of the ground when rock underground slips. |
| active | A volcano that is erupting, or could erupt soon. |
| dormant | A volcano quiet for a long time that could erupt again. |
| extinct | A volcano not expected to ever erupt again. |
| model | A made-up, simpler version that helps us think. |

## 9. Grown-up panel

**What this teaches:** that the ground is rock; the three rock families and how they form; that rocks change family very slowly; weathering, erosion and deposition; that undisturbed rock layers are read bottom up, and folds can overturn them; Earth's layers; tectonic plates; how the real Pyrenees formed; that sea fossils in mountains show old sea floor; fast and slow Earth changes; what earthquakes and volcanoes are; and how volcano status is judged.

**What it does not claim:** Willowbank, Hob's Cut, the Bent Cliff, the coin stone's finding and the millstone are fiction. The two benches are made-up models: their numbers are not real rates. Dates for the Pyrenees and for Garrotxa's last eruption are estimates that scientists still refine, and the pack says so. Nothing here records mastery or scores. No bench or quiz gives safety advice.

**Sources in brief:** US Geological Survey (Earth's interior; plate speeds), Catalan and Spanish geological sources on the Pyrenees and the Garrotxa Volcanic Zone, the Spanish National Geographic Institute and seismological studies on Pyrenean earthquakes, published research on how river pebbles round, the Italian Civil Protection Department on Vesuvius, and the US CDC on earthquake safety. Full list in the source audit.

**Earthquake safety:** The safety card gives Drop, Cover, and Hold On as published by the US CDC. French (Géorisques) and Spanish civil-protection advice says the same in its own words: shelter under sturdy furniture or by a strong interior wall, keep away from windows, stay inside. Please check what your own area advises.

**About Garrotxa's label:** the game says "dormant." Spanish hazard planners officially treat the field as active, meaning an eruption is possible, though the Catalan geological survey judges the risk very small.

**Off-screen activities (optional, adult-led):**
- *Pebble look:* collect a few pebbles from a dry path or garden and sort them from sharpest to roundest. Adult-led near any water; never collect from streams, cliffs, protected sites or parks where collecting is forbidden. No hammers or chisels.
- *Settling jar:* a grown-up shakes garden soil in a closed jar of water and lets it stand overnight; look at the layers. Use a lidded plastic jar, tap water only, and no heat.
