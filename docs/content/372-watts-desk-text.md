# Watts Desk 372 · finite text

## Opening and model

The town's upper storeroom needs a new winch. The miller asks, “Which transfers energy faster? Is that always the one that transfers more altogether?” Try the number model before deciding what to tell the miller.

These are invented constant-power winches, not instructions for real machines. Energy comes from an outside source and goes into the gravitational store of the load and Earth. We ignore losses. The source loses exactly the energy the load gains. We are counting energy delivered to the load, not a real motor's electricity use. No load weight or height is being calculated here.

A watt tells how many joules transfer each second. A joule measures an amount of energy. Power is a rate; energy is an amount. Our model seconds move when you press a button. They do not measure how fast you tap.

## Manual example

1. Reed transfers 20 joules each second. Over 5 seconds it transfers 100 joules: 20 watts × 5 seconds = 100 joules.
2. Willow transfers 40 joules each second. Over the same 5 seconds it transfers 200 joules. Equal time; different energy amounts.
3. Let Reed run 10 seconds instead. It now transfers 200 joules too. Equal energy can take different times.

## Glossary

- Energy: An amount that can be stored and transferred. We count it in joules here.
- Power: How quickly energy is transferred. One watt means one joule each second.
- Ideal model: A deliberately simplified number model. Here we leave out losses, start-up changes and real machine limits.
