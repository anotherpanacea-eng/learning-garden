Companion to [spec 118](../specs/118-keepers-desk-entry.md) (conventions, records and acceptance) and [spec 120](../specs/120-keeper-of-the-ways.md) (Act VI, the three Ways, the exchange plan). Travel times and loads are the story's own and must stay consistent with [Case 3](118-seed-exchange-case.md) (the 2 : 3 rate) and [Case 4](118-flood-bell-case.md) (the courier's 3 hours on the river path, unladen, by day).

Case ID `exchange-plan`. Appears in Act VI with *Compare routes and keep a plan*.

## 1. Why this case

Act VI asks Willowbank and Vadoalto to keep the Ways open for good: "deciding who carries what, on which Way, and how often". Spec 120's act prompt keeps a plan in one tap. The desk version writes the plan in full: the amounts each way, the trips they need, the time each Way costs in each season, and what happens when a Way is closed. It draws together the ratio from Case 3, the timing from Case 4 and the Cartographer's routes, and it ends with a plan both towns will actually follow, which is the act's point: shared agency.

## 3. The Ways (always visible in a side drawer)

| Way | One way, with a loaded cart | Carts? | When it's open |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **River path** (blue ribbon) | 4 hours | Yes | All year, except when the brook is high and the bell has rung |
| **Bridge road** (gold ribbon) | 7 hours | Yes | All year |
| **High ford** (red ribbon) | 3 hours, on foot | No: two baskets or two bundles carried | May to October only |

**Loads:** a cart carries **6 baskets** of apples or **4 bundles** of reed (reed is bulky).

**What each town wants, each week in the apple season:** Vadoalto wants **12 baskets** of apples. Willowbank wants **8 bundles** of reed. The agreed rate is still **2 bundles for every 3 baskets**.

## 4. Steps

1. **Does the trade balance?** (ratio check)
2. **How many trips?** (division with loads; combining trips)
3. **Which Way, which season?** (time, constraints and trade-offs)
4. **When a Way is closed** (contingency)
5. **The plan** (writing a plan two towns can follow)

Steps open in any order.

## 5. Step 1: Does the trade balance?

**Learn card:** the ratio table from Case 3, reshown (fold-away as usual):

> 3 baskets go with 2 bundles. Multiply both by the same number to scale up.

Tasks (Check my working):

- **1a.** At 2 bundles for 3 baskets, how many bundles should 12 baskets bring? *Answer: 12 is 4 groups of 3, so **8 bundles**.*
- **1b.** "Does what each town wants match the rate?" *Answer: **yes**: 12 baskets up, 8 bundles down.*
- **1c.** "In spring, Vadoalto wants only **6 baskets** a week, but Willowbank still wants 8 bundles. What's fair?" Not marked. Choices: "Willowbank sends 6 baskets and gets 4 bundles."; "Willowbank pays for the other 4 bundles another way, perhaps with honey cake."; "Vadoalto sends 8 bundles anyway; it'll balance out by autumn." Each reply works out what the choice means in baskets and bundles and who carries the difference. The reply to the third adds: "That's a promise over time. It works if both towns keep a record of who owes what."

**I'm stuck (1a):** as Case 3's 1a ladder, with 12 baskets.

## 6. Step 2: How many trips?

**Learn card:**

> When a load has a limit, divide the amount by the limit. If there's anything left over, it still needs a trip: 13 baskets in carts of 6 is 2 full carts and 1 more, so **3 trips**.
>
> A cart that goes up full can come back full. One round trip can carry apples up and reed down.

**Guided first task (2a):** 12 baskets shown as 12 icons, grouped into carts of 6.

Tasks:

- **2a.** Carts of 6 baskets: how many cart trips to take 12 baskets up? *Answer: 12 ÷ 6 = **2**.*
- **2b.** Carts of 4 bundles: how many cart trips to bring 8 bundles down? *Answer: 8 ÷ 4 = **2**.*
- **2c.** If each cart goes up with apples and comes back with reed, how many **round trips** a week? *Answer: **2**.*
- **2d.** In the harvest week Vadoalto asks for **15 baskets**. How many cart trips up, and how many bundles should come down? *Answers: 15 ÷ 6 = 2 remainder 3, so **3 trips**; 15 baskets is 5 groups of 3, so **10 bundles**, which is 10 ÷ 4 = 2 remainder 2, so **3 trips** down.*

**I'm stuck (2d):**

1. *Hint:* "Two full carts take 12 baskets. How many are left?"
2. *Similar example:* the learn card's 13 baskets.
3. **Skip for now.**

**Replies to likely errors:**

| Entered | Misconception | Reply |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2.5 for 2d's trips up | Giving the quotient as a fraction of a trip | "Half a cart still has to make the whole trip. How many times does a cart go?" |
| 2 for 2d's trips up | Dropping the remainder | "Two carts take 12. Who takes the other 3 baskets?" |
| 4 for 2c | Counting each direction as a separate round trip | "A round trip goes up *and* comes back. Each one can carry apples up and reed down." |

## 7. Step 3: Which Way, which season?

**Learn card:**

> A plan is a choice between costs. The fastest Way might not always be open. The safest might take all day. Write down what each choice costs before choosing.
>
> Worked example: one round trip by the bridge road is 7 + 7 = 14 hours. That's a long day for a cart driver.

Tasks (Check my working):

- **3a.** One round trip by the **river path**? *Answer: 4 + 4 = **8 hours**.*
- **3b.** Two round trips a week: total hours by river path, and by bridge road? *Answers: **16 hours**; **28 hours**.*
- **3c.** "How much longer does the bridge road take, over the two trips?" *Answer: **12 hours** more.*
- **3d.** In summer, could the high ford carry the apples? *Answer: **no carts**: on foot, two baskets a person, so 12 baskets would need **6 people** walking. Choices about whether that's sensible follow; none marked.*
- **3e.** Build the season table. For each season, choose the Way for the weekly carts and the Way for urgent letters. Not marked; **Check my working** checks only that no choice breaks a rule (no carts on the high ford; no high ford from November to April). The reply to a valid table names what it costs in hours each week.

  | Season | Carts | Urgent letters |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | Spring (Mar–May) | ? | ? |
  | Summer (Jun–Aug) | ? | ? |
  | Autumn (Sep–Nov) | ? | ? |
  | Winter (Dec–Feb) | ? | ? |

  Replies to rule breaks: "The high ford is closed in winter: a red ribbon can't help in snow." / "No cart can take the high ford. Who would carry the baskets?"

**Note for the build:** the seasons above overlap the high ford's May-to-October window in spring and autumn. The rule check uses the months, so the high ford is accepted for spring and autumn letters only with the reply: "Fine in May, or in September and October. Earlier or later, which Way instead?"

## 8. Step 4: When a Way is closed

**Learn card:**

> A good plan says what to do when something goes wrong, before it goes wrong. People in a hurry follow the plan they already have.

Tasks:

- **4a.** "It's November. The bell has rung, so the river path is closed for two days. What happens to this week's carts?" Choices:
  - "They go by the bridge road, and leave earlier." ← fits the Ways
  - "They wait until the river path opens."
  - "They take the high ford."

  Reply to the second: "That works if the apples can wait. Can Vadoalto's bakers?" Reply to the third: "It's November. Check when the high ford is open."
- **4b.** "The bridge road is blocked by a fallen tree, and the river path is open. Anything to change?" Free choice: use the river path as usual; send word to Vadoalto; ask the town to clear the tree. Each has a reply.
- **4c.** "If **every** Way is closed, what should the plan say?" Supplied answers to choose or adapt: "Letters wait at the post office and go first when a Way opens."; "Each town keeps a week of the other's goods in store before winter."; "Nobody travels. Not even with a ribbon."
