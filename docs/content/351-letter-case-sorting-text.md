## Drawer and comparison templates

- Title: The Letterpress Drawer
- Entry: Try capital and lowercase letters. Print a theater label with Dot.
- Opening: Dot is sorting the theater's printing letters after the storm. One letter can have two forms. Shall we try them?
- Model prompt: Your partner can name this letter. Here are its capital and lowercase forms.
- Choose a letter; Try the other case; Show the pair; Turn the card over; Another letter; Look closely; Print a label; Back to my letter.
- Capital target: Find the lowercase form of capital {capital}.
- Lowercase target: Find the capital form of lowercase {lowercase}.
- Choice accessible name: {case} {glyph}.
- Pair heading: {capital} and {lowercase}.
- For grown-ups.
- Contrast selectors/headings: A and a; C and c; b and d.
- Pair accessible name: Capital {capital} and lowercase {lowercase}, two forms of the same letter.
- Literal choice: You put {targetCase} {target} with {choiceCase} {choice}.
- Fitting comparison: These are two forms of the same letter. Your partner can name it with you.
- Other comparison (fill the target letter's pair): Our drawer pairs capital {capital} with lowercase {lowercase}. You can swap your choice or keep looking.
- Visit reminder: This drawer and its poster wall are for this visit. Nothing here is saved.

## Look closely

- Aa: A and a look different. They are two forms of the same letter.
- Cc: C and c have a similar curve. They are two forms of the same letter.
- b/d: B pairs with b. D pairs with d. The lowercase letters b and d are different letters.
- Size: Making a letter larger does not turn it into a capital. Both of these are lowercase a.
- Size accessible names: Lowercase a, smaller print. / Lowercase a, larger print.

## Grown-up interpretation

For grown-ups: This tool explores letter case and adult-led letter naming. The printed words, meanings and pictures are supplied. A paired choice or a poster is not evidence of independent naming, sounds, decoding, handwriting or knowing when capitals are required. Mixed case here is playful, not conventional writing. These four common-noun labels tolerate playful mixtures of case; case can matter differently in names and other contexts, including PIN as an acronym. Shapes vary between fonts. The large lowercase example is still lowercase. No speech is assessed or recorded, and this visit's work is not saved.
