## Six manual teaching pages

Every source freezes the complete pages below. Example fields include their own masses, initial velocity, force program, selected frame and history flag; none reads current work. Source title/labels never supply a real person or learner identity.

1. “Velocity tells speed and direction. Right is positive; left is negative. Our arrows show velocity in meters per second. A horizontal velocity line above zero means steady movement to the right, not rest.” Example masses[1,2],v0=2,forces[0,0,0],frame0,history=true.
2. “Each chosen mass is the whole cart and its load, measured in kilograms (kg). Force is measured in newtons (N). Our card is the total horizontal force after opposing pushes and pulls are combined. Divide that force by the mass to find acceleration: how velocity changes each second. The heavier cart changes by a smaller amount under the same nonzero force.” Example masses[1,2],v0=0,forces[4,0,−4],frame1,history=false.
3. “Zero net force means no change in velocity. A moving cart keeps moving at the same velocity in this model. A cart at rest stays at rest. Zero force does not tell us the carts have equal masses.” Example masses[1,2],v0=0,forces[4,0,−4],frame2,history=true.
4. “A force against the motion can slow a cart before it reverses. Force direction and velocity direction need not match. A smaller velocity change does not always mean a smaller speed at that moment. The lighter cart reaches rest after the first second; keep the leftward force for the next second and it reverses.” Example masses[1,2],v0=4,forces[−4,−4,0],frame1,history=true.
5. “These carts finish with the same velocity they started with. Their velocities changed along the way. Matching end values do not mean matching histories. Compare the lines between the start and the finish.” Example masses[1,2],v0=0,forces[4,0,−4],frame3,history=true.
6. “With equal total masses, equal starting velocities and the same force cards, the velocity histories coincide. The graph predicts velocity through time. Straight pieces come from a constant force during each second. Our model leaves out friction and air resistance; vertical forces balance. Each load stays fixed during a run. Changing a load begins again from the starting velocity. Real carts would need real observations.” Example masses[2,2],v0=0,forces[4,0,−4],frame1,history=true.

## Turn language and representations

Title **Push & Drift**; entry “Give two carts the same pushes. Try different loads.” Page labels **Plan**, **Run**, **History**. Plan: “Choose the two loads. Then choose a net force for each second.” Starting notice: “This matches the starting setup. You can change every load and force card.” **Swap loads**; **Second1/2/3**; **Net force for this second**. Mass changes preserve all force cards; force changes preserve both loads. Both reset the clock to0, with “New setup. The clock is back at zero; your other choices are still here.”

Run shows the predicted graph prefix through the selected second, with future points blank. **Before the pushes** / **After second N**; **Previous second**, **Next second**, **Restart**. Velocity displays name each cart, mass, exact signed velocity, units and direction. Zero: “At rest at this instant.” Do not call acceleration velocity or negative velocity slower. Formula/readout labels: **Force amount**, **Mass**, **Acceleration amount**, **Direction of the change**, **Velocity before**, **Change this second**, **Velocity now**. Amounts are nonnegative; left/right supplies direction, with signed values also printed. For a nonzero constant prefix only: **Change after N identical pushes**; show acceleration amount×elapsed seconds and the signed velocity change. Do not use that shortcut for mixed force programs or describe zero force as identical pushes. Zero-force prefixes use the no-change wording.

History: **Predicted velocity over time**. Axes **Time (seconds)** and **Velocity (meters per second)**; one fixed source-scale for both carts. Distinct line/marker styles plus cart names. “The arrows and graph share one scale throughout this scene.” Table retains both velocities, force amounts and the acceleration/change amounts for each second. Those numeric values plus the plotted constant-rate spans and elapsed times are prior arithmetic context, not observed calculation or reading. Coincident histories: “For this program, the two velocity histories coincide. Check the loads and forces before explaining why.” End-equal only: “The final velocities match. Earlier steps differ.” Otherwise: “These carts have different final velocities.”

**How the carts work**, **Read this teaching page**, **Read this step**, **Read this history**, **Back to my program**. Readers explicitly state signs, fractions, units, cart names and whether values are predictions. Signed fraction−4/3 is read as “negative4over3meters per second”, not silently rounded. Direction is also named; zero is directionless. Drawn arrow lengths encode velocity magnitude on the shared scale; force cards have their own Newton labels and never borrow that velocity scale.

**Keep this program**, **Work on a copy**, **Another copy**, **New blank setup**, **Working programs**, **Kept programs**, **Motion and evidence**. Unkept notice: “Your loads and force cards wait in this open page. Keep a program to include it in Notes.” Return after a kept title: “Tell someone what this model predicts. What would you still need to find out about a real cart?” No automatic chapter or practical-competence credit.

Capacity refusal: “This program could not be kept safely. Your choices and earlier programs are unchanged. Download Notes or keep exploring.” Computed-view refusal: “This result could not be opened safely. Your program and earlier work are unchanged. Download Notes or try again.” **Retry view** only restores its own unedited proposal; any new input clears it. New pending context on return may use safe Plan until qualification succeeds; the requested view remains locally retryable. No refused numbers enter text, diagram, hidden narration or aria labels.

Unknown example: “This earlier example picture cannot be drawn. Its words and your program are still here.” Unknown row: “This earlier format stays unchanged in Notes. You can still make another program.” Root change: “The kept programs changed. Reopen them before continuing.” Save notices describe actual saving-off/uncertain/device result when Keep happened. Do not imply download/save just from looking at a history.

Grown-up: “These are given force programs and predictions from a constant-mass, one-dimensional model. Kept loads, forces and opened views are constructions, not observed physical trials, independent calculation, understanding or mastery. Initial choices are instrument starts. View flags name requests in this version, not attention or whether its current setup was run. Displayed positive integer arithmetic records prior fact exposure for matching current and later math in this explorer’s notebook through the existing helper; it records no new answer or fresh independent evidence. Other interpretations and real investigations remain human work.”
