## F4 · supplied glossary

Header: **Words for the water and garden tables**
Instruction: Read one sentence. Choose a spelling. Then read your version. Your other sentences will wait.
Prompt: Find three slips on this glossary. You can revise, compare or keep it unfinished.

### g1 · Catchment

Original: **Catchment: the land wich drains toward the same river outlet.**
Flagged word: wich. Frame: `Catchment: the land ` + choice + ` drains toward the same river outlet.`
Choices, in order: `which` (a, matches) · `witch` (b) · `wich` (c, Leave as printed).
Feedback a: Which fits this sentence. It points to the land being described.
Feedback b: Witch names a character. Here the sentence needs which to describe the land.
Feedback c: This glossary uses which here. Compare the letters in your version with which.
Hint: Which word links land to the words describing it?
Example steps: Look at “the path which floods.” Which links path to its description. Now look at the land in this sentence.

### g2 · Permeable

Original: **Permeable: lets water pass through. Our garden path is permiable.**
Flagged word: permiable. Frame: `Permeable: lets water pass through. Our garden path is ` + choice + `.`
Choices: `permeible` (a) · `permeable` (b, matches) · `permiable` (c, Leave as printed).
Feedback a: This word uses -able. The family word permeation is a clue, not a rule for every word.
Feedback b: Permeable matches the checked spelling. Permeation helps us notice perme.
Feedback c: Compare permeation and permeable: both keep perme before the ending.
Hint: Look at perme in permeation, then check the ending.
Example steps: Find perme in permeation. Find able on the word card. Put the letter groups together and check permeable.

### g3 · Embankment

Original: **Embankment: a raised bank. It has an affect on where floods go.**
Flagged word: affect. Frame: `Embankment: a raised bank. It has an ` + choice + ` on where floods go.`
Choices: `affect` (a, Leave as printed) · `efect` (b) · `effect` (c, matches).
Feedback a: Here “an effect” names a result. Affect is usually a verb in sentences like “Floods affect the road.”
Feedback b: The noun effect has two f's. Compare it with the checked word.
Feedback c: Effect names the result here: an effect on where floods go.
Hint: The words “has an” need a noun naming a result here.
Example steps: “The wall affects the flow.” Affects is the doing word there. “The wall has an effect.” Effect names the result. Now read this sentence.

Glossary help ids `g1-example`, `g2-example`, `g3-example` reveal these steps one at a time, after recording the chosen help id. Printed hint is shown with the current sentence from the start; it is support, not an independence observation.

## F1–F3 · supplied jobs

Each job has three choices in this order. Compare outcome: unchosen=`incomplete`, fitting=`fit`, otherwise=`different`; no automatic next job. The printed teaching lead appears with the job. The optional example has its own title and all steps below. A correct answer gets the explanation; other choices get their own feedback then the explanation. Shared incomplete line: Choose a word, or skip for now. Shared different line: Look at the clue and your choice. You can revise or keep this version.

### F1 · A family clue (guided)

Teaching lead: A family word can remind us of letters. Check the ending against the word card too.
Guided steps: 1. Find perme in permeation. 2. This word uses -able. 3. Check both parts in your chosen spelling.
Prompt: Permeation is in its family. Our word ends in -able. Which spelling matches these clues?
Choices: permeable (a, fit) · permeible (b) · permiable (c).
Feedback b: This ending is -able, not -ible. Check the word card.
Feedback c: Permeation has perme. Keep those letters here.
Hint: Check the family letters and then the ending.
Explanation: The checked spelling is permeable: perme and able.
Example title: **A family can help**
Example steps: 1. Define and definition share defin. 2. A family may show a useful letter group. 3. Still check the whole word and its ending.

### F2 · A plant word

Teaching lead: A word's history can offer spelling clues. The checked spelling is still our reference.
Prompt: This plant word has ph at the start and y in synthesis. Which spelling matches?
Choices: fotosynthesis (a) · photosynthesis (b, fit) · photosinthesis (c).
Feedback a: The word card starts photo, with ph.
Feedback c: Synthesis has y after s. Look at that letter group.
Hint: Check photo, then synthesis.
Explanation: Photosynthesis keeps ph in photo and y in synthesis.
Example title: **Look at a spelling clue**
Example steps: 1. Photograph begins with photo. 2. Point to its ph. 3. A clue helps us look closely; it does not settle every word's spelling.

### F3 · Two kinds of history

Teaching lead / card: Photosynthesis: Greek-derived parts. Catchment: catch with -ment. Algorithm: from al-Khwarizmi's name; its later spelling was reshaped to resemble a Greek word.
Prompt: Which note describes a word whose spelling was reshaped to resemble a Greek word?
Choices: photosynthesis (a) · catchment (b) · algorithm (c, fit).
Feedback a: This note describes Greek-derived parts, not the reshaping named in the question.
Feedback b: This note describes catch with -ment, not that reshaping.
Hint: Read the note about a name.
Explanation: Algorithm's history starts with a name. Its later Greek-looking spelling is a different story from building photosynthesis from Greek-derived parts.
Example title: **Read what a history note actually says**
Example steps: 1. One note may name a word's parts. 2. Another may describe a later spelling change. 3. Use the note's evidence; length alone cannot tell the history.

## Grown-up text and narration boundary

Grown-up summary: These are six visible subject-word cards, an authored glossary with three revisions and three supplied recognition jobs. The printed spellings, hints and clues are support. Piece boundaries are teaching choices, not a complete morphological or syllable analysis. Matching a card is not spelling from memory; choosing a supplied correction is not proofreading the child's own writing. No science investigation, pronunciation assessment or full spelling-topic coverage is observed. Invite a spoken explanation or an off-screen label if useful; neither is captured.

Meaning limit: These short meanings identify the intended sense. They do not explain a complete water cycle, photosynthesis mechanism, flood-protection design or algorithm theory. Permeable does not mean every amount of rain will soak in at any rate. The glossary and parcels are fictional; real flood conditions need real evidence.

Narration allowlist: arrival story; current instruction; each card's context sentence only; current F1/F2/F3 prompt only. Excluded: printed spelling-only labels; piece/choice lists; trials; clues and histories; teaching/examples; glossary alternatives/readbacks; feedback/check/keep summaries. Visible Hear buttons name only the allowed text. Browser voice quality is unqualified; no recording is made.
