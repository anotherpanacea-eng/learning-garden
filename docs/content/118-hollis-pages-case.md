# Keeper's Desk, Case 6: What did Hollis write?

Companion to [spec 118](../specs/118-keepers-desk-entry.md) (conventions, records and acceptance) and [spec 120](https://github.com/anotherpanacea-eng/learning-garden/blob/claude/project-thread-zpfie6/docs/specs/120-keeper-of-the-ways.md) (the Keeper's Pages and their three versions). Status: **proposed case text, 2026-10-23. Not reviewed, not build-ready.** Hollis and the pages are fiction.

Case ID `hollis-pages`. Appears when page 1 dries (end of Act I). Pages 2, 3 and 4 join the case as each one dries; page 5 is blank and page 6 is a clean letter, so neither is in it. Saved work on an earlier page is never changed when a later page joins.

**Ownership.** Spec 120 owns Hollis's pages and their wording. This case needs one thing spec 120 leaves open: what each `[smudged]` word was, and which letters survive. Section 3 proposes both; the story thread should confirm or replace them, and this case follows its answer.

## 1. Why this case

Spec 120 sets Hollis's original pages at the desk so an older learner can "transcribe, reconstruct missing words from context, mark what is uncertain and read Hollis's numbers". This case is that work. It builds on the Source Trail, which already asks where a claim came from, and adds the editor's discipline: write down exactly what is there, mark what you guessed and how, and notice when a retelling says more than the original did.

## 2. Hook

> The librarian lays a sheet of glass over the dried page so it can't curl.
>
> "Everyone in town has read the short version of this page. Pip has read it eleven times. But the short versions were written by us, from the original. Nobody has checked them against what Hollis actually wrote, letter by letter. That's the job. It's slow. It's the kind of slow I like."

## 3. The smudges (proposed for spec 120 to confirm)

Each smudge shows some surviving letters. Underscores are lost letters; the facsimile art draws a stain over them with the visible strokes left clear. The proposed hidden word is an authoring note. Replies discuss surviving evidence and uncertainty; they never use that note to certify the explorer's guess.

| Page | Line around the smudge | Surviving letters | Proposed word | What a reader can use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The Sponge holds the spring water and lets it go [__], so the mill boards are seldom needed. | `s _ _ _ l y` | slowly | Letter shape; the sense of "holds … lets it go"; page 3's "the water's ways" |
| 1 | Scale: one finger to a [__] walk. | `_ u _ _ _ _ _ - h _ _ _ ' s` | quarter-hour's | Hyphen and apostrophe; follows *a*, so it can't start with a vowel sound; the map runs from the high ford to the mill, and Vadoalto is a day's walk away, so a finger must stand for much less than a day |
| 2 | [__] the gaps grow shorter. | `_ _ _ _ l y` | Lately | Capital at line start; the ledger's own years, which do get closer |
| 3 | A town that closes them [__] finds out where the water goes instead. | none | always | Nothing but sense; this is the truly missing word |
| 4 | the apples this autumn are [__]. | `m a g _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _` | magnificent | Letter shape; the longer version's "best harvest anyone remembered" |
| 4 | [__] someone should ask them. | `P _ _ _ _ _ s` | Perhaps | Capital; length; Hollis's habit of hedging ("I admit", "Wet years, or our doing?") |

The page 2 smudge has at least two plausible readings. `Lately` means recently; `Surely` expresses confidence in the claim and need not predict the future. Both fit six letters ending in *-ly*. The ledger may support a trend in recorded intervals, but it cannot identify which of these words Hollis wrote. The proposed hidden word is an authoring note, never a key for grading an uncertain reconstruction.

## 4. Steps

1. **Exactly what's there** (careful transcription)
2. **Sure, guessed or missing?** (marking certainty)
3. **Filling a gap** (inference from evidence, and whether it changes the meaning)
4. **Did the retelling say more?** (original against retelling)
5. **The editor's note** (writing for the almanac)

Steps are open in any order only within an authored page pack. The checked transcription, guided lines and retelling below use page 4 and must not appear while only page 1 is available. Before commissioning this case, supply a finite per-page task/copy map for pages 1–3, or scope the first case release explicitly to the available open transcription/uncertainty/note work without those checked tasks. A page not reached is neither revealed nor credited. The editor-note route can keep a visibly supplied generic note without inventing a page 2 guess or earlier work.

## 5. Step 1: Exactly what's there

**Learn card:**

> A **transcription** copies a handwritten page into print, exactly. Not what it should say: what it says. Keep Hollis's spelling, Hollis's capitals and Hollis's punctuation. If a word is lost, don't fill it in yet. Write **[smudged]** in its place.
>
> Worked example. The page shows: *Year 22. The Sponge is drained and the brook cut straight past the new fields.*
> Transcription: `Year 22. The Sponge is drained and the brook cut straight past the new fields.`
> Not: `In year 22, the Sponge was drained and the brook was straightened.` That's a retelling.

**Guided first task (1a):** the first sentence of page 4, with the facsimile line and an empty field; a word bank of the line's words in order is shown unless folded.

Tasks:

- **1a.** Transcribe page 4's first two sentences. **Check my working** compares word by word with the authored transcription and highlights any word that differs, ignoring extra spaces. Answer:

  `Year 22. The Sponge is drained and the brook cut straight past the new fields. The orchard keepers are very pleased and so, I admit, am I: the apples this autumn are [smudged].`

- **1b.** "Bramble's transcription says *The orchard keepers were very pleased.* What's different, and does it matter?" Choices:
  - "She changed *are* to *were*, so it no longer sounds written at the time." ← fits the page
  - "Nothing that matters: *are* and *were* mean the same here."
  - "She made a spelling mistake."

  Reply to the second: "They both say the keepers were pleased. But *are* presents the event as current, while *were* looks back. Tense alone cannot date the writing: a later writer can use a historical present."

**I'm stuck (1a):**

1. *Hint:* "Copy one word at a time. When you reach the stain, write [smudged] and carry on."
2. *Similar example:* "The page shows *The reeds are [stain] this spring.* Transcription: `The reeds are [smudged] this spring.` Now page 4's first line."
3. **Skip for now.**

**Replies to likely errors:**

| Entered | Misconception | Reply |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A guessed word in place of [smudged] | Filling the gap during transcription | "Good guess, maybe. Step 3 is for guesses. For now, write what's actually on the page." |
| *were* for *are*, or *was* for *is* | Changing tense to sound like history | "Keep Hollis's words. They use the present tense, which changes the voice of the account. That alone does not prove when they wrote it." |
| Missing *I admit* | Leaving out words that seem unimportant | "Every word counts in a transcription. *I admit* tells us how Hollis felt." |

## 6. Step 2: Sure, guessed or missing?

**Learn card:**

> Editors mark three kinds of words:
>
> - **Sure:** you can read every letter.
> - **Guessed:** some letters survive, and the rest makes one word likely. Write the guess in square brackets with a question mark: `[slowly?]`
> - **Missing:** nothing survives. Leave `[smudged]`, or write your best idea in the margin, never in the line.
>
> Worked example: `s _ _ _ l y` with a page about holding water and letting it go. Six letters, *s* first, *ly* last. *slowly* fits all of it. That's a guess, a good one, so: `[slowly?]`.

**Guided first task (2a):** page 1's first smudge, with the letter boxes visible.

Tasks:

- **2a.** For each smudge on the chosen page, choose **sure**, **guessed** or **missing**. *Fitting choices:* all six smudges in section 3 are **guessed**, except page 3's, which is **missing**.
- **2b.** "Pip says he can read the page 3 word. It's *always*." Choices:
  - "There are no letters left. *Always* fits, but it's a guess with nothing to check it." ← fits the page
  - "Pip's right: *always* is the only word that makes sense."
  - "We should leave the whole sentence out."

  Reply to the second: "*Always* makes sense. So do *soon*, *one day* and *eventually*. Can the page tell us which?"

**I'm stuck (2a):**

1. *Hint:* "Can you see any letters at all? If not, it's missing."
2. *Similar example:* "`_ _ _ _` with no letters is missing. `c _ t` on a page about a cat is guessed. A clear *cat* is sure."
3. **Skip for now.**

## 7. Step 3: Filling a gap

**Learn card:**

> When you guess a lost word, say what the guess rests on. Evidence can come from:
>
> - **the letters** that survive,
> - **the sentence** around it,
> - **the rest of the page**,
> - **other pages** by the same writer.
>
> Then ask: if I'm wrong, does the meaning change? A guess stays labelled as a guess either way. If it changes the meaning, explain that uncertainty too.

**Guided first task (3a):** page 4's *the apples this autumn are [m a g _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _]*, with the evidence types as tick boxes.

Tasks:

- **3a.** Page 4, *the apples this autumn are m a g _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _*. Choose a word and the evidence it rests on. *Fitting word: magnificent* (11 letters; *mag* survives; the longer version calls it the best harvest anyone remembered). Other 11-letter *mag* words are offered so the letter count does the work.
- **3b.** Page 2, *_ _ _ _ l y the gaps grow shorter.* Two candidates fit the letters: **Lately** and **Surely**. "What does each one make Hollis say?" Matching task, with these sentences to place:
  - *Lately:* "Hollis has seen the gaps between floods getting shorter."
  - *Surely:* "Hollis expresses confidence that the gaps grow shorter; that can describe the same recorded trend."
- **3c.** "Can the ledger tell us which word was written?" The explorer can open page 2's table. The intervals are **11, 12, 4, 3, 2, 2 years**: the later ones are shorter, though not every consecutive interval shrinks. Reply: "Both readings can fit that pattern. The ledger cannot choose the missing word. Keep a guess with a question mark, or leave [smudged]." A mechanical interval check may check those six differences; the word guess is ungraded.
- **3d.** "Does the guess change what the page means?" For each smudge, choose **No**, **A little** or **Yes**. Authored replies; none is marked wrong. The reply for page 2's smudge notes that it's the one where the guess matters most.

**I'm stuck (3c):**

1. *Hint:* "Work out how many years between each flood and the next."
2. *Similar example:* "Floods in years 2, 10, 14 and 16: gaps of 8, 4 and 2. They're getting shorter. Now try 4, 15, 27, 31, 34, 36 and 38."
3. **Skip for now.**

**Replies to likely errors (3c):**

| Entered | Misconception | Reply |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gaps of 15, 12, 4, 3, 2, 2 | Counting the first gap from year 0 | "The first gap is between the first flood and the second, years 4 and 15." |
| "Surely, because Hollis was sure" | Confidence about an uncertain word | "That is one possible reading. Confidence is not surviving ink; mark it as your guess." |

## 8. Step 4: Did the retelling say more?

**Question panel:** page 4's **Longer** version (spec 120) beside the explorer's transcription.

**Learn card:**

> A retelling is written by someone who has read the original. It can be clear and kind and still add things the original never said.
>
> Worked example. Original: *The orchard keepers are very pleased.* Retelling: *The orchard keepers were delighted, and threw a party.* The pleased part is in the original. The party isn't.

Tasks:

- **4a.** Sort each sentence from the Longer version into **In Hollis's page**, **Added by the retelling** or **Changed by the retelling**. *Fitting sorts:*
  - "In their twenty-second year of keeping, the town drained the Sponge to make new orchard fields": **in the page**, except *to make new orchard fields*, which is **added** (the page says *past the new fields*, which implies it but doesn't say why).
  - "and straightened the brook so it would run past them quickly": **added** (*so it would run past them quickly* is a reason Hollis never gave).
  - "That autumn the orchard had the best harvest anyone remembered": **changed** (Hollis wrote only that the apples were *[smudged]*, probably *magnificent*; *anyone remembered* is new).
  - "Five years later, the brook came 120 centimeters up the flood post": **in the page** (*Year 27. The brook to 120 at the post*).
- **4b.** "Hollis wrote *Wet years, or our doing?* Did the retelling keep that question?" Choices:
  - "No. The retelling leaves Hollis's doubt out, and Pip jumps to a cause." ← fits the pages
  - "Yes. It's the same story, just longer."
  - "It doesn't matter, because the retelling is for younger readers."

  Reply to the third: "Shorter versions have to leave things out. The question is whether what's left out changes what the reader will believe."

**I'm stuck (4a):**

1. *Hint:* "For each sentence, find the words in Hollis's page that say the same. If you can't find them, it was added."
2. *Similar example:* the pleased-and-party example from the learn card, sorted.
3. **Skip for now.**

## 9. Step 5: The editor's note

**Question panel:**

> The almanac will print your transcription beside Hollis's page, so everyone can see both. The librarian asks for a short **editor's note** to go at the top.

Three parts, each with **Write it yourself** (free text) and **Choose and adapt**:

1. **How you marked the page.** Supplied: "Words in square brackets with a question mark are my guesses. [smudged] means nothing could be read."
2. **The guess that matters most.** Supplied, only after page 2 is available: "On page 2, Lately and Surely both fit the remaining letters. The ledger does not tell me which word was written." Otherwise: "I have left an unreadable word as [smudged], rather than invent it."
3. **One thing a reader should know.** Supplied: "The short versions of these pages were written later, by the town. They sometimes say more than Hollis did."

**Keep this note** saves it. **Print it in the almanac** shows the page with the note.

**The librarian's response (the same for every note):**

> The librarian reads your note, then lifts the glass and looks at the stain for a while. "It goes in the almanac next to Hollis's page. Anyone who reads it can look from one to the other and judge for themselves. If a clearer copy ever turns up, we'll look again."

**Story link:** when Hollis returns in Act VI, an optional line can appear if this case was kept. Hollis never says which word they wrote, because that would answer the case. Hollis looks at the transcription for a long time and asks: "Which did you think it said?" Whatever the explorer kept, Hollis acknowledges the effort without validating the guessed text: "Thank you for sitting with those pages. The water has kept some words to itself." (Sent to spec 120 for placement in the "Someone comes home" scene.)

**Supported route:** Choose and adapt alone completes Step 5; steps 1 to 4 can stay open.
