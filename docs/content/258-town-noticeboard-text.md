# The Town Noticeboard: all child-facing text

Companion to [spec 258](../specs/258-town-noticeboard.md). Use as written, after review. `{guide}` is never gendered. Pronouns: Pip, the baker, Hob he/him; Dot, Bramble, Wren Halloway, the orchard keeper, the miller she/her; the librarian they/them; Puff takes no pronouns. Sources: [audit](../source-audits/258-town-noticeboard.md). Nothing is scored, timed or locked.

**Writing rule:** the child's own writing is spoken or on paper and read by a person. Nothing the child writes or says is typed, recorded, stored or graded. On screen the child works only with supplied notices, recounts, letters and cards. **Opinion rule:** no opinion is ever marked right or wrong. Items key only structure: whether a reason fits an opinion when the card's facts decide it, the order of timed events, a linking word whose job the card states, a closing line, a stated purpose.

**Labels used throughout:**
- **A story from Willowbank** marks the town, its people, their notices and their cards (fiction).
- **A true thing about language** marks how writing works.
- **A true thing about living things** marks the two real facts on the spring question cards (from pack 161's audit).

**No Hollis, no pages:** nothing here names the old Keeper, the parcel or a page.

## Story scenes

### Scene 1. The board comes back (Act II, the weekly market)

**Hook (A story from Willowbank):** The market is back. Dot hangs the old noticeboard by the post office door. Pip pins up his first notice. It says SOUP.

**Short:**
> Pip's notice just says SOUP. Dot asks what he thinks about soup, and why. Pip says it out loud first. Then he writes it.

**Longer:**
> Dot taps in the last pin. The noticeboard hangs by the post office door, facing the market.
>
> Pip pins up a card. It says SOUP.
>
> "Soup what?" says Dot.
>
> "Soup good," says Pip.
>
> "Say the whole thing out loud first," says {guide}. "What do you think?"
>
> Pip takes a breath. "I think the market should have a soup stall."
>
> "Why?"
>
> "Because mornings here are cold, and soup is hot." He thinks. "And the bakery door is blue."
>
> Dot looks at the bakery door. It is blue. "Is that a reason for soup?"
>
> "No," Pip admits. "That's a reason for paint."
>
> He writes the rest. The notice is longer now. It still says SOUP at the top, very large.

### Scene 2. Two notices, one board (Act II, the weekly market)

**Hook:** Bramble reads Pip's soup notice. Then she pins her own notice right beside it. Hers says no soup.

**Short:**
> Bramble thinks the market should not sell soup. Both notices stay up. Hers has four parts. Pip's just stops, so he adds an ending.

**Longer:**
> Bramble reads Pip's notice twice. Then she pins hers right beside it.
>
> *This notice is about soup. I think the market should not sell soup. That's because soup spills, and spills ruin ribbons. So no soup, please.*
>
> "She's wrong," says Pip.
>
> "She thinks differently," says Dot. "That's what a noticeboard is for. Both stay up."
>
> Pip reads Bramble's notice again, slowly. "Hers has an ending. Mine just stops."
>
> "Hers says what it's about, what she thinks, why, and then says it again," says {guide}.
>
> "Four parts," says Pip, counting. He adds a last line to his: *So let's have soup!*
>
> Bramble adds a line to hers: *Or keep the soup far from the ribbons.*
>
> Dot straightens both notices. "Two opinions," she says, "and one good idea between them."

**Keeper's note (A true thing about language):**
> A notice like this is an opinion piece. The Common Core writing standards, used in many US schools, ask young writers for these parts: say the topic, give an opinion, give reasons, link them with words like *because* and *also*, and end with a closing line.

### Scene 3. Say it before you paint it (Act II, the weekly market)

**Hook:** The baker has one board and one pot of paint. He wants a sign for his buns. Paint is hard to take back.

**Short:**
> The baker says his sign three ways out loud before he paints. The one he picks tells people when the buns are ready.

**Longer:**
> The baker stares at a blank board, brush ready.
>
> "Say it first," says Dot. "Paint is hard to unsay."
>
> The baker tries it out loud. "Buns."
>
> "Buns what?" says Pip, hopefully.
>
> "Fresh buns, warm from the oven."
>
> "When?" asks {guide}. "People will want to know when."
>
> The baker tries again. "At ten o'clock, fresh buns come out of the oven."
>
> He says all three again, listening. Then he paints the one that says when: *At ten o'clock, fresh buns come out of the oven.*
>
> At ten, he opens the door. "Fresh buns!" he calls.
>
> Pip writes in his notebook: *"Fresh buns!" called the baker.*
>
> "Your exact words," Pip explains. "I'm keeping them."

**Keeper's note (A true thing about language):**
> England's national curriculum asks writers of about five or six to say a sentence out loud before writing it. A year or two later, they rehearse sentences aloud, including what people say, and try out new words and ways to build a sentence.

### Scene 4. Read it out (Act II, the weekly market)

**Hook:** At noon Dot rings the bell. Anyone who wrote a notice can read it out loud. Puff can't see the top row.

**Short:**
> Pip reads his notice into his scarf. Nobody at the back can hear. He looks up, reads clearly, and pauses at each period.

**Longer:**
> At noon Dot rings the bell. "Notices, read out loud! Not everyone can see the top row."
>
> Puff, at the front, nods hard.
>
> Pip goes first. He holds his notice up to his nose and reads it into his scarf.
>
> "Mmf mmf soup mmf."
>
> "What?" calls Hob from the back.
>
> "Look up," says {guide}. "Read to Hob, not to the paper."
>
> Pip lowers the notice. He reads it again, loud and slow, with a small pause at each period.
>
> "I think the market should have a soup stall."
>
> At the back, Hob nods. "Heard every word. Still not sure about soup."
>
> "That's allowed," says Dot.

### Scene 5. What happened on market day (Act II ch. 2, the town starts keeping records)

**Hook:** Wren Halloway keeps the town's records. She wants the first market day written down, so next time we'll know. Pip offers. He starts at the end.

**Short:**
> Pip tells the market day backward. Wren asks what happened first. The times on Dot's stall list put the day in order.

**Longer:**
> Wren Halloway opens a fresh page. "The first market day, for the town's records. So next time we'll know."
>
> "Easy," says Pip. "We packed up. The soup ran out. The bell rang. The stalls came."
>
> Wren's pen waits. "In that order?"
>
> "It all happened," says Pip.
>
> "It did," says {guide}. "But a recount goes in the order things happened. What came first?"
>
> Dot brings her stall list. Every line has a time. *Seven: stalls arrive. Nine: bell rings. Noon: soup runs out. Five: pack up.*
>
> Pip starts again. "First, at seven, the stalls arrived. Then, at nine, the bell rang."
>
> Wren writes it down, line by line.
>
> Later, Pip writes the same day as a letter to Ember, who missed it. "Same day," he says. "Different job."

### Scene 6. The spring question (Act II ch. 2, the town starts keeping records)

**Hook:** Wren pins a question card on the noticeboard: When do the valley's young animals arrive? Anyone can bring a card that helps.

**Short:**
> The town shares one question. Hob, the orchard keeper and the librarian bring cards. Pip brings a guess. Not every card answers the question.

**Longer:**
> Wren pins a card at the top of the board: *When do the valley's young animals arrive? So next spring, we'll be ready.*
>
> By evening, the board is full of cards.
>
> Hob's says: *My lambs come in spring.*
>
> The orchard keeper's says: *My hens sit on their eggs in spring.*
>
> The librarian pins a page from the bird book: *A hen's egg, kept warm, hatches after about 21 days.*
>
> The miller's says: *My cat sleeps by the oven.*
>
> "True," says Dot, "but does it answer the question?"
>
> The miller thinks. "It answers where the cat is."
>
> Pip's card says: *I think ducklings hatch in March.*
>
> "Did you check?" asks Wren.
>
> "Not yet."
>
> Wren moves it to a new corner labeled STILL TO CHECK. "Not wrong. Not checked."

**Keeper's note (A true thing about living things):**
> A hen's egg needs warmth for about 21 days before the chick hatches. A hen gives that warmth by sitting on her eggs. Farmers can use a warm box called an incubator instead.

## Teach cards

Each card is 30 words or fewer; worked-example steps appear one tap at a time. Cards are **A true thing about language**.

**Card 1. An opinion says what you think**
> An opinion tells what someone thinks or likes. Other people may think something different. "I think" often starts one.

Worked example: *Pip's notice.*
1. Pip's card says: SOUP.
2. What does he think about soup?
3. "I think the market should have a soup stall." Now it is an opinion.

**Card 2. A reason says why**
> A reason tells why you think it. The word *because* can join your opinion to your reason.

Worked example: *Pip says why.*
1. Opinion: The market should have a soup stall.
2. Why? Mornings are cold, and soup is hot.
3. Join them: "...a soup stall because mornings are cold."

**Card 3. A reason has to fit**
> A reason fits when it is about your opinion and helps it. A true sentence about something else is not a reason for this opinion.

Worked example: *The blue door.*
1. Opinion: The market should have a soup stall.
2. "The bakery door is blue." True, but about paint.
3. "Soup is hot on cold mornings." About soup, and it helps.

**Card 4. Two opinions can both go up**
> People can think different things about the same question. Each opinion needs its own reasons. The board has room for both.

Worked example: *Pip and Bramble.*
1. Pip: soup, because mornings are cold.
2. Bramble: no soup, because spills ruin ribbons.
3. Both reasons fit. Both notices stay up.

**Card 5. An opinion notice has four parts**
> Say what it's about. Say what you think. Say why. Then end by saying your opinion again.

Worked example: *Bramble's notice.*
1. About: "This notice is about soup."
2. Think: "I think the market should not sell soup."
3. Why: "That's because soup spills."
4. End: "So no soup, please."

**Card 6. Linking words**
> *Because* joins an opinion to its reason. *Also* adds another reason. *For example* shows one case. Each word has its own job.

Worked example: *Dot's roof notice.*
1. The board needs a roof *because* rain makes ink run.
2. *Also*, the wind tears the paper.
3. *For example*, rain made Dot's hat poster run last Tuesday.

**Card 7. A recount tells what happened, in order**
> A recount tells real events in the order they happened. Time words like *first*, *then*, *after that* and *at last* show the order.

Worked example: *Dot's stall list.*
1. Seven: stalls arrive. Nine: bell rings.
2. Seven comes before nine, so stalls come first.
3. "First the stalls arrived. Then the bell rang."

**Card 8. Writing has jobs**
> A recount tells what happened. A notice tells everyone what you think or want. A letter tells one reader, by name, your news.

Worked example: *One market day, two jobs.*
1. Wren's record: what happened, in order. A recount.
2. Pip's letter starts "Dear Ember". One reader.
3. Same day, different jobs.

**Card 9. Say it before you write it**
> Say your sentence out loud before you write it. Try it two or three ways. Keep the one that does the job.

Worked example: *The baker's sign.*
1. Job: tell people when buns are ready.
2. "Buns." "Fresh buns, warm from the oven." "At ten o'clock, fresh buns come out."
3. Only the last says when.

**Card 10. Ways to start**
> A sentence can start by telling when, like "At ten" or "When the bell rings". Then comes the main part. Writers often put a comma after the when part.

Worked example: *Two starts, one idea.*
1. "The buns come out at ten."
2. Move the when to the front: "At ten, the buns come out."
3. Same idea, a different start.

**Card 11. Words someone says**
> Quotation marks hold the exact words someone says. Words outside them can tell who spoke.

Worked example: *"Fresh buns!" called the baker.*
1. Inside the marks: Fresh buns!
2. Outside: called the baker.
3. Say it in the baker's voice: "Fresh buns!"

**Card 12. Read it out**
> When you read your writing aloud, look up, speak loud and clear, and pause a little at each period.

Worked example: *Pip and his scarf.*
1. Into the scarf: nobody hears.
2. Look up at the listener at the back.
3. Read slowly. Pause at each period.

**Card 13. One question, many helpers**
> A shared question gets cards from many people. Ask of each card: does it help answer? Who could tell us more? Has it been checked?

Worked example: *Wren's spring question.*
1. "My lambs come in spring." Helps.
2. "My cat sleeps by the oven." True, but it doesn't help.
3. "I think ducklings hatch in March." Someone could check it, but nobody has. Still to check.

### (a) Pin the Reasons (Sprout, Seedling)

**Header:** Tap each reason that fits. It gets pinned under the notice.

Tiles: Mornings at the market are cold. ✔ · The bakery door is blue. · Hot soup warms you up. ✔ · Soup spills on ribbons.

**Tile feedback:**
- About something else (the door, the bell): True, but it's not about soup at this market.
- A reason for the other side: That reason helps the other notice. Try it there.

**Dot's roof notice strips:** This notice is about our noticeboard. · I think the board needs a roof. · That's because rain makes the ink run. · So let's give our board a roof!

**Slot feedback:**
- An ending strip in the first slot: That line starts with "So". It wraps things up, so it goes last.
- A "That's because" strip before the opinion: A reason needs an opinion before it. What is it a reason for?

**Linking words:** Pick the word that does the job. *(Items B2 to B4, same choices in the same order.)*

**End it:** Pick the line that ends Dot's notice. *(Item B5.)*

### (c) What Happened First? (Seedling)

**Header:** Put Pip's market day in order. Use the times.

**Strips:** At nine, the bell rang and the market opened. · At five, we packed up and went home. · At seven, the stalls arrived. · At noon, the soup ran out.

**Time words:** These four lines are in order. Put a time word on each.
- Lines: 1. ___ we set out the tables. 2. ___ we hung the flags. 3. ___ we filled the soup pot. 4. ___ the bell rang.
- Tiles: First · Then · After that · At last
- *First* fits line 1 and *At last* fits line 4. *Then* and *After that* both fit lines 2 and 3, in either order.
- Feedback: *At last* early: "At last" means the very end. More happens after this line. · *First* later: Only one thing can come first.

**What is it for?** Match each piece to its job.
- "Buy our buns! They are warm." → tells everyone what you want
- "On Saturday the market came back. First the stalls arrived." → tells what happened
- "Dear Ember, The market came back today!" → tells one reader your news

**On paper:** Write what happened at the Willowbank market, in order. Keep going as long as you like.

## Problem sets

Every item shows its own card, with read-aloud. The first item in each set shows its steps. Opinions are never keyed.

### Set A. Opinions and reasons (Sprout, Seedling)

**A1 (3-choice, guided).** Steps shown first: 1. Read what Pip thinks. 2. Is the choice about soup at the market? 3. Does it help Pip's side?
- Card: Pip's notice: *I think the market should have a soup stall.*
- Prompt: Which reason fits Pip's notice?
- Choices: The bakery door is painted blue. (`off-topic-reason`) · Hot soup helps on cold mornings. ✔ · Soup spills and spoils Bramble's ribbons. (`reason-for-other-side`)
- Feedback: Door: True, but it's about paint, not soup. · Spills: That's a reason against soup. It fits Bramble's notice.
- Hint: Does it help say why the market needs soup?
- Explanation: A reason fits when it is about the opinion and helps it. Hot soup on cold mornings does both.

**A2 (3-choice).**
- Card: Bramble's notice: *I think the market should not sell soup.* Bramble sells ribbons.
- Prompt: Which reason fits Bramble's notice?
- Choices: Spilled soup could ruin the ribbons. ✔ · Hot soup warms people on cold mornings. (`reason-for-other-side`) · The bell rings at nine every market day. (`off-topic-reason`)
- Feedback: Hot soup: That helps Pip's side, not Bramble's. · Bell: True, but it says nothing about soup.
- Hint: Bramble is against soup. Find a reason against it.
- Explanation: Bramble's reason fits her opinion, and Pip's fits his. Both reasons can be true at once.

**A3 (3-choice).**
- Card: Three sentences for a market notice.
- Prompt: Which sentence is an opinion?
- Choices: The market opens at nine o'clock. (`checkable-as-opinion`) · There are twelve stalls at the market. (`checkable-as-opinion`) · Market day is the best day. ✔
- Feedback: Nine o'clock: You can check that on the clock. · Twelve stalls: You can count the stalls to check.
- Hint: An opinion says what someone thinks. Can you check it?
- Explanation: "Best" is what someone thinks. Someone else may think another day is best.

**A4 (3-choice).**
- Card: Dot's notice: (1) The Saturday market is back. (2) I think everyone should come. (3) There is music by the well.
- Prompt: Which sentence says what Dot thinks?
- Choices: Sentence 1 (`topic-as-opinion`) · Sentence 2 ✔ · Sentence 3 (`reason-as-opinion`)
- Feedback: Sentence 1: That tells what the notice is about. · Sentence 3: That's a reason to come. It tells why.
- Hint: Look for "I think".
- Explanation: "I think everyone should come" is Dot's opinion. The music is one reason to come.

**A5 (say it, to a grown-up; never graded, nothing stored).**
- Prompt: Pick a town question. Tell a grown-up what you think, and why.
- Topic cards: Should the noticeboard get a roof? · Should the market have a soup stall? · Should the bell ring earlier?
- Grown-up guide: Any opinion is fine. Listen for what they think and a reason. If there is no reason, ask "Why?" once.

### Set B. Building a notice (Seedling, Sapling)

**B1 (order, guided).** Steps shown first: 1. Find the line that says what it's about. 2. Find "I think". 3. The line starting "So" goes last.
- Card: Four strips of Dot's notice, mixed up: That's because rain makes the ink run. · So let's give our board a roof! · This notice is about our noticeboard. · I think the board needs a roof.
- Prompt: Put Dot's notice in order.
- Answer: This notice is about our noticeboard. → I think the board needs a roof. → That's because rain makes the ink run. → So let's give our board a roof!
- Hint: What it's about, what she thinks, why, and the end.
- Explanation: About, think, why, end. The reason comes after the opinion it supports.

**B2 (3-choice).**
- Card: Dot's sentence: *The board needs a roof ___ rain makes the ink run.* The missing word joins the opinion to its reason.
- Prompt: Which word joins Dot's opinion to her reason?
- Choices: although (`contrast-for-reason`) · because ✔ · after (`time-for-reason`)
- Feedback: Although: That sets up a surprise. Rain is Dot's reason, not a surprise. · After: That tells when. Dot is giving a reason why.
- Hint: Which word means "and here's why"?
- Explanation: *Because* joins an opinion to its reason.

**B3 (3-choice).**
- Card: *Rain makes the ink run. ___, the wind tears the paper.* What changes: the next sentence adds a second reason.
- Prompt: Which word adds Dot's second reason?
- Choices: Also ✔ · Instead (`replace-for-addition`) · So (`result-for-addition`)
- Feedback: Instead: That swaps one thing for another. Dot keeps both reasons. · So: The wind doesn't come from the ink. It's a new reason.
- Hint: The card says the sentence adds a reason.
- Explanation: *Also* adds one more reason to the first.

**B4 (3-choice).**
- Card: *Rain spoils notices. ___, Dot's hat poster ran last Tuesday.* What changes: the next sentence gives one case of it happening.
- Prompt: Which words show one case of rain spoiling a notice?
- Choices: On the other hand (`contrast-for-example`) · For example ✔ · Instead (`replace-for-example`)
- Feedback: On the other hand: That brings in the other side. The poster backs Dot up. · Instead: Nothing is being swapped.
- Hint: The card says the sentence gives one case.
- Explanation: *For example* shows one real case. Dot's poster is a case of rain spoiling a notice.

**B5 (3-choice).**
- Card: Dot's notice so far: *This notice is about our noticeboard. I think the board needs a roof. That's because rain makes the ink run.*
- Prompt: Which line ends Dot's notice?
- Choices: So let's give our board a roof! ✔ · The baker sells cinnamon buns on Fridays. (`new-topic-ending`) · Some notices are written in green ink. (`detail-ending`)
- Feedback: Buns: That starts a new topic at the very end. · Green ink: A detail. It doesn't say Dot's opinion again.
- Hint: A good ending says the opinion again.
- Explanation: The last line reminds readers what Dot thinks: the board needs a roof.

### Set C. Recounts and letters (Seedling)

**C1 (order, guided).** Steps shown first: 1. Find the earliest time. 2. Seven, nine, noon, five. 3. Put each strip by its time.
- Card: Pip's strips, mixed up: At nine, the bell rang and the market opened. · At five, we packed up and went home. · At seven, the stalls arrived. · At noon, the soup ran out.
- Prompt: Put Pip's market day in order.
- Answer: At seven → At nine → At noon → At five.
- Hint: The market day starts in the morning and ends in the evening.
- Explanation: Seven and nine are morning. Noon is midday. Five comes after noon. A recount follows the clock.

**C2 (3-choice).**
- Card: Three lines in order: 1. *First, we set out the tables.* 2. *___ we hung the flags.* 3. *At last, the bell rang.*
- Prompt: Which time word fits line 2?
- Choices: First (`start-word-twice`) · At last (`end-word-in-middle`) · Then ✔
- Feedback: First: Line 1 already came first. · At last: That means the very end, but line 3 comes after.
- Hint: Line 2 comes after line 1 and before line 3.
- Explanation: *Then* means next. It fits a step in the middle.

**C3 (3-choice).**
- Card: Three pieces from the board. Piece A: *Buy our buns! They are warm.* Piece B: *On Saturday the market came back. First the stalls arrived.* Piece C: *1. Take a ticket. 2. Wait for your number.*
- Prompt: Which piece tells what happened?
- Choices: Piece A (`notice-for-recount`) · Piece B ✔ · Piece C (`steps-for-recount`)
- Feedback: Piece A: That asks people to buy. It tells what someone wants. · Piece C: Those are steps telling you what to do.
- Hint: Which one tells about something that already happened?
- Explanation: Piece B is a recount. It tells real events in order, with *first*.

**C4 (3-choice).**
- Card: Pip's letter: *Dear Ember, The market came back today! First the stalls arrived. Then the bell rang. I saved you some soup. From Pip*
- Prompt: Who is Pip's letter for?
- Choices: Ember ✔ · Everyone at the market (`letter-for-everyone`) · Pip himself (`writer-for-reader`)
- Feedback: Everyone: A notice is for everyone. "Dear Ember" names one reader. · Pip: Pip wrote it. His name is at the end.
- Hint: Look at the first two words.

## "I'm stuck" ladder

**Step 1, hint:** the item's own hint.

**Step 2, an easier case and how to start:**
- Reason items: "Try this one first: 'I think we need a fire, because it's cold.' Cold says why. Now check yours."
- Notice items: "Try this one first: about, think, why, end. Count four parts on your fingers. Now find each one."
- Linking items: "Try this one first: 'I like buns because they're warm.' *Because* gives the why. Now read your card's job."
- Recount items: "Try this one first: breakfast, lunch, supper. Morning to evening. Now look at your times."
- Say-it items: "Try this one first: say 'At noon, we eat.' Hear the when first? Now say yours."
- Read-it-out items: "Try this one first: whisper 'soup', then say it clearly. Which could Hob hear? Now choose."
- Question items: "Try this one first: the question is 'when?' A card with a time helps. Now look at yours."

**Step 3:** Skip for now. You can come back any time.

## Key terms (glossary)

- **Opinion:** what someone thinks or likes. Other people may think differently.
- **Reason:** why someone thinks it.
- **Notice:** writing pinned up for everyone to read.
- **Linking word:** a word that joins ideas, like *because*, *also* or *for example*.
- **Recount:** writing that tells real events in the order they happened.
- **Time word:** a word that shows order, like *first*, *then* or *at last*.
- **Letter:** writing for one reader, who is named at the start.
- **Period:** the dot at the end of a sentence.
- **Quotation marks:** the marks that hold someone's exact spoken words.
- **Publish:** put your writing where its readers will see it.
- **Research:** finding things out to answer a question.
- **Checked:** someone has found out whether it is true.

## Off-screen cards (adult-led)

**Card 1 (marked: with a grown-up):** Pick a town question from the board. Say what you think and one reason. Then write it on paper as a notice with four parts. Read it aloud: look up, and pause at each period. Pin it up where its readers will see it, if you like.

## Grown-up panel (details)

**What this place teaches:** saying and then writing an opinion with reasons; the four parts of an opinion piece and the linking words *because*, *also* and *for example*; recounts in time order and writing for different jobs (recount, notice, letter); saying sentences aloud before writing them, including a fronted time opener and spoken words; reading writing aloud clearly; and taking part in a shared research question.

**What is never kept:** the child's opinions, spoken words, paper writing and read-alouds. The screen keeps only which supplied choices were picked on keyed items, as codes. Opinions are never marked right or wrong; a choice is keyed only when the card's facts decide it.

**Terms:** this pack uses US terms. In England's curriculum a period is a *full stop*, and quotation marks are *inverted commas* or *speech marks*. A "when" opener such as "At ten," is a *fronted adverbial* in England's grammar terms.

**Real events:** the on-screen recounts are Willowbank's. If you choose a real event for Card 2, nothing about it is entered or kept.
