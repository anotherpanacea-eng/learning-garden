# Listening at the Meeting: all child-facing text

Companion to [spec 275](../specs/275-listening-at-the-meeting.md). Use as written, after review. `{guide}` is never gendered. Pronouns: Pip, the baker, Hob he/him; Dot, Bramble, the miller, the orchard keeper, Wren Halloway, Nuria she/her; the librarian they/them. Sources: [audit](../source-audits/275-listening-at-the-meeting.md). Nothing is scored, timed or locked.

**Speaking rule:** nothing the child says is recorded. Every "say it" task is spoken to a grown-up or a sibling, and the device keeps no trace of it. Every speech below is authored audio played to the child, with its words on screen.

**Fairness rule:** no line in this pack says which plan for the low fields is best. Every plan speech states its own cost.

**Labels used throughout:**
- **A story from Willowbank** marks the town, its people and their speeches (fiction).
- **A true thing about language** marks how claims, reasons, paraphrase, summary and picture shows work.
- **A made-up model** marks counts inside speeches (eleven people on flour sacks, nine in the parcel line) and Nuria's rain table.

## Arriving

**Place title:** Listening at the Meeting

**First visit** (A story from Willowbank):
> At the back of the library there's a corner with a bench, a speaker's stand and a basket of speech cards. Each card holds a speech. Tap one and the speaker says it again.
>
> "People say things fast at meetings," says {guide}. "Here you can hear them twice."

**Two sets on the shelf:**
- **The New Market Bench** (open from the start): three neighbors, three places, one bench.
- **The Low Fields** (opens on the shelf once page 4 has come free in your town): the town meeting about the three plans.

**Every listening screen (always shown):** Hear it again · Show the words · Hear one sentence

**If sound is off:** The words are on screen. You can read them, or ask a grown-up to read them aloud.

## Story scenes

### Scene 1. The new market bench (Act II, the weekly market; also standalone)

**Hook (A story from Willowbank):** Willowbank has wood for one new bench at the market. Three neighbors want it in three different places.

**Short:**
> There is wood for one bench. The baker, Dot and the orchard keeper each want it somewhere else. Wren asks everyone to listen first.

**Longer:**
> The market stalls are back, and so is the old problem: nowhere to sit.
>
> "I have wood for one bench," says Wren Halloway, tapping her ledger. "One. Not three."
>
> "By the bakery," says the baker.
>
> "Beside the post office," says Dot.
>
> "Under the willow," says the orchard keeper.
>
> Pip suggests three very small benches. Nobody takes this seriously.
>
> "Then each of you gets a turn to speak," says Wren. "And everyone else listens to the end before they answer. That's the rule."
>
> Pip sits on a flour sack. The flour sack sighs.

### Scene 2. The meeting about the low fields (Act IV, chapter 4)

**Hook (A story from Willowbank):** The bell rings. The library fills. Three plans are on the board, and everyone has something to say.

**Short:**
> Five people speak at the meeting. Each plan has a cost. Your job tonight is to listen well, and hear each speaker to the end.

**Longer:**
> The whole village squeezes into the library. Wren Halloway stands by the board with the three plans on it, drawn in the librarian's best pen.
>
> "Everyone may speak," she says. "Nobody interrupts. Nobody has to agree tonight."
>
> The orchard keeper speaks first, about her trees. Then the miller, about her mill. Nuria shows pictures of Vadoalto's reed bed and turns pink when everyone listens. The librarian speaks for the Sponge.
>
> Then Bramble stands up, and the room goes quiet, because Bramble is not usually serious.
>
> {guide} leans over. "Listen for what each one wants," they whisper. "Then listen for why."

**Keeper's note (A true thing about language):**
> A speaker's claim is what they want you to think or do. Reasons say why. Evidence shows a reason is true. Good listeners keep the three apart, even when they like the speaker.

### Scene 3. Pip missed the middle (Act IV, Keeper's Desk)

**Hook (A story from Willowbank):** Pip fell asleep on the library cushions halfway through the meeting. Now he wants to know what everyone said.

**Short:**
> Pip slept through half the meeting. You make him a short picture show, so he hears every plan, fairly.

**Longer:**
> Pip wakes up on the library cushions with a crumb on his nose and the meeting over.
>
> "Did I miss it?" he says. "Who won?"
>
> "Nobody won," says Dot. "It was a meeting."
>
> "Then what did they say?"
>
> {guide} hands you a stack of picture cards and caption cards from the librarian's desk. "Make him a short show," they say. "Three plans. Every cost. No sneaking in your favorite."
>
> Pip settles in to watch, looking very awake now.

## Speech scripts (authored audio; words always on screen)

Speech scripts are passages, not prompts. Sentences are short so that **Hear one sentence** works. Numbers in brackets are sentence ids used by the benches.

### The New Market Bench (no pages, no story knowledge needed)

**M0. Wren opens** (A story from Willowbank):
> [1] Thank you for coming. [2] We have wood for one new bench at the market. [3] Three neighbors want it in three places. [4] Each will speak. [5] Listen for what they want and why. [6] Then we'll talk.

**M1. The baker** (A story from Willowbank; the count is a made-up model):
> [1] Put the new bench by the bakery door. [2] Here's why. [3] People buy warm bread and want to eat it right away, but there's nowhere to sit. [4] Last market day I counted eleven people sitting on my flour sacks. [5] The sacks did not enjoy it. [6] Older neighbors wait in my morning line, and standing a long time is hard for some of them. [7] I'll be honest about the problem. [8] The morning line is crowded, and a bench might squeeze the path. [9] Also, my cinnamon buns are the best in the valley.

**M2. Dot** (A story from Willowbank; the count is a made-up model):
> [1] I think the bench should go beside the post office. [2] On market day, the parcel line goes out the door. [3] At noon last week I counted nine people waiting, and some had heavy parcels. [4] People like to open their letters right away, and it's hard to read a letter standing in a crowd. [5] And benches look nice by blue doors. [6] Our door is blue. [7] Here's the problem. [8] The post office is at the quiet end of the market. [9] Fewer people walk past, so the bench might sit empty in the afternoon.

**M3. The orchard keeper** (A story from Willowbank):
> [1] I'd put the bench under the big willow on the green. [2] Market days in summer are hot, and the willow gives the only shade on the green. [3] Last summer, by noon, every apple seller had moved their crates into that shade. [4] From under the willow you can see every stall, so it's a good place to wait for someone. [5] There is a problem, and I won't hide it. [6] After rain, the grass under the willow turns to mud. [7] We'd need flat stones under the bench legs, and that's more work.

**M4. Wren closes** (A story from Willowbank):
> [1] Thank you, all three. [2] We heard three places, three sets of reasons and three honest problems. [3] Now it's your turn to talk it over.

### The Low Fields (opens after page 4 comes free; Act IV)

**W1. Wren opens** (A story from Willowbank):
> [1] Welcome, everyone. [2] Tonight we talk about the low fields. [3] Three plans are on the board: the Wall, the Sponge, and Some of Each. [4] Every plan costs something. [5] We will listen to each speaker to the end. [6] Nobody has to agree tonight.

**S1. The orchard keeper: what each plan means for her** (A story from Willowbank):
> [1] My family has farmed the low fields for three generations. [2] Those trees are older than I am. [3] Lately the bottom corner stays wet all year, and reeds grow there that nobody planted. [4] I've read Hollis's map. [5] I know there was marsh there once. [6] Here's what each plan means for me. [7] The Wall keeps my trees. [8] The Sponge takes my lowest rows. [9] Some of Each takes a few. [10] I don't know yet which is right. [11] I came to listen, and I'll say so if I change my mind.

**S2. The miller: Some of Each** (A story from Willowbank; ledger pattern from spec 120's page 2, the later floods higher and closer together; the storm's 160 cm from the town's new mark):
> [1] I said in the Gazette it was just a bad storm. [2] I still think big storms are most of it. [3] But I can't prove that, and nobody can prove the other side yet either. [4] My mill sits just below the low fields. [5] Hollis's ledger shows floods coming higher and closer together. [6] This year's storm came 160 centimeters up the post. [7] So I'd do a bit of both: a lower wall above the mill, and a small wet meadow at the low corner. [8] The wall protects the mill now, the meadow slows some water, and most trees stay. [9] The cost? [10] It's two jobs, not one: some stone, some trees. [11] Half a wall and half a marsh might be too little of both. [12] And nobody gets everything they wanted, me included.

**S3. Nuria: a picture talk about Vadoalto's reed bed** (A story from Willowbank; the rain table is Nuria's letter 4, a made-up model). Pictures change at each marker.
> [Picture 1: Vadoalto's reed bed beside the river.]
> [1] Buenas tardes. Good afternoon. [2] Thank you for letting a guest speak. [3] This is our reed bed, our *carrizal*, beside the river.
> [Picture 2: the reed bed full of water after a storm.]
> [4] After big rain it fills with water. [5] Then it lets the water go slowly, over days.
> [Picture 3: people cutting reeds and tying them in bundles.]
> [6] We cut some reeds and use them for mats and roofs. [7] We might buy reeds from Willowbank one day. [8] I can't promise how many.
> [Picture 4: the rain table: 6, 5, 6, 8 big rains, ten years at a time.]
> [9] This is our rain gauge table. [10] It counts big rains, ten years at a time: six, five, six, then eight. [11] It doesn't say how heavy each rain was. [12] So it can't settle your argument by itself.
> [Picture 5: a small, shy frog among the reeds.]
> [13] And this is one of our frogs. [14] They are very shy. [15] I'm a guest here. [16] It's your town, and your choice.

Spanish line: "Buenas tardes" (Good afternoon) sits beside its English. *carrizal* is glossed by "our reed bed" in the same sentence. Both need a fluent Spanish reader before build.

**S4. The librarian: the Sponge** (A story from Willowbank):
> [1] I'm speaking for the Sponge. [2] Let the lowest orchard fields go back to marsh. [3] My first reason: a marsh holds water and lets it go slowly, so less reaches the mill at once. [4] My second: Hollis's map shows the Sponge right where the low fields are now. [5] And Hollis wrote that the mill boards were seldom needed then. [6] My third: reeds are already coming back at the low corner. [7] I must be fair about the cost. [8] The orchard keepers would lose trees, and some of their living. [9] The reed trade is a hope, not a promise. [10] A marsh takes years to grow back. [11] And five years apart is not proof, so I can't say draining the Sponge caused the floods.

**S5. Bramble: the Wall** (A story from Willowbank; sentences 1 to 4 are spec 120's own lines):
> [1] Everyone keeps talking about frogs. [2] What about the orchard keepers? [3] Those trees are their whole year. [4] If you want them to give up their fields, you'd better be very sure, and you'd better help them with what comes after. [5] So I'm speaking for the Wall. [6] A higher wall along the brook keeps the town and the orchard as they are. [7] It protects us now, not years from now while a marsh grows. [8] And a new apple tree takes years to give a good crop, so lost trees aren't quickly replaced. [9] I'll say the hard part too. [10] A wall needs mending forever. [11] Water held back here may go somewhere else, so we'd have to ask Les Saules. [12] And a flood could come higher than any wall.

**Voices from the room** (short clips, A story from Willowbank):
- **V1. Pip:** "If we bring the Sponge back, we'll never flood again!"
- **V2. Hob Pennick:** "Build the wall high enough and we'll never think about floods again."
- **V3. Dot builds on the librarian:** "The librarian said reeds are coming back. I'd add that Nuria's town already uses reeds."
- **V4. The orchard keeper, after Nuria's talk:** "I thought reeds were only weeds. Nuria's pictures show people cutting them and using them. I've changed my mind about that part. Not about the plans. Just the reeds."

**W2. Wren closes** (A story from Willowbank):
> [1] Thank you. [2] We heard three plans, each with a cost, and the orchard keeper's account. [3] Nobody has to agree tonight. [4] The plans stay on the board.

V4 is a bench example of explaining a changed mind. It is not spec 120's Act IV ending line, which stays tied to the explorer's kept work.

## Teach cards

Each card is 30 words or fewer; worked-example steps appear one tap at a time. All are **A true thing about language**.

**Card 1. Get ready to listen**
> Before a meeting, read what you can about the question. Bring one useful fact and one question. Then you can follow along and join in.

Worked example: *Getting ready for the market bench talk.*
1. The question: where should the bench go?
2. A useful fact: the green has the only shade.
3. A question to bring: "Who needs a seat most?"

**Card 2. What does the speaker want?**
> A speaker's claim is what they want you to think or do. It is often said first, and again at the end.

Worked example: *The baker.*
1. His first words: "Put the new bench by the bakery door."
2. That's what he wants.
3. His claim: the bench should go by the bakery.

**Card 3. Reasons and evidence**
> A reason says why. Evidence shows the reason is true: something counted, seen or written down.

Worked example: *The orchard keeper.*
1. Claim: the bench should go under the willow.
2. Reason: the willow gives the only shade on the green.
3. Evidence: by noon, every apple seller had moved into that shade.

**Card 4. Not everything is a reason**
> Speakers also joke, tell stories and say what they like. Ask: does this help show the claim is right? If not, it isn't a reason.

Worked example: *The baker's buns.*
1. "My cinnamon buns are the best in the valley."
2. Does that show where people need to sit?
3. No. It's a nice boast, not a reason.

**Card 5. Say it your way**
> To paraphrase, say the same meaning in your own words. Same words is copying. A new meaning is a mistake.

Worked example: *The orchard keeper.*
1. She said: "The willow gives the only shade on the green."
2. Same meaning, new words: "Nowhere else on the green is shady."
3. Check: did the meaning stay the same? Yes.

**Card 6. Build on what you heard**
> To build on a point, add a fact or example, or ask a question about it. Repeating it or changing the subject doesn't build.

Worked example: *After the baker speaks.*
1. The baker: "Standing a long time is hard for some people."
2. Build: "Yes, and some of them carry heavy baskets too."
3. Or ask: "How long do they stand?"

**Card 7. Does it hold up?**
> Check each reason. Is it about this claim? Is there evidence? Watch big words like "never" and "always." They need very strong evidence.

Worked example: *Ember's "everybody".*
1. Ember: "Everybody loves lemon cake!"
2. The evidence: three guests asked for lemon.
3. Three is not everybody. "Everybody" goes too far.

**Card 8. Summary and conclusion**
> A summary gives the main points of the whole talk, briefly, without your own opinion. A conclusion says what all of it lets you say.

Worked example: *Puff, Ember and Dot each suggest a picnic spot.*
1. Main points: three spots, each with a reason.
2. Summary: "Each friend suggested a spot and gave a reason."
3. Conclusion: "There is more than one good spot."

**Card 9. Pictures that help**
> In a picture show, each picture should help the main idea. A funny picture that's off the point pulls eyes away from it.

Worked example: *A slide about the bakery.*
1. Caption: "By the bakery: warm bread, nowhere to sit."
2. People sitting on flour sacks: helps.
3. The magpie stealing a bun: funny, off the point.

## Benches

### (a) Before the Bell

**Header:** Get ready before the speeches. Pick two useful facts and one question.

**Market prep cards:** The green has the only shade · Nine people waited at the post office at noon · The bakery sells cinnamon buns · Bramble's magpie likes shiny things · Rain makes grass muddy

**Meeting prep cards:** Hollis's map shows the Sponge · Hollis's ledger of flood heights · Page 4: the fine harvest, then the flood · Nuria's rain table · Ember's birthday menu

**Market question cards:** Who needs a seat most? · Do you like benches? · Is it market day today?

**Meeting question cards:** How long would a marsh take to grow back? · How often would a wall need mending? · Would a small wet meadow be enough? · Do you like eating the apples from the orchard? · Is the meeting in the library today?

**Feedback for an off-topic card:** Does this help with the question? Try another.

**After the speeches (talk card, spoken to a grown-up, nothing stored):** Before, I thought ___. Now I think ___, because ___.

### (b) Find the Reasons

**Header:** Tap a sentence. Is it the claim, a reason, evidence, a problem, or something else?

**Speaker map (fills as you sort):** Claim at the top. Reasons below it. Evidence under each reason.

**Sort feedback:**
- A cost sentence put under reasons: That's a problem the speaker admits. Put it under "problems."
- A joke or boast put under reasons: Does it show the claim is right? Try "something else."
- An evidence sentence put under claim: That's something counted or seen. It backs up a reason.
- A sentence that only says a problem is coming, put under problems: Which sentence is the problem itself?

**Problems tray:** Good speakers say what their idea costs. Put those here.

**Something else tray:** Jokes, boasts and "here's why" sentences go here.

### (c) Say It Your Way

**Header:** Hear one part again. Which choice says it in new words, same meaning?

**Then (spoken to a grown-up, nothing stored):** Now say it your own way, out loud.

### (d) Add to It

**Header:** Someone just made a point. Which reply builds on it?

**Talk cards (for a grown-up or sibling; spoken, never stored):**
- Say it back: "So you're saying…"
- Add on: "I'd add…"
- Ask for evidence: "How do you know?"
- Ask a clear question: "What happens if…?"
- Agree or disagree, with a reason: "I agree with ___ because ___."
- Change your mind out loud: "I used to think ___. Now I think ___."

**Talk rules card:** Everyone gets a turn. Give a reason. Ask what others think. Changing your mind is allowed.

### (e) Does It Hold Up?

**Header:** A speaker said this. Does the support hold up?

**Three checks (shown as tabs):** Is it about this claim? · Is there evidence? · Does it go too far?

### (f) What We Heard

**Header:** Put it all together. What were the main points? What can we say now?

**Nuria's Picture Talk (Keeper's Desk):** Watch Nuria's five pictures with her voice. Then tell back what the pictures and numbers showed.

**Then (spoken to a grown-up, nothing stored):** Tell the main points in three sentences or fewer.

### (g) The Picture Show

**Header:** Make a short show. Choose a picture for each caption, then put them in order.

**Market show: Three Places for One Bench (Sapling)**
- Caption cards: By the bakery: warm bread, but a crowded path. · By the post office: a long wait, but a quiet corner. · Under the willow: shade, but mud after rain. · One bench, three places. What do you think?
- Picture cards: people sitting on flour sacks · a parcel line out the post office door · willow shade on a sunny day · muddy grass under the willow · Bramble's magpie stealing a cinnamon bun · a map of the whole valley
- Sound card (choose one or none): the market's chatter · a birthday song · loud drums

**Meeting show: What the Meeting Heard, for Pip (Keeper's Desk)**
- Caption cards: Three plans for the low fields. · The Wall: protects the town now. Cost: mending forever. · The Sponge: slows the water. Cost: some apple trees. · Some of Each: some of both. Cost: two jobs. · The orchard keeper: three generations of trees.
- Picture cards: a high stone wall along the brook · a wet meadow full of reeds · a small wall above the mill and a wet corner · old apple trees in a row · Ember's birthday cake · Bramble's magpie with a ribbon

**Show feedback:**
- Off-point picture: Fun! But does it help this caption's main idea?
- Too broad: That picture shows everything. Which one shows this caption?
- A plan left out (meeting show): Pip will hear only two plans. Add the third, so it's fair.
- A cost left out (meeting show): Every plan costs something. Add its cost.
- Off-point sound: Does that sound fit a market? Try another, or none.

**Play your show:** Play it for a grown-up. Read each caption aloud as its picture shows.

**Grown-up line:** The show stays on this device. Nothing is recorded or sent.

## Problem sets

Every item has the "I'm stuck" ladder. Choices appear in the order written; ✔ marks the right one. Tags in backticks name the misconception a wrong choice tests. Items marked *spoken* are said to a grown-up or sibling and never stored.

### Set A. Before the bell (Seedling, Sapling)

**A1 (Seedling, guided, market).** Steps shown first: 1. What is the question? 2. Which card is about seats? 3. Bring that one.
- Prompt: Wren asks you to bring one useful fact. Which helps most?
- Choices: My favorite color is green. (`off-topic-prep`) · Nine people waited at the post office at noon. ✔ · Benches are made of wood, and wood comes from trees. (`too-general`)
- Feedback: Favorite color: That's about you, not the bench. · Wood: True, but it doesn't help choose a place.
- Hint: Which card helps decide where the bench goes?
- Explanation: A useful fact is about the question. Nine people waiting shows a need.

**A2 (Sapling, meeting).**
- Prompt: You can reread one thing before the meeting. Which helps most?
- Choices: Hollis's map of the Sponge ✔ · Ember's birthday party menu (`off-topic-prep`) · The first postcard Dot delivered (`off-topic-prep`)
- Feedback: Menu: Fun to read, but the meeting is about the low fields. · Postcard: Lovely, but it isn't about the fields.
- Hint: What is the meeting about?
- Explanation: The map shows where the Sponge was. Every speaker talks about that place.

**A3 (Sapling, meeting).**
- Prompt: Which question would help most at the meeting?
- Choices: Do you like eating the apples from the orchard? (`off-point-question`) · Is the meeting in the library today? (`already-known`) · How long would a marsh take to grow back? ✔
- Feedback: Apples: Almost everyone does. It won't help choose. · Today: You already know that. You're at it.
- Hint: Which answer would help you weigh a plan?
- Explanation: A good question asks for something the plans depend on.

### Set B. Find the reasons (Seedling, Sapling)

**B1 (Seedling, guided, the baker).** Steps shown first: 1. Hear his first sentence. 2. What does he want? 3. That's the claim.
- Prompt: What does the baker ask the town for?
- Choices: The bench by the bakery door ✔ · More people buying his bread every day (`detail-as-claim`) · A wider path for his line (`detail-as-claim`)
- Feedback: More bread: He sells bread already. What does he ask for? · Wider path: He worries about the path. He doesn't ask for it.
- Hint: Listen to his very first words.
- Explanation: His claim is what he asks for: the bench by his door.

**B2 (Seedling, the baker).**
- Prompt: Which is one of the baker's reasons?
- Choices: His cinnamon buns are the best in the whole valley. (`aside-as-reason`) · The morning line is crowded. (`cost-as-reason`) · People want to eat warm bread right away. ✔
- Feedback: Buns: A boast. It doesn't show where people need to sit. · Crowded: That's a problem he admits, not a reason.
- Hint: Which one tells why a bench would help?
- Explanation: A reason says why the claim is right. People need a seat to eat.

**B3 (Seedling, sort, the baker).** Sort each sentence: claim, reason, evidence, problem, or something else.
- Answer: claim [1]; reasons [3], [6]; evidence [4]; problem [8]; something else [2], [5], [7], [9].
- Feedback for [9] under reasons (`aside-as-reason`): Buns are nice. Do they show where to sit?
- Hint: Find the claim first. Then ask "why?" of it.
- Explanation: One claim, two reasons, one piece of evidence and one honest problem.

**B4 (Sapling, Bramble).**
- Prompt: What does Bramble say to show lost trees are hard to replace?
- Choices: Everyone keeps talking about frogs. (`aside-as-reason`) · New trees take years to grow many apples. ✔ · A marsh holds water and lets it go slowly. (`other-speaker`)
- Feedback: Frogs: That's a complaint. It says nothing about new trees. · Marsh: The librarian said that, for the Sponge.
- Hint: Listen for what she says about new trees.
- Explanation: New trees take years to give a good crop. That backs her point.

### Set C. Say it your way (Seedling, Sapling)

**C1 (Seedling, guided, the baker).** Steps shown first: 1. Hear sentence 3 again. 2. What does it mean? 3. Find new words, same meaning.
- Prompt: Which says the baker's sentence in new words?
- Choices: People buy warm bread and want to eat it right away. (`copy-not-paraphrase`) · Folks with fresh bread have no seat to eat it on. ✔ · People should buy less bread at the market. (`changed-meaning`)
- Feedback: Copy: Those are his own words. Try new ones. · Less bread: He never said that. The meaning changed.
- Hint: Same meaning, different words.
- Explanation: New words, same meaning: that's a paraphrase.

**C2 (Seedling, Dot).**
- Prompt: Dot said, "The parcel line goes out the door." Which paraphrase fits?
- Choices: On market day, the parcel line goes out the door. (`copy-not-paraphrase`) · Dot wants a bigger post office. (`changed-meaning`) · Lots of people line up for parcels on market day. ✔
- Feedback: Copy: Same words as Dot's. · Bigger post office: She didn't ask for that.
- Hint: Picture the line. Say what you see.
- Explanation: Same meaning, new words: many people wait in line.

**C3 (Sapling, the orchard keeper).**
- Prompt: She said, "Those trees are older than I am." Which paraphrase fits?
- Choices: The trees have lived longer than she has. ✔ · She is older than all of her trees. (`changed-meaning`) · Those trees are older than I am, she says. (`copy-not-paraphrase`)
- Feedback: Older than trees: That flips the meaning. · Copy: Those are her words, just moved around.
- Hint: Who is older, the trees or her?
- Explanation: The trees are older. A paraphrase keeps that the right way round.

**C4 (Sapling, Nuria).**
- Prompt: Nuria said the table "doesn't say how heavy each rain was." Which fits?
- Choices: It counts big rains, not how hard each one fell. ✔ · It shows the big rains got heavier every single year. (`changed-meaning`) · It doesn't say how heavy each rain was, she says. (`copy-not-paraphrase`)
- Feedback: Heavier: She said it doesn't show heaviness at all. · Copy: Same words, just moved around.
- Hint: What does the table count? What does it leave out?
- Explanation: It counts rains. It doesn't measure how heavy they were.

### Set D. Add to it (Sapling, Keeper's Desk)

**D1 (Sapling, market).**
- Prompt: Dot says people wait a long time. Which reply builds on her point?
- Choices: People wait a long time for parcels. (`repeat-not-build`) · The miller's cat likes to sit on the parcels. (`change-subject`) · Yes, and the line is longest at noon. ✔
- Feedback: Repeat: That's her point again. What can you add? · Cat: Fun, but it changes the subject.
- Hint: Add something new about the same thing.
- Explanation: Adding a fact about the same point builds on it.

**D2 (Sapling, meeting).**
- Prompt: The miller wants a lower wall above her mill. Which question is clearest?
- Choices: What do you think about it all? (`vague-question`) · Would a lower wall keep your mill dry? ✔ · Do you like living and working at the mill? (`off-point-question`)
- Feedback: Think about it all: Too wide. What exactly do you want to know? · Like living there: That's not about the floods.
- Hint: Ask about one thing she said.
- Explanation: A clear question asks one thing, tied to her point, that she hasn't answered yet.

**D3 (Keeper's Desk, spoken with a grown-up or sibling).**

### Set E. Does it hold up? (Sapling, Keeper's Desk)

**E1 (Sapling, market).**
- Prompt: The baker says his buns are best. Does that support his claim?
- Choices: Yes, good buns mean people should sit there. (`aside-as-reason`) · No, buns don't show where people need a seat. ✔ · We can't tell anything at all from what he said. (`withhold-too-much`)
- Feedback: Yes: Buns might be lovely. They're not about seats. · Can't tell: We can tell. It's not about seats at all.
- Hint: Is the boast about seats?
- Explanation: A true thing can still be off the point. This one doesn't support the claim.

**E2 (Sapling, market).**
- Prompt: Which evidence best supports Dot's claim?
- Choices: She counted nine people waiting at noon. ✔ · Benches look nice by blue doors. (`weak-reason`) · Dot runs the post office, so she knows best. (`who-not-what`)
- Feedback: Blue doors: Nice, but it's about looks, not who needs a seat. · Runs it: Who says it isn't evidence. What shows it?
- Hint: Which one was counted?
- Explanation: Counting people waiting shows the need. That's the strongest support.

**E3 (Keeper's Desk, V1).**
- Prompt: Pip says, "We'll never flood again!" Which answer holds up?
- Choices: Yes, because a marsh can hold a lot of floodwater. (`overclaim-accepted`) · A marsh slows water, but "never" goes too far. ✔ · No, a marsh does nothing at all to water. (`dismiss-side`)
- Feedback: Yes: Holding water slows it. Nobody showed it stops every flood. · Nothing: A marsh holds water and lets it go slowly.
- Hint: Look at the big word in his sentence.
- Explanation: The evidence supports "slows." It doesn't support "never."

**E4 (Keeper's Desk, V2).**
- Prompt: Hob says a high wall means no more flood worries. Which answer holds up?
- Choices: That's true, because a wall stops every bit of water. (`overclaim-accepted`) · That's wrong, because a wall can't protect anything. (`dismiss-side`) · A wall helps, but a flood could still come higher. ✔
- Feedback: Every bit: A big enough flood can come over a wall. · Can't protect: Walls protect what's behind them.
- Hint: What did Bramble admit about her own plan?
- Explanation: A wall protects, but "no more worries" goes too far.

**E5 (Keeper's Desk, Nuria's table).**
- Prompt: Big rains: six, five, six, then eight. What can you fairly say?
- Choices: The table's last ten years had the most big rains. ✔ · Draining the Sponge must have caused all of the floods. (`overclaim-cause`) · This table tells us nothing at all. (`withhold-too-much`)
- Feedback: Caused: The table counts rain. It can't show what caused floods. · Nothing: It does show counts. Eight is the most.
- Hint: Find the biggest number. Which ten years?
- Explanation: Eight is the most. That's all the table can show by itself.

### Set F. What we heard (Sapling, Keeper's Desk)

- Prompt: Which is the best summary of the market bench talk?

- Feedback: Buns: One small detail, and not a main point. · Willow best: That's an opinion. A summary reports what was said.

## "I'm stuck" ladder

**Step 1, hint:** the item's own hint.

**Step 2, an easier case and how to start:**
- Prep items: "Try this one first: the question is 'Which cake for Ember?' Useful fact: Ember loves lemon. Now look at your question."
- Claim and reason items: "Try this one first: 'Let's walk, because the bus is late.' Claim: let's walk. Reason: the bus is late."
- Paraphrase items: "Try this one first: 'The soup is too hot.' New words: 'The soup needs to cool.' Now try yours."
- Build-on items: "Try this one first: 'It's raining.' Build: 'Yes, and the path is getting muddy.' Now find yours."
- Hold-up items: "Try this one first: 'All cats hate water.' One cat that swims shows 'all' goes too far."
- Summary items: "Try this one first: three friends each pick a game. Summary: three games, three reasons. Now try yours."
- Show items: "Try this one first: caption 'Pip's lunch.' Picture of a sandwich helps. Picture of the moon doesn't."

**Step 3:** Skip for now. You can come back any time.

## Key terms (glossary)

- **Claim:** what a speaker wants you to think or do.
- **Reason:** why the speaker thinks the claim is right.
- **Evidence:** something counted, seen or written down that shows a reason is true.
- **Paraphrase:** the same meaning in your own words.
- **Summary:** the main points, said briefly, without your own opinion.
- **Conclusion:** what everything you heard lets you say.
- **Build on:** add a fact, an example or a question to what someone just said.
- **Relevant:** about the thing being decided.
- **Generation:** parents are one generation; their children are the next.
- **Marsh:** wet, soft land where reeds grow and water stays.
- **Reed bed:** a marsh full of tall reeds.
- **Caption:** the words that go with a picture.
- **Picture show:** pictures and captions in order, shown while someone speaks.

## Grown-up panel

**What this teaches:** listening for a speaker's claim, reasons and evidence; paraphrasing a part; building on what someone said; judging whether support holds up; summarizing a talk and a picture talk with numbers; drawing a conclusion from a discussion; and choosing pictures that help a short show. The English national curriculum calls these spoken language skills (and spells it "summarise"); the US Common Core lists them under Speaking and Listening for grades 4 and 5.

**What is never recorded:** the child's voice, face and words. The speeches are recorded by adult performers and played to the child. Every "say it" task is for you to hear. The device keeps only which cards and choices were tapped.

**Why the pack never picks a plan:** the town's question about the low fields has more than one honest answer. Every plan speech names its own cost. Please don't tell the child which plan is right; ask which reasons they found strongest, and why.

**Talking together:** the talk cards work at the table, in the car or on a walk. Take turns, give reasons, and let changing your mind be a good thing.
