## Story scenes

### 1. The nest under the bridge (Act I, mend the village, spring)

*A story from Willowbank.*

**Short:** The storm washed the dipper's nest from under Willow Bridge. Two days later she is back, with a beak full of moss.

**Longer:** Pip leans over the bridge rail. "Her nest is gone," he says. "Where will she sleep?" {guide} points. The dipper bobs on a wet stone, dives into the fast water and pops up again. Then she flies under the bridge with a beak full of green moss. "She's building," says the librarian, and opens the blank book to its first page. "A round roof of moss, on a ledge, right by the spray." Dot straightens her hat. "Why not somewhere dry?" "Ask the Home Finder," says the librarian. Pip writes the first line, slowly: *Dipper. Came back. Building.* Then he asks whether moss is nice to eat. It is not.

**Keeper's note:** *A true thing from science.* Real white-throated dippers build domed nests of moss on ledges, under bridges and sometimes behind waterfalls. They feed by walking and swimming underwater in fast streams, so a nest beside the water is right next to their food.

### 2. Who wakes at night? (Act II, garden across the seasons, summer)

*A story from Willowbank.*

**Short:** Pip naps all afternoon on the library cushions. "Who's awake while I'm asleep?" he asks. The librarian dims the lamps.

**Longer:** Pip yawns so wide his whiskers shake. "I'm a fox," he says. "Foxes are night animals. That's why I nap." "Real foxes often hunt at dusk," says the librarian. "You just like cushions." They unroll a long painting of the valley and turn a little dial. The painted sky goes dusky blue. In the meadow, the marmots slip into their burrows. Under the old oak, a hedgehog wakes up and snuffles. Bats flicker over the brook. A tawny owl opens its eyes. Bramble's magpie hops onto the windowsill and tucks its head in. "Everyone swaps," says Dot. "Some go to bed. Some get up." "Who is awake?" asks {guide}. "Let's watch," says the librarian.

**Keeper's note:** *A true thing from science.* A tawny owl's ear openings sit at different heights on its head. A sound reaches each ear at slightly different moments, which helps the owl pinpoint a mouse in the dark. Its tube-shaped eyes work well in dim light, but no animal sees in total darkness.

### 3. The bone-breaker (Act III, letters up and down the river)

*A story from Willowbank.*

**Short:** Léon writes from Les Saules: a huge bird dropped a bone on the rocks, then ate the pieces. The librarian smiles.

**Longer:** Léon's letter is slightly damp, as always. "Dear Willowbank," the librarian reads. "A bird as big as a sail flew over the ferry today. It carried a bone in its feet, climbed high, and let go over the rocks. Crack! Then it landed and ate the pieces. Is that normal? Le gypaète barbu, says my neighbor. *(The bearded vulture.)* Damp regards, Léon." Pip looks worried. "It eats bones? Only bones?" "Mostly bones," says the librarian. "And it's perfectly normal." They pull a thin file from the Red List Shelf. On its cover is the same bird, gold and gray, with a little beard under its beak. "It nearly disappeared from some mountains," says the librarian. "Let's read why, and how it came back."

**Keeper's note:** *A true thing from science.* Bone makes up most of a bearded vulture's food, about 85% by one estimate. Bearded vultures were gone from the Alps by about 1913, while a small number held on in the Pyrenees. Zoo-bred birds were released in the Alps from 1986. In Spanish the bird is *quebrantahuesos*, "bone-breaker".

### 4. Tracks up the valley (Act IV, the town has two stories)

*A story from Willowbank. No one meets the bear.*

**Short:** Bear tracks are seen far up the valley. Hob counts his ewes twice. All there. Bramble says, "Bears don't belong here."

**Longer:** The *Willowbank Gazette* prints one line: *Bear tracks seen on the high path.* Hob Pennick counts his ewes at the ford. Then he counts again. All forty are there. "Bears don't belong here," says Bramble. "They frighten the sheep." "Bears lived in these mountains long before the village," says the librarian. "They almost vanished. People brought a few back." Bramble folds her arms. "And who helps when a sheep is lost?" "That's a fair question," says the librarian. "People are still working it out." Hob scratches his chin. "A shepherd in Les Saules keeps a big white dog with her flock. A patou. It grew up with the sheep. It doesn't fight. It just stands there, being enormous." The next week the patou arrives, sniffs Pip politely, and lies down among the ewes.

**Keeper's note:** *A true thing from science.* By the mid-1990s only a handful of brown bears were left in the Pyrenees. Three bears from Slovenia were released in 1996. In 2024, French wildlife officers reported at least 96 bears there. Guardian dogs, shepherds and night pens help protect flocks, and people still debate what works best on steep ground.

### 5. The café goldfish (Act V, the night garden project)

*A story from Willowbank.*

**Short:** Pip wants to set the café goldfish free in the brook. "Free is good," he says. The librarian shakes their head, kindly.

**Longer:** The café goldfish swims in slow circles. "Poor thing," says Pip. "Let's take it to the brook. It can be free." The librarian sits down beside the bowl. "A pet that's let go might not cope out there," they say. "Or it might cope too well. Some animals moved to new places spread and crowd out the ones that already live there." "Like the balsam on Hob's bank," says {guide}. "Exactly like that." Bramble frowns. "So every new animal is bad?" "No," says the librarian. "It's only called invasive if it spreads and causes harm." Ember looks into the bowl. "Then the goldfish stays," says Ember. "And we get it a nicer plant." Pip chooses the plant. It is very large.

**Keeper's note:** *A true thing from science.* Gray squirrels were brought to Britain from North America in the 1800s. They carry a virus called squirrelpox without falling ill, but it is often deadly to red squirrels. Pets should never be released into the wild.

## Teach cards

**T1. A home has a job.** A home keeps an animal out of the weather, away from hunters, and keeps its young safe.
- Worked example, revealed one step at a time: *The dipper's nest.* (1) Made of moss. (2) Under the bridge, out of the rain. (3) Close to the stream where she feeds. (4) Eggs inside, safe and dry.

**T2. Make or find?** Some animals build a home from moss, mud, wax or silk. Others find one ready-made.
- Worked example: *Two homes.* (1) The dipper gathers moss and builds: **make**. (2) The hermit crab moves into an empty shell: **find**.

**T3. Nocturnal means the day is swapped.** A nocturnal animal is busy at night and rests in the day. It still sleeps.
- Worked example: *Flip the valley.* (1) Daytime: marmots feed, the owl sleeps. (2) Turn to night. (3) Marmots go into the burrow. (4) The owl wakes and hunts.

**T4. A signal needs a receiver.** A signal only works if another animal senses it and does something.
- Worked example: *The marmot whistle.* (1) A marmot sees an eagle. (2) It whistles. (3) The others hear. (4) They dive into the burrow.

**T5. Wild, farm and pet are about people.** Wild animals look after themselves. Farm animals are kept for what they give. Pets are kept for company.
- Worked example: *Three rabbits.* (1) A rabbit in the meadow finds its own food: **wild**. (2) A rabbit on a farm is kept for meat or fur: **farm**. (3) A rabbit in a hutch, kept as a friend: **pet**.

**T6. Endangered or extinct?** Endangered means still alive but at risk of disappearing. Extinct means none are left anywhere.
- Worked example: *Two Pyrenean animals.* (1) The desman still swims in mountain streams: **endangered**. (2) The last bucardo, a wild goat, died in 2000: **extinct**.

**T7. Invasive means moved, then harmful.** People moved it to a new place. There it spreads and harms the animals or plants already living there.
- Worked example: *Gray squirrels in Britain.* (1) People brought them from North America. (2) They spread. (3) They carry a virus that harms red squirrels. (4) So they are invasive.

## Bench text

### Home Finder

Animal and home pairs (make or find): dipper, dome of moss (make); barn swallow, mud cup (make); marmot, burrow (make); badger, sett (make); honeybee, wax comb in a hive (make); garden spider, web (make); hermit crab, empty shell (find); tawny owl, hole in an old tree (find).

**What does this home do?** Job chips: *keeps out the weather* · *hides from hunters* · *keeps young safe*. Any true chip is accepted; each home shows which chips fit.

**Mismatch line:** *The badger won't fit in there. Try another home.*

**Home or habitat?** *The meadow is the marmot's habitat: the whole place it lives. The burrow is its home.*

### Night Watch

**Prompt:** Turn the valley to night. Who is awake? (Awake: tawny owl, hedgehog, pipistrelle bat, badger. Asleep: marmot, dipper, barn swallow.)

**Feature to job:**
- Owl's tube-shaped eyes: *gather what little light there is* ✔ · *make their own light* · *see in total darkness* (`dark-vision`).
- Owl's uneven ears: *tell exactly where a sound comes from* ✔ · *keep its head warm* · *help it sing*.
- Bat's calls: *echoes show where insects are* ✔ · *scare the owls away* · *keep it warm*.
- Desman's long snout: *feels for food in the stream* ✔ · *digs burrows in rock* · *keeps its head dry*.

**Card:** *No animal sees in total darkness. Night animals use the little light there is, plus hearing, smell and touch.*

### Signal Board

Each loop: tap **sender**, **channel**, **receiver**, **response**, then a **purpose**.
- Marmot whistle: marmot, sound, other marmots, dive into burrow. Purpose: danger.
- Glow-worm light: female glow-worm, light, male glow-worm, flies to her. Purpose: a mate.
- Honeybee dance: forager, dance, hive-mates, fly to the flowers. Purpose: food. *(Read-only; see the Bee Meadow, spec 150.)*
- Fox scent mark: fox, scent, other foxes, keep away. Purpose: territory.
- Cat's tail: the miller's cat, body language, Pip, backs off. Purpose: "leave me alone." *(A story from Willowbank.)*

**Daylight toggle (glow tile):** *In bright sun the glow is lost. A signal needs a channel the receiver can sense.*

### Wild, Farm or Pet?

Cards: marmot, wild rabbit, wild boar, dipper (wild) · Hob's ewe, Hob's patou, Hob's pig (farm) · a pet rabbit, the miller's cat (pet). Because chips: *finds its own food and home* · *kept for what it gives or does* · *kept for company*.

- Miller's cat in Farm, with "kept for what it does": *Fair! She catches mice at the mill. She's also a pet. It depends.*
- Patou in Pet: *It's friendly, but Hob keeps it to guard the sheep. That's a farm job.*
- Wild boar: *Farm pigs come from wild boar, tamed long ago. Same family, different lives.*
- Ember card: *Ember is from a story. Real animals only on this board.*

**Watch from a distance:** *Wild animals look after themselves. Watch from far away. Never feed or touch them.*

### The Red List Shelf

*A true thing from science* on every file. Each file has three panels: **What the evidence shows**, **What people think**, **What nobody knows yet**.

- **Pyrenean desman.** Shows: a small river animal, now Endangered; its range has shrunk a lot. Think: dams, dirty water and less water hurt it. Not known yet: how much escaped American mink harm it.
- **Bucardo (Pyrenean ibex).** Shows: a wild mountain goat, now extinct; the last one died in 2000. Not known yet: how much hunting, disease and other causes each mattered. Then: a related ibex was released in the French Pyrenees from 2014, 14 years later.
- **Bearded vulture.** Shows: gone from the Alps by about 1913; zoo-bred birds released there from 1986; the first wild Alpine chick in 1997. Think: people stopped shooting and poisoning them. Not known yet: how big the Pyrenean population is now; counts disagree.
- **Brown bear.** Shows: three Slovenian bears released in 1996; at least 96 reported for 2024. Think: more unrelated bears are needed. Not known yet: the exact number; the model says about 97 to 123.
- **California condor.** Shows: only 27 left in 1987, all taken into care; 561 in 2022, 347 of them wild. Think: breeding worked because there were wild places to release them.
- **Gray squirrel.** Shows: brought to Britain from North America; carries squirrelpox. Think: the virus, competition and habitat loss all hurt red squirrels.

**Red List ladder** (*A made-up model: the real list uses population size, range and decline, measured carefully.*). Rungs, safest to gone: Least Concern, Near Threatened, Vulnerable, Endangered, Critically Endangered, Extinct in the Wild, Extinct. Place: desman (Endangered), bucardo (Extinct), Alpine marmot (Least Concern), brown bear (Least Concern worldwide, though rare in the Pyrenees). Card: *The list rates a whole kind of animal across the world. A kind can be common in one place and rare in another.*

**True or not true?** *Extinct animals can easily be brought back.* Answer: not true (`easy-undo`). Feedback: *Extinct means gone. There is no easy way back, so we protect animals before then.*

**Threat to help:** too few left to breed in the wild → breed in care and release · too few and too related → bring in animals from elsewhere · sheep lost to bears → guardian dogs and night pens · rivers blocked by dams → clean, flowing water and ways past barriers · spare help: *a bigger cage* (fits none). Breeding with nowhere safe to release shows a flat line: *A made-up model: young bred, nowhere safe to go.*

**Invasive or not?** Gray squirrel in Britain (invasive) · Himalayan balsam on Hob's bank (invasive, spec 151) · Alpine marmot in the Pyrenees (*moved by people; any harm is not well measured yet; scientists use different words*) · a goldfish in the café bowl (a pet, not in the wild). Feedback on the marmot as invasive: *Moved by people isn't enough. Invasive also means it spreads and causes harm.* (`nonnative-invasive`)

## Key terms

- **Home:** an animal's own shelter, made or found.
- **Habitat:** the whole place where an animal lives and finds what it needs.
- **Nocturnal:** busy at night, resting in the day.
- **Diurnal:** busy in the day, resting at night.
- **Echolocation:** finding things by listening for echoes of your own calls.
- **Signal:** a message one animal sends and another senses.
- **Wild animal:** an animal that finds its own food and shelter.
- **Farm animal:** an animal kept for what it gives or does.
- **Pet:** an animal kept for company.
- **Endangered:** still alive, but at risk of disappearing.
- **Extinct:** gone; none are left anywhere.
- **Red List:** scientists' list that ranks how at risk each kind of living thing is.
- **Invasive:** moved by people to a new place, where it spreads and causes harm.
- **Guardian dog:** a dog raised with sheep to keep predators away.
