# The Sign Painter: all child-facing text

Companion to [spec 125](../specs/125-sign-painter.md). Use as written, after the same independent review. `{guide}` is the explorer's chosen companion. Pip is a fox (he/him), Dot a ladybug (she/her), Bramble a girl witch (she/her) with a magpie, Marisol the captain of *La Garza* (she/her). The baker, the miller, the orchard keeper and Moss have no pronouns in this text. Lines marked `[check]` need a science or licence check before build. Nothing here judges how a sign looks.

## Arriving

**Place title:** The Sign Painter

**First visit:**
> Behind the market there's a shed with a long bench, a row of paint pots and a stack of blank boards. A note is pinned to the door: *Signs wanted. Ask inside.*
>
> Inside, the boards are already labelled with orders. "Every shop's sign blew away in the storm," says Pip. "Everyone's asking for a new one. And nobody here can paint."
>
> {guide} picks up a brush. "We can learn."

**Later visits:** The orders are on the board. Paint's ready.

## Bench 1: Shapes

**Bench title:** Shapes
**Tray:** square · rectangle · right-angled triangle · equal-sided triangle · half circle · circle · diamond · hexagon
**Controls:** Turn (a quarter turn) · Flip across · Flip up and down · Bigger · Smaller · Bring forward · Send back · Paint it · Remove
**Outline toggle:** Show the outline · Hide the outline

**Worked example (first visit):** one step per tap.
1. "Here's a triangle. Press Turn. It turns a quarter of the way round, like a clock hand going from 12 to 3."
2. "Press Flip across. Now it's flipped over, like turning a page."
3. "Two right-angled triangles can make a square. Try it: turn one, and push them together."
4. "Trying things until they fit is exactly how sign painters work."

**Outline filled:** Your shapes cover the outline.
**Outline not yet filled:** Part of the outline is still showing. You can keep going, or hide the outline and make it your own way.

## Bench 2: Mirror signs

**Bench title:** Mirror signs
**Mirror choices:** Mirror line up and down · Mirror line across · Mirror line corner to corner
**Controls:** Paint a square · Rubber · Fold to check · Clear

**Worked example (first visit):**
1. "This line is a mirror line. A mirror sign looks the same on both sides of it, as if you folded the board along the line."
2. "Paint this square. Where does its partner go? Count how many squares it is from the mirror line. Its partner is the same number of squares away on the other side, straight across at a square corner to the line."
3. "Now press Fold to check. The board folds along the line. Squares that land on the same color are fine. Squares that land on a different color get a little dot."
4. "Squares right on the mirror line are their own partners. They stay where they are."

**Fold to check results:**
- Every square lands on its partner. It's a mirror sign!
- {n} squares land on a different color. They're marked with a dot.
- Nothing painted yet. Paint some squares on one side, then their partners on the other.

**Corner-to-corner tip (Details):** With a sloping mirror line, "straight across" means straight across *to the line*, at a square corner to it. Partners sit on a slant from each other. Try one, then fold to check.

**Two mirror lines (after one is checked):** Add a second mirror line? Your sign will need to match across both.

## Bench 3: Borders

**Bench title:** Borders

**Find the unit (first visit):**
> Here are three borders from old signs. Each one is made from a small piece, repeated. Draw a box round the smallest piece that repeats.

| Border | Pattern (in words) | Unit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The bakery's old border | wheat, loaf, loaf, wheat, loaf, loaf, … | wheat, loaf, loaf |
| The post office's old border | hat, hat, letter, hat, hat, letter, … | hat, hat, letter |
| Bramble's ribbon border | red ribbon, gold ribbon, green ribbon, red, gold, green, … | red, gold, green |

**Unit check messages:**
- That's the smallest piece. Repeat it and you get the whole border.
- That piece repeats, but there's a smaller one inside it. Can you find it?
- If you repeat that piece, it doesn't make this border. Look again at where it starts over.

**Make a tile:** Paint a small tile. Then choose how it repeats.
**Repeat choices:**
- Slide: the same tile, again and again.
- Flip: every other tile flipped over.
- Half turn: every other tile turned half way round.
**Preview line:** Your tile repeats {n} times round the sign.

## Bench 4: Paint and lamplight

**Bench title:** Paint and lamplight

**Paint palette heading:** Paint pots
**Pots:** red · yellow · blue · white
**Mix prompt (before each first mix):** What do you think you'll get? · Choose a color, then mix.
**Prediction choices:** shown as named swatches, three per mix.
**Mix results:**
- red and yellow make orange.
- yellow and blue make green.
- red and blue make purple.
- red, yellow and blue make a dark brown. That is this palette's authored mix; real paints vary.
- {color} and white make a lighter {color}.
**After the first mix, either prediction:** "{result}! That is what these model paints made. White paint can make a mix lighter." (The child's prediction is never marked right or wrong.)
**Real paints note (Details):** Real paints vary. Some reds and blues make a muddy purple, depending on the paint. `[check]`

**Lamplight heading:** Lanterns on the white wall
**Lanterns:** red lantern · green lantern · blue lantern
**Controls:** Turn on · Turn off · Dim · Brighten · Move
**Mix results (where the lights overlap):**
- red and green light make yellow.
- green and blue light make cyan, a sky blue-green.
- red and blue light make magenta, a pinky purple.
- red, green and blue light together make white.
**After the first overlap:** "{result}! That is the color of these overlapping lights." `[check]`
**White wall (Details):** A white wall reflects much of the visible light that reaches it. That's why every lantern's color shows up on it. A dark wall would soak up most of the light. `[check]`

**Why are they opposite? (older Details):**
> Paint soaks up some colors of light and gives back the rest. Mixing different pigments changes which light comes back. Some mixes look darker or duller; adding white can lighten them. Lanterns each make their own light. Shine two together, and you add their light: lighter. `[check]`

**Colors in words:** every swatch and lantern also shows its name, so you never have to tell colors apart by looking alone.

## Older benches

**Turn signs:**
- Title: Turn signs
- Card: A turn sign looks the same after you turn it. Some look the same after a half turn. Some after a quarter turn, too.
- Controls: Half turn · Quarter turn · Turn to check
- Results: It looks the same after a {half/quarter} turn! · {n} squares land on a different color after the turn. They're marked.
- Worked example: "The mill wheel looks the same after a quarter turn. Count how many quarter turns it takes to get all the way round: four. Each one looks the same."

**Floor tiles:**
- Title: Floor tiles
- Card: Which shapes can cover a floor with no gaps and no overlaps? Try each one.
- Shape choices: squares · equal-sided triangles · regular hexagons · regular pentagons · a triangle you draw · a simple four-sided shape you draw
- Try prompt: Lay copies down. Turn them if you need to.
- After trying squares, triangles or hexagons: These cover the floor. They always do.
- After trying pentagons: There's always a gap. Regular pentagons can't cover a floor without gaps or overlaps, however you lay them. `[check]`
- After trying a drawn triangle: Every triangle can cover a floor. Turn every other copy half way round. `[check]`
- After trying a drawn four-sided shape: Every simple four-sided shape, with no crossing sides, can cover a floor, even a lopsided one, by turning every other copy half way round. `[check]`

- One ask isn't met yet: {ask}.

## Lantern Night (winter festival)

> The lanterns are out on the footbridge tonight, shining on the white wall of the bakery. Someone has set up the lamplight bench right there. "Every year, we try a new color," says Pip.

The festival's color suggestion rotates by year: yellow, cyan, magenta, white.

## Off-screen invitations

- With a grown-up, mix two paint colors on a paper plate. Did you get what you expected?
- Fold a piece of paper, paint on one half, and press the halves together. Open it up: you've made a mirror picture.
- Look for a repeating border at home: on a plate, a rug, wallpaper, a tiled floor. Can you find its smallest repeating piece?
- With a grown-up, shine two torches with colored cellophane on a white wall in a dark room. `[check: safety and materials]`

Off-screen invitations record nothing.

## Grown-up notes

- No sign is ever judged for how it looks. Is it ready? checks only the order's asks, such as a mirror line or a border type.
- Paint mixing is subtractive and light mixing is additive. The game keeps them on separate benches and calls out the contrast, because mixing them up is very common, even for older students.
- The mirror bench never mirrors for the child. Constructing the other half and checking by folding is the skill.
