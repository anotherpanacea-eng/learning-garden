About the picture: This curve shows the steady part of a computer signal changing over time. It is not a recording of a ruler, a pressure measurement or the path of an air particle. Faster repetition makes a higher simple tone. Bigger signal size does not add cycles. More cycles does not make the signal taller. Signal size means amplitude: how far the curve reaches from the middle line. With the same pitch and listening setup, a bigger signal makes a louder tone.

Compared with the held setting: “Same frequency and signal size.” / “The frequency changed; signal size stayed the same.” / “Signal size changed; frequency stayed the same.” / “Both frequency and signal size changed.” No held setting: “Hold one setting, then change a control to compare.”

For listening: These are simple computer tones, not recordings of a bell, ruler or drum. Start with your device volume low. Stop whenever you want. To compare loudness, keep pitch and your listening setup the same. Equal signal amplitudes at different pitches may not sound equally loud. Your speaker, volume setting and surroundings affect what you hear. No sound-level or hearing result is measured here.
