## Teaching miniature

1. Start with 4x + 10. Their positive integer common divisors are 1 and 2. Check: 2 times 2 is 4, and 2 times 5 is 10. Try a side of length 2 centimeters.
2. Divide both numbers by 2. The other side has numerical length 2x + 5. The proposal is 2(2x + 5).
3. Expand to check: 2 times 2x is 4x; 2 times 5 is 10. The two terms return: 4x + 10.
4. At x = 3, 4 times 3 is 12, plus 10 is 22. For the product, 2 times 3 is 6, plus 5 is 11; 2 times 11 is 22. The matching coefficients tell us more: these expressions agree for every positive x in this model.

## Two contrasts

A valid factor need not be the greatest. 18x + 12 = 2(9x + 6), and 18x + 12 = 6(3x + 2). Both factorizations work. Inside the first brackets, 9 and 6 still share 3. Divide inside: 9 divided by 3 is 3; 6 divided by 3 is 2. Move that factor outside: 2 times 3 is 6. Inside the new brackets, 3 and 2 share only 1, so the outside factor cannot grow. 6 is the greatest positive integer that divides both 18 and 12.

One matching value is not enough. Compare 6x + 3 with 3(x + 2). At x = 1, both are 9. At x = 2, they are 15 and 12. Expanding gives 3x + 6, whose coefficients differ from 6x + 3.
