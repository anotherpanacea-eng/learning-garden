# The Puppet's Bones: all child-facing text

Companion to [spec 159](../specs/159-puppets-bones.md). Use as written, after independent review and an anatomy-fact check. `{guide}` is the explorer's chosen companion. The librarian (they/them) builds the puppets. Pip is a fox (he/him), Dot a ladybug (she/her) and the baker he/him. The Traveler is a puppet and is called "it". Nothing here is scored.

**House rules for this text:** every body fact is about "a person" or "a grown-up", shown on the Traveler or the drawn person chart. No line asks about the explorer's own body, senses or breathing. The "you" in prompts is the explorer doing the task.

## The story

### Scene 1. The floppy Traveler (Act II, chapter 4)

**Hook:** The first show needs a traveler. The librarian's cloth puppet keeps falling over. Pip thinks it needs a snack.

**Short:**
> The cloth puppet flops over again. "It has nothing inside to hold it up," says the librarian. "It needs a frame."

**Longer:**
> The first show is in nine days. The show is about a traveler walking the old Ways, and the librarian has made a traveler out of cloth and stuffing.
>
> They stand it up. It folds in half and sits down.
>
> "Maybe it's tired," says Pip. "Maybe it's hungry."
>
> "It has nothing inside," says the librarian. They pick up a bundle of carved wooden pieces. "A person has a frame inside. Bones. They hold a person up. So this one gets a frame too."
>
> Dot peers at the pieces. "That round one looks like a helmet."
>
> "Good eye," says the librarian. "That one's job is keeping something safe."
>
> {guide} sorts the pieces into a row. "Let's find out what each bone is for."

**Keeper's note:**
> A grown-up's skeleton is usually counted as 206 bones. A newborn has more separate pieces, perhaps 270 or more, and many of them join together while a person grows. Bones are alive: they grow, mend and, inside, make blood cells.

### Scene 2. The arm that won't wave back

**Short:**
> Pip pulls the Traveler's arm string. The arm bends. He pushes the string. Nothing happens. "Broken?" he asks.

**Longer:**
> The librarian has strung the Traveler's arm with two cords, one in front and one behind. "Like a person's muscles," they say.
>
> Pip pulls the front cord. The arm bends up in a wave.
>
> "Now wave back down," he says, and pushes the cord. It goes wiggly. The arm stays bent.
>
> "It's broken," says Pip.
>
> "It's working," says the librarian. "Try to push a piece of string. Muscles are the same. They can only pull."
>
> "So how does the arm come back down?"
>
> {guide} points at the second cord, behind the elbow. "Something has to pull the other way."

**Keeper's note:**
> In a person, the biceps at the front of the upper arm pulls to bend the elbow. The triceps at the back pulls to straighten it. While one pulls, the other relaxes. Muscles that work against each other like this are called a pair.

### Scene 3. The puppet that can't sing

**Short:**
> The show has a song. "Can the Traveler sing?" asks Dot. "Puppets don't breathe," says the librarian. "People do. Look."

**Longer:**
> The show has a song in the middle. Dot has written it herself.
>
> "Can the Traveler sing it?" she asks.
>
> "Puppets don't breathe," says the librarian. "Someone backstage will sing."
>
> "Then how do people breathe?" asks Pip. "Do the lungs puff themselves up?"
>
> The librarian fetches a glass jar from the science shelf. Inside are two balloons on a tube, and a rubber sheet stretched across the bottom.
>
> "A model," they say. "It's wrong in several ways. It's still useful. Pull the sheet."
>
> Pip pulls. The balloons fill.
>
> "And who is pulling the Traveler's strings?" asks {guide}.
>
> "Me," says the librarian. "A person has something better than strings. Messages."

**Keeper's note:**
> Lungs have no muscles of their own. A dome-shaped muscle under them, the diaphragm, tightens and flattens. Muscles between the ribs lift the ribs. The chest space grows, and air flows in.

## (a) Frame or flop

### Teach

**Card 1 (A true thing about bodies):** A person has a skeleton inside: a frame of bones. It does three jobs: support, protection and movement.

**Card 2:** Support means holding the body up and giving it shape. Without bones, a person would flop like the cloth puppet.

**Card 3:** Bones are alive. They grow and mend.

**Card 3b (Keeper's layer):** Inside many bones, soft marrow makes blood cells. That is a fourth job.

**Worked example, one tap per step: the skull**
1. Find the skull on the chart. It is round, like a helmet.
2. It is hollow. Inside it sits the brain.
3. The brain is soft and very important.
4. So the skull's job is protection. It keeps the brain safe.

**Card 4:** The ribs make a cage around the heart and lungs. The spine is many small bones stacked up. The thigh bone is the longest bone.

**Card 5, body plans:** A snail and an earthworm move with no bones. An insect wears its skeleton on the outside. A person's is inside.

### Guided item

**Prompt:** What job do the ribs do?
**Visible steps:**
1. The ribs curve around like a cage.
2. Inside the cage are the heart and lungs.
3. A cage around something soft keeps it safe.

Choices: Support · Protection (fits) · Movement
- Protection: Yes. The ribs keep the heart and lungs safe.
- Support `squishy-frame`: Ribs help hold the chest's shape, but look at what's inside the cage.
- Movement `only-limbs`: Ribs do move a little when a person breathes. Their main job is a cage.

### Practice

**Prompt:** Tap a bone, then tap its job.
Skull → Protection · Ribs → Protection · Spine → Support (Movement also fits) · Thigh bone → Support (Movement also fits)
**Stand them up button:** Stand them up
**Caption after:** The cloth puppet flops. The wooden frame stands. That's support.

## (b) String Arm

### Teach

**Card 1:** Muscles move bones by pulling. A muscle gets shorter and pulls. It can never push.

**Worked example, one tap per step: bending the arm**
1. The arm is straight. Both cords are relaxed.
2. Pull the front cord. It gets shorter.
3. It pulls the forearm up. The elbow bends.
4. The back cord is relaxed. It is pulled long.

**Card 2:** In a person, the front muscle is the biceps. The back muscle is the triceps. They work as a pair.

**Card 3:** A person chooses to move arm and leg muscles. The heart and the gut's muscles work by themselves.

### Guided item

**Prompt:** The arm is bent. Which cord straightens it?
**Visible steps:**
1. A cord can only pull.
2. The front cord pulls the arm up.
3. To go down, something must pull from behind.

Choices: The back cord (fits) · The front cord, pushed · Either cord
- The back cord: Yes. The back cord, like the triceps, pulls the arm straight.
- The front cord, pushed `muscles-push`: Try pushing it. The cord goes wiggly. Cords and muscles only pull.
- Either cord `one-muscle-both-ways`: Each cord pulls one way only. One bends, the other straightens.

### Bench text

**Buttons:** Pull front cord · Pull back cord · Push front cord · Push back cord · Let go
**Cord states:** pulling, shorter · relaxed, pulled long
**Push caption:** A cord can't push. It just goes wiggly.
**Both pulling caption:** Both pulling: the arm stays put.
**Keeper's card:** The knee has a pair too. The front thigh muscles straighten the knee. The back ones bend it.

## (c) Joint shop

### Teach

**Card 1:** A joint is where two bones meet. Different joints move in different ways.

**Card 2:** A hinge joint bends one way, like a door. The elbow and knee are hinges.

**Card 3:** A ball-and-socket joint has a round end in a cup. It swings in many directions. The shoulder and hip are like this.

**Worked example, one tap per step: fitting the elbow**
1. The forearm needs to bend up and straighten down.
2. That is one direction, back and forth.
3. A hinge moves back and forth in one direction.
4. Fit the hinge. Test it. The arm bends.

### Guided item

**Prompt:** Fit the knee. Which piece?
**Visible steps:**
1. A knee bends the lower leg back.
2. Then it straightens it again.
3. Is that one direction, or many?

Choices: Hinge (fits) · Ball and socket
- Hinge: Yes. The knee bends one way, like a door.
- Ball and socket `all-joints-same`: Test it. The leg swings sideways. A real knee doesn't do that.

### Practice

**Prompt:** Fit the shoulders and hips. Then test each one.
**Test captions:** Hinge: forward and back only. · Ball and socket: forward, back, out and around.
**Wrong-fit caption (shoulder with a hinge):** This shoulder only swings forward and back. A real shoulder can swing around.

### Keeper's layer: tendons and ligaments

**Card 4:** A tendon joins a muscle to a bone. It carries the muscle's pull to the bone.

**Card 5:** A ligament is a strong strap joining bone to bone. It holds a joint together.

**Card 6:** Smooth cartilage covers bone ends. A slippery fluid helps the joint move without grinding.

**Card 7:** The elbow works like a lever. The hoist yard at the mill shows how levers work.

**Tag prompt:** Place a tendon tag and a ligament tag on the elbow.
- Tendon on cord-to-bone: Yes. This joins muscle to bone.
- Tendon on bone-to-bone `tendon-ligament-swap`: Bone to bone is a ligament. Tendons join muscle to bone.
- Ligament on cord-to-bone `tendon-ligament-swap`: Muscle to bone is a tendon. Ligaments join bone to bone.

## (d) Message relay

### Teach

**Card 1:** People often name five senses. Each one notices a different kind of thing.

**Card 2:** Eyes notice light. Ears notice sound. Skin notices touch, heat, cold and pain. The tongue notices tastes. The nose notices smells.

**Card 3:** The brain sits inside the skull. Nerves carry messages between the brain and the body.

**Worked example, one tap per step: the baker's bell**
1. The baker rings the bell. The person's ears catch the sound.
2. A nerve carries the message to the brain.
3. The brain makes sense of it: the bread is ready.
4. A nerve carries a message out to the leg muscles.
5. The muscles pull. The person walks to the bakery.

**Card 4:** The ears notice the sound. The brain works out what it means. Both are needed.

**Card 5, strings and nerves:** Puppet strings join the puppeteer to the arms, like nerves. Nerves carry messages. They never pull. Messages go both ways.

### Guided item

**Prompt:** The smell of bread. Put the first two cards in order.
**Visible steps:**
1. Which part notices a smell?
2. Then where does the message go?

Cards: Nose notices the smell · Nerve carries the message to the brain
- In order: Yes. First the nose notices, then a nerve carries the message in.
- Reversed `one-way-nerves`: The message starts at the nose. Then it travels in to the brain.

### Practice: match the senses

**Prompt:** Tap a sense organ, then what it notices.
Eyes → light · Ears → sound · Skin → touch, heat and cold · Tongue → tastes · Nose → smells

### Keeper's layer: the shortcut

**Card 6:** A hot cup! Here a message can turn around in the spinal cord. The hand pulls back fast.

**Card 7:** That fast move is a reflex. The brain gets the message too, a moment later.

**Card 8:** People have more than five senses. Balance comes from inside the ears. Another sense knows where arms and legs are.

## (e) Bell-jar bench

### Teach

**Card 1:** Lungs are spongy. They have no muscles. They can't puff themselves up.

**Card 2:** Under the lungs is a dome-shaped muscle, the diaphragm. When it tightens, it flattens. The chest gets roomier.

**Card 3:** Rib muscles lift the ribs up and out. More room inside means air flows in.

**Card 4:** To breathe out, the muscles relax. The diaphragm domes up again. Air flows out.

**Worked example, one tap per step: one pull of the sheet**
1. The rubber sheet is the model's diaphragm.
2. Pull it down. The space inside the jar gets bigger.
3. Air flows in through the tube.
4. The balloons fill, like lungs filling.

### Guided item

**Prompt:** Which part of the model stands for the diaphragm?
**Visible steps:**
1. The diaphragm is a muscle under the lungs.
2. Find the part under the balloons.
3. It is the part you pull.

Choices: The balloons · The tube · The rubber sheet (fits)
- The rubber sheet: Yes. It sits under the balloons, like the diaphragm under the lungs.
- The balloons `model-is-body`: The balloons stand for the lungs. Look underneath them.
- The tube: The tube stands for the windpipe and its two branches.

### Practice: model or body?

**Prompt:** Is each card true of the model, the body, or both?
- The space gets bigger, so air flows in. → Both
- The bottom is pulled from outside. → Model only
- The muscle tightens by itself and flattens. → Body only
- The sides can't move. → Model only
- The ribs lift up and out. → Body only
- The lungs are spongy, full of tiny air sacs. → Body only

**After the sort:** A model gets some things right and some wrong. Scientists still use it, and say what's wrong.

**Bench buttons:** Pull down · Let go
**Label mode prompt:** Tap a part. Then tap what it stands for.

## "I'm stuck" worked examples

Each item's ladder: hint, then this easier worked example and how to start, then **Skip for now** ("That's fine. It will be here later.").

- **Bones (A1-A3):** The Traveler's head piece is round and hollow. Something soft would fit inside. A hollow case keeps soft things safe. *To start:* look at where the bone sits, then at what's under it.
- **Cords (A4-A6, A8):** A door rope pulls a door shut. Pushing the rope won't open it. *To start:* ask which cord is pulling right now.
- **Joints (B1, B5, C7):** A cupboard door swings one way. That's a hinge. *To start:* test the joint on the Traveler and watch its moves.
- **Relay (B2-B4, B7):** The baker's bell: ears, nerve, brain, nerve, legs. *To start:* find the sense first.

## Key terms

- **skeleton:** the frame of bones inside a person.
- **bone:** a hard, living part of the skeleton.
- **skull:** the bony case around the brain.
- **ribs:** curved bones making a cage around the heart and lungs.
- **spine (backbone):** a column of small bones down the back.
- **thigh bone (femur):** the bone in the upper leg; the longest bone.
- **muscle:** a body part that gets shorter to pull.
- **relax:** when a muscle stops pulling and can be pulled long.
- **biceps and triceps:** the front and back muscles of the upper arm.
- **joint:** where two bones meet.
- **hinge joint:** a joint that bends one way, like a door.
- **ball-and-socket joint:** a round end in a cup; it swings around.
- **tendon:** joins a muscle to a bone.
- **ligament:** a strong strap joining bone to bone.
- **senses:** the ways a body notices the world.
- **nerve:** a pathway that carries messages around the body.
- **brain:** the control center inside the skull.
- **reflex:** a very fast move that happens without deciding.
- **lungs:** spongy organs in the chest that air flows into.
- **diaphragm:** the dome-shaped muscle under the lungs.
- **model:** a simpler copy that shows one idea; it is never exactly the real thing.

## Grown-up panel

**What this teaches:** the skeleton's jobs and four named bones; pull-only muscles working in pairs; hinge and ball-and-socket joints, with tendons and ligaments for older explorers; the five senses and nerves as messengers between senses, brain and muscles; what the diaphragm does, and how to judge a model.

**What it does not claim:** the puppet and chart are simplified; the String Arm and bell jar are models with their faults stated; brain anatomy, injury, health and exercise are not covered. Nothing here is health advice.

**Privacy:** the pack never asks about your explorer's body, senses or breathing, and records nothing about them. "Say why" answers are spoken to you and never saved.

**Sources in brief:** anatomy textbooks (OpenStax-based university texts), Cleveland Clinic, Harvard Health, European Lung Foundation teaching guides, and reference lists. Full list in the source audit.

**Optional off-screen activity (grown-up-led): a cardboard arm.** Cut two cardboard strips (the bones). Join them at one end with a paper fastener (the elbow). Tape one string to each side of the lower strip, just past the joint. Run each string up its own side of the upper strip through a tape loop (the two muscles). Pull one string, then the other. A grown-up handles the scissors and the fastener's sharp points. Nothing is entered in the game.
