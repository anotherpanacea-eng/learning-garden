# Grid and Compass: all child-facing text

Companion to [spec 187](../specs/187-grid-and-compass.md). Use as written, after independent spec review and a math check. `{guide}` is the explorer's chosen companion and is never gendered; `{courier}` is the explorer's courier. Pip is he/him, Dot she/her, Bramble she/her (with her magpie), the librarian they/them, Hob Pennick he/him. Before the end of Act III nobody names the old Keeper; this pack never needs the name. Real-world facts are in the [source audit](../source-audits/187-grid-and-compass.md); items still marked `[check]` stay off screen until cleared. Nothing here is scored or timed.

## Labels used on every card

- **A story from Willowbank**: the town, its map, its places and every character are made up.
- **A true thing about math**: checked math, explained simply.
- **A true thing from history**: something people really did or wrote.
- **A made-up model**: a simple game rule, shown on the card, that is not a real map or real measurement.

## Story scenes

### Scene 1. A map on the floor (Act II, chapter 1: the weekly market)

**Hook:** The market is coming back. Dot wants a map big enough to stand on.

**Short:**
> Dot chalks the valley on the post office floor. "Put the bakery stall next to the honey," she says. "Not over there. Next to."

**Longer:**
> The market is coming back, one stall at a time. Nobody can agree where the stalls go.
>
> So Dot pushes the benches to the walls and chalks the whole valley on the floor, in squares.
>
> "Where does the spoon man go?" asks Pip.
>
> "Over there," says Bramble, pointing.
>
> "Over where?" says Dot. "Over there is everywhere."
>
> Bramble sighs. "Between the honey stall and the bridge. In front of the café."
>
> "Thank you," says Dot. She puts a button on the spot.
>
> Pip unfolds his old map beside the chalk one. "Mine has a marsh," he says. "Yours doesn't."
>
> "Then one of them is mistaken," says Dot. "Or old."

**Keeper's note** (A true thing about math):
> A position word only works with a landmark: next to *what*, in front of *what*. "Over there" points, but nobody can follow a point from far away.

### Scene 2. Bramble's mirror copy (Act II, chapter 1: the weekly market)

**Hook:** Bramble traces the market plan to pin up at her ribbon stall. Something has gone strange.

**Short:**
> Bramble's copy of the market plan has the bakery on the other side. Every stall has swapped. "I traced it through the back," she says.

**Longer:**
> Bramble wants her own copy of the market plan. She lays thin paper on the floor map and traces it with chalk. Then she turns the paper over, because the chalk side smudges.
>
> She pins it up. The bakery is now on the right of the bridge. The honey stall is on the left. Her magpie tilts its head at it.
>
> "It's backward," says Pip.
>
> "It's a mirror copy," says {guide}. "Every stall is the same distance from the middle line, on the other side."
>
> Bramble looks for a long time. "So if I fold it along the middle line, it matches the real one?"
>
> "Try it."
>
> She doesn't fold it. She hangs it in front of a mirror instead, which works, and is much more Bramble.

**Keeper's note** (A made-up model):
> Flipping the paper over reflects every stall in the up line through the bridge, at 5 along. Left becomes right; up stays up. The Bakery at (2, 4) lands at (8, 4).

### Scene 3. The upside-down map (Act III, chapter 3: the route by the high ford)

**Hook:** The old map marks the high ford. The mark seems to be in a strange place.

**Short:**
> The old map puts the ford down below the mill. "Impossible," says Dot. The librarian turns the map a half turn. "Try it this way."

**Longer:**
> {courier} must find the high ford to reach Vadoalto. Pip's old map marks it with a tiny blue ribbon.
>
> Dot moves the floor map's center to Willow Bridge, so the map can grow in every direction. She reads the old map's mark: 4 right of the bridge, 3 down.
>
> "That's below the mill," she says. "There's no ford below the mill. There's a mill pond below the mill."
>
> The librarian looks at the old map's writing. It is upside down. "Whoever drew this stood on the far bank," they say. "Turn it a half turn about the bridge."
>
> Dot turns it. Right becomes left. Down becomes up.
>
> "4 left, 3 up," says Pip. "In the hills, past the orchard."
>
> Hob Pennick, at the door with eggs, nods. "That's my ford," he says. "Wet feet, every time."

**Keeper's note** (A made-up model):
> A half turn about the bridge flips both signs: (4, −3) becomes (−4, 3). A mirror flips only one sign. That is how you can tell a turned map from a mirrored one.

### Scene 4. Two ways to the ford (Act VI, chapter 2: compare routes and keep a plan)

**Hook:** Dot wants the exchange plan written as grid moves, so any courier can follow it.

**Short:**
> "Post Office to the ford," says Dot. "The hill path is 1 left, 5 up. The bridge way is longer, but dry."

**Longer:**
> Dot chalks two routes from the post office to the high ford.
>
> "Hill path: 1 left, 5 up. Six steps."
>
> "Bridge way: 3 right, 2 up to the bridge. Then 4 left, 3 up to the ford. Twelve steps."
>
> "Twice as long," says Pip. "Why would anyone go that way?"
>
> "Because the hill path floods in spring," says {courier}. "My boots have opinions."
>
> Dot writes both routes in the plan, with a season beside each one.

**Keeper's note** (A made-up model):
> Grid moves describe a route anyone can follow. The number of steps is the route's length on the map, not the straight-line distance.

## Teach cards

Each card is under 30 words. Worked steps appear one tap at a time.

**T1. Where is it?** (A true thing about math)
> A position word needs a landmark. Say the thing, the word, then the landmark: "The hat is on top of the box."
Worked example: 1. Thing: the letter. 2. Word: under. 3. Landmark: the shelf. 4. "The letter is under the shelf."

**T2. Left and right can swap** (A true thing about math)
> Left and right depend on which way you face. When Pip faces you, his right paw is on your left side.
Worked example: 1. Face the same way as Pip: his right is your right. 2. Pip turns to face you. 3. His right paw is now on your left.

**T3. Turning on the spot** (A true thing about math)
> A turn spins you on one spot. A whole turn faces the same way again. A half turn faces the opposite way.
Worked example: 1. Pip faces the door. 2. Half turn: he faces the stove. 3. Another half turn: the door again. 4. Two halves make a whole turn.

**T4. Clockwise and counterclockwise** (A true thing about math)
> Clockwise turns the way clock hands go. Counterclockwise turns the other way. A quarter turn makes a square corner: a right angle.
Worked example: 1. Pip faces the door. 2. Quarter turn clockwise: the window. 3. His start and end make a right angle. 4. Three quarters clockwise: the counter.

**T5. Moves on a grid** (A true thing about math)
> Say across first, then up or down. Count the lines you jump, not the squares. "2 right, 3 up."
Worked example: 1. Start at the Post Office. 2. Count jumps up the lines: 1, 2, 3. 3. You reach the Bakery: 3 up.

**T6. Coordinates** (A true thing about math)
> Start at (0, 0), the corner. Along the corridor first, then up the stairs. (2, 4) means 2 along, 4 up.
Worked example: 1. Start at (0, 0). 2. Go along to 2. 3. Go up to 4. 4. The Bakery is at (2, 4).

**T7. Find the missing corner** (A true thing about math)
> A rectangle's missing corner takes its first number from one neighbor and its second number from the other. Check it, don't guess by eye.
Worked example: 1. Corners: (3, 1), (3, 3), (7, 3). 2. Below (7, 3): first number 7. 3. Beside (3, 1): second number 1. 4. Missing: (7, 1).

**T8. A slide** (A true thing about math)
> In a slide, every point moves the same way. Right adds to the first number. Up adds to the second.
Worked example: 1. Start at (1, 2). 2. Slide 3 right: (4, 2). 3. Slide 1 up: (4, 3).

**T9. A mirror** (A true thing about math)
> In a mirror, each point lands the same distance on the other side, at a square corner to the mirror line. The shape comes out flipped.
Worked example: 1. Mirror line goes up through 4. 2. Point (2, 4) is 2 left of it. 3. Go 2 right of it: (6, 4).

**T10. Four parts of the map** (A true thing about math)
> Put zero in the middle. Left of zero and below zero use minus numbers. A minus sign is a direction.
Worked example: 1. Start at (0, 0), the bridge. 2. (−2, 3): first number minus, so 2 left. 3. Then 3 up.

**T11. Which part?** (A true thing about math)
> Read the two signs. (+, +) is up right. (−, +) is up left. (−, −) is down left. (+, −) is down right.
Worked example: 1. Point (2, −3). 2. First sign plus: right. 3. Second sign minus: down. 4. Down right.

**T12. A turn about a point** (A true thing about math)
> A turn needs a center, an amount and a direction. A half turn about (0, 0) flips both signs.
Worked example: 1. Point (3, 1). 2. Half turn about (0, 0): (−3, −1). 3. Quarter turn clockwise about (0, 0): (1, −3).

**T13. How the numbers change** (A true thing about math)
> Each move has its own number rule. Start each move from the same corner and watch its numbers change.
Worked example: 1. Corner (1, 4). 2. Mirror in the up line through zero: (−1, 4). 3. Or a half turn instead: (−1, −4). 4. Or a mirror in the line y = x: (4, 1).

**T14. Same shape after the move** (A true thing about math)
> Slides, mirrors and turns never change size or shape. Only a mirror flips the shape over.
Worked example: 1. The flag's long side is 3 squares. 2. After the slide: 3. 3. After the turn: 3. 4. Same size, same shape.

### The rule table (Keeper's Desk, with T13)

The flag has corners A (1, 1), B (1, 4) and C (3, 3).

| Move | Rule | A | B | C |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Slide 3 right, 1 down | (x + 3, y − 1) | (4, 0) | (4, 3) | (6, 2) |
| Mirror in the y-axis (up line) | (−x, y) | (−1, 1) | (−1, 4) | (−3, 3) |
| Mirror in the x-axis (across line) | (x, −y) | (1, −1) | (1, −4) | (3, −3) |
| Mirror in the line y = x | (y, x) | (1, 1) | (4, 1) | (3, 3) |
| Half turn about (0, 0) | (−x, −y) | (−1, −1) | (−1, −4) | (−3, −3) |
| Quarter turn clockwise about (0, 0) | (y, −x) | (1, −1) | (4, −1) | (3, −3) |

**Column note** (Keeper's Desk):
> Mathematicians write a slide as a column: the across number on top, the up number below. "3 right, 1 down" is 3 on top, −1 below.

## Real-world and history cards

**H1. Grid squares in old China** (A true thing from history; Sapling and up)
> About 1,800 years ago in China, a mapmaker named Pei Xiu wrote rules for good maps. Two rules: draw an even grid, and use a scale.
Keeper's note:
> Pei Xiu lived from 224 to 271 and worked for the Jin court. His own maps are lost, but his rules survive in an old history book. A stone map carved in 1136 in China has a grid of squares, each standing for 100 li (an old Chinese measure of distance).

**H2. Naming chess squares** (A true thing from history; Seedling and up)
> In 1737, a chess player from Aleppo, in Syria, printed a book that named chess squares with a letter and a number.
Keeper's note:
> His name was Philipp Stamma, and his book came out in France. Today's chess names work the same way: a letter for the column, a to h, then a number for the row, 1 to 8. Across first, then up, like our coordinates. His letters for the pieces were different from today's.

**H3. Numbers for shapes** (A true thing from history; Keeper's Desk)
> In 1637, René Descartes printed a book called *La Géométrie*. It showed how to describe lines and curves with numbers.
Keeper's note:
> Grids with two numbers are called **Cartesian** after him. His own drawings did not use our two crossed number lines with four parts. Mathematicians after him built that picture from his idea.

## Problem sets

Twenty-four three-choice items put the right answer at A eight times, B eight times and C eight times.

### Set A. Where and which way (Sprout)

Pictures and words only; no numbers.

**A1 (Sprout, 3-choice)**
Prompt: Where is the red parcel?
- A. Under the box. *(tests: swapping opposite words)*
- B. On top of the box. *(right)*
- C. Over there. *(tests: a vague word with no landmark; withholds the position the picture shows)*
Hint: Look at the box first. Is the parcel higher or lower?
Explanation: The parcel sits on the box, so it is on top of the box.
Feedback A: Under is lower, beneath. Is the parcel lower?
Feedback B: Yes. Thing, word, landmark: on top of the box.
Feedback C: Over where? Name the landmark so Dot can find it.

**A2 (Sprout, 3-choice)**
Prompt: Dot's hat is between the cup and the lamp. Where is it?
- A. In the middle, with the cup on one side, the lamp on the other. *(right)*
- B. Next to the cup, on the side away from the lamp. *(tests: holding only one relation)*
- C. On top of the lamp, above the cup. *(tests: between read as above)*
Hint: Between needs two landmarks. Is the hat beside both?
Explanation: Between means in the middle of two things.
Feedback A: Yes. Beside the cup and beside the lamp.
Feedback B: That is next to the cup only. Is it near the lamp?
Feedback C: Between is in the middle, not on top.

**A3 (Sprout, 3-choice)**
Prompt: Pip faces you. He waves his right paw. Which side, for you?
- A. Your right side. *(tests: judging from your own facing)*
- B. You can't tell which paw is his right. *(tests: withholding; his facing is given)*
- C. Your left side. *(right)*
Hint: Turn around so you face the same way as Pip.
Explanation: Facing each other, his right is on your left.
Feedback A: That's your right. Pip faces you, so his sides swap.
Feedback B: You can tell. He faces you, so his right is your left.
Feedback C: Yes. Facing you, his right paw is on your left.

**A4 (Sprout, place)**
Prompt: Put the letter above the shelf. Put the stamp below the shelf.
Answer: letter higher than the shelf; stamp lower than it.
Tests: above and below swapped; both placed beside the shelf.
Hint: Above is higher. Below is lower.
Explanation: The shelf is the landmark. One thing goes higher, one lower.

**A5 (Sprout, 3-choice)**
Prompt: Pip faces the door. He makes a half turn. What does he face?
- A. The window. *(tests: a quarter turn for a half)*
- B. The door again. *(tests: a whole turn for a half)*
- C. The stove, opposite the door. *(right)*
Hint: A half turn faces the opposite way.
Explanation: Half a turn faces Pip the opposite way: the stove.
Feedback A: The window is a quarter turn. Keep going.
Feedback B: Facing the door again is a whole turn.
Feedback C: Yes. Half a turn, the opposite way.

**A6 (Sprout, 3-choice)**
Prompt: Which one is a turn?
- A. Pip spins on his spot to face the window. *(right)*
- B. Pip walks across the floor to the window. *(tests: a turn as travel)*
- C. Pip steps sideways to his left. *(tests: a sideways step as a turn)*
Hint: A turn stays on one spot.
Explanation: A turn spins on the spot. Walking moves you somewhere else.
Feedback A: Yes. His feet stay on the spot.
Feedback B: That's walking. A turn stays in one place.
Feedback C: A step moves him. A turn spins him.

### Set B. Turns and grid steps (Seedling)

Facings, seen from above: door at the top, window right, stove at the bottom, counter left.

**B1 (Seedling, 3-choice)**
Prompt: Pip faces the door. Quarter turn clockwise. What does he face?
- A. The window. *(right)*
- B. The counter. *(tests: counterclockwise for clockwise)*
- C. The stove. *(tests: a half turn for a quarter)*
Hint: Which way do clock hands go?
Explanation: Clockwise from the door, one quarter reaches the window.
Feedback A: Yes. One quarter, the way clock hands go.
Feedback B: That's a quarter turn counterclockwise.
Feedback C: The stove is two quarters away: a half turn.

**B2 (Seedling, 3-choice)**
Prompt: Pip faces the door. Three quarter turns clockwise. What does he face?
- A. The window. *(tests: stopping after one quarter)*
- B. The stove. *(tests: miscounting the quarters)*
- C. The counter. *(right)*
Hint: Count each quarter aloud: one, two, three.
Explanation: Window, stove, counter. Three quarters clockwise ends at the counter.
Feedback A: That's one quarter. Count two more.
Feedback B: That's two quarters. One more to go.
Feedback C: Yes. And one quarter counterclockwise lands there too.

**B3 (Seedling, 3-choice)**
Prompt: Pip makes a quarter turn. What corner do his start and end make?
- A. A right angle, a square corner. *(right)*
- B. A straight line. *(tests: a half turn's shape for a quarter)*
- C. No corner. Turns don't make corners. *(tests: turns and angles kept apart)*
Hint: Point where you start. Turn a quarter. Point again.
Explanation: A quarter turn always leaves a right angle between start and end.
Feedback A: Yes. A quarter turn makes a square corner.
Feedback B: A straight line comes from a half turn.
Feedback C: Point at start and end. Your arms make a corner.

**B4 (Seedling, move builder)**
Prompt: Post Office to Café. What move?
Answer: 4 right.
Worked steps: 1. Post Office (2, 1). 2. Café (6, 1). 3. Count jumps along: 4. 4. No up or down.
Tests: 4 up (the wrong direction); 6 right (the Café's number, not the move).
Hint: Same height. Count the jumps across.
Explanation: A move counts jumps, not where you land.

**B5 (Seedling, 3-choice)**
Prompt: Post Office to Bakery. What move?
- A. 3 right. *(tests: across and up swapped)*
- B. 4 up. *(tests: the Bakery's number used as the move)*
- C. 3 up. *(right)*
Hint: Is the Bakery across from the Post Office, or above it?
Explanation: From 1 up to 4 up is 3 jumps up.
Feedback A: The Bakery is straight above. The move goes up.
Feedback B: 4 is where it lands. Count the jumps from 1.
Feedback C: Yes. Three jumps up.

**B6 (Seedling, move builder)**
Prompt: Bakery to Library. What moves?
Answer: 5 right, 1 up.
Worked steps: 1. Bakery (2, 4). 2. Library (7, 5). 3. Across: 2 to 7 is 5 right. 4. Up: 4 to 5 is 1 up.
Tests: 1 right, 5 up (across and up swapped); 7 right, 5 up (landing numbers used as the move).
Hint: Do across first. Then up.
Explanation: Across moves change the first number. Up moves change the second.

Explanation: A rectangle's corner shares numbers with its two neighbors.

### Set D. Moving points and shapes (Sapling)

The flag has corners A (1, 1), B (1, 4) and C (3, 3).

**D1 (Sapling, number pair)**
Prompt: Slide (2, 3) 4 right and 2 up. Where does it land?
Answer: (6, 5).
Worked steps: 1. Right 4: 2 + 4 = 6. 2. Up 2: 3 + 2 = 5. 3. (6, 5).
Tests: (4, 7) (right added to the up number); (6, 3) (the up move left out).
Hint: Right changes the first number only.
Explanation: Do across, then up. Each changes one number.

**D2 (Sapling, 3-choice)**
Prompt: A point moves from (2, 3) to (5, 3). Describe the move.
- A. 3 right. *(right)*
- B. 5 right. *(tests: the landing number as the move)*
- C. 3 right and 3 up. *(tests: changing both numbers)*
Hint: Which number changed? By how much?
Explanation: Only the first number changed, by 3. So 3 right.
Feedback A: Yes. 2 to 5 is 3 jumps right.
Feedback B: 5 is where it lands. Count from 2.
Feedback C: The second number stayed 3. No up move.

**D3 (Sapling, 3-choice)**
Prompt: Slide the flag 3 right, 1 down. Where does B (1, 4) land?
- A. (4, 5) *(tests: down treated as up)*
- B. (0, 7) *(tests: the two moves swapped)*
- C. (4, 3) *(right)*
Hint: Right adds to the first number. Down takes from the second.
Explanation: 1 + 3 = 4 across; 4 − 1 = 3 up.
Feedback A: Down takes away. 4 − 1 is 3.
Feedback B: Right changes the first number, not the second.
Feedback C: Yes. (4, 3).

**D4 (Sapling, number pair)**
Prompt: Mirror (2, 2) in the up line through 4. Where does it land?
Answer: (6, 2).
Worked steps: 1. (2, 2) is 2 left of the line. 2. Go 2 right of the line: 4 + 2 = 6. 3. Height stays: (6, 2).
Tests: (5, 2) (counting the mirror line as 1, so one short); (4, 2) (stopping on the mirror line).
Hint: How far is the point from the mirror line?
Explanation: Same distance on the other side, along a line at a square corner to the mirror.

**D5 (Sapling, 3-choice)**

- B. Same size and same shape. *(right)*
- C. You can't tell without measuring every side again. *(tests: withholding; a slide moves every point the same)*

Explanation: A slide moves every point equally, so size and shape stay.

Feedback B: Yes. A slide keeps size and shape.
Feedback C: Every point moved the same. The sides can't change.

**D6 (Sapling, 3-choice)**
Prompt: The first flag points right. Bramble's copy points left, top still up. What move?
- A. A slide. *(tests: a slide that flips)*
- B. A mirror in an up line. *(right)*
- C. A quarter turn. *(tests: a turn that keeps the top up)*

Explanation: Only a mirror flips the shape. Top stays top, left and right swap.

## Key terms (glossary)

- **position**: where something is, compared with a landmark.
- **landmark**: the thing you describe a position from.
- **between**: in the middle of two things.
- **left and right**: sides that swap when you turn to face the other way.
- **turn**: spinning on one spot.
- **quarter turn**: a turn that makes a right angle.
- **half turn**: a turn to face the opposite way.
- **three-quarter turn**: three quarter turns in a row.
- **whole turn**: all the way around, back to the start.
- **clockwise**: turning the way clock hands go.
- **counterclockwise**: turning the opposite way to clock hands.
- **right angle**: a square corner.
- **grid**: lines crossing in squares.
- **coordinates**: two numbers for a point: along first, then up or down.
- **origin**: the point (0, 0), where you start counting.
- **quadrant**: one of the four parts of a grid around zero.
- **slide (translation)**: a move where every point goes the same way.
- **mirror (reflection)**: a flip across a mirror line.
- **turn about a point (rotation)**: a turn around a center, by an amount, in a direction.
- **congruent**: exactly the same size and shape.
- **Cartesian**: named after Descartes; used for grids with two numbers.

## Off-screen invitation (optional, grown-up-led)

**Turn on the spot** (Sprout and Seedling): stand on a sticky note. Face the door. Make a quarter turn clockwise, then name what you face. Try a half turn and a whole turn. *Grown-up note: a sticker on the right hand helps. Mixing left and right is normal early on; play, don't drill.*

**Chalk grid** (Seedling and up): chalk a small grid outside or use floor tiles. Call "2 along, 3 up" and stand on the spot. Then swap and give the moves. *Grown-up note: keep it on flat, open ground.*

## Grown-up panel

**What this teaches:** position words with a landmark; left and right from another viewpoint; whole, half, quarter and three-quarter turns, clockwise and counterclockwise, and a quarter turn as a right angle; describing grid moves; coordinates in the first quadrant and then all four; plotting and completing shapes; slides, mirrors and turns about a point, and how each changes coordinates; that all three keep size and shape.

**What it does not claim:** the valley and map are made up. It does not teach measuring angles with a protractor (spec 181 does) or enlargement and similar shapes. Mirror lines are upright, across, or the line y = x only. Body turns and real grids teach things a screen cannot.

**Sources in brief:** Pei Xiu's map rules and the 1136 Chinese grid map via encyclopedia and university sources; Philipp Stamma's 1737 chess book via encyclopedia and chess-history sources; Descartes' *La Géométrie* (1637) via encyclopedia and university sources. Full list in the source audit.
