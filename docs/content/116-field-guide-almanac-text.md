## Sky

**Prompt:** Did you see the moon? Pick the shape that looks most like it.

**Always shown:** Only look at the moon. Never look straight at the sun.

**Buttons (northern setting):**
- A thin curve, lit on the right
- Half, lit on the right
- Nearly full, lit on the right
- A full circle
- Nearly full, lit on the left
- Half, lit on the left
- A thin curve, lit on the left
- I couldn't see the moon

In the southern setting, "right" and "left" swap in every button and message.

**After a shape:** Noted. Dot drew it on the sky page.

**After "I couldn't see the moon":** That happens a lot. Clouds can hide it, and some nights the moon rises after bedtime. Sometimes it's up in the daytime instead. Have a look tomorrow afternoon.

**Already noted tonight:** Tonight's moon is in the almanac. Look again tomorrow night.

### Explanations (Sprout and Seedling)

**Is it the same moon?**
Yes. It's always the same moon. It looks different on different nights, and it changes slowly, a little each night.

**Where does the moon go at new moon?**
It's still there. On those nights we can't see the part that's lit, so it looks as if it's gone.

**Is there a pattern?**
The moon grows from a thin curve to full, then shrinks back again. From one full moon to the next takes about 29 and a half days, close to a month. The word *month* comes from *moon*. **[check]**

### Why it changes shape (Sapling)

Two pictures side by side: from far above, and from our sky.

> Sunlight always lights up half of the moon, the half facing the Sun. As the moon travels around Earth, we see different amounts of that lit half. When we can see all of it, that's a full moon. When the lit half faces away from us, that's a new moon.
>
> The dark part is **not** Earth's shadow. When Earth's shadow really does fall on the moon, that's a lunar eclipse. Eclipses only happen a few times a year, because the moon's path is a little tilted. **[check]**

## Garden neighbors

**Opening card:**
> Minibeasts are small animals with no bones inside. Insects, spiders, snails, slugs, worms and woodlice are all minibeasts. Lots of them live in gardens, and they all have jobs there.

**Care card (always before the key):**
> Look with your eyes, not your fingers. If you lift a stone or a log, ask a grown-up first, and put it back gently. Leave every minibeast where you found it.

**Start button:** I've found one. Help me name it.

### The key

Each step shows one question, two answers with lifelike pictures, **Go back**, and **I can't tell**.

| Step | Question | Answers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Does it have legs? | Yes → 4 · No → 2 |
| 2 | Does it carry a shell on its back? | Yes → Snail · No → 3 |
| 3 | Does it have feelers on its head? | Yes → Slug · No → Earthworm |
| 4 | Does it have more than eight legs? | Yes → 5 · No → 7 |
| 5 | Does it have more than twenty legs? | Yes → 6 · No → Woodlouse |
| 6 | Are there two pairs of legs on most body sections? | Yes → Millipede · No, one pair → Centipede |
| 7 | Does it have eight legs? | Yes → 8 · No, six → 9 |
| 8 | Is its body in two parts? | Yes → Spider · No, one round piece → Harvestman |
| 9 | Is its back hard and shiny, like a closed lid? | Yes → 10 · No → 11 |
| 10 | Does the lid have spots? | Yes → Ladybug · No → Beetle |
| 11 | Can you see wings? | Yes → 12 · No → 14 |
| 12 | Are the wings big, with colors or patterns? | Yes → Butterfly or moth · No → 13 |
| 13 | Does it have a narrow waist? | Yes → Bee or wasp · No → Fly |
| 14 | Does it have a narrow waist? | Yes → Ant · No, it's soft and long → Caterpillar |

**I can't tell**, by step:

| Steps | Message |
| --- | --- |
| 1, 4, 5, 7 | Legs are hard to count when something is moving. Watch it for a moment, or wait until it stops. |
| 2 | Look at its back. A shell is hard and curly, and the animal can pull itself inside. |
| 3 | Look at the front end. Feelers are soft and can stretch out and pull back in. |
| 6 | Look at one section of its body, near the middle. Count the legs on just that section. |
| 8 | Look for a thin waist partway along its body. A spider has one; a harvestman doesn't. |
| 9, 10 | Is its back smooth and shiny, with a line down the middle? |
| 11, 12, 13 | Wings can be folded flat. Wait until it moves, or look for something see-through on its back. |
| 14 | Look at where the body gets thinner between the middle and the back end. |

**Then:** Go back · Finish as "something else"

**Something else:** You found something the key can't name yet. Want to give it a name of your own? (field, optional) · Add to my field guide

**Caterpillar note (at step 14):** A caterpillar has six true legs at the front, plus soft stumpy legs behind. Count only the ones near its head. **[check]**

**Ladybug note (at step 10):** Most ladybugs have spots, but a few kinds don't.

**Woodlouse note (at step 5):** Woodlice have fourteen legs: seven pairs. **[check]**

### Build a key (Sapling)

**Intro:** Now you make the key. Choose two yes/no questions that sort these four into four different endings.

**Cards:** Spider · Ant · Snail · Woodlouse

**Candidate questions:**

| ID | Question | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `shell` | Does it have a shell? | Splits snail from the rest. |
| `eight-legs` | Does it have eight legs? | Splits spider from the rest. |
| `six-legs` | Does it have six legs? | Splits ant from the rest. |
| `any-legs` | Does it have legs? | Splits snail from the rest. |
| `big` | Is it big? | Vague: big compared with what? |
| `nice` | Is it nice? | No clear answer: people disagree. |
| `backbone` | Does it have a backbone? | Clear, but every card answers no. |

The child picks a first question, then a second question for each side that still holds more than one card.

| Situation | Message |
| --- | --- |
| A vague question | "Is it big?" is hard to answer the same way every time. Big compared with what? Try a question about something you can see. |
| A no-clear-answer question | People could answer "Is it nice?" differently. A key needs questions everyone would answer the same way. |
| A question that sends all one way | Every card answered "no", so nothing was sorted. A good question has to split this set. |
| Two cards in one ending | {a} and {b} ended up together. Add a question that tells them apart. |
| A working key | Every card has its own ending. That's a working key. |
| A second working key | That's a different key, and it works too. There's often more than one. |

### Tally

**Prompt:** Here's day {d}. Which row gets a mark?

**Buttons:** Next day · Back

**Fives (Details):** Every fifth mark crosses the other four. That makes a gate of five, so you can count by fives.

**End:** Every day is tallied.

**A row differs:** Your {row} row has {x} marks, and the log has {y} {row} days. Want to recount that row?

**Recount button:** Recount that row

**All match:** Your tally matches your log.

### Pictogram

**Key, always on screen:** Each picture stands for 1 day.

**Prompt:** Add pictures to each row until it matches your tally.

**Buttons:** + · −

**Done:** Each row matches your tally.

**Sapling option:** Make each picture stand for 2 days.

**Key with option:** Each picture stands for 2 days. Half a picture means 1 day.

**Preview question:** {row} had 7 days. How many pictures will that need now? · 3 · 3 and a half · 7

**After the preview:** 7 days is 3 whole pictures for 6 days, and half a picture for the 1 day left.

### Bar graph

**Prompt:** Raise each bar to match your tally.

**Scale:** Counting in ones · Counting in twos (Sapling)

**Done:** Your bar graph shows the same numbers as your tally and your pictogram.

### Comparing months (Sapling)

**Prompt:** This month had more {x} than last month. Does that mean next year will too?

**Weather and climate (Details):**
> Weather is what the sky is doing today. Climate is what the weather is usually like in a place over many years: how warm, how wet, how windy, season by season. One month of your log is weather. Scientists usually use about 30 years of records to describe a climate. **[check]**

**Which timescale?** Sort each card into **Weather** or **Climate**.

| Card | Pile |
| --- | --- |
| It's raining today. | Weather |
| This month had more sunny days than last month. | Weather |
| Summers here are usually warm and dry. | Climate |
| It snowed once in a place where it hardly ever snows. | Weather |
| Over many years, this town gets lots of rain in autumn. | Climate |

**A card in the other pile:**
- "It's raining today." That's about today, so it's weather.
- "This month had more sunny days…" One month is still weather. Climate needs many years.
- "Summers here are usually…" "Usually," over many summers, is climate.
- "It snowed once…" One snowy day is weather. It doesn't change what a place is usually like.
- "Over many years…" Many years of the same pattern is climate.
