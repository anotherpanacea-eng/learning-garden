## Teaching (each job freezes these pages, using its own cast names)

The first three pages are one separate, complete miniature rehearsal, in this exact order. Each page reveals just its own cue and can be revisited; stage state does not accumulate. These pages are not copied into work. Freeze actual cast names/IDs at source creation, using first and second cast members as below. Store example text without brackets.

1. “A script is a plan for a performance. A cast list tells us the characters. A cue is one part of the script. A speaker's name tells an actor whose words to say.” Example: first cast speaker says “Will you show us the letter?” No event.
2. “In this script convention, brackets hold directions for the performers. The audience can see the action without hearing these words.” Example: direction “SECOND holds up the letter.” Event second cast/letter/“a letter”. Replace SECOND with the actual second character name in the frozen words.
3. “Now the other actor replies. The name before the line tells us who speaks.” Example: second cast speaker says “Here it is.” No event. This completes the tiny question/action/reply scene.
4. “What if we say the direction instead? The audience hears the sentence. Our picture does not carry out that action. Some shows have a narrator who reads directions aloud; that is not wrong. Here a puppet can try those words too.” Example: second cast speaker says the exact direction from page2, with no event. This is an alternative frame, not a fourth cue in the miniature scene.
5. “Changing a speaker can change who knows something. Moving a cue can change what a reply seems to answer. Try your version; then compare with the writer's plan. A person may stage it another way.” No example.

## Controls and limits

Every event freezes its propName: letter → “a letter”, umbrella → “an umbrella”, paper-moon → “a paper moon”; null prop → null name. Do not use the existing Moon crunch snack as a moon prop. A simple original paper-moon drawing may be used.

Title **Rehearsal Cues**. “Choose a cue. Decide whether it is spoken or followed, then choose who speaks.” **Read this cue** is always available on Arrange. **How rehearsal works** opens teaching manually. Roles **Words to say**, **Stage direction**; **No speaker yet**. Pages **Arrange**, **Rehearse**, **Compare**. **Earlier**, **Later**, **Previous cue**, **Next cue**, **Restart rehearsal**, **Keep this script**, **Work on a copy**, **New blank version**, **Working scripts**, **Kept scripts**, **Story Loft**. Rehearsal labels **Audience words**, **Backstage direction**. A direction-only reader is **Read this direction**, distinct from **Hear audience words**. No speaker: “Choose who says this.” A neutral reading of the words remains available. Direction without an authored event: “The actors can interpret this direction together.” Comparison: “The writer's plan is one way to stage these words. Your version can tell a different story. Try both with a person. Some shows have a narrator read directions aloud; here a puppet can try those words too.”

“In this little picture, only an authored direction can show its given action. Saying those words does not make the puppet do it.” All movement is manual and illustrative. A static prop beside a highlighted actor is a symbolic cue, not realistic action animation.

“Unkept scripts wait in this open page. Keep a version to include it in Notes.” Refusal: “This version could not be kept safely. Your work and earlier scripts are unchanged.
