# The Four Beds: all child-facing text

Companion to [spec 127](../specs/127-four-beds.md). Use as written, after the same independent review and a science content check. `{guide}` is the explorer's chosen companion. Moss has no pronouns in this text. Lines marked `[check]` need a science check before build. Nothing here is scored.

## Arriving

**Place title:** The Four Beds

**First visit:**
> At the edge of the garden, Moss has dug four new beds and edged them with planks from the storm.
>
> "These four are yours," says Moss. "Grow whatever you like. But here's the thing about gardens: this year's choices change next year's soil. A good gardener thinks a few years ahead."

**Later visits:** Moss is leaning on a spade by your beds. "What's the plan this year?"

The cards use simplified game groups, not botanical families; real plants in one group do not all share the same pests or needs.

**Every report (always shown):** This is a simple model of a garden. Real soil and real plants are more complicated.

## The model crop groups

| Family | Card | Picture labels |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Beans | Beans and peas. They don't need rich soil, and they leave the soil better than they found it. | peas · runner beans · broad beans |
| Leaves | Leafy crops. Hungry: they need rich soil. | cabbage · kale · broccoli |
| Fruit | Crops we eat the fruit of. Fairly hungry. | squash · tomatoes · courgettes |
| Roots | Crops we eat the roots of. They don't need much. | carrots · beetroot · parsnips |
| Clover | A rest year. Clover and grass grow, and the soil gets better. No harvest. | clover · grass |

**Why do beans help? (Details):**
> Beans and peas have tiny living things in little bumps on their roots. Together they take something plants need from the air and leave it in the soil. So after beans, especially if their roots are left in the ground, the soil is often a little better for the next crop. `[check]`

## Soil goodness

**Meter label:** Soil goodness: {n} of 4
**About goodness (Details):**
> Plants make their own food from air, water and sunlight. But they also need minerals from the soil to grow healthy, a little like the vitamins in your food. Soil goodness is how much of those the soil has.

## The first-year walk-through (first visit)

One step per tap. Button: **Next**. Last button: **Plan my beds**.
1. "Each bed's soil starts with a goodness of 3."
2. "Each family needs a certain goodness for a good harvest. Leaves need 3. Fruit needs 2. Roots need 1. Beans need none at all."
3. "When a crop grows, it changes the soil. Leaves and fruit use some up. Beans put some back."
4. "And if a family grows in the same bed two years running, pests and diseases that like that family are waiting in the soil."
5. "So: what goes where this year?"

## Moss explains: moving the families round (after year 4, optional)

One step per tap.
1. "Here's a trick gardeners have used for a long time. Move each family to a new bed every year."
2. "Beans first. Then leaves, which love the soil the beans leave behind. Then fruit. Then roots, which don't need much. Then beans again."
3. "If each bed follows that order, starting at different places, every family grows every year, and no bed grows the same family twice in a row."
4. "Try it and see what your reports say." `[check: rotation order is a simplification of real practice]`

## Older pages

**Neighbors:**
- Toggle: Neighbors share pests
- Card: Your beds are in a row. If two beds side by side grow the same family, pests can hop from one to the other, and both do a little worse.
- Grid prompt (only after the kept four-year grid is checked to contain each of the four groups exactly once per row and column): Here's your plan as a grid. Look along each row, and down each column. What do you notice?
- Answer reveal (on tap): Each family appears once in every row and once in every column. Grids like this are called Latin squares.

**The wet bed:**
- Toggle: Add the wet bed
- Card: Down at the low corner of the orchard, the ground is always wet now. Moss has marked out a fifth bed there.
- New family card: Wet-lovers. Reeds and watercress. They love wet ground and do badly in dry beds.
- Vadoalto line (after Act III's seed exchange): Nuria's town sent reed seed. It likes wet feet.
- Harvest question: What grows best depends on the place. What would you grow in the low corner, and why?

**Weather you can't know:**
- Toggle: Weather matters
- Card: Some summers are wet and some are dry, and you plant before you know. Wet summers are hard on fruit and roots. Dry summers are hard on leaves.
- Report line: This year was {an ordinary / a wet / a dry} summer.
- Seasons mode line: Your almanac has {n} reported rain days, {m} reported sun days and {u} unreported dates in this date range. These reports do not measure how much rain fell. Choose a fictional model-weather scenario for the garden.
- Goal card: Can you find a plan that does well whatever the weather?

## Grown-up notes

- The model is a simple story about crop rotation: beans add to the soil, hungry crops use it, and the same family in the same place builds up pests. Real gardens depend on much more.
- *Soil goodness* stands for minerals, and the text keeps saying that plants make their own food from air, water and light, so the later science stays open.
- Grow with the seasons ties the garden to the real year, with the harvest arriving in autumn. Nothing is lost if a season is missed.
