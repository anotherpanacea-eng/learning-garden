# The Keeper of the Ways: story text

Companion to [spec 120](../specs/120-keeper-of-the-ways.md). Child-facing text for the build to use as written, after the same independent review as the spec. `{guide}` is the chosen companion's name. `{courier}` is the explorer's courier. Pip is a fox (he/him); Dot is a ladybug (she/her); Bramble is a girl witch (she/her); Hollis is a grey heron (they/them); Nuria is an otter (she/her). Willowbank, Willow Brook, the Sponge, Hollis and Vadoalto are fiction. Nothing here is scored.

Spanish and French text is a first draft for review by fluent readers. The Spanish uses *ustedes* throughout. Léon (kingfisher, he/him) keeps the ferry at Les Saules, the French town downstream.

## 1. The parcel

Shown once, on the garden home, after the storm film on a first visit.

> The rain has stopped. Everything drips.
>
> Pip is tugging at something wedged against the flowerpot. "It's a parcel," says Dot. "A very wet parcel."
>
> The label is smudged, but you can still read it: **To the Keeper of the Ways, Willowbank, by Willow Brook.**
>
> "Who's the Keeper of the Ways?" asks Pip.
>
> {guide} turns the parcel over. "I don't know. Somebody sent this a long way to find them."
>
> Inside, the pages are stuck together in one damp lump. "We dry them," says Dot. "Slowly. Nobody is peeling anything."

**Button:** Keep it safe
**After:** Pip puts the parcel in his satchel. He will check on it every few minutes.

**For an explorer who began before the parcel existed** (shown once, next visit to the garden home):

> Pip has been keeping something for you. "I found it after the storm," he says, "and I didn't open it. Mostly."

Then the parcel text above from "The label is smudged."

## 3. The Keeper's Pages

The almanac gains a section. **Heading:** The Keeper's Pages. **Under it:** *Drying on the bakery windowsill.* A page that hasn't come free shows its stuck edge and one word you can read, given below.

Each page has three versions: **Short** (with read-aloud), **Longer**, and **Hollis's page**, the original at the Keeper's Desk. In Hollis's page, `[smudged]` marks a word the water took. The desk invites the reader to guess the word from the rest of the page and to mark how sure they are.

### Page 1: The old map

**Edge word before it comes free:** *Sponge*

**When it comes free** (during the welcome-back supper):
> While everyone is eating, Dot slips out to check the windowsill. She comes back holding one page by its corners. "It came free," she says. "It's a map."

**Short:** An old map of our valley. The brook wanders through a big green patch called *the Sponge*. The orchard is there now.

**Longer:** It is a map of the whole valley, drawn by hand. Willow Brook doesn't run straight the way it does now. It wanders in loops through a wide green patch with tiny reeds drawn all over it. Someone has written *the Sponge* across the middle. Pip puts his paw on the spot. "That's where the orchard is," he says. "There's no Sponge there now." Dot looks at the map for a long time. "Then where did it go?"

**Hollis's page:**
> The valley, drawn in my second year of keeping. Willow Brook from the high ford to the mill. The Sponge holds the spring water and lets it go [smudged], so the mill boards are seldom needed. Reeds, frogs, a great many herons, one of them me. The bridge road runs on the dry side. Scale: one finger to a [smudged] walk.

**Guide line after reading:** "{guide}: A map is a picture of what was there when someone drew it. I wonder what changed."

### Page 2: The flood ledger

**Edge word:** *mark*

**When it comes free** (at the Act II celebration):
> The second page peels away on its own, the way a leaf comes off a puddle. It's a table of numbers.

**Short:** A list of floods. Each line says a year and how high the brook came up the mill's flood post. At the bottom: *Ask upstream.*

**Longer:** The page is a table. Each line has a year and a height, measured on the flood post at the mill, where the brook comes over its banks. The first floods are small and far apart. The later ones are higher, and closer together. At the very bottom, in tiny writing, Hollis has added two words: *Ask upstream.* "Upstream of what?" says Pip. "Upstream of us," says Dot. "Where the brook comes from."

**Hollis's page** (heights are above the brook's ordinary level):

| Year of my keeping | Height at the flood post |
| --- | --- |
| 4 | 40 cm |
| 15 | 60 cm |
| 27 | 120 cm |
| 31 | 80 cm |
| 34 | 120 cm |
| 36 | 140 cm |
| 38 | 100 cm |

> Years not written had no flood worth the ink. [smudged] the gaps grow shorter. Ask upstream.

**After the table, a new line the town adds:** The miller has scratched a fresh mark on the post, higher than any of Hollis's. *The storm: 160 cm.* The almanac offers: **Add the storm to the ledger.**

### Page 3: The Ways

**Edge word:** *ways*

**When it comes free** (at the end of Act III, while Nuria is visiting):
> Nuria is reading the drying pages over your shoulder when the third one comes free. She goes very still. "I know this writing," she says.

**Short:** A letter about *the Ways*: the paths between the river towns, and the paths the water takes. Nuria knows who wrote it.

**Longer:** The page is a letter. It says there are three Ways between Willowbank and the towns upstream: the river path, the bridge road and the high ford. Then it says there are other ways too: "the water's ways, which are where it goes when there is too much of it." Nuria puts her paw on the signature, a long thin H. "That's Hollis," she says. "Hollis taught me to read our flood post. Then they went to live by the sea."

**Hollis's page:**
> There are three Ways between Willowbank and Vadoalto: the river path, the bridge road and the high ford. Keep them open, even in winter, even when nobody seems to need them. Someone always does. There are also the water's ways, which are where it goes when there is too much of it. Those need keeping too. A town that closes them [smudged] finds out where the water goes instead. H.

### Page 4: The year of the fine harvest

**Edge word:** *harvest*

**When it comes free** (opening Act IV):
> The fourth page is short. Pip reads it out loud, then reads it again, slower.

**Short:** In the year the Sponge was drained, the orchard grew its best apples ever. Five years later came the first big flood.

**Longer:** Hollis wrote that in their twenty-second year of keeping, the town drained the Sponge to make new orchard fields, and straightened the brook so it would run past them quickly. That autumn the orchard had the best harvest anyone remembered. Five years later, the brook came 120 centimeters up the flood post. "So draining the Sponge caused the floods," says Pip. Bramble, who has been listening from the gate, snorts. "My hat and my boots both got wet that year too. Doesn't mean my hat did it."

**Hollis's page:**
> Year 22. The Sponge is drained and the brook cut straight past the new fields. The orchard keepers are very pleased and so, I admit, am I: the apples this autumn are [smudged]. Year 27. The brook to 120 at the post, the highest in my keeping. Wet years, or our doing? I have only my own post to go by. Vadoalto keeps a rain gauge. [smudged] someone should ask them.

### Page 5: For the next Keeper

**Edge word:** *next*

**When it comes free** (opening Act V):
> The rest of the pages come apart all at once. They're blank. Across the top of the first one, Hollis has written a heading and nothing else.

**Short and Longer:** *For the next Keeper.* The pages are empty. They're waiting for someone to fill them.

**Guide line:** "{guide}: Blank pages are the best kind of invitation."

### Page 6: Hollis's letter

Not in the parcel. Hollis brings it home at the end of Act VI and gives it to the explorer at supper. One version, with read-aloud.

> To whoever is keeping the record,
>
> I addressed this parcel to the Keeper of the Ways because I didn't know your name. I still don't, and that is all right.
>
> For forty years I read the flood post and walked the Ways. I wrote down what the water did, and I kept the paths open between our towns, because the brook doesn't stop at the edge of Willowbank and neither should we.
>
> My legs prefer the seaside now. So I wrapped up my pages and sent them home for someone to carry on. Then the storm came, and I thought they were lost.
>
> They found you anyway. Perhaps you mended a bridge, or wrote in the almanac, or carried a letter upstream. Perhaps you argued about the wall. Then you had already started.
>
> Anyone can be the Keeper of the Ways. The job belongs to whoever keeps the record and keeps the ways open. It's yours for as long as you want it. When someone new arrives, show them the post.
>
> Hollis

## 4. Act openings and endings

Each act starts with a **hook**: one or two sentences, 30 words at most, shown on the village scene and read aloud on request. The longer opening follows for anyone who wants it.

### Act I. After the Storm

**Hook:** The storm has passed. Pip found a wet parcel for someone called the Keeper of the Ways. Nobody knows who that is.

Act I uses the existing First Morning and supper text. Two additions:

- **Supper ending, after the existing six-table celebration:** the page 1 text in section 3.
- **Almanac line:** *Act I: After the Storm. Willowbank is mended. One page is dry.*

### Act II. A Town of Our Own

**Hook:** Willowbank is mended. Now it gets to decide what kind of town it wants to be. And Pip's old map shows a marsh that isn't there anymore.

**Opening** (first visit to the village after the supper):
> Willowbank is mended. Now it has to decide what kind of town it wants to be. The market is coming back, the garden is changing with the seasons, and someone has heard it's Ember's birthday soon. Pip has the map in his satchel. He keeps looking at the green patch.

**Chapter lines:**
- *The weekly market.* The orchard keeper's stall has the reddest apples in Willowbank. Pip shows her the map. "Marsh?" she says. "Under my trees? My grandfather never said."
- *A garden across the seasons.* The librarian has an idea. "If we'd written down what the weather did, we might have seen the storm coming. From now on, Willowbank keeps records." Dot has already started.
- *Ember's birthday.* Bramble arrives with a basket of ribbons. "My grandmother taught me to tie these on the old paths," she says, "so nobody gets lost. I just like the colors." Then, quieter: "Mostly I like the colors."
- *The first show.* At the curtain call, the baker asks Dot if she's staying in Willowbank. "I already unpacked," says Dot. "Twice."

**Ending** (the *A Town of Our Own* celebration):
> Lanterns, music, a show, too much cake. Somewhere in the middle of it, the second page comes free.
>
> As the evening ends, {courier} comes up the bridge road with a letter. It's from a town called Vadoalto, upstream. The storm came through there too.

Then page 2 and Nuria's first letter (section 5).

### Act III. Beyond Willow Bridge

**Hook:** A letter came from Vadoalto, up the river. The storm hit them too. The old path by the high ford is marked with faded ribbons.

**Opening:**
> Upstream is closer than it sounds. Vadoalto is a day's walk by the high ford, if you can find the path. The faded ribbons on the old fence posts look a lot like Bramble's.

**Chapter lines:**
- *The floating market.* Boats come down from Vadoalto loaded with reed baskets and big round loaves. Pip tries to trade one apple for one loaf. The baker from Vadoalto laughs and says they'll need to talk about sizes.
- *The seed exchange.* Vadoalto grows a tall reed that loves wet ground. "It's good for baskets and roofs," says Nuria, "and the ground near the river is always wet anyway." Willowbank's orchard keepers want reed for baskets. Vadoalto wants apple seedlings.
- *The route by the high ford.* "Ribbons," says {courier}, at the first fork. "Blue on the left post. That must be the way."
- *Letters up and down the river.* Letters go up to Nuria and down to Léon with {courier}.

**Ending** (*Both Ways Across Willow Bridge*):
> A parcel of apple seedlings goes up the river. A bundle of reeds and a jar of Vadoalto honey come down. Nuria comes with it, to see the mill. While she's reading over your shoulder, the third page comes free.

Then page 3.

### Act IV. The Town Has Two Stories

**Hook:** The pages say the town drained the Sponge, and then the floods got worse. Did one cause the other? Everyone has an opinion.

**Opening:**
> Now everyone has read the pages, and everyone has an opinion. Some people say the floods are just weather. Some say the town brought them on itself, the year it drained the Sponge. Bramble says both sides should stop shouting and look at the numbers. Then she starts shouting about the numbers.

**Chapter lines:**
- *The old map and Hollis's account.* Page 4, with Bramble's line about her hat.
- *A puzzling change.* "The bottom corner of my orchard is always wet now," says the orchard keeper. "Reeds are coming up. I never planted reeds."
- *The newspaper's two explanations.* The Willowbank Gazette runs two headlines side by side: **JUST A BAD STORM, SAYS MILLER** and **WE MADE IT WORSE, SAYS LIBRARIAN**. Pip reads both twice. "They can't both be right," he says. "Maybe they can," says {guide}. "A bit."
- *What other places did.* The librarian opens the Travelers' Archive. "Willowbank isn't the first town to have this argument. Let's see what others decided, and what happened after."
- *The proposal for the low fields.* The bell rings for a town meeting.

**The three plans** (shown on the meeting board; the explorer can open each):

| Plan | What it is | What it costs | What it protects |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The Wall** | A higher flood wall along the brook through town. | Stone, and many hands to build it. The water still has to go somewhere: downstream, past the wall. | The orchard stays exactly as it is. Everything behind the wall stays dry. |
| **The Sponge** | Let the lowest orchard fields go back to marsh. | Some apple trees, and some of the orchard keepers' living. They would trade reed with Vadoalto instead. | The marsh holds water and lets it go slowly, so less of it reaches the mill at once. Frogs. Herons. |
| **Some of Each** | A lower wall above the mill, and a smaller wet meadow at the orchard's low corner. | A little of both costs. Some trees, some stone. | Some of both protections. Nobody gets everything they wanted. |

**Bramble at the meeting:**
> "Everyone keeps talking about frogs. What about the orchard keepers? Those trees are their whole year. If you want them to give up their fields, you'd better be very sure, and you'd better help them with what comes after."

**Someone changes their mind** (the line depends on the evidence the explorer opened; one is shown):
- After the model runs: "I thought the Sponge was just a soggy field. But I watched the same rain take twice as long to reach the mill. I've changed my mind about that part." (the orchard keeper)
- After the archive: "I thought nobody ever undid a wall. Then I read about the Dutch rivers. I'm not saying we copy them. I'm saying it's been done." (the miller)
- After Vadoalto's rain gauge: "I was sure it was all the Sponge. But the last few years were wetter too. It's probably both. I hate it when it's both." (Pip)

**Keeping a plan:**
**Prompt:** Which plan would you keep for Willowbank?
**Choices:** The Wall · The Sponge · Some of Each
**Optional:** *Add a worry* (a choice of three authored worries for each plan, plus "No worry to add")
**After choosing:** "Your plan goes in the almanac, with your name for it on the cover. People will keep arguing. That's allowed."

**Ending** (*The Map We Changed*):
> The meeting goes late. Nobody agrees about everything. But the map on the meeting board has new pencil on it now, and it isn't the same map it was this morning.

### Act V. A Question of Your Own

**Hook:** The last pages are blank, headed *For the next Keeper*. What do you want to find out?

**Opening:** page 5.
> "So," says {guide}. "What do you want to find out?"

**Project path invitations** (each opens the matching authored path from spec 49):
- *A night garden.* "Some flowers only open in the dark. Who visits them?"
- *The Willowbank Gazette.* "Willowbank has a lot of news and no newspaper."
- *A sky-watching night.* "The library has a telescope nobody has used in years."
- *A travelling show.* "Vadoalto has never seen a Willowbank show."
- *A new courier route.* "There must be a quicker way to the coast."
- *The Sponge, again.* "Somebody should keep watching the low corner."

**Ending** (*A Question of Our Own*):
> Your project is bound into the Keeper's Pages, with its first version and the one after. On the last line, you've written one thing it can't tell us yet. Hollis would have liked that line.

### Act VI. The Opening of the Ways

**Hook:** Willowbank and Vadoalto want to keep the Ways open for good. Then the rain starts, and it doesn't stop.

**Opening:**
> Willowbank and Vadoalto want to keep the Ways open for good: goods down the river, news and seeds up. That means deciding who carries what, on which Way, and how often.

**Chapter lines:**
- *Choose a partner and a purpose.* Nuria writes: "Our bakers want your apples. Your basket-makers want our reed. Everybody wants your café's honey cake, which seems unfair, since the honey is ours."
- *Compare routes and keep a plan.* "The river path is fastest, but it floods. The bridge road is slow but always dry. The high ford is short, and only safe in summer."
  **Prompt:** Keep this exchange plan? **Choices:** Keep it · Make it smaller · Not yet
  **After keeping:** "Nuria will get a copy. So will Bramble, who says she's only reading it for the ribbons."

**The long rain:**
> It rains for three days. Upstream, Nuria watches Vadoalto's flood post climb past the third mark, then the fourth. She writes one line, and {courier} runs.
>
> **The brook is rising at Vadoalto. You have until evening.**
>
> In Willowbank, the bell rings. Everyone knows what to do, because they've talked about nothing else for months.
>
> Then Dot remembers Les Saules. "If it's coming here," she says, "it's going there next."

**Passing the warning on** (the explorer sends it; Sprout taps **Send**, Seedling chooses the French sentence that matches the English, Sapling and the desk write it from the word cards):
> **The brook is rising. It will reach you tomorrow morning. From Willowbank.**
> **Le ruisseau monte. Il arrivera chez vous demain matin. De la part de Willowbank.**

**After sending:** {courier} is already halfway down the river path.

Then the scene for the explorer's kept plan:

- **The Wall:**
  > The water climbs the new wall and stops. Behind it, the orchard is dry and the mill boards hold. Downstream, past the end of the wall, the water spreads wide over the meadow by Léon's ferry, and the ferry can't run for two days. The wall did what walls do. It kept this side safe and sent the water on.

- **The Sponge:**
  > The water reaches the low fields and spreads out. The reeds bend under it. It slows. The brook reaches the mill an hour later than it used to, and it never comes over the boards. At the low corner, three apple trees stand up to their knees in water. The orchard keeper sighs, and goes to check on the reeds.

- **Some of Each:**
  > The wall above the mill turns the water toward the wet meadow, which fills like a bath. The mill boards hold, just. Some of the orchard gets its feet wet. Nobody got everything they wanted. Nobody lost everything, either.

Every version ends:
> The bridge holds. The mill holds. Everything you mended holds. The miller scratches a new mark on the flood post, and writes the date beside it, and then, after a moment, writes *Warned in time.*

**The first journey:**
> The rain passes. The first regular parcel goes up the river under your plan: apples, two of Pip's drawings and a honey-cake recipe that Vadoalto says it will improve. {courier} comes back the next evening with reed bundles and a note: *Same time next week?*

**Welcome someone new:**
> On the way back from the landing, you see a tall grey heron coming slowly up the river path, stopping to look at the brook every few steps.
>
> "Hollis!" says Nuria.
>
> Hollis looks at the flood post for a long time. Then at the almanac. Then at you.
>
> "I hear my parcel got a bit wet," they say.

Then page 6, Hollis's letter.

**Ending** (*The Opening of the Ways*):
> Bramble ties a ribbon on each Way: blue for the river path, gold for the bridge road, red for the high ford. "So nobody gets lost," she says. "And because I like the colors."
>
> Down at the bridge, someone new is standing with an unfamiliar parcel, looking at the label like it might bite. Dot gets there first.
>
> "I was new once," she says. "Come and see the post. {guide} and I will show you. Well. Mostly the Keeper will."
>
> She looks at you.

**After the ending, the village line becomes:** *Willowbank, by Willow Brook. The Ways are open.*

## 5. Letters up and down the river

Shown in the existing letter-and-reader pattern, at three lengths. The explorer chooses English, Spanish, or both side by side.

### First letter (end of Act II)

**English, short:**
> Hello, Willowbank! The storm came here too. Our brook rose very high. Can we compare notes? Write back!
> Nuria, Vadoalto

**English, longer:**
> Hello, Willowbank!
> The storm came through Vadoalto too. Our flood post says the brook rose higher than anyone here can remember. Did yours? I am learning to keep our post, and I would like to compare notes with whoever keeps yours. Will you write back?
> Nuria, apprentice at the flood post, Vadoalto

**Spanish, short:**
> ¡Hola, Willowbank! La tormenta también pasó por aquí. Nuestro arroyo subió muchísimo. ¿Comparamos notas? ¡Escríbanme!
> Nuria, Vadoalto

**Spanish, longer:**
> ¡Hola, Willowbank!
> La tormenta también pasó por Vadoalto. Nuestro poste de crecidas dice que el arroyo subió más alto de lo que nadie aquí recuerda. ¿Y el de ustedes? Estoy aprendiendo a cuidar nuestro poste y me gustaría comparar notas con quien cuide el suyo. ¿Me escriben?
> Nuria, aprendiz del poste de crecidas, Vadoalto

### Léon's first letter (Act III)

**English, short:**
> Hello, Willowbank! I'm Léon. I keep the ferry at Les Saules, downstream. It's running again. Send news!

**English, longer:**
> Hello, Willowbank!
> My name is Léon. I keep the ferry at Les Saules, where your brook joins the big river. After the storm, the water came over our landing and the ferry couldn't cross for a week. It's running again now. Nuria from Vadoalto says you are keeping records. Will you send us yours? We'll send ours.
> Léon

**French, short:**
> Bonjour, Willowbank ! Je m'appelle Léon. Je garde le bac aux Saules, en aval. Il fonctionne de nouveau. Envoyez des nouvelles !

**French, longer:**
> Bonjour, Willowbank !
> Je m'appelle Léon. Je garde le bac aux Saules, là où votre ruisseau rejoint la grande rivière. Après la tempête, l'eau est montée sur notre embarcadère et le bac n'a pas pu traverser pendant une semaine. Maintenant, il fonctionne de nouveau. Nuria, de Vadoalto, dit que vous tenez des registres. Vous nous envoyez les vôtres ? Nous vous enverrons les nôtres.
> Léon

### Léon's reply after the long rain (Act VI)

> **English:** Thank you! Your warning came in time. We tied up the ferry and moved everything up the hill. Next time it rains, we'll pass it on too. Léon
>
> **French:** Merci ! Votre message est arrivé à temps. Nous avons attaché le bac et tout monté sur la colline. La prochaine fois qu'il pleut, nous le ferons passer aussi. Léon

### The rain gauge letter (Act IV)

**English, longer:**
> Dear Willowbank,
> You asked whether our rain gauge goes back far enough. It does! Our grandparents started it. I have copied out the big rains for you, ten years at a time. Some of the years were wetter. Some were not. I don't think it settles your argument, but I think it helps.
> Nuria

**Spanish, longer:**
> Querido Willowbank:
> Me preguntaron si nuestro pluviómetro es lo bastante antiguo. ¡Sí lo es! Lo empezaron nuestros abuelos. Les he copiado las lluvias grandes, de diez en diez años. Algunos años fueron más lluviosos. Otros, no. No creo que esto resuelva su discusión, pero creo que ayuda.
> Nuria

**The copied table** (fictional; aligned to Hollis's years of keeping):

| Years of Hollis's keeping | Big rains at Vadoalto |
| --- | --- |
| 1 to 10 | 6 |
| 11 to 20 | 5 |
| 21 to 30 | 6 |
| 31 to 40 | 8 |

The table is built to support both stories in part. Big rains were steady across the first three decades while floods at Willowbank rose after year 22; the last decade was also wetter. An honest reader concludes "probably both," which is Pip's line in Act IV.

## 10. Archive pack outline: Valencia, 1957

Joshua chose this as the next archive pack (2026-10-23). This is an outline for the pack's own spec and source review. Every fact below must be checked against primary or official sources before any child text is final; the dates marked *(check)* disagree across the sources consulted so far.

**Title:** *The River That Moved* / *El río que se mudó*

**Why it belongs:** Valencia had a terrible flood, then made two big choices. The city moved its river, and its people turned the empty riverbed into a garden. Act IV's argument asks what a town should do about its river; here is a real city that answered, in two parts, with people disagreeing along the way.

**Timeline for the pack:**

| When | What happened |
| --- | --- |
| 14 October 1957 | The Turia flooded Valencia, in two waves on the same day. Much of the city was under water. Many people died. |
| July 1958 | The national government chose the *Plan Sur*: dig a new channel for the river around the south of the city. |
| 23 December 1961 | A national law (Ley 81/1961, published in the BOE) made the plan official. This is a public-domain Spanish primary source. |
| 22 December 1969 | The new channel officially opened; work went on until about 1973. It is about 12 km long; sources give its width as 175, 200 or 250 m *(check)*. |
| 1966 | A city plan proposed building a road along the dry old riverbed. |
| 1970s | Neighbors campaigned for a park instead, under the slogan *El llit del Túria és nostre i el volem verd*: "The Turia's bed is ours, and we want it green." |
| 1978 or 1979 *(sources disagree)* | The city council decided the whole old riverbed would be a garden. The Keeper's Desk case turns this disagreement into a task. |
| 1981 | The architect Ricardo Bofill was asked to plan the park. |
| 1986 *(check)* | The first parts of the garden opened. Today it runs for about 9 km through the city *(check)*. |

**Short text (English):** In 1957, the river in Valencia flooded the city. Afterward, the city dug a new path for the river, around the outside. The old riverbed was left dry. Some people wanted a road there. Neighbors wanted a garden. Today it is a long, green park.

**Short text (Spanish):** En 1957, el río de Valencia inundó la ciudad. Después, la ciudad excavó un camino nuevo para el río, por fuera. El cauce viejo quedó seco. Algunas personas querían una carretera allí. Los vecinos querían un jardín. Hoy es un parque largo y verde.

**The slogan, for older learners:** The neighbors wrote it in Valencian, the language many people in Valencia speak alongside Spanish. In Spanish it would be *El cauce del Turia es nuestro y lo queremos verde.* Comparing the two is a small lesson in how close the languages are.

**Jobs, one per depth:**

| Depth | Job |

**Care with the hard part.** The flood killed people; official and unofficial counts differ widely. Child text says "many people died" once, plainly, with no number in the short version. The desk version may give the sourced figures with their disagreement, which is itself a lesson in sources. A grown-up note says that Valencia flooded again in 2024, that this pack is about 1957, and that it does not cover the recent flood; per spec 120, the pack does not use it.
