## Six word cards and thirty frames

### seed

Letters s,e,e,d. Phonemes s,i,d. Grown-up reference /sid/.
Context: A seed is on the prop table.
Meaning: A seed is a plant part from which a new plant can grow.
Art key seed395. Alt: Two pictures: a large seed on the prop table, then a matching seed in a pot of soil with a small green sprout and two seed leaves.
Frames:
1. Read seed together. Your partner says the whole word.
2. Point to s. Your partner says the first sound.
3. Point to ee. Your partner says the vowel sound in seed.
4. Point to d. Your partner says the last sound.
5. Four letters, three sounds in our model of seed. The two e letters together represent one vowel sound here.
Adult guide: The reference is /s i d/. Model /s/, /i/ and /d/ without adding a vowel to either consonant. The two written e letters form one vowel spelling in this word.

### head

Letters h,e,a,d. Phonemes h,ɛ,d. Grown-up reference /hɛd/.
Context: The puppet has a round head.
Meaning: The head is the body part with the face. This picture shows a puppet's head.
Art key head395. Alt: A large close-up of a puppet's round head and face is shown above a small cropped neck and shoulders.
Frames:
1. Read head together. Your partner says the whole word.
2. Point to h. Your partner says the first sound.
3. Point to ea. Your partner says the vowel sound in head, like the vowel heard in bed.
4. Point to d. Your partner says the last sound.
5. Four letters, three sounds in our model of head. The same letters ea can represent a different vowel in another word.
Adult guide: The reference is /h ɛ d/. The earlier bed card can help recall this vowel. Bead and head differ in both first phoneme and vowel, while their final /d/ is shared. Head is a selected alternative for ea, not a rule that every ea has /ɛ/. The partner may supply the comparison word bed; no independent reading of bed is assumed.

## Three comparisons

seed/feed — title seed and feed; controls Compare seed and feed on either card. Text: Say seed and feed together. The first sound changes. The vowel and last sound stay the same in our model. In both words, ee represents one vowel sound. Highlight first group in both.

meet/meat — title meet and meat; controls Compare meet and meat. Text: Say meet and meat together. All three sounds are the same in our model, but the words have different spellings and meanings. Here ee and ea represent the same vowel sound. Highlight middle group in both.

bead/head — title bead and head; controls Compare bead and head. Text: Say bead and head together. Their first sounds and their vowel sounds differ; their last sound is the same. The letters ea represent different vowels in these two words. Highlight middle group in both; do not hide the changed first consonant in the explanation.

## Extra grown-up interpretation

This set uses the General American phoneme model /i/ for the long-e vowel and /ɛ/ in head. Three dots represent three phonemes, not syllables or measured sound lengths. The printed words, meanings and pictures are supplied. A partner may model every word; counters and visits do not establish independent reading or spelling. Other words use these spellings differently. This adds no accepted audio, recording or oral assessment.

Compare labels cover all six IDs: seed/feed → Compare seed and feed; meet/meat → Compare meet and meat; bead/head → Compare bead and head. Each side of each pair has its own key.

Artist requirement for meat: Make the drumstick recognizable and distinct from the existing sliced-ham picture. This instruction is not alt text and is not read as part of the scene.
