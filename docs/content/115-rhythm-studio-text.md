Companion to [spec 115](../specs/115-rhythm-studio.md). Use as written, after the same independent review and a music-content check. `{guide}` is the explorer's chosen companion. Pip is a fox (he/him), Dot a ladybug (she/her), Bramble a girl witch (she/her) with a magpie. Nothing here is scored.

Rhythm notation in this file uses the spec's codes: `w` whole, `h` half, `q` quarter, `e` eighth, `dh` dotted half, `dq` dotted quarter, and `r` before a code for a rest (`rq` is a quarter rest).

## Listen first

Shown on the first visit, one step per tap. The bar fills as each line plays. Button: **Next**. Last button: **Start making**.

1. "Here's one **whole note**. It lasts the whole bar: ONE two three four."
2. "Here are two **half notes**. Each one lasts half the bar. Together they last just as long as the whole note."
3. "Here are four **quarter notes**. Each is a quarter of the bar. Still the same bar, just cut into more pieces."
4. "Now you try. Fill a bar any way you like."

## Details panels

**What's a bar?**
Music is often counted in groups of beats. Each group is a bar. In this studio most bars have four beats: ONE two three four. Fill a bar, and the next one begins.

**Why is a half note called half?**
A half note lasts half of a four-beat bar: two beats. Two half notes fill the bar. A quarter note lasts a quarter of the bar, so four of them fill it. An eighth note lasts an eighth of the bar, so it takes eight.

**What's a rest?**
A rest is a planned silence. It still takes up time in the bar. Musicians count through a rest, the same as through a note.

**What does the dot do?** (Sapling cards)
A dot makes a note half as long again. A half note lasts 2 beats. Half of 2 is 1, so a dotted half note lasts 2 and 1 more: 3 beats. A quarter note lasts 1 beat, so a dotted quarter note lasts 1 and a half.

**Bars of three** (Sapling cards)
Some music counts in threes: ONE two three, ONE two three. Waltzes do. In a bar of three beats, a whole note won't fit, because it needs four.

## Steady beats

**`hammer-four`**
- **Card:** The bridge crew hammers four times in every bar, steady and even. Make a bar of four hammer blows, then play it.
- **Met:** Four quarter notes fill the bar. Each one is a quarter of the bar, so four quarters make one whole. Hear the beat, and you'll find each hammer blow lands on it.
- **Not yet:** The crew wants four even blows in one full bar. Your bar has {summary}. Try four notes that are all the same length.

**`long-and-short`**
- **Card:** The rope crew pulls long, then short, short. Make a bar with one long sound and two short ones. Where will you put the long one?
- **Met:** One half note and two quarter notes fill the bar: 2 beats and 1 and 1. Try moving the long note to a different place. Does the rhythm feel different?
- **Not yet:** This card asks for one half note and two quarter notes in a full bar. Your bar has {summary}.

**`rest-for-breath`**
- **Card:** Singers need to breathe. Make a full bar with a rest somewhere in it.
- **Met:** Your bar has a rest in it, and it's still full. The rest takes up time, just like a note. Where would you take a breath?
- **Not yet:** This card asks for a full bar with at least one rest and at least one note. Your bar has {summary}.

**`own-beat-sprout`**
- **Card:** Make any rhythm for any job in the garden. Then play it for someone and tell them what it's for.
- **Kept:** That's your rhythm. Who would use it, and for what job?

**End of Steady beats:** That's every Steady beats card. Try *Same length, new rhythm*, or make another of your own.

## Same length, new rhythm

**`same-as-half`**
- **Card:** Bar 1 has one half note and a half rest. In bar 2, make sounds that last exactly as long as that half note, then a half rest. Use quarter notes. Then try eighth notes.
- **Met, quarters:** Two quarter notes last as long as one half note: 1 beat and 1 beat make 2. Now try it with eighth notes.
- **Met, eighths:** Four eighth notes last as long as one half note too: half a beat four times makes 2 beats. Same time, more pieces.
- **Met, mixed:** You mixed note lengths and still filled the same time as the half note. That works too.
- **Not yet:** Bar 2 needs sounds for the first 2 beats and silence for the last 2. Your bar 2 has {summary}.

**`two-ways-whole`**
- **Card:** Fill bar 1 one way and bar 2 a different way. Both bars must be full.
- **Met:** Two different rhythms, and each one fills a whole bar. Play them both. They take exactly the same time.
- **Not yet, same:** Your two bars are the same. Change something in bar 2.
- **Not yet, not full:** Both bars need to be full. {bar} has {space} left.

**`echo`**
- **Card:** Bramble's magpie taps a rhythm on the window. Can you build it again?
- **Bramble, on starting:** "Bet you can't catch this one."
- **Bramble, after a match by ear:** "Fine. You caught it. My magpie has a trickier one."
- **Bramble, after a match with the rhythm shown:** "Looking is allowed. I looked at plenty of things when I was learning."
- **Bramble, on a different rhythm:** "Close. Listen again: that isn't quite what my magpie tapped." The bar is kept as it is.
- **Show me the rhythm:** Here it is. Build it from the picture, then play both and listen.

## Threes, dots and two parts

**`waltz`**
- **Card:** The café is holding a dance, and this dance counts in threes: ONE two three. Fill two bars of three beats.
- **Met:** Two full bars of three. Turn on Hear the beat and count along: ONE two three, ONE two three.
- **Not yet:** Both three-beat bars need to be full. {bar} has {space} left.

**`dotted-long`**
- **Card:** Fill a three-beat bar with just one note.
- **Met:** A dotted half note lasts 3 beats: a half note's 2, and half as much again. It fills the bar on its own.
- **Not yet:** Look for a note that lasts exactly 3 beats. What does the dot do?

**`dotted-swing`**
- **Card:** Try a dotted quarter note followed by an eighth note: long, short. Fill a four-beat bar with that pattern twice.
- **Met:** Long-short, long-short. 1 and a half beats plus half a beat makes 2, and twice makes 4. That uneven pattern is what gives a lot of music its swing.
- **Not yet:** This card asks for dotted quarter, eighth, dotted quarter, eighth. Your bar has {summary}.

**`two-bells`**
- **Card:** Two bells ring on a steady count. Choose how often each one rings, then find when they ring together again.
- **Controls:** Bell A rings every \_\_ eighths · Bell B rings every \_\_ eighths · (2 to 6) · Play the bells
- **Prediction (optional):** When will they ring together again? Tap the cell.
- **After play:** Bell A rang every {a} and Bell B every {b}. They met again at {m}, because {m} is the first count that both {a} and {b} fit into evenly. That's called a **common multiple**.
- **Cross-rhythm note** (shown for 2 and 3, or 3 and 2): Musicians call two-against-three a cross-rhythm. Listen to how the bells seem to chase each other.
- **Same number:** Both bells ring every {a}, so they ring together every time. Try two different numbers.
- **One fits the other** (for example 2 and 4): Every time Bell B rings, Bell A rings too, because {b} is a multiple of {a}.
- **Past the timeline:** They don't meet again on this timeline. Can you work out where they would?
- **Prediction matched:** Your prediction matched.
- **Prediction different:** You predicted {p}. They met at {m}.

**`own-beat-sapling`**
- **Card:** Make a waltz, a march, or a rhythm of your own. Choose four beats or three for each bar. Then tell someone which you made, and why it fits.
- **Bar length choice:** Four beats · Three beats
- **Kept:** Play it for someone. Could they march to it, or waltz?

**End of Threes, dots and two parts:** That's every card. Every rhythm you kept is still here.

## Off-screen invitations

Shown as plain text on each group's last card. Nothing is recorded.

- Ask a grown-up to tap a steady beat on the table. Clap your bar along with it.
- Find something at home that makes a long sound and something that makes a short sound.
- Sing a song you know. Clap once for every word part. Are some claps longer than others?
- (Threes, dots and two parts) March around the room counting in fours, then waltz counting in threes. Which is harder to keep going?
