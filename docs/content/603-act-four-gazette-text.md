**Title** (script, chapter heading). The newspaper's two explanations, and what other places did

**Short** (script). The Gazette prints two headlines. They can't both be right. Or can they? The archive shows what other places did.

**Longer** (script; the headline bold is dropped). The *Willowbank Gazette* comes out with two headlines side by side, because the librarian, who edits it, could not decide between them: JUST A BAD STORM, SAYS MILLER and WE MADE IT WORSE, SAYS LIBRARIAN. Pip reads both twice. "They can't both be right," he says. "Maybe they can," says {guide}. "A bit." Then the librarian opens the Travelers' Archive. "Willowbank isn't the first town to have this argument," they say. "Let's see what other places decided, and what happened after. Real places. Real floods."

**Keeper's note** (script, without its editorial parenthetical). The archive's packs are real history, with sources. Paris measured its great flood of 1910 and studied it. Valencia moved its whole river after 1957. The Netherlands, famous for its dikes, chose in many places to give its rivers more room.
