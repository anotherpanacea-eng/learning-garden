# Keeper's Desk, Case 10: What should Willowbank write down?

Companion to [spec 118](../specs/118-keepers-desk-entry.md) (conventions, records and acceptance) and [spec 120](../specs/120-keeper-of-the-ways.md) (Act II, "A garden across the seasons"). Status: **proposed case text, 2026-10-23. Not reviewed, not build-ready.** Fiction throughout.

Case ID `town-record`. Appears with Act II's chapter *A garden across the seasons*, when the librarian says "From now on, Willowbank keeps records." It must not use page 2 (the flood ledger), which dries at the end of Act II.

## 1. Why this case

Spec 120's four-depths table gives the desk this job in Act II: "Design Willowbank's record. What should the town write down about the brook, how often, and where? What would we wish we had written, the next time it floods?" Every later flood case depends on records someone chose to keep, and most of their limits come from choices made here: what counts, how it's measured, how often, and what happens on a missed day. This case makes the explorer the one who chooses, before they discover in Act IV how much Hollis's choices mattered.

## 2. Hook

> The librarian has a fresh, empty book open on the desk. The first page says *Willowbank Records* in her best handwriting, and nothing else.
>
> "I said we'd keep records," she says. "Then I opened the book and realised I have no idea what to write. 'The brook looked high' isn't a record. Is it?"

Buttons: **Help design the book** · **Later**.

## 3. Steps

1. **What can be written down?** (measurable against vague; units and zero)
2. **How often?** (sampling in time; what a schedule misses)
3. **The record sheet** (designing a table; missing readings)
4. **What we'd wish we had** (a written plan with its limits)

Steps open in any order.

## 4. Step 1: What can be written down?

**Learn card** (Marble research for Recording Data: record in the moment, and every record should answer a question):

> A useful record is something two people would write the same way. "The brook looked high" depends on who looked. "The brook was at 52 cm on the flood post at 09:00" doesn't.
>
> A measurement needs three things: a **number**, a **unit**, and a **zero** (what you measured from).

**Guided first task (1a):** the first two cards sorted together.

Tasks:

- **1a.** Sort each into **A record two people would write the same way** or **Depends who's writing**:
  - "The brook was high today." *(depends)*
  - "The brook was at 52 cm on the flood post at 09:00." *(same)*
  - "It rained a lot." *(depends)*
  - "The rain jar held 18 mm at 09:00, emptied after reading." *(same)*
  - "The ford was too deep to cross on foot." *(depends, a little: replies note that a yes/no record can be useful if everyone agrees what counts)*
  - "Three carts crossed before noon." *(same)*
- **1b.** "The flood post's zero is the brook's ordinary level. Why does the record need to say so?" Choices:
  - "So someone reading it in fifty years knows what 52 cm is measured from." ← fits the learn card
  - "It doesn't: everyone knows where the zero is."
  - "So the numbers look more official."

  Reply to the second: "Everyone knows *now*. Will they in fifty years?"
- **1c.** Rewrite one vague card as a record. Free text, with **Choose and adapt** starters: "The brook was at __ cm on the flood post at __:__."; "The rain jar held __ mm."

**I'm stuck (1a):**

1. *Hint:* "Could two people disagree about it while both being honest?"
2. *Similar example:* "'The soup is hot' depends who tastes it. 'The soup is 70 degrees' doesn't. Now: 'The brook was high today.'"
3. **Skip for now.**

## 5. Step 2: How often?

**Question panel** (the miller's notes from the week of a small flood, read every six hours; heights in cm above the brook's ordinary level):

| Day | 03:00 | 09:00 | 15:00 | 21:00 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mon | 20 | 20 | 21 | 20 |
| Tue | 20 | 22 | 22 | 21 |
| Wed | 21 | 23 | 26 | 30 |
| Thu | 45 | 60 | 70 | 50 |
| Fri | 35 | 28 | 25 | 23 |
| Sat | 22 | 21 | 21 | 20 |
| Sun | 20 | 20 | 20 | 20 |

**Learn card:**

> A record only catches what happens when someone is looking. Read the brook once a week, and a flood that comes and goes in a day might never appear.
>
> Reading more often catches more, but it costs more: someone has to go out to the post every time, in the rain, in the dark.

**Guided first task (2a):** the Monday 09:00 cell highlighted.

Tasks (Check my working):

- **2a.** If the town read the brook **once a week, Monday at 09:00**, what's the highest it would have recorded this week? *Answer: **20 cm**.*
- **2b.** **Once a day at 09:00**? *Answer: 20, 22, 23, 60, 28, 21, 20; highest **60 cm**.*
- **2c.** **Once a day at 21:00**? *Answer: 20, 21, 30, 50, 23, 20, 20; highest **50 cm**.*
- **2d.** **Every six hours**? *Answer: **70 cm**, Thursday 15:00.*
- **2e.** How many readings a year would each schedule take? *Answers: weekly **52**; daily **365**; every six hours 365 × 4 = **1,460**.*
- **2f.** "Could even every-six-hours have missed the real highest point?" Choices:
  - "Yes. The brook could have peaked between 15:00 and 21:00 on Thursday." ← fits the table
  - "No. Four readings a day catches everything."
  - "Only if somebody read the post wrongly."

  Reply to the second: "Between two readings there are six hours nobody saw. What could happen in them?"
- **2g.** "Which schedule would you choose?" Not marked. Choices: weekly; daily; daily, and every six hours whenever the brook is above 30 cm. Each reply names what it costs and what it might miss. The reply to the third adds: "That's how a lot of real records work: more often when it matters."

**I'm stuck (2b):**

1. *Hint:* "Read down the 09:00 column only. Which number is biggest?"
2. *Similar example:* "If you only read at 03:00, you'd see 20, 20, 21, 45, 35, 22, 20: highest 45. Now the 09:00 column."
3. **Skip for now.**

**Replies to likely errors:**

| Entered | Misconception | Reply |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 70 for 2b | Reading the whole table instead of one column | "70 is the highest of all the readings. Someone reading only at 09:00 wouldn't see it." |
| 365 for 2d's reading count (2e, every six hours) | Forgetting four readings a day | "That's one a day. Every six hours is how many a day?" |
| 30 for 2c | Reading the Wednesday row only | "Read down the 21:00 column for all seven days." |

## 6. Step 3: The record sheet

**Learn card** (Marble research for Recording Data: fix the headings before you start, so the record can be added up later):

> A record sheet is a table you draw **before** you start. Decide the columns, the units and the zero first. If you change them halfway, nobody can compare the first half with the second.
>
> And decide what to write when a reading is missed. A blank could mean "nothing happened" or "nobody looked". Those are very different.

Tasks:

- **3a.** Build the sheet. Choose columns from cards (any order, any number): date; time; brook height (cm, from the post's zero); rain in the jar (mm, emptied after reading); who read it; anything unusual; weather picture. **Check my working** checks only that the sheet has a date, a time and at least one measurement with a unit; everything else is the explorer's choice and gets a reply, never a mark.
- **3b.** "What should someone write when they couldn't get to the post?" Choices:
  - "*Not read*, with the reason." ← fits the learn card
  - "Leave it blank."
  - "Copy yesterday's number."

  Reply to the second: "In fifty years, will a reader know if a blank means a quiet day or a missed one?" Reply to the third: "Then the record says the brook was a height nobody saw. Someone might trust it."
- **3c.** "The baker offers to read the post when she walks past, 'whenever'. Is that a good idea?" Choices: "Yes, more readings is better."; "Yes, if she writes the exact time each time."; "No, only one person should ever read it." The second gets: "That's the key. Readings at odd times are fine if the time is written down." The third gets: "One reader makes it consistent, but what happens when they're ill?"

## 7. Step 4: What we'd wish we had

**Question panel:**

> The librarian wants a page at the front of the book saying how it should be kept.
>
> What you've kept so far: *[generated from kept answers, or "not yet"]*

Four parts, each with **Write it yourself** (free text) and **Choose and adapt**:

1. **What we write down.** Generated from the explorer's sheet in 3a, editable.
2. **How often, and when more often.** From 2g, editable.
3. **What a missed reading looks like.** From 3b, editable.
4. **What this record still won't tell us.** Supplied: "What happens upstream before the water reaches us."; "How high the water came between readings."; "Why the brook rose, only how high."

**Keep these rules** saves them. **Write them in the book** shows the first page.

**The librarian's response (the same for every set of rules):**

> She copies your rules onto the first page in her best handwriting, slowly. "There. Now someone in fifty years will know what we meant." She looks at the page a moment longer. "I wonder if anyone ever did this for us."

(That last line points towards Hollis's ledger, which the explorer meets at the end of Act II, without naming it.)

**Story link:** if the case is kept, the almanac's weather page can show a "Brook" row using the explorer's schedule, as a record the town keeps from then on. In Act IV, [Case 5](118-ledger-and-gauge-case.md)'s final step offers the explorer's own rules as one answer to "the record that would settle it".

**Supported route:** Choose and adapt alone completes Step 4; steps 1 to 3 can stay open.
