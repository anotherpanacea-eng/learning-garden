## Complete story: The Second Knock

Story ID `second-knock`. Threads: `leena`, **Leena outside**, stripe symbol; `rowan`, **Rowan inside**, dots symbol. Story frame: **At the library's new town collection, an old photograph is due before the doors open. Two people are close enough to hear each other, but neither has the whole picture.**

Every scene below is the complete supplied text for that scene. The learner moves scenes, never edits their words. The full story is available from the start. Paragraphs within a scene stay in this order.

### a1 · A careful delivery · Leena

Leena carried the photograph flat between two boards. It showed the old footbridge before the storm, with three people standing where the water now ran. The archivist had asked her to bring it to the library before the collection opened. At the courtyard entrance, she found the door shut.

### b1 · The voice in the headphones · Rowan

Inside, Rowan was testing a recording for the photograph's display. He put on the headphones. An unfamiliar voice described the footbridge, then stopped halfway through a sentence. Rowan wound the recording back. He wanted visitors to hear the whole account, not just the part that sounded certain.

### a2 · No answer · Leena

Leena knocked with her free hand. A burst of laughter came from somewhere inside, but nobody opened the door. She checked the address on the folder. Perhaps they had forgotten her. She could leave the photograph on the step, but the first spots of rain were darkening the stone.

### b2 · The wrong entrance · Rowan

Rowan lifted one ear of the headphones. Was that a knock? He went to the front entrance and looked down the empty steps. The courtyard was on the other side of the building. He did not think of the courtyard door. He went back for the recording sheet.

### a3 · A second pattern · Leena

Leena tucked the boards under her coat. This time she knocked twice, waited, then knocked twice again. Through the glass she could see a strip of bright wall and the edge of an empty frame. Someone had made room for the photograph. She decided to wait a little longer.

### b3 · The pause between knocks · Rowan

At the desk, Rowan heard two taps, a pause, and two more. The sound came from behind the display wall. He took off the headphones completely and walked around it. A dark shape moved beyond the courtyard glass. He reached for the latch.

### together · The whole account · Leena and Rowan

“I thought you had gone,” Rowan said when the door opened. “I thought you hadn't heard,” Leena answered. He had heard part of her knocking; she had seen part of his work. They set the photograph beside the recording sheet. Before the first visitors arrived, they wrote a clear sign for the courtyard door.

Writer's order: a1,b1,a2,b2,a3,b3,together.
Starting arrangement, clearly labeled **A starting arrangement to change**: a1,a2,a3,b1,b2,b3,together.

Writer's note: **I left Leena waiting while the reader met Rowan and the headphones. The reader could then know something Leena did not. Later I returned to each person before they met. Another order might delay that information or make the misunderstanding feel different.**

This story is complete at seven scenes. It is not a condensed external novel, a historical account or a claim that every reader responds in the same way. Rearranging may put a consequence before its cause in reading order; the scene words still describe the same events.

## Frozen-source mapping and definitions

Source title = **The Second Knock**; context = its Story frame; task = Shared Task; notice = Shared Notice; returnQuestion = Shared Return question; writerNote = Writer's note. The narrative thread/scene/order fields, complete demo and teaching frames map literally as specified. The source also keeps the following definitions:

- **Scene:** A connected part of the story shown here as one movable piece. Its words stay together when you move it.
- **Thread:** Connected events following a person or group. Our labels are given; a scene can belong to both threads.
- **Reading order:** The order in which the reader meets the scenes. It need not be the order in which the events happened.
- **Interruption or cut:** A place where this reading leaves one thread to follow another. A cut can leave a question waiting, but not every reader will feel the same way.
- **Return:** A thread appears again after one or more scenes without it. Its first appearance and adjacent appearances are not returns on this map.
- **Gap:** The number of intervening scenes without a thread before it returns. It is not a measure of time or suspense.

App UI constants, not saved source fields: encounter title **Two Threads**, Story job, Comparison, Draft warning, Read/Reference/Map headings, Map note, No-return wording and Both label.
