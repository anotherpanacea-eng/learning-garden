## Shared child copy

**Title:** Ferry Deck

**Opening:** The same crossing can look different from another place. Step the clock, then watch from the ferry or the other boat. What moves compared with you?

**Model notice:** A made-up model: straight, steady motions for six seconds. The boats are in separate lanes. This is not a picture of water currents or a plan for a real crossing.

**View caption:** Watching from {observer} · clock {t} seconds. Right is positive. Zero is the marked point you are watching from.

**Axis caption:** Position in meters from {observer}. A position tells where something is now; it does not tell which way it is moving.

**Velocity legend:** The arrow shows the courier's velocity compared with {observer}, in meters per second. Its length uses a different scale from the position ruler. It is not a trail the courier has already traveled.

**Courier position:** Courier: {signed q} m from {observer}.

**Courier velocity:** {signed u} m/s compared with {observer}: {right / left / same position in this view}.

**Same-moment view change:** Same clock moment. We changed what we compare the motion with.

**Clock endpoint:** This little model ends at six seconds. That does not mean the boats stop there.

**Units:** A meter is a unit of length. Meters per second tells how much a position changes in each second, including direction here. Speed is the size of that velocity, without its left/right sign.

**Visit:** These settings last for this visit. They are not added to your notes.

**Grown-up:** Invite a prediction aloud, then step one second or change views. “Still compared with what?” is a useful question. The readings are supplied by the model; this bench does not record, grade or diagnose an explanation. The bank is a convenient reference, not an absolute view. These frames keep the same directions and move steadily; turning or accelerating viewers need more careful models.

**Readings table:** The same moment, compared with {observer}. Columns: Thing · Position (m) · Velocity (m/s). Rows Bank marker / Ferry origin / Courier / Other-boat origin. Each velocity cell includes right, left, or no position change relative to this observer. Signed0 is printed0, never−0. Name both units above the values; do not require a reader to infer which numbers are positions.

**Explanation arithmetic:** Courier relative to {observer}: courier relative to bank ({signed vC} m/s) minus {observer} relative to bank ({signed vA} m/s) = {signed uC} m/s. Parenthesize a negative operand. This combines signed velocities, not a rule to subtract unsigned speeds in every situation.

**Shared explanation:** The objects have not changed their motions because we changed views. We described the same crossing from another moving origin. A number to the right of zero can still be moving left in this view.

## Four exact worked frames

Every frame label: **Worked example, not your crossing. Part {1..4} of4.** All frames use riding; no working visit is changed. Headings/text:

1. **Start with the bank view.** Clock0s. Ferry origin−4m, courier−2m, other-boat origin+6m, bank0m. The courier sits2m along the ferry's deck. Both courier and ferry move right2m/s compared with bank.
2. **Let one second pass.** Same bank view, clock1s. Ferry origin−2m, courier0m, other boat+7m, bank0m. Courier and ferry each changed position by+2m. Being at position0m does not mean being at rest.
3. **Keep the moment; change the view.** Still clock1s, now watch from ferry. Ferry origin0m, courier+2m, other boat+9m, bank+2m. Courier-ferry is0m/s. The bank is to the right of the origin now but moves left2m/s compared with ferry. Position and velocity tell different things.
4. **Another second from the ferry.** Clock2s, ferry view. Ferry origin0m, courier+2m, other boat+8m, bank0m. Courier stays at the same deck position. Bank moved from+2m to0m, a leftward change of2m. This view and the bank view describe the same crossing.
