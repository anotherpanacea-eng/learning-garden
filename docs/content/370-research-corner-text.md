# 370 · Research Corner: finite original material

Proposed extension of277's Research Corner. Original learning-card words, supplied notes and task text; not quotations of the external biological sources. All writing/checks are practice with material supplied by the game. The child's own paragraphs stay spoken or on paper.

**Research Corner**

“These are supplied notes. Your own words stay spoken or on paper.”

Source list: **Sources beside these supplied notes**.

Unavailable reference: “A source reference is not available here. Your original draft is retained in Notes, and other notes stay open.”

## Three frozen cards

**guide · Common frogs in Britain**

Origin: “Original learning card based on Froglife and Amphibian and Reptile Conservation's common-frog guides.”

Notice: “Biological information about common frogs in Britain. These are our learning-card words, not those organisations' exact wording.”

1. `g1` “In Britain, common-frog spawn often appears in late winter or spring. Timing varies with weather and location.”
2. `g2` “Tadpoles hatch and grow in water.”
3. `g3` “The back legs grow before the front legs.”
4. `g4` “The tail shrinks as tadpoles become froglets, which eventually leave the water.”

**pond · The miller's pond notes**

Origin: “An invented Willowbank record for this activity.”

Notice: “Fictional observations from one pond in a story year. These are not real field data or a timetable for every frog.”

1. `p1` “March 3: spawn was noted at the millpond.”
2. `p2` “March 20: tadpoles were wriggling in the water.”
3. `p3` “June 12: a froglet was seen on a lily pad.”

**pip · Pip's claim**

Origin: “An invented character's words for this activity.”

Notice: “An untested claim, not a measured comparison.”

1. `u1` “Pip says, ‘I think frogs are the best swimmers in the whole world.’”
2. `u2` “He has not compared them with other animals.”

## Six open tasks

Each task has the shared reference packet above. Choice `parts` fallbacks include each displayed card’s title, origin, notice and all numbered lines: all three cards for the question task; guide for the note task; pond for scope; Pip for opinion. Rich readable cards replace their matching fallback part, never the status labels. Task-specific references below are comparison aids, including for wrong choices; they do not certify that a selected answer is true. Choice order is as printed and freezes with the source. Existing Check/Keep/Next draft/help controls remain. Research-only empty source selection and sort-mismatch feedback use the wording below; other task outputs are unchanged. The six tasks have independent supplied reader jobs; a selected question does not carry into the other tasks.

### research-question · A question these cards can answer · choice

Reader: “For visitors reading a short pond page.” Prompt: “Which question can the displayed cards answer?”

- `spawn-date` **When was spawn noted in this story pond?** Fitting; compare pond/p1.
- `count` **How many frogs live in all of Willowbank now?** No card counts that population. Authoring only: no line locator; all three cards stay available.
- `croak` **Why do adult frogs croak at night?** No displayed line explains croaking. Authoring only: no line locator; all three cards stay available.

Fit: “The pond note records spawn on March 3. That answers when spawn was noted in this story pond, not when every frog spawns.”

Hint: “Look for what each card actually records.” Example: “A cafe card says ‘opens at dawn’. It can answer when that cafe opens, not how many visitors came.” Guided steps: “Read the cards.” / “Read each question.” / “Find the words that could answer it.”

### research-sources · Sources for a pond page · tap cards

Reader: “For a page about spawn timing and how tadpoles change.” Prompt: “Tap every card that helps this page.” Research tap previews use “Selected source cards” and “No source cards are selected yet.”, with each selected fallback separated. They omit the joined full-card block. This applies to current, kept and Notes previews even if optional metadata cannot open.

Empty selection: “No source cards are selected yet. You can keep this version or select some cards.”

Card order: guide, pond, pip. All are tappable; fitting set guide+pond. The factual guide describes general timing/development; the fictional record supplies that one pond's recorded dates. Pip's preference doesn't answer either report heading.

Fit: “The guide gives general information; the pond note gives one story pond's recorded observations. Keep those kinds of statement distinct.” Missing: “Check whether the guide or pond record could help with timing or development.” Different Pip feedback: “Pip's claim tells us what he says he thinks. It is not about this report's timing or development question.”

Hint: “Useful for which question? Read the reader's job.” Example: “An opening-times card helps a visitor know when to arrive. A favorite-bun opinion answers a different question.”

### research-note · Keep the order · choice

Display the factual guide card, with comparison line3 highlighted and origin/notice available. Prompt: “Which brief note keeps which legs grow first?”

- `front-first` **tadpoles—front legs, then back legs**. Changes the source order.
- `back-first` **tadpoles—back legs, then front legs**. Fitting.
- `legs-only` **tadpoles—legs**. Loses which legs come first.

All3 compare with guide/g3. Fit: “The short note keeps the order without repeating the whole sentence.” Hint: “Which words carry the order?” Example: “If a card says ‘put on socks before boots’, ‘socks, then boots’ keeps the order. ‘Clothes’ loses it.”

Append to this task’s fit feedback; use as its entire `feedback.general` (the core then adds the standard closing once): “A full sentence can also be a useful note. Mark copied words as a quotation and give its source. Here the job asks for a brief note that keeps the order.”

### research-scope · One recorded date · choice

Display the pond card, with comparison line1 highlighted. Prompt: “Which sentence keeps what this pond note can tell us?”

- `everywhere` **Every common frog in Britain and Willowbank spawns on March 3.** Adds an unsupported all-places rule.
- `nothing` **The note cannot tell us any date when spawn was recorded.** Withholds the date the note actually gives.
- `local` **The story pond note records spawn on March 3.** Fitting.

All3 compare with pond/p1. Fit: “The record gives a date for a recorded observation in one invented pond. It does not set a universal date.” Hint: “Keep the date and the place limit.” Example: “A cafe note ‘one bun sold at dawn’ does not count every bakery, but it does record that sale.”

### research-opinion · Useful for a different question · choice

Display Pip’s card and both lines. Prompt: “What could Pip's card support?”

- `measured` **What scientists measured about the world's fastest swimmers last summer.** No measurements or scientists appear here.
- `stated-view` **What Pip said he thought about frogs as swimmers.** Fitting.
- `no-view` **Pip’s card contains no statement of his view of frogs.** The card gives his stated opinion.

All3 compare with pip/u1/u2. Fit: “Pip's words can show his stated view. They do not establish a measured ranking of animals.” Hint: “Separate a speaker's words from proof of the speaker's claim.” Example: “Saying ‘I like cherry buns best’ can show my preference, not everyone's favorite.”

### research-organise · A pond page with its sources · sort

Reader: “For visitors who need two short paragraphs: spawn timing and tadpole changes.” Prompt: “Choose a note, then its heading. Its source label stays with it.”

Headings: `spawn` **When spawn appears**; `change` **How tadpoles change**.

Supplied notes (initial order season,legs,pond-date,tail):
- `season` **Britain—spawn often late winter or spring; timing varies.** Fitting heading spawn; compare guide/g1.
- `legs` **Tadpoles—back legs, then front legs.** Fitting heading change; compare guide/g3.
- `pond-date` **Story pond—spawn noted March 3.** Fitting heading spawn; compare pond/p1.
- `tail` **Tail shrinks; froglets later leave the water.** Fitting heading change; compare guide/g4.

Fit: “Each note has a heading that matches this reader's job. Its source label still lets someone inspect the card.” Per-note different, prefixed by that note’s own text: “Is this note about spawn timing or a change in the tadpole? Its source stays the same whichever heading you try.” Incomplete: use the existing waiting-strip message; keeping is always allowed.

Hint: “Read the two headings. Time of spawn, or a developing tadpole?” Example: “For a bakery page, ‘opens at dawn’ goes under When to visit; ‘seed rolls’ goes under What's for sale. Both keep the card they came from.”

## Manual teaching for research tasks

The existing step-by-step teaching panel uses these five strings (intro, different example, three manually revealed parts). They are supplied teaching, never independent response evidence:

1. “A useful research note keeps the meaning you need and a source another reader can inspect.”
2. “Different example: a cafe card, line 1, says ‘The bakery opens at dawn.’ A short note is ‘bakery—opens at dawn’.”
3. “Keep the important fact. ‘Bakery’ alone loses when it opens.”
4. “Keep the source label: cafe card, line 1. Those are this activity card's lines.”
5. “Put the note under a heading that helps your reader. The source label travels with it.”

## For grown-ups only

“Research Corner models choosing among supplied questions/cards, preserving meaning in short supplied notes and organising those notes with source-card locators. The sources and references are provided, not found or independently cited by the child. Checks remain guided/supported practice with frozen task material. First/latest versions preserve the elected arrangements and their record's source labels. The factual guide is an original paraphrase; the pond dates and Pip's words are fiction. Original notes, paragraphs, source searching, formal bibliography and extended synthesis remain unobserved or off-screen. Nothing here assesses independent research, transfers a level or earns story credit.”
