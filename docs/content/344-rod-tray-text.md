## Teach cards

**T1. Zero pairs**
> One red rod and one black rod make zero. Adding or removing a zero pair never changes what the tray is worth.
Worked example: 1. Put out 3 red and 3 black. 2. Pair them off. 3. Three zero pairs: the tray is worth 0.

**T2. Adding moves along the line**
> Adding a positive moves right. Adding a negative moves left. You can go straight through zero.
Worked example: 1. 2 + (−7). 2. From 2, move 7 to the left. 3. Two steps reach zero, five more reach −5.

**T3. Taking away what you don't have**
> To take away rods that aren't there, add zero pairs first. They don't change the total.
Worked example: 1. 0 − (−2). 2. The tray is empty. 3. Add 2 zero pairs. 4. Take away 2 black. 5. 2 red are left: 2.

**T4. Two jobs for one sign**
> A minus sign can mean "below zero", as in −3. It can mean "take away". Brackets keep them apart.
Worked example: 1. 5 − (−3): take away negative three. 2. Put out 5 red. 3. Add 3 zero pairs. 4. Take away 3 black. 5. 8 red: 8.

## Cases

Read-aloud names: "+" is "plus"; the operation "−" is "take away"; a bracketed "(−n)" is "negative n".

### 1. cross-down · Down through zero (guided)
Prompt: **What is 4 + (−7)? Build it on the tray.**
Guided steps (shown before answering):
1. **Put out 4 red rods.**
2. **Add 7 black rods.**
3. **Pair them off and count what is left.**
Hint: **Adding a negative moves down. How many steps reach zero?**
Explanation: **4 + (−7) = −3. Four zero pairs leave the tray. Three black rods are left.**
Feedback for 11: **You added the sizes. Adding a negative moves down, not up.**
Feedback for 3: **Right size. Which kind of rod is left over?**

### 2. two-below · Further below
Prompt: **What is −5 + (−3)?**
Hint: **Both numbers are below zero. Does adding more black rods go up or down?**
Explanation: **−5 + (−3) = −8. Eight black rods, no red ones to pair with.**
Feedback for 8: **"Two minuses make a plus" doesn't work for adding. Count the black rods.**
Feedback for −2: **You took the sizes apart. Adding puts more black rods in, not fewer.**

### 3. pay-off · The baker pays off a debt
Prompt: **The baker owes $6. Then he takes in $10. What is his account now?**
Sentence shown: **−6 + 10**
Hint: **Six black rods for what he owes. Add ten red. Pair them off.**
Explanation: **−6 + 10 = 4. Six dollars clear the debt. Four dollars are left over.**
Feedback for 10: **That is what he took in. Part of it pays off the $6 first.**
Feedback for −4: **Right size. After paying off $6, is he still below zero?**

### 4. take-black · Take away what isn't there
Prompt: **What is 1 − (−3)?**
Hint: **One red rod and no black. Add zero pairs so you can take 3 black away.**
Explanation: **1 − (−3) = 4. Add 3 zero pairs, take away 3 black rods, and 4 red are left.**
Feedback for −2: **Taking away a negative doesn't make it more negative. Try the zero pairs.**
Feedback for −4: **One minus means take away. The other means below zero. Look at the brackets.**

### 5. take-red · Same numbers, one sign changed
Prompt: **What is 1 − 3?**
Hint: **One red rod. You need to take away 3 red. Add zero pairs first.**
Explanation: **1 − 3 = −2. Add 2 zero pairs, take away 3 red rods, and 2 black are left.**
Feedback for 2: **Right size. You took away more than you had. Which side of zero is that?**
Feedback for 4: **That answers 1 − (−3). Here you take away 3 red rods.**

Contrast caption shown beside cases 4 and 5: **Same numbers, one sign changed. Compare the two trays.**

### 6. remove-charge · A charge taken off
Prompt: **The account shows −$2. A $6 charge was a mistake. Take it off.**
Sentence shown: **−2 − (−6)**
Hint: **Taking off a debt is taking away black rods. Do you have six?**
Explanation: **−2 − (−6) = 4. Add 4 zero pairs, take away 6 black, and 4 red are left.**
Feedback for −8: **That adds the charge again. Here it is taken away.**
Feedback for −4: **Right size. Taking off a debt moves the account up past zero.**

## Grown-up notes

Heading: **The Rod Tray**.

What it teaches: **Adding and taking away whole numbers from −12 to 12 with zero pairs, and seeing each move as an arrow on a number line.**

What a check means: **"Matches" means the chosen answer matched this case once, with the help shown. It is not a grade or a level.**

What is kept: **For each case, the first and latest check, how many checks, the tray at each check, and which help was opened first. Attempts in between are not kept. Spoken reasons are never stored.**

Off-screen idea: **With a deck of cards, use red cards as +1 and black as −1. Deal a few, pair them off, and say what the hand is worth.**
