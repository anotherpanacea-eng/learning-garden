## Page2 · The flood ledger

Reveal: “The second page peels away on its own, the way a leaf comes off a puddle. It's a table of numbers.”

Short: “A list of floods. Each line says a year and how high the brook came up the mill's flood post. At the bottom: Ask upstream.”

Longer: “The page is a table. Each line has a year and a height, measured on the flood post at the mill, where the brook comes over its banks. The earlier entries are small and far apart; several later entries are closer together and higher. They do not rise in every row. At the very bottom, someone has added two words: Ask upstream. ‘Upstream of what?’ says Pip. ‘Upstream of us,’ says Dot. ‘Where the brook comes from.’”

Original label **Water-stained original · story**. Heading **The flood ledger**. Table headers **Year of my keeping** / **Height at the mill's flood post (cm)**. Heights above the brook's stated ordinary level. Rows exactly `(4,40),(15,60),(27,120),(31,80),(34,120),(36,140),(38,100)`. Caption: “Seven entries from an invented record. A year without an entry is not a measured zero.” Original after-table text: “Years not written had no flood worth the ink. [smudged word] the gaps grow shorter. Ask upstream.” Explain below: “Those are the writer's words about which floods were entered. This copy cannot tell us every unrecorded height.”

The table and optional separated storm annotation are visible with any reading amount; never imply reading Original is necessary for an older learner. No interpretation is graded. Guide: “{guide}: A record can give us a new question. What would you want to ask the town upstream?”

## First letter and close

Invitation: “As the evening ends, {courier} comes up the bridge road with a letter. The stamp is from a town called Vadoalto, up the river. The storm went through there too.” Heading **A letter from Vadoalto**. Button **Open the letter**.

English Short, preserving line breaks:

> Hello, Willowbank! The storm came here too. Our brook rose very high. Can we compare notes? Write back!
> Nuria, Vadoalto

English Longer:

> Hello, Willowbank!
> The storm came through Vadoalto too. Our flood post says the brook rose higher than anyone here can remember. Did yours? I am learning to keep our post, and I would like to compare notes with whoever keeps yours. Will you write back?
> Nuria, apprentice at the flood post, Vadoalto
