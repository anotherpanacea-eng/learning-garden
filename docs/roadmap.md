## Owner priorities, in order

Set by the owner on 2026-10-24. **When choosing what to work on next, take the lowest-numbered priority below that has work ready, that is, work not waiting on review or an owner answer.** A later-numbered priority does not wait for an earlier one to be finished; it waits only while the earlier one has ready work. Bug fixes, review repairs and publication (the [release and maintenance queue](#release-and-maintenance-queue)) are never blocked by this order.

The plan is three big pushes, then steady work. A push is a short run of PRs toward one visible result. After the pushes, priorities 1 to 5 continue as ongoing work at a sustainable pace; nothing here is meant to be done all at once.

### Clarity sources

The [2026-10-22 text review and six area files](reviews/text-review-2026-10-22/README.md) are now included as a historical working list for Push C. They describe `a9f4394`, not current main; their line numbers, suggested rewrites and accuracy statements need current verification. [PR66 and PR72](reviews/2026-10-22-text-prs-review.md) already repaired some wording. The cross-cutting findings below are a triage checklist, not a verified inventory of current defects. For example, the three ending-film canvases already have `role="img"`. Check current code and behavior before commissioning a repair:

1. **Grown-up and spec language on the child's screen** is the biggest problem: record-keeping caveats ("not a verdict about what you know", "recorded as support") and spec words ("supplied", "observation", "chronology") beside the child's buttons. Keep brief child-facing purpose and action guidance; move record-keeping and evidence caveats into the Grown-up panels. Preserve instructions needed to play, accessible descriptions, source/fiction labels and any task-specific safety guidance.
2. **One thing, many names.** A pause has five names, "explorer" means both the character and the save slot, and some text names buttons that were renamed. Write one short glossary of control and place names and use it everywhere.
3. **British spellings** on child screens and in narration (labelled, neighbour, millilitres, theatre, practising).
4. **Symbols that read badly aloud:** slashes, blanks, arrows and icon glyphs inside buttons; film canvases without `role="img"`; French without `lang="fr"`.
5. **Answer giveaways:** a correct choice that is noticeably longest, a picture label that states the answer, a translation hint that gives it away.

The earlier 2026-10-18 UX review also flagged hidden hint buttons, the question below the fold on phones, models that respond only to buttons, and emoji characters and generic dots in pictures. Some may already be fixed; check main before working each one.

Keep, Skip and change remain available. Activity-owned listening/read-together tools remain separate; choosing a preference is not evidence of using support. Existing per-explorer storage and local overrides suffice; no global level or new profile service is required. Current reading-note descriptions replace older shared-notebook wording with this explorer’s local notebook; frozen sources remain unchanged.
2. **Author different tasks within the same story invitation.** Choose one narrative beat, then design genuinely different quantities, representations or reading demands, with an interesting supported route at each. Preserve mixed strengths: a strong reader can choose foundational arithmetic; a French beginner can choose deeper science. Each new variant needs its own teaching, contrasts and bounded review. Three labels alone do not supply three lessons.
3. **Author later-act arrivals and returns.** Part of Push B: [spec 120 §10, PR127](https://github.com/anotherpanacea-eng/learning-garden/pull/127) proposes the three arrivals. Supply a short recap and a meaningful first mission so an explorer can join the thriving town or a wider exchange without replaying recovery. Keep the relevant story facts understandable without treating the recap as completed learning. Later-act stories and intermediate lessons still need authoring; a start selector cannot supply missing content.
4. **Connect suggestions only where evidence fits.** Reuse a specific supported/familiar/independent task observation to offer a relevant next invitation, with a reason and an override. Do not transfer an age, global ability label, story completion or guessed proficiency between strands. Preserve earlier sources, quantities, choices and outcomes when preferences change.

This sequence makes different starting points usable while the broader six-act curriculum grows. It does not promise age-complete routes, automatic placement or an already authored course through the roughly-thirteen horizon.

## Story order and review rule

The six acts are **recovery → community making → wider exchange → evidence and disagreement → learner-originated projects → Opening of the Ways**. Act II’s market, garden, gathering and show make the town a place the learner helps shape; later acts return to those tools for reciprocal exchange, qualified claims and a chosen contribution. This is a story and teaching design, **not six age grades, access locks or a claim that later acts are taught**. A learner may enter any available destination with support and revisit an earlier representation. A celebration or film can close a story beat while intermediate instruction remains unfinished. [Spec 120](specs/120-keeper-of-the-ways.md) (proposed) gives the acts a plot: the storm parcel, the Keeper's Pages, the Sponge question and seasonal festivals under the acts.

Select a small concept, model, contrast, practice, new use and return for each increment. Source topics and prerequisite edges guide authoring, not child placement. Preserve separate records for support, familiarity and independent attempts; parent talk and physical work stay honest about what was actually observed. Review each substantive spec independently, then its authored content, implementation and real browser behavior before release. Keep source, rights, privacy and save-history boundaries in the linked specs and [project instructions](../AGENTS.md).
