## The knowledge

Plain adult statements, each sourced in the [audit](../source-audits/149-orchard-year.md) (search-excerpt checks, 2026-10-25; no open checks remain).

**Parts and trees.** Flowering plants have roots, stem, leaves and flowers; trees share the plan, with a woody trunk (stem) and branches. Roots are routinely left out of children's drawings, so every diagram shows soil cut away.

**Deciduous and evergreen.** Deciduous trees (apple, oak) drop all leaves for part of the year; their twigs carry live buds. Evergreens (holly, pine) keep foliage all year while shedding old leaves gradually. Larch is a deciduous conifer. Autumn yellows appear as chlorophyll breaks down and reveals pigments already there; reds are made new.

**Germination.** A seed takes in water and swells; the radicle emerges first and grows down in response to gravity whichever way the seed lies; the shoot then grows up. A bean starts on damp paper without soil or light; its leaves need light once open. Some species need light to germinate, so the text speaks of the bean. A bulb starts from a large food store.

**Part functions.** Roots take in water and small amounts of minerals and anchor; the stem supports and carries water; leaves make food using light; flowers make seeds. Soil supplies water and minerals, not food.

**Pollination, fruit and seeds.** Pollen moved between flowers of the same kind of plant (by insects or wind) lets seeds start; the flower's base swells into fruit; pollen does not grow into a plant. Apples add a rule: nearly all varieties need pollen from a different variety blooming at the same time (a few, such as Golden Delicious, are partly self-fruitful). Honeybees and wild bees (bumblebees, mason and mining bees) carry it. An apple core has five chambers, normally up to two seeds each. Apple seeds do not grow true: named varieties are grafted (scion onto rootstock).

**Seed dispersal.** Seeds spread away from the parent's competition for light, water and space, mostly passively, and shape is evidence: wings (maple key) and parachutes (dandelion) for wind; hooks (burdock) for fur; fruit (apple) eaten by animals; gorse pods split and fling seeds a few meters; alder seeds have air-filled edges, float and also blow. Common reed seed heads carry long silky hairs that help wind dispersal. Pollination makes the seed; dispersal moves it: two steps children confuse.

**Water transport.** Water enters at the roots and rises through fine stem tubes (xylem, named only in a Keeper's note) to leaves and flowers; evaporation from leaf pores draws more up. Colored water in celery or a white flower shows the path over hours; a cross-cut shows colored dots.

**Van Helmont's willow (Keeper's Desk).** Jan Baptist van Helmont (1580-1644; 1579 in the old-style calendar) dried 200 pounds of soil, planted a 5-pound willow, watered it with rain water and covered the pot against dust. After five years the willow weighed about 169 pounds 3 ounces; the dried soil had lost about 2 ounces. He credited the gain (about 164 pounds) to water alone, and wrote that he had not weighed the leaves shed in four autumns. Published posthumously in *Ortus medicinae* (1648). Local pounds then varied (17th-century Dutch and French pounds ran about 470-495 g against today's 454 g), so modern conversions are approximate and appear in no child answer. He was right that the mass did not come from soil and wrong that water alone built it: dry wood is about half carbon, from carbon dioxide in the air. One pot and no comparison pot is a limit worth naming.

**Who came next.** 1771: Joseph Priestley found a mint sprig could "restore" air spoiled by a candle, inconsistently. 1779: Jan Ingenhousz showed only green parts do this, and only in light.

**Photosynthesis (Keeper's Desk).** Chlorophyll captures light energy, which drives the making of glucose from carbon dioxide (air, through leaf pores) and water (soil, through roots and stem); oxygen is released. Light supplies energy and is no ingredient; chlorophyll is not used up; minerals are small amounts and not food. Plants respire day and night; in good light they make more than they use. No symbol equation or cell biology.

## The lesson

## The game or activity

Every screen: *These are simplified pictures. Real plants vary.* Each model adds "A made-up model." No score, timer, lock or mastery label. All drags have tap-to-place equivalents.

- **(a) Orchard Wheel.** Four segments repaint one apple tree with one line each. **Label the tree:** six labels onto the tree, then onto a bean plant beside it, soil cut away; per-label feedback.
- **(b) Seed Jar.** Two beans against glass, one each way up; model-day buttons 0, 1, 3, 5, 7, 10 (invented timing); order four stage cards; **Turn the jar** prediction at day 4, revealed at day 6; one seed-or-bulb card.
- **(c) Blossom to Apple.** Invented varieties *Lantern Red* and *Brook Gold*; tap the bee, then blossoms; pollen comes from the last blossom visited. **Model rule:** other-variety pollen sets fruit; same-variety or none does not (a stated simplification of self-incompatibility). The bee mechanic is only a delivery tool here; why bees visit and how pollen sticks belong to spec 150's Pollen Path, linked under the bench. Fast-forward to autumn; **cut the apple** (chambers 2, 2, 1, 2, 1).
- **(d) Seed Travel Table.** Six seeds (seven at Sapling with the Vadoalto reed) matched to five ways to travel; mismatches get "look at its shape"; a **Why travel?** card after three matches.
- **(e) Thirsty Stalk.** Five picture times; **model:** blue climbs 2 cm per hour on an 18 cm stalk (made up). **Draw the path** with arrows; down-the-stem and air-to-leaf arrows get gentle questions. The real demo is an adult-led off-screen card.
- **(f) Willow in the Pot.** Twin-pan balance with van Helmont's reported numbers in his units; **Build the argument** (claim, evidence, reasoning, three cards each, tagged wrong cards with feedback); what he got right and missed; three Long Line cards.
- **(g) Leaf Kitchen.** Six cards (carbon dioxide, water, light, minerals, sugar, oxygen) sorted into five places, then the word equation and a night card on respiration.
