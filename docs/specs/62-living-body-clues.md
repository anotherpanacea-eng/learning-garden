## Purpose and story

After the storm, the garden crew is making illustrated labels for a wildlife corner. “Which small neighbour did we notice? Look at its body, then follow our picture key.” Habitat investigations already ask where animals might find useful conditions. This pack introduces a different action in the same Living Garden: inspect a supplied diagram, distinguish features, follow two questions and keep a revisable identification trail. A matching habitat is not proof of identity. This is a knowledge pack in an existing place, drawing on [PR47](https://github.com/anotherpanacea-eng/learning-garden/pull/47), not another world shell or an assessment of field identification.

The four animals are **pictured adults**. The key identifies only these four supplied pictures, not arbitrary animals, photographs or species outdoors. “I cannot tell from this view” and “Something else / not in this key” are valid ways to stop rather than force an identification. No real observation, captured animal, upload, location, learner prose, microphone, external AI, score, timer, access lock or new food reward. The body-clues module leaves old habitat games, suggestions, waiting parcels, Café stock and multiplication exposures unchanged. The existing host connection panel is a scoped exception described below. The pretend café supplies are unrelated to animal diets.

## The exact supplied key

Question1: **“How many jointed walking legs can we see in the detail?”** Choices: “0”, “6 · three pairs”, “14 · seven pairs”, “A different count”, “I cannot tell”. Question2 depends on the first choice:

- 0: **“Which body clue can we see?”** Coiled shell / Long ringed body, no external coiled shell / Something else / I cannot tell.
- 6: same question: Paired hard wing cases meeting in the middle / Something else / I cannot tell.
- 14: same question: Flattened body with overlapping plates / Something else / I cannot tell.
- Different count: stop at “This is outside our four-picture key.” No invented second-clue question.
- Cannot tell: stop at “We need a clearer look before choosing a branch.” No count inferred from the absence of a visible leg.

The four supplied leaves are `(0,shell) → Garden land snail`, `(0,rings) → Earthworm`, `(6,cases) → Ladybird beetle`, `(14,plates) → Woodlouse`. Other/unclear second clues stop without naming an animal. Changing the leg choice clears the dependent second-clue draft only, never old recorded checks. Changing a second clue is unrestricted. An explicit check requires a leg selection, and a second selection only on a numeric branch. This minimum just makes the chosen trail meaningful; help and every page remain open.

Feedback preserves **three separate things**: the chosen key trail, what that trail names (or why it stops), and how each selected feature compares with this diagram. It must not say a named animal is confirmed merely because it is a valid key leaf: `(0,rings)` on the snail diagram follows the key to earthworm, while the pictured shell disagrees. “This trail names Earthworm. Our picture has a coiled shell; try the other zero-leg branch or inspect again.” Unknown features record null/unobserved, not false, and an outside-key result does not claim that the supplied picture really depicts a fifth animal. There is no aggregate score. A result explains supplied features; it does not establish memory, independent observation or transfer. Naming a matching leaf without the two selected features is not a bypass.
