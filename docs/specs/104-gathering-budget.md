# Gathering Budget: choose a plan for a pretend welcome

**Status:** specification/content/art and implementation/release reviews cleared; merged in [PR109](https://github.com/anotherpanacea-eng/learning-garden/pull/109). Deployment remains separate.

## Problem and bounded action

The Coin Counter makes alternative coin combinations for **one fixed price**; Market Change finds a return from fixed tender. The Café asks for a plate and later gathers ingredient stock without money. None lets a learner choose and revise **several supplies** under a stated limit while checking whether the chosen supplies meet a separate request. This table adds that action. It is a fictional planning exercise, not earned play currency, access to a celebration, a financial judgment, or a real purchase.

Enter through a new Workshop link at `#workshop/budget`. Two authored requests stay openly selectable. Each has a fixed pretend-cent limit, four priced packets, fixed stock at this independent stall, and two minimum quantities. The learner adds or removes packets, sees the pictured contents and running cost, optionally proposes a total, checks the two constraints separately, and revises freely. Several baskets fit each request. The activity never recommends a cheapest, most generous, or morally best plan. A working plan is one that both meets the request and fits the limit; spending every cent is unnecessary. Other baskets remain worth exploring.

The stall has no effect on the Café cupboard, supper lanterns, orchard, Coin Counter mats, Market Change rounds, saved learning evidence, adaptive selection, or any route's access. No rewards, points, timer, grade, mastery, real price, household budget, or financial advice follow from a check. These are paper props in a make-believe town gathering. Existing unfinished work waits as before.

## Two complete authored requests

Model ID `gathering-budget-v1`. Every cost is an integer number of **pretend cents per packet**, not the value of coins displayed or a cost per individual card. Stock is the most packets this table offers in this request; adding one does not decrement stock elsewhere. A packet's contents are countable and never split. Exceeding a minimum is allowed. No other catalog items, tax, change, restock, discounts, or substitutions exist.

| Request ID and exact card copy | Limit and minimums | Catalog: ID, visible label, one packet brings, price, stock |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `welcome-table`: **Welcome table**. “The neighbors are setting a pretend welcome table. Bring at least three place cards and one welcome sign. You can spend up to 16 pretend cents.” | Limit 16¢; place cards ≥3; welcome signs ≥1. | `plain-card` · Plain card · 1 place card · 3¢ · 3 packets; `folded-pair` · Folded card pair · 2 place cards · 5¢ · 2; `paper-sign` · Paper welcome sign · 1 welcome sign · 6¢ · 1; `leaf-sign` · Leaf-shaped welcome sign · 1 welcome sign · 7¢ · 1. |
| `path-gathering`: **Path gathering**. “The neighbors are marking a pretend path to the gathering. Bring at least four path markers and two map copies. You can spend up to 18 pretend cents.” | Limit 18¢; path markers ≥4; map copies ≥2. | `star-marker` · Star marker · 1 path marker · 3¢ · 4 packets; `diamond-pair` · Diamond marker pair · 2 path markers · 5¢ · 2; `map-copy` · Map copy · 1 map copy · 4¢ · 2; `map-pair` · Map pair · 2 map copies · 7¢ · 1. |

Each packet illustration depicts exactly its stated contents. Plain card and folded pair use one versus two separable card shapes; signs have distinct rectangular versus leaf outlines. Star and diamond markers differ by shape as well as color; map copies are outlined map sheets. Each quantity is also text beside the picture, so the model never relies on color or visual counting alone. The basket shows selected packet count and total delivered units for each need, without duplicating objects outside their packet frames. An unselected catalog picture is clearly labeled “At the stall,” not shown in the basket.

The limits matter. In `welcome-table`, folded pair + plain card + paper sign brings three cards and one sign for 14¢; three plain cards + paper sign costs 15¢; two folded pairs + paper sign brings four cards and costs 16¢. Two folded pairs + leaf sign meets the request but costs 17¢. Folded pair + paper sign costs 11¢ but brings only two cards. In `path-gathering`, two diamond pairs + one map pair bring four markers and two maps for 17¢; one diamond pair + two star markers + one map pair cost 18¢; two diamond pairs + two map copies cost 18¢. Four star markers + a map pair cost 19¢. These examples establish multiple accepted plans and both sorts of near miss, not a prescribed solution for the learner. Keep live answers out of the pre-check worked example.

## Two short turns and exact child copy

The first visit opens a compact request chooser. It shows the two titles, complete request sentences, and **Choose this request** buttons. It allocates no saved root. Selecting a request opens **1. Make a basket**. A two-button turn bar moves freely between **Make a basket** and **2. Check a plan**, with no correctness gate. The current title, two minimums, and spending limit remain visible above either turn. The chooser is one native disclosure once a request is active. Switching requests retains each case's transient basket and cost-idea draft during this visit. Every case or turn change clears displayed check feedback; check again when ready. A new visit starts empty.

Make a basket shows four compact illustrated packet cards. Each card states contents, price **per packet**, and “At this stall: N packets.” Native **Add one packet** and **Remove one packet** buttons are at least 44px high, with contextual accessible names. The first is disabled at stock; the second at zero. The current count reads “In our basket: N packets.” The live summary says, for example, **“Place cards: 2 of at least 3. Welcome signs: 1 of at least 1. Basket cost: 11¢ of 16¢ available.”** It is a supplied running model, not an independent total answer. The visible basket picture groups the selected packets and labels how many units they bring. Empty state: **“The basket is empty. Add any packet to try a plan.”** A **Check this plan** button opens turn 2 even for an empty or over-limit basket; nothing is locked.

The second turn keeps the same request and budget context, current pictured basket, each quantity, and computed cost. It asks **“What do you think these packets cost in all?”** A small optional numeric field accepts a draft of 0–99 pretend cents or an explicit **Not sure** / **Leave blank** choice. Only ASCII digits, optional leading zeroes, and at most two typed digits form a numeric proposal. The field can be revised independently of the basket; an invalid draft remains editable, with a format prompt, and creates no check. An empty or Not sure idea is allowed. **Check basket and idea** reports three distinct things: (1) current basket total versus the spending limit; (2) current delivered counts versus both minimums; (3) whether a definite proposed total matches the pictured packet sum. A numeric mismatch never makes an otherwise feasible basket infeasible. Return to basket and all packet controls remain open.

Exact neutral feedback templates, with computed values and item names inserted:

- Limit fit: **“The packets cost {cost}¢. That is within the {limit}¢ limit; {left}¢ is unspent.”** Limit miss: **“The packets cost {cost}¢, which is {over}¢ above the {limit}¢ limit. Try a different packet choice.”**
- For each minimum: **“{name}: {have} of at least {need}. This part of the request is met.”** or **“{name}: {have} of at least {need}. This part still needs {missing}.”**
- Both fit: **“This is one working plan for the pretend gathering. You can keep exploring other choices.”** Otherwise: **“This basket can be revised. The cost limit and the requested supplies are separate checks.”**
- Definite cost idea: **“Your cost idea was {idea}¢. The pictured packets total {cost}¢. {The idea matches the packet total. | The idea can be revised without changing the basket.}”** No idea: **“No cost idea was checked. The basket was still checked.”**

On any basket or cost-idea edit, remove the old “current check” display and say **“The plan changed. Check this version when you like.”** No earlier result is presented as current. Do not create a checked history for a transient table. An optional **“Why might a swap help?”** disclosure says **“Count what each packet brings. Add each selected packet price once. Compare the cost with the limit, then compare the counts on the packets with the request. A swap can change both.”** The separate worked example uses **an unrelated rehearsal table**: **“A rehearsal table has a 5¢ limit. A 2¢ tag and a 3¢ ribbon cost 5¢ together. If it also needs a sign, that basket still misses its request.”** It demonstrates the two checks without revealing a live basket. A local **Hear the request** control may read the exact current request and supplied values; it neither changes state nor claims recorded support.

The only human reflection prompt is optional and ungraded: **“Which packet would you swap, and what would change in the cost or the supplies?”** It is spoken or thought aloud, with no text submission, keyword grading, or saved family story. A grown-up note on the page says **“Pretend prices and packets make this a planning model. Families make real choices for many reasons. This activity does not judge those choices.”**

## Arithmetic and interaction contract

Represent a case as four nonnegative integer packet counts `q[i]`, each `0..stock[i]`. The rendered cost is `Σ q[i] × price[i]`; each delivered need count is `Σ q[i] × units[i][need]`. Calculate those from the authored case, never from optional typed text. `budgetFit` means cost≤limit; `requestFit` means **every** delivered count≥its minimum; `working` means both. Thus `0` cost fits the budget but does not meet a positive request. Report exact `left=limit−cost` only when nonnegative and `over=cost−limit` otherwise. Stock is per catalog entry, not a shared or persistent inventory. For the finite two-case pack, enumerate every allowed vector and assert ≥2 working baskets per case, plus a budget-only and a request-only near miss. No “optimal basket” function or preference ordering.

Every control remains usable with keyboard/touch, no drag requirement. Disabled bounds controls do not call mutators. After rerender, focus the corresponding enabled control; if it becomes disabled, focus its enabled reverse control, then the turn heading if both unavailable. Preserve the optional idea's caret on ordinary input; turn changes place focus on the destination heading. A detached button, old chooser, old disclosure, or stale speech request must not change a different case or explorer. Check event handlers guard connected scope, exact route, captured explorer, and current ephemeral session/case identity before action. No dialog is needed.

## Minimal lifecycle and integration

This first increment is **transient practice**: one in-memory session tied to the active explorer and this exact route. It holds two case vectors, current case, current turn, optional idea/mode per case, and at most the last computed check for the currently unchanged vector/idea. It never writes to the notebook, localStorage, shared notes download, progress import, or adaptive records. No source-owning saved record exists, so no frozen outcome or recovery path is invented. The authored case table is versioned locally to keep calculations explicit; future persistent work would need a separately reviewed own-source contract. The chooser can explain **“This table is for this visit. Leaving or reloading starts an empty basket. Your other garden work stays saved as before.”**

Leaving `#workshop/budget` for any route, switching explorer, importing a notebook into a separate explorer, clearing progress, or reloading discards the session. Entering with another explorer starts fresh. `#workshop/budget` hashes used to move between turns stay within the same session only if the route remains the bench; simplest implementation uses in-view buttons and one hash. Stop any local narration on route/explorer change under the existing speech lifecycle. Avoid an additional saved root, transfer allowlist entry, notes section, report preflight, or broader workshop registry because no bench action writes to exportable state. Add an early `budget` branch alongside `recut`/`coins` in `workshopPage`, before its older `current(state)` experiment path; forward the active explorer ID from the app route as `recut` already does, and bind this table through the existing `bindWorkshop` call. Reset the small session at the app's existing explorer/import/reset and route-leave hooks. One link on the Workshop table list is sufficient; a Café link may be added only if it clearly says the tables and cupboards stay separate.

Text alternatives name packet counts, coverage and cost. The request cannot be understood from illustration or color alone.
