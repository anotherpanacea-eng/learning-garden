# 99 · Exhibit Label Maker

**Status: independently reviewed specification and content at `ecaa99074f8c616a9b42906964afe1c60a3f58cb`; an implementation candidate is built, pending independent implementation and release review.** [Source audit](../source-audits/99-exhibit-label-maker.md) · [phone/tablet teaching mock](assets/99-exhibit-label-mock.html).

## One small writing move

At optional `#archive/labels`, a fictional visitor has time to read a short label beside a real museum photograph. The learner arranges **three complete, original source-backed sentences** and revises one broad draft claim into wording that names what The Met reports or interprets. Reading the whole label aloud changes which idea the visitor meets first. The app describes that opening and the claim's source boundary; it does not decide that one order is the only good paragraph. A person can read an alternative arrangement and say why it works.

This is a composition and revision step after the existing English Library reading of two Egyptian writing objects. The Library asks questions about a passage; Travelers' Archive selects sources for a reply; Follow the Source Trail compares claims; Puppet Notice Printer reorders *words within one sentence*. None has the learner organize several complete factual sentences for a reader, then repair an overclaim in the resulting label. This first pack is deliberately three sentences from **one museum record per object**, not research across multiple sources, original-prose grading or a general exhibit builder. The broad draft is visibly marked **Draft claim to inspect**, never presented as a museum fact or an independently established historical conclusion. The exact audience is “a visitor reading one short label beside this object”; no age or reading-level claim.

Both real objects are held by The Metropolitan Museum of Art, not Lucie's fictional Paris bookstall. The Library bookstall can invite this different task, with a clear boundary: *“The book showed us two real objects. Make a short label for a visitor using The Met's object records.”* Existing Library visits remain separate and unchanged. Neither finishing a Library story nor a question score gates the label activity; `#archive` and `#paris` offer open routes. No atlas, topic completion, adaptive-reading or historical-proficiency event is emitted.

## Full original first pack

The photo and **source card** stay beside the live label. The source card identifies the object number, approximate date, material and museum; it offers the museum record link and says which sentences are a modern summary of that record. Opening the photo alone does not certify the museum's interpretation. The photo is contextual evidence, while the record supplies the reported use and interpretation. All child-facing object text below is original modern prose; the audit distinguishes it from The Met's own wording. Do not show an invented translation of the marks, identify an unnamed owner, infer a learner's age/gender, or claim the two approximate object dates establish a strict order.

| Case / reader request | Source card and photo | Three complete sentence strips, identified by role | Initial draft |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `writing-board` / “What was this board, and why does its writing interest museum researchers?” | **Writing board of an apprentice scribe.** Ancient Egypt; about 2030 BCE; wood, whitewash and ink. The Met, object 28.9.5. Local full-object photo `egypt-writing-board-28-9-5.jpg`, alt **“A pale rectangular writing board with dark signs of varied shapes and spacing.”** Credit **“The Metropolitan Museum of Art, 28.9.5. Public Domain / CC0.”** [Record](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/545112). | `object`: **“This wooden writing board from ancient Egypt has a pale whitewash surface with ink signs.”** `use`: **“Ink could be removed and the board coated again for another round of writing.”** `claim` careful: **“The Met interprets the uneven signs as a learner's practice; the record does not name that learner.”** `claim` broad draft: **“Every Egyptian child practiced on a board like this.”** | claim, object, use; broad draft. |
| `scribe-palette` / “What did this palette hold, and what remains on it now?” | **Scribe's Palette.** Ancient Egypt; about 2030–1550 BCE; wood and pigment. The Met, object 10.176.62. Local full-object photo `egypt-scribe-palette-10-176-62.jpg`, alt **“A long narrow wooden palette with a central slot and two round ink recesses; one recess has dark material.”** Credit **“The Metropolitan Museum of Art, 10.176.62. Public Domain / CC0.”** [Record](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/560805). | `object`: **“This wooden palette from ancient Egypt has a long slot and two round places for ink.”** `use`: **“Scribes used palettes to hold ink and reed brushes for writing.”** `claim` careful: **“The Met reports black ink still on this palette and very little red; it does not name an owner.”** `claim` broad draft: **“This palette proves its owner used only black ink.”** | use, claim, object; broad draft. |

The image crop keeps the **whole** board or palette and their aspect ratio. The board's uneven marks are visible, but a photograph does not independently prove who made them. The palette photo has no visible original reed brushes; its use comes from the museum record. The palette sentence says scribes used **palettes**, not that a named person owned this one. “Very little red” is an original paraphrase of The Met's “almost gone,” not a claim that no red remains. The board date and palette range are approximate object dates, not photograph or discovery dates.

The source card also carries this exact shared note: **“The photograph shows this surviving object. The Met's record supplies its date, materials and explanation. Our label sentences are modern summaries, not words written on the object.”** A native **Open The Met record** link opens a new tab with `noopener noreferrer`. If the local photo fails or a saved source names an unavailable retired image, show the **saved** alt, credit, object number and record link in a visible text fallback; do not substitute today's photo. Keep v1 asset filenames immutable. No remote image fetch at runtime.

### Teaching, composing and a visitor response

Before Start, the chooser may show both current cases, object title and reader request, without allocating saved work or claiming any current draft. Start is explicit. A started case has three **open, short views**: **Learn**, **Arrange**, and **Read & Keep**. Their nav buttons are always reachable; switching views never resets order, wording, source, last response or kept copy. The view selection is transient UI state, not a new saved page field: it survives redraws of the same live case (so Ask/Keep does not jump away from its response), then resets on case, explorer or route change. A new Start opens Learn; reopening a readable case may open Arrange. Learn shows the full source card, full-object photo/credit, an optional short model and help. The optional model demonstrates **photo observation → museum explanation → a limit**, without silently fixing the child's draft:

| Frame | Board example: exact text / caption | Palette example: exact text / caption |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **“A photograph can show this board's shape and written marks.”** / **“That is a visual observation.”** | **“A photograph can show this palette's long shape and ink recesses.”** / **“That is a visual observation.”** |
| 2 | **“The Met explains that a board like this could be recoated and used again.”** / **“That is a museum explanation about use.”** | **“The Met says palettes held ink and brushes.”** / **“That is a museum explanation about use.”** |
| 3 | **“A photograph cannot tell us that every Egyptian child used one.”** / **“The larger claim would need more evidence.”** | **“A photograph cannot identify the person who used this palette.”** / **“The owner is not named in the record.”** |

These are original teaching sentences about the case's own object, not a second saved label or a photograph-based proof of museum interpretation. Native Previous/Next reveals the three frames in order; captions and a readable static text alternative always remain available. The model is labelled **Supplied example** and records `example` support if requested.

In **Arrange**, show the reader request, a **compact own-source reference** (saved title, object number, small full-object thumbnail/alt and Met record link) and a closed native **See the full source and photo** disclosure with the saved full card, credit and image. That disclosure is reachable without leaving the draft. Do **not** stack the full Learn card or duplicate the complete label above the controls. The three numbered strips themselves display the full current sentences in order. Each has **Earlier** and **Later** native buttons with the role/sentence/position in its accessible name; a boundary button disables. Movement is one position, not dragging. The claim strip has two native choices: **“Draft claim to inspect”** and **“Careful source wording.”** The first displays the exact broad draft with the saved `draftWarning` **immediately beside that sentence**; the second displays the exact careful sentence without a false-warning marker. The learner can move/revise in either order. No removal, insertion, generated wording or forced canonical sequence. A concise live announcement reports the changed position or wording; keyboard focus stays on the moved strip's enabled control or the strip heading. All six permutations are allowed and remain readable, including a claim-first opening. A reader may prefer identification first, but the machine never treats that preference as truth or grammar correctness.

**Read & Keep** shows the compact source reference and full ordered label once, with the broad `draftWarning` **immediately before or beside the claim sentence inside the label**, wherever that strip was moved. It offers **Back to Arrange**, **Ask our visitor**, optional Hear, and Keep/Replace; existing kept material sits in a closed native disclosure unless deliberately opened. Full photo/card remains one disclosure away on this view too. The learner can return to Arrange or Learn at any time. A broad warning is also adjacent to a broad **kept** label in its disclosure and in grown-up notes, before the claim sentence rather than only at the top of a long paragraph. The approved careful sentence reads naturally without a warning. Do not render a broad sentence as an unmarked historical assertion in any current, earlier, kept or notes surface.

**Ask our visitor to read this label** is an explicit action. Its visible response combines **one opening observation** and **one source-boundary observation** selected from the saved source's exact lines below. It is descriptive, never a score, a pass, or an exhaustive prose assessment. The current label remains visible. A later move/wording change marks that response **“Earlier response to a different draft. Ask again to hear about this version.”** It does not rewrite the old response or speak it as current. Asking again replaces only the last response; a kept label retains its own response if one was available. The opening observations are:

| First strip | Exact response |
| --- | --- |
| `object` | **“You introduce the object first, so I know what the label is about before its other details.”** |
| `use` | **“You begin with what people did with this kind of object, then let me find out which object it is.”** |
| `claim` | **“You begin with the claim sentence before identifying the object. I can still read it; would another opening help this visitor?”** |

The claim observations are case-specific:

| Case and wording | Exact response |
| --- | --- |
| Board, broad draft | **“This record describes one board. It does not show that every Egyptian child practiced on one. I would narrow that sentence.”** |
| Board, careful | **“I can tell this is The Met's interpretation of the uneven writing, and I know the learner is unnamed.”** |
| Palette, broad draft | **“The Met reports black ink remaining and red almost gone. That does not prove an owner used only black ink.”** |
| Palette, careful | **“I can tell what The Met reports about both inks without inventing an owner.”** |

Order changes the first observation; changing the claim changes the second. Neither observation says any of the six orders is incorrect. The careful version addresses the specified source overclaim; it does **not** show the child can independently evaluate all museum claims. Source-owned common copy is exactly:

| Field | Text |
| --- | --- |
| `instruction` | **“Move a whole sentence with Earlier or Later. More than one order can work for a reader.”** |
| `help` | **“Look at what the photograph itself shows. Read the museum record for a date, use or interpretation. Which sentence says more than this one source can show? Try naming who says it, or naming what is not known.”** |
| `draftWarning` | **“Draft claim to inspect. This sentence says more than this source supports.”** |
| `carefulNotice` | **“Careful source wording names what The Met reports or interprets.”** |
| `earlierResponse` | **“Earlier response to a different draft. Ask again to hear about this version.”** |
| `humanRead` | **“Tell a person your own version. You can speak, sign or write elsewhere; the app does not judge your words.”** |

Smaller example is the three model frames above. **Think together** and **Hear this label** are optional separate support actions. Local Hear reads the exact current ordered label or the explicitly earlier visitor response; it is browser speech, not commissioned phonics audio, and its success is not recorded as independent reading. For any **broad** current or kept label, the speech request **begins with the saved `draftWarning` before the ordered label**; the claim remains visibly warned in the corresponding text. Careful labels have no warning prefix. Even if a supported saved source changed wording, use its own stored warning and sentence text, never today's copy. The app does not automatically read aloud or grade a child's own voice.

**Keep this label** stores one immutable source-owned snapshot of the current arrangement, wording, support and last response only when that response belongs to this exact arrangement. An unfinished or broad-draft label may be kept, explicitly labelled **Draft with a broad claim to inspect** as well as carrying the adjacent source-owned warning. A person may compare the initial draft, current draft and kept label in Read & Keep. **Replace the kept label** needs a live confirmation naming the one slot it would replace and offering a notes download; Cancel changes nothing. An unreadable kept slot remains raw and cannot be overwritten. A short **“Tell a person your own version”** invitation allows speaking, signing or writing elsewhere; no typed field, audio recording, automatic scoring or extra storage root. A link to the existing Story Loft is clearly for **fictional storytelling**, not a historical evidence submission.

## Own-source and record boundary

One optional top-level `exhibitLabels` root is absent until Start: `{version:1,cases:{[safeCaseId]:work},retainedRaw?}`. No open route, chooser, notes render, photo load or source link creates it. Each work owns `{source,order,wording:'broad'|'careful',support:[],lastResponse:null|record,earlierRawResponses?:[],kept:null|{snapshot,response:null|record}}`. The source owns `version:1,model:'three-sentence-label-v1',id,title,readerRequest,sourceCard:{objectNumber,date,materials,alt,credit,recordUrl,assetId,sharedNote},sentences:{object,use,claim:{broad,careful}},initialOrder,initialWording,modelFrames:[{text,caption}],copy:{instruction,help,draftWarning,carefulNotice,earlierResponse,humanRead},visitor:{openings:{object,use,claim},claims:{broad,careful}}`. The first pack has **two** cases, **three** strips/case and the exact prose above; supported saved v1 cases need not match today's list or count. A case ID is `^[a-z][a-z0-9-]{0,63}$`; a root may contain other safe IDs from prior versions. Source keys and wording are frozen at Start. The source asset ID selects only an approved local immutable asset; an unknown asset shows saved text fallback and never becomes a network URL. The saved record URL is rendered only if it is a well-formed `https:` URL on `www.metmuseum.org` for an object record; otherwise show escaped URL text without a clickable link. No general remote-source browser is introduced.

Supported source v1 has exactly three distinct roles with nonempty bounded prose and an initial permutation; the visitor response strings and source-card strings are complete, so future content edits cannot change a waiting or kept label. A readable case's dictionary key equals its own `source.id` and is looked up as an own property, never through inherited object properties. Bound each sentence, visitor line and copy/model caption to **800 UTF-8 bytes**, each short label/title/material/date/credit/alt to **240 bytes**, each record URL to **300 bytes**, model frames to **1–3 complete frames** (three in this authored pack), and the serialized source to **12 KiB**. Strings must be printable text (reject C0 controls other than ordinary spaces; escape HTML and attributes). Source-card URL/asset restrictions govern display safety, not equality to current catalog. All source IDs/roles are stored as codes, not reconstructed from visible wording. An admitted source can use different saved sentence text, object number, case count and a retired safe case ID while remaining readable. Unsupported model/version/structure stays raw; do not invent a current case from an old ID. Admission can establish internal shape and self-consistency, **not the historical truth of arbitrary imported prose**. Previously saved source text is labelled as a saved version; this spec's museum/content review applies to the exact authored first pack, not to every imported JSON file.

`order` is a permutation of the *saved source's* three roles. Support is a unique list from `help|example|together|narrationRequested|visitorAsked`; no independent-attempt or reading-level claim. `lastResponse` is `{snapshot:{order,wording,support},openingText,claimText}` with the two exact selected source-owned lines and bounded strings; it is current only if order/wording match its snapshot, with object-key order irrelevant. If support changes afterward, the displayed response remains a response to the same label but new support is reported separately; the old support snapshot stays unchanged. A kept snapshot copies the **full source** plus order, wording and support, and its applicable lastResponse or null. Structural and semantic admission checks compare each response to its own frozen source and snapshot, not today's response table; contradictory records are retained raw/unavailable while the readable work remains open. An imported overclaim stays labelled draft; do not auto-repair it. No automatic score, evidence ledger or adaptive suggestion reads this root.

If root/case/response/kept material is malformed, keep the exact imported JSON subtree intact. A malformed sibling case does not hide a readable case; an unreadable `lastResponse` does not prevent moving the current strips. On the next Ask, move that exact malformed value to `earlierRawResponses` before storing the new readable `lastResponse`, atomically subject to the full report preflight. Never silently discard an earlier raw response; show its count in grown-up notes and retain it in the JSON download. A later readable Ask replaces only the previous **readable** last response, which this lesson explicitly keeps as one latest response, not a full response history. An occupied unreadable authored case cannot be silently replaced by Start; offer notes and **Start a separate label notebook**, with live confirmation. That recovery nests the **entire old root**, including prior `retainedRaw`, under the new root's `retainedRaw` and preflights the candidate; depth/byte refusal leaves everything unchanged. Preserve unknown fields and unrelated optional roots through import, export, save-off, explorer switching and reset. No pruning/history wall is needed: only one latest readable response and one kept copy exist per case; imported extra safe cases and raw siblings remain. `retainedRaw` can contain any bounded JSON permitted by the generic importer; do not strip it because it is not understood.

**Every write** (Start, move, wording, support, Ask, Keep/Replace and recovery) builds a detached candidate and calls the host's actual `notesReport(candidate)` → formatted JSON → `prepareProgressImport` preflight before committing. Check the report wrapper, byte limit and depth, not just the isolated root. Refusal is atomic: preserve all draft positions, raw siblings, IDs, last response and prior kept label. Show a focused actionable notice suggesting notes download or a separate explorer. No help/narration/visitor response appears as newly recorded when its required write refuses. Live handlers check the current route, explorer, attached workspace and current case before mutation; stale/detached buttons, closed confirmations and a case switch are inert. Confirmations become invalid after Cancel/close/successful use. A fully readable case remains usable under its own source even if authored content or assets change later.

## Visual and integration contract

The [static mock](assets/99-exhibit-label-mock.html) shows **Arrange**, using the existing board public-domain photo as a compact thumbnail, source disclosure and three letterpress-like sentence strips. At 320 and 820 CSS pixels, the full object remains reachable in the source disclosure without horizontal scrolling, and nav/sentence/claim controls have at least 44px touch targets. Numbered strips and DOM order are the exact reading order. The visitor response in Read & Keep is a single focused `role=status` region after Ask; a visually hidden live announcer handles Arrange changes without repeating a visible paragraph. The photo has real alt text and credit. Do not use color alone to mark the broad draft. Respect reduced motion; no animation required.

Register `#archive/labels` **before** the existing generic `#archive` branch. Preserve the Travelers' Archive itself, English/French Library visits, the two photo invitations and notes. Add a compact invitation from the bookstall and/or Archive landing, one scoped stylesheet, a **named `exhibitLabels` destination label and optional nonobject/raw-root allowlist entry** in `progress-transfer.mjs`, a safe notes formatter in `destinationNotes`, and activity exclusion from generic invitations. Test all three integration seams with actual file import; listing a root in only one importer structure is insufficient. Preserve existing broad source-exposure and visit records; this activity adds no new exposure signal. If implementation finds a real route conflict, narrow the route before changing either established game.
