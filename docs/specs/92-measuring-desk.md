## Exact first method card

Source ID `same-jug-readings`, version1, model `reported-jug-ml-v1`, unit `mL`.

- Title: **The Measuring Desk**.
- Intro: **A real jug has its own marks. With an adult, read the same water twice and keep what you report. This screen cannot see your jug.**
- Materials: **A small measuring jug with readable mL marks, a little cold tap water and a towel. An adult helps.**
- Step1: **Ask the adult to choose a safe jug and a flat, steady surface. Find the mL scale together.**
- Step2: **The adult adds a little cold water. Leave that water in the same jug for both readings. Do not add, pour out or move it between readings.**
- Step3: **Look level with the water. Ask the adult how to read this jug's water surface and marks. Read it once, look away, then read it again. Say each reading before entering it.**
- Step4: **If you can tell, note how many mL lie between two neighboring small marks. This is the gap between marks, not the number of marks. You can leave it unreported.**
- Safety: **Use cold tap water. An adult handles glass or heavy jugs. Keep the screen away from water and wipe spills before moving. You do not need to drink anything.**
- Missing materials: **No safe jug or readable mL scale today? Our picture jug is still open. Nothing here needs a real result.** Link **Use pictured practice** to `#cafe/recipes`, and **Read the real-jug card** to `#field/jug`. Neither creates a measurement record or Field Kit outcome.
- Provenance: **These are readings you and an adult report, not measurements verified by the game.**
- Labels: **First reported reading (mL)**; **Second reported reading (mL)**; **Gap between neighboring marks (mL, optional)**.
- Precision note: **Keep the digits you and your adult can reasonably report. Do not add decimal places just because the box allows them. A real scale may not show an exact value.**
- Unit/format note: **This small notebook accepts mL entries from 0 to 2000, with up to three decimal places. That is an entry format, not a claim about your jug's accuracy. Leave an uncertain entry unfinished. We do not convert cups or ounces here.**
- Comparison prompt: **What do our two recorded numbers say?** Choices, in stable source order: **First is larger** (`first`), **They match** (`same`), **Second is larger** (`second`); optional **Leave our choice blank**. Their fit depends on the actual entered numbers, not a fixed expected physical reading.
- Same summary: **Our two recorded numbers match.**
- Different summary: **Our two recorded numbers differ.**
- Limit: **Matching readings do not prove the exact amount of water. Different readings do not tell us why they differ. Talk together about what to check next.**
- Help: **mL means milliliters, a unit for liquid volume. Keep the same jug and fill for both readings. Read its marks with an adult at water level. We compare the numbers entered here, not the real water. A gap between marks is different from a reading.**
- Reflection: **Would you reread at water level, ask the adult about the marks, or leave the question open? All three are useful choices.** This is an ungraded discussion prompt, not a stored confidence score or diagnostic answer.

Practical wording deliberately delegates the actual household jug's surface/scale reading to an adult. Do not universally prescribe the top or bottom of every surface, call a kitchen jug a calibrated cylinder, infer uncertainty from half a division or make two matching reports proof of accuracy. Optional grown-up source detail can explain that the cited laboratory examples concern water in particular graduated instruments; it must not replace this method with a calibration exercise.
