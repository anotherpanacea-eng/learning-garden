# Word Garden: words and questions

Companion to [spec 34](34-word-garden.md). Child-facing text for the build to use as written, after the literacy review spec 04 requires. Pip is a fox (he/him); Dot is a ladybug (she/her). Nothing here is scored.

## Opening

**First visit:**
> Behind the library is a little walled garden where words grow. Every plant is a word. Snip a cutting from the basket, add it to a plant, and see if something new grows.

**The basket:** *un-* · *re-* · *-er* · *-ful* · *-less* · *-ness*

**What each cutting does** (shown on tap, and read aloud as a whole sentence):

| Cutting | Card |
| --- | --- |
| *un-* | Goes at the front. Means **undo** or **not**. |
| *re-* | Goes at the front. Means **again**. |
| *-er* | Goes at the end. Means **a person who** does it. |
| *-ful* | Goes at the end. Means **full of**. |
| *-less* | Goes at the end. Means **without**. |
| *-ness* | Goes at the end. Turns a describing word into a **thing you can have**. |

## Words that grow

Each grown word blooms with its scene sentence and a one-line meaning. Scenes are set around Willowbank's repairs so the words connect to the town.

| Word | Scene sentence | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| unpack | Pip **unpacks** the tool basket at the footbridge and finds a sandwich he forgot about. | to take things out |
| repack | Dot **repacks** the basket so the hammer is not on top of the sandwich. | to pack again |
| packer | Dot is the best **packer** in Willowbank. She has a system. | a person who packs |
| untie | Pip **unties** the old rope from the bell. | to undo a knot |
| retie | The knot slips, so Pip **reties** it, tighter this time. | to tie again |
| unlock | The baker **unlocks** the bakery door for the first time since the storm. | to open with a key |
| relock | At night she **relocks** it. Nobody is sure who she thinks will steal a bun. | to lock again |
| reread | Dot **rereads** the recipe card, because it says "a pinch" and she wants to know how big a pinch is. | to read again |
| reader | The library's youngest **reader** has a book about ducks. | a person who reads |
| rebuild | The village works together to **rebuild** the mill wheel. | to build again |
| builder | Every **builder** at the mill gets a cup of cocoa. | a person who builds |
| repaint | Pip **repaints** the post office sign. It now says POST OFICE. He will **repaint** it again. | to paint again |
| painter | The **painter** fixes the spelling. | a person who paints |
| unkind | It would be **unkind** to laugh at the sign. Dot laughs a very small amount. | not kind |
| kindness | Bringing cocoa to the builders was a **kindness**. | being kind |
| helpful | The ducks are not **helpful** at the bridge. They sit on the planks. | full of help; useful |
| helper | {guide} is the best **helper** in Willowbank. | a person who helps |
| unhelpful | The ducks are being **unhelpful** again. | not helpful |
| replay | The bell's song was so good that Pip asks to **replay** it. | to play again |
| player | Every bell **player** pulls the rope three times. | a person who plays |
| playful | The miller's cat is feeling **playful** and chases the flour dust. | full of play |
| careful | Be **careful** on the bridge. One plank still wobbles. | full of care |
| careless | A **careless** step could mean wet paws. | without care |

*unhelpful* grows in two steps: graft *-ful* onto **help**, then *un-* onto the new **helpful** bloom. The bloom card says: "Two cuttings! *un-* + *help* + *-ful*."

## Cuttings that don't take

When a graft makes no word in the table, the cutting wilts gently and goes back to the basket. Dot explains, using the part's meaning. Use the specific line when there is one, otherwise the general line for that part.

**General lines by part:**

| Cutting | Line |
| --- | --- |
| *un-* | "*un-* means undo or not. Can you un-___? Dot can't picture it." |
| *re-* | "*re-* means again. It doesn't make a word we know here. Try another plant." |
| *-er* | "*-er* means a person who does it. That one doesn't make a word we know." |
| *-ful* | "*-ful* means full of. Can something be full of ___? Not in this garden." |
| *-less* | "*-less* means without. That doesn't make a word we know here." |
| *-ness* | "*-ness* goes on describing words, like kind. ___ isn't a describing word." |

**Specific lines (funnier, for likely tries):**

| Try | Line |
| --- | --- |
| un + read | "You can't un-read something. Dot has tried. She still remembers the sign." |
| un + build | "Unbuilding is just knocking down. We've had enough of that this year." |
| un + paint | "Un-painting would be lovely for the post office sign, but it isn't a word." |
| un + play | "*Unplay* isn't a word. Pip says it means 'tidying up,' and Dot says nice try." |
| re + kind | "*Rekind* isn't a word, but being kind again is a great idea." |
| pack + ful | "A *packful*? Dot likes it, but it isn't a word in our garden." |
| care + er | "*Carer* is a real word in some places! It isn't one of our garden words yet." |
| kind + er | "*Kinder* is a real word, but it means 'more kind,' not 'a person who is kind.' That's a different *-er*, so it grows in another garden." |
| help + less | "*Helpless* is a real word, but it's a heavy one, so we're saving it for later." |

The *kinder* and *helpless* lines are deliberate: real words that the garden leaves out, explained honestly instead of called wrong.

## Wondering with Dot

After three new words, and again after every three more, Dot asks one question. The choices are read aloud, every choice can be talked through, and the answer shows the scene sentences that help. Use the first question whose words the child has grown.

1. **(*re-*)** "If *re-* means again, what does Pip do when he **rereads** a map?" · He reads it again. · He reads it for the first time. · He throws it away.
2. **(*un-*)** "The baker **unlocks** the door. What was the door like before?" · Locked. · Open. · Painted.
3. **(*-er*)** "A **painter** paints. What does a **packer** do?" · Packs things. · Eats packed lunches. · Opens packs.
4. **(*-ful* and *-less*)** "**Careful** and **careless** both grow from *care*. Which one would you want on a wobbly bridge?" · Careful. · Careless.
5. **(two cuttings)** "**Unhelpful** has two cuttings. What does it mean?" · Not helpful. · Very helpful. · Helping again.
6. **(new word)** "Here's a word that isn't in our garden: **unhappy**. Using what you know about *un-*, what might it mean?" · Not happy. · Happy again. · Full of happy.
7. **(new word)** "Another outsider: **thankful**. Which cutting is in it, and what do you think it means?" · *-ful*: full of thanks. · *re-*: thanking again. · *-er*: a person who thanks.
8. **(make your own)** "Pick a cutting and a plant that we *haven't* tried together. Do you think it makes a word? Say it out loud and see how it sounds." *(Open task: **We tried it** / **Not today**. No answer is checked.)*

Questions 6 and 7 are the transfer questions: the child applies a part's meaning to a word they have not grown.

## Library signs (town job)

When a garden visit grows at least one new word, the end screen offers **Send to the town** (spec 33). The library sign job fills, and one of the child's grown words appears on a sign over the reading corner. The sign rotates through grown words each visit. Sign caption: "Words from the Word Garden."
