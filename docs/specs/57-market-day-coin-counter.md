# Market Day: the Coin Counter

Status: **build-ready** after [independent specification review](../reviews/57-coin-counter-spec-review.md); runtime and release gates remain separate.

## Why this loop

The neighborhood is ready to prepare a weekly market. At a Workshop table, make two different ways to pay a pretend stall's marked price. Teach coin count versus value, value versus physical size, and different combinations with the same value. This is a small foundation slice, not a change/budget simulation or a real economy. Café food, cupboard supplies, guests and existing win conditions stay separate: no one must earn money to eat or play. The practice coins are freely borrowed props.

Use a declared **US cents** pack: penny1¢, nickel5¢, dime10¢, quarter25¢. Show names and values explicitly; do not assume recognition, a country or a school level. Original schematic coins have no copied portraits, dates or Mint artwork. A dime is drawn smaller than a nickel while its value is larger; labels and text alternatives carry the meaning. This does not establish real-coin recognition or handling competence. No dollars, decimals, pounds/pence, notes, change, budgeting, scarcity, competitive commerce or price-realism claim.

## Three open pages

All pages stay available through short **Coins → Make → Compare** tabs. Keep the pretend price, unit and current request in a compact ticket beside the immediate action; keep the active mat and a compact view of the other mat. Clear back links return to the Workshop or Café. All tap/select controls have visible labels and44px targets; no dragging, hover, auto-play, timer or forced success. Existing earlier investigations/orders are unaffected.

**Coins.** Four labelled original coin drawings give the names and values. A short concrete comparison explains that one nickel and one dime are each one coin, but have different values; the smaller dime is worth more. Optional value dots show one cent per dot, with a caption distinguishing dots from actual coins. Reuse countable groups, not a decorative shower of unrelated symbols. Values remain available throughout; this is supported practice, not an independent diagnostic. No coin identity is tested from an unlabeled picture.

**Make.** Choose an authored stall request. Eight stable id/price pairs: `ribbon`5, `leaf-card`6, `paper-star`10, `soup-label`12, `seed-tag`15, `puppet-string`18, `story-program`20, `paper-boat`25 cents. These are invented small prices for pretend stall materials, not food charges or market evidence. A and B are **two alternatives for paying once**, not a combined payment. Select A/B to edit one mat's four add/remove coin controls; both sets remain visible in compact form. Show coin count separately from value in cents. Never equate the number of coins, their size, and their total worth.

Each mat is a complete vector `{penny,nickel,dime,quarter}` of nonnegative integers, with at most25 coins and total value at most50 cents. Zero coins has value0. Adding beyond either bound is refused with a short explanation and no mutation. Removal stops at0. Countable coin pictures agree with the actual vector; the active mat's controls and totals stay near its current question. Coin labels/values are supplied throughout, so no broad independent-addition/coin-recognition claim is made.

Offer reversible exchanges within the active mat: five pennies ↔ one nickel; two nickels ↔ one dime; two dimes and one nickel ↔ one quarter. Require enough of every input coin and a valid result under the same bounds. An enabled exchange conserves **value**, while the number of physical coins can change. Name both before/after counts and unchanged cents; never call a quarter25 coins. No stock, score or response to another game is changed. Optional help/audio/together/value-dot codes remain separate from the numerical result.

**Compare.** A and B remain separate alternatives. “Check these payments” takes a frozen snapshot of each mat with coin count and cents and emits separate `below|exact|above` relations to the price, plus `different` (vectors differ) and `fulfilled` (both exact and different). Describe below/extra as something to revise. Two exact identical vectors are both valid payments; invite another combination rather than calling them wrong. Two different exact vectors earn an unscored scene thank-you: the market sign can show two ways to pay. No access lock or new film. Trying another request, returning to Make or leaving is always available.

Editing a mat after checking keeps every retained earlier check unchanged and marks it clearly as an **earlier check**, not feedback on the edited mat. Only an explicit new check computes a new outcome. Supplied live counts/value may update while earlier recorded outcomes remain frozen. If the current vectors still match the latest check, it can be labelled the latest check of these mats; this compares ids/counts only, not a regrade of historical results.

“Another request” cycles the eight ids in authored order, starting after the current request; a chooser can start any request directly. Both are explicit new-round actions and preserve the previous round, up to12 readable rounds. New rounds start empty with no copied help or answers. Choosing the same id again is familiar practice; no adaptive-placement or limitless-new-content claim. Disclose the12-round limit and oldest-readable eviction before the next explicit new-round action at capacity. No ordinary render/navigation/check evicts a whole round.

Do not impose unread support/familiarity ordering invariants.

Unreadable rounds stay as escaped raw work and readable siblings remain playable; choose a readable current fallback without rewriting raw data. New actions retain unreadable entries. A future/invalid envelope remains exact and read-only while all other destinations remain open.
