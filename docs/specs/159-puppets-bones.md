## Purpose

Strand S2 has no pack for the human skeleton, muscles, joints, senses or nerves; the only claims on Marble's *The Human Body* domain are in proposed [spec 148](https://github.com/anotherpanacea-eng/learning-garden/pull/139) (digestion, teeth, heart, breathing). Spec 148 left a human bones-and-muscles pack as a documented gap and called its breathing coverage partial (no acted-on diaphragm model). This pack fills both gaps.

A child comes to understand that a person's skeleton of living bones does three jobs (support, protection, movement); a few named bones (skull, ribs, spine, thigh bone); that **muscles pull and never push**, so they work in pairs such as the biceps and triceps, and some muscles work without choosing (heart, gut); that a **hinge** (elbow, knee) bends one way and a **ball and socket** (shoulder, hip) swings around, with **tendons** and **ligaments** at the deepest depth; that each of the five senses detects a different kind of thing and **nerves** carry messages between senses, brain and muscles (a simplified picture); and that the **diaphragm** drives breathing, shown with a **bell-jar model** whose faults the child names.

**Never the child's body.** Every fact is about "a person", shown on the Traveler puppet or a drawn person chart. No screen asks the explorer to feel, measure or describe their own body, senses or breathing, and there are no "feel your own" activities. The one off-screen card builds a cardboard arm model and records nothing.

**Out of scope:** injury, illness, first aid, exercise advice, posture, eye and ear anatomy (spec 146 shows the ear), brain parts and neurons.

## The knowledge

All facts below are checked (source audit). Numbers are given with "about" where sources vary.

**The skeleton.** A grown-up's skeleton is usually counted as 206 bones (the count varies a little between people). A newborn has more separate pieces (estimates run from about 270 to over 300) that fuse during growth. Bones are living: they grow, mend and, in the marrow of many bones, make blood cells (spec 148's card, revisited at Keeper's Desk as a fourth job).

- **Skull:** a bony case around the brain. Hollow inside, not solid.
- **Ribs:** 12 pairs, forming a cage around the heart and lungs; all attach to the spine at the back.
- **Spine (backbone):** many small bones (vertebrae) stacked in a column; 33 at birth by the usual count, 24 staying separate in most adults (7 neck, 12 chest, 5 lower back); the rest fuse into the sacrum and coccyx (the coccyx varies from 3 to 5 pieces). It holds the body up and lets it bend. The 12 pairs of ribs join the spine at the 12 chest vertebrae (most ribs touch two of them).
- **Thigh bone (femur):** the longest and strongest bone, the only bone in the thigh; around 18 in (about 45 cm) long in many adults.
- **Hands and feet:** 27 bones per hand (with wrist), 26 per foot; 106 in all, over half.

**Contrast (body plans, building on specs 97 and 150).** The snail and earthworm on the Movement Bench move with muscles and no bones. An insect (spec 150) has its skeleton on the outside. A person's skeleton is inside. One card; animal body groups are not taught here.

**Muscles.** A skeletal muscle moves a bone by **contracting**: getting shorter and pulling. It cannot push. To move a bone back, a second muscle on the other side pulls the other way while the first relaxes. At the elbow, the **biceps** (front of the upper arm) contracts to bend the arm while the **triceps** (back) relaxes; to straighten, the triceps contracts and the biceps relaxes. A relaxed muscle does not push; it is pulled long. Another pair: the front and back thigh muscles at the knee (quadriceps and hamstrings), Keeper's Desk only. A person moves skeletal muscles by choice; heart muscle and the gut's muscles work without a person deciding (spec 148 shows the gut's squeezing; this pack names it).

**Joints.** Where bones meet. A **hinge joint** (elbow, knee) bends and straightens in one direction, like a door. A **ball-and-socket joint** (shoulder, hip) has a rounded end in a cup and moves in many directions; the hip's socket is deeper and steadier, the shoulder's shallower and freer. Some joints barely move (the skull's plates join firmly). Keeper's Desk: **tendons** join muscle to bone and carry the muscle's pull; **ligaments** are strong bands joining bone to bone that hold a joint together and limit how far it moves; smooth **cartilage** caps bone ends and a slippery fluid keeps a moving joint from grinding. The elbow works as a lever; spec 154 owns levers and this pack only points there.

**Senses and messages (a simplified picture).** Eyes detect light; ears detect sound; skin detects touch and pressure, heat and cold, and pain; the tongue detects tastes; the nose detects smells. Every basic taste can be sensed wherever the tongue has taste buds; the "tongue map" of zones is a myth. Five basic tastes are usually named: sweet, sour, salty, bitter, savory (umami). Much of what people call flavor comes from smell. The sense organ detects; the brain makes sense of the message. **Nerves** carry messages from the senses to the brain and from the brain to the muscles. The brain sits inside the skull and weighs about 3 lb (about 1.4 kg) in an adult. Fast nerves carry messages at roughly 70 to 120 m per second; the pack uses 100 as a round example. Keeper's Desk: in a **reflex** (pulling a hand from something hot), the message turns around in the spinal cord, and the muscles move before the brain has finished noticing. People have more than five senses (balance, from the inner ear; knowing where body parts are without looking); one Keeper's note only.

**Breathing (building on spec 148).** Spec 148 teaches the air path (nose or mouth, windpipe, two lungs) and that breathed-out air is a changed mixture. This pack teaches what moves the air. Lungs are spongy and have no muscles of their own; they cannot move by themselves. Breathing in: the **diaphragm**, a dome-shaped muscle under the lungs, contracts and flattens, and rib muscles lift the ribs up and out; the chest space grows and air flows in. Breathing out, at rest: the muscles relax, the diaphragm domes up, the space shrinks and air flows out.

**The bell-jar model.** Jar (chest), Y-tube (windpipe and branches), two balloons (lungs), rubber sheet (diaphragm). Pull the sheet down: the space grows and the balloons fill. **What it gets wrong:** the jar cannot move, and real ribs do; the sheet is pulled from outside, and the real diaphragm contracts by itself and flattens rather than bulging downward; balloons are empty bags, and real lungs are spongy with tiny air sacs; the model has no heart, no rib muscles and a stiff tube.

## The game or activity

**Every screen:** *The Traveler is a puppet. The chart shows a drawn person.* Each bench has its own model line too.

**(a) Frame or flop.** A cloth puppet and the Traveler's wooden frame. **Stand them up**: the cloth one slumps. Then the drawn person chart: tap a bone (skull, ribs, spine, thigh bone), then a job tile (Support, Protection, Movement). Skull and ribs fit Protection; spine and thigh bone fit Support or Movement (both accepted). Model line: *A simplified drawing. Real skeletons have about 206 bones; this chart shows four.*

**(b) String Arm, model `string-arm-v1`.** A side view of the Traveler's arm: upper-arm bone, forearm bone, pinned elbow, a **front cord** (biceps) and a **back cord** (triceps), each tied across the elbow. Buttons: **Pull front cord**, **Pull back cord**, **Push front cord**, **Push back cord**, **Let go**. Three poses: straight, half-bent, bent. Pulling the front cord bends the arm one pose; pulling the back cord straightens it one pose. Pushing a cord makes it go slack and wavy, and the arm does not move ("A cord can't push."). Each cord is labeled *pulling, shorter* or *relaxed, pulled long*. Both pulls at once hold the arm still ("Both pulling: the arm stays put."). Model line: *A made-up model. Real muscles shorten by themselves; here, the cords are pulled. A real arm has many more muscles.*

**(c) Joint shop.** Four empty joint sockets on the Traveler (elbows, knees, shoulders, hips; two of each). Two joint pieces in a tray: **hinge** and **ball and socket**. Tap a piece, tap a socket, then **Test**: the limb swings through that joint's moves. A wrong fit is shown honestly (a hinge shoulder only swings forward and back) and can be refitted. Keeper's layer: two tag types, **tendon** and **ligament**, to place on a close-up elbow (tendon from cord to bone; ligament bone to bone). Model line: *Puppet joints are wood and pins. Real joints have smooth cartilage, slippery fluid and living straps.* The kept Traveler stores only which piece sits in each socket.

**(d) Message relay.** A town moment plays on a drawn person (never the Traveler, which has no senses): the baker's bell, the smell of bread, a hot cup. Five relay cards appear: *sense organ notices*, *nerve carries the message in*, *brain makes sense and decides*, *nerve carries a message out*, *muscles pull*. Order them; each placed card lights a line on the chart. Then a match board: five sense organs to five things they detect. Keeper's layer: the hot-cup relay adds a **shortcut** card (the spinal cord turns the message around). Model line: *A simplified picture. Real nerves carry many messages at once, both ways, very fast.* A puppet-analogy card says how strings are like nerves and how they differ.

**(e) Bell-jar bench, model `bell-jar-v1`.** A bell jar from the library's science shelf. **Pull down** and **Let go** on the sheet (two states): pulling shades the growing space, arrows show air entering, balloons fill; letting go reverses it. Label mode: tap a part, then its body name. Then a "model or body?" sort of six cards. Always visible: *A model. The jar can't move, the sheet is pulled from outside, and balloons are not spongy like real lungs.* No health advice comes from the model.
