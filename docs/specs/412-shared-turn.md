## Mathematical model and exact geometry

Angles are amounts of turn in degrees. One full turn is 360°; half is 180°. In Build, A=first and B=whole−first. They share one vertex and a dividing ray, cover the named whole in order, with no gap or overlap. A+B=whole. The other measure follows from the whole and first part; this is an immediately visible relation, not a graded answer. Show actual integer measures and `A° + B° = W°` in ordinary HTML. At zero, say **No turn · 0°**; at 360, say **One full turn · 360°**. Coincident endpoint directions do not make a full selected turn0°.

One common pin at(160,160) in a320×320 viewBox. Direction at angle α is(cosα,−sinα). Starting ray direction=tilt, divider=tilt+A, ending ray=tilt+whole. Use exact cardinal endpoints or reasonable float tolerance, never recover angle names by rounded pixels. Visible ray lengths115,135,100 respectively; widths10/2/6, gold starting ray solid, dark divider solid, blue ending ray dashed. Thin dashed continuations follow each ray through radius152 and arrow tips at155 with bases at146, meeting the radius145 whole outline; start/end/divider arrowhead base widths14/10/6 preserve visible layers. They follow the same mathematical rays, never offset them sideways. Draw starting ray first, ending ray next, divider last, so coincident styles remain visible. They stand for rays, not finite sticks. The end ray at a full turn overlays the start ray: distinct width/style and text identify both. Divider may coincide at A=0 orA=whole; identify all roles in the text, rather than separating their directions for visibility. Exact added copy: **The ending ray points along the starting ray after one full turn.** At A=0: **The divider points along the starting ray.** At A=whole: **The divider points along the ending ray.** When all three line up: **All three rays point the same way. Follow the marked sweeps to distinguish no turn from one full turn.** Show only this all-three sentence when whole360 and A is0 or360. Otherwise show the full-turn sentence for whole360, and the appropriate starting/ending divider sentence for an endpoint A; show no degenerate sentence for interior A on a half turn.

A's counterclockwise curved sweep has radius 70, B's radius 100, and target-whole outline radius 145. Different radii keep sweeps distinguishable; these are angle measures, not comparisons of arc length or colored area. Every positive sweep follows its selected counterclockwise path, including reflex portions. Arc helper splits into positive segments of at most180°; SVG y inversion uses sweep flag0;360° uses two180° arcs and0° none. Use distinct line patterns plus labels for A/B, not color alone; arc arrowheads use polygons tangent to the counterclockwise endpoint (screen direction −sinθ,−cosθ), absent for0°; no repeated internal SVG IDs. Do not replace a reflex sweep with its smaller complement. Diagram rotates rigidly with tilt; its labels remain readable HTML at least16px.

Cover uses the exact corresponding adjacent-part geometry, with first fixed by the job. While closed, B's visible/accessible measure is **?° · covered label**, and equation **G° + ?° = W°**. The drawing may help estimation; the exact answer follows from the sum. While closed, no hidden B/reference measure is rendered in markup: no collapsed/hidden/visually hidden node, title, caption, status, aria target, data attribute, id/class token or input value carries it. Only SVG geometry attributes (d, x/y coordinates, points, transform) depict B. Visible W/G, a raw draft and the independently kept own idea may happen to equal R; this is not a blanket ban on that number. Test field ownership and absent reference nodes/metadata, not a global numeric-string search (job2 has G=R=90). When open, a separate reference says **The other part is R°. W° − G° = R°. Together, G° + R° = W°.** and the main B label/equation shows R. Never label an own idea as the actual angle. ## Four complete teaching cards

The quoted text is exact child copy; drawing instructions are author notes. All examples are labeled **Worked example · not your parts**. No lesson writes or fills a child's work.

1. **One whole, two parts.** “A half turn is 180°. These two parts meet at the same pin. They fill the half turn without a gap or overlap. 60° + 120° = 180°. We add amounts of turn, not the lengths of the drawn arms.” Diagram whole180, first60.
2. **All the way around.** “A full turn is 360°. The starting and ending rays point the same way after one full turn. That is still a full turn, not no turn. Here 90° + 270° = 360°. Follow the marked second sweep from the dividing ray to the ending ray. Don't replace it with the smaller 90° turn between its ends.” Diagram whole360, first90. This worked pair is different from all eight jobs.
3. **Cover a label.** “The two parts fill 180°. One part is 135°. Call the missing measure x. That letter stands for a measure we do not yet know. 135° + x = 180°, so x = 180° − 135° = 45°. Check: 135° + 45° = 180°.” Diagram whole180, first135, numeric second45 shown because this is an explicit worked example, a different pair from all eight jobs.
4. **Check the gap.** “Do the marked parts fill the half turn? Here two 60° parts leave a 60° gap inside a half turn. 60° + 60° = 120°, not 180°. We must count the gap too. Add parts to find the whole only when they really fill it, with no gap or overlap.” Separate worked diagram: outer180; first arc from 0 to 60 at radius 70; second arc from 120 to 180 at radius 100; dotted uncounted gap 60 to 120 at radius120. All three intervals share the pin; show rays at0/60/120/180. The60° ray has solid length135/width2 and the120° ray solid length125/width2; both continue to152 with arrow tips155. Gap arc uses dotted3 5 and a grey label, distinct from the ending ray's dashed10 6 pattern. Third uncounted gap label **Gap 60°**, textual equation **60° + 60° + gap 60° = 180°**. This finite gap illustration is not passed through the two-adjacent-parts pose as if it were adjacent. It rotates with the selected tilt. No overlapping-angle or vertically-opposite rule is commissioned.

Build teaching line: **A degree is one of 360 equal parts of a full turn. These two marked parts share one pin and fill the whole. Turn the whole picture: their measures stay the same. The curves are drawn at different sizes so you can tell them apart. Their size does not change the turn.** Repeat those two curve-size sentences below every Build/Cover diagram, so a direct Cover entry has the same explanation. Diagram description template: **A drawing of a W-degree [half/full] counterclockwise turn at one pin. Starting ray, dividing ray and ending ray show first part A degrees and second part [B degrees / covered]. Ray lengths do not set the angle measures.** Degenerate explanations add **The divider points along the [starting/ending] ray.** All text is original. Original diagram only, no external pictures/fonts.
