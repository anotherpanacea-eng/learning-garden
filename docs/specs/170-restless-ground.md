## 1. Purpose

Draft [spec 139](https://github.com/anotherpanacea-eng/learning-garden/pull/138) teaches what the ground is (rock families, Earth's layers, plates, the Pyrenees collision, what quakes and volcanoes are, volcano status). The rest of Marble's **Volcanoes & Earthquakes** domain has no reference in `curriculum/topic-coverage.csv`. This pack asks: **how do people understand restless ground, and what do they do about it?**

A child comes to understand volcano insides and eruption styles, the three kinds of plate edge, how shaking is measured, how volcanoes are watched under uncertainty, what a tsunami is, and that people cannot stop these events but reduce harm, with trade-offs. The tone is calm: watching, measuring, building, preparing. No death counts, no peril for the cast, no "it could happen to you."

## 3. The knowledge

Plain adult prose, sourced in the audit. Labels: "A true thing from science," "A story from Willowbank," "A made-up model."

**Inside a volcano.** Melted rock is *magma* underground and *lava* at the surface: one material, named by place. Magma collects in a *magma chamber* below the volcano, rises through a *vent*, and comes out at a *crater*. Lava glows because it is hot; it is not fire (spec 139; revisited with Ember).

**Eruption style and shape.** Runniness and gas matter most. Runny magma lets gas out easily; lava flows far and builds a broad **shield volcano** (Mauna Loa, one of the world's largest active volcanoes). Sticky magma traps gas until it bursts out; ash, rock and lava layers build a steep **stratovolcano** (Vesuvius). Runny, gassy magma can fountain and build small **cinder cones**, like Garrotxa's (Hawaiian to Strombolian eruptions, some steam blasts where magma met water). It is a spectrum, and shape is a clue, not a rule: some steep cones are runny basalt.

**Eruptions build as well as change land.** Surtsey, off Iceland's south coast, rose from the sea in eruptions from 1963 to 1967. Plants arrived from 1965; it is protected for science (UNESCO World Heritage, 2008). Pompeii is taught as a record in [spec 157](https://github.com/anotherpanacea-eng/learning-garden/pull/142) and as a card in spec 139; this pack cites it and does not retell it.

**Plate boundaries.** Earthquakes happen when rock under strain suddenly slips along a *fault*. At plate edges (USGS):
- *Moving apart (divergent):* magma rises into the gap and makes new crust; volcanoes tend to be gentler. The Mid-Atlantic Ridge runs through Iceland and spreads about 2.5 cm a year.
- *Pushing together (convergent):* an ocean plate diving under another brings deep earthquakes and explosive volcanoes (the Andes); continents resist sinking, so crust crumples into mountains (Iberia and Europe, the Pyrenees; spec 139).
- *Sliding past (transform):* plates grind sideways; earthquakes can be large, and there are usually no volcanoes (the San Andreas Fault).
- *An exception:* Garrotxa sits in the European Cenozoic Rift System, away from a plate edge; stretched crust cracked and eruptions followed the faults.

**Measuring earthquakes.** A seismometer's hanging mass lags behind by inertia as the frame shakes; the difference is recorded as a seismogram. Magnitude is one number for an earthquake's size: each whole step is ten times taller wiggles (amplitude) and about 32 times more energy (USGS). Intensity is shaking at a place and falls with distance; Europe uses the 12-degree EMS-98, assigned from what people felt and what happened to buildings. One earthquake has one magnitude and many intensities. Faster P waves arrive before S waves, and the gap grows with distance (a labeled Keeper's Desk model only).

**Watching volcanoes.** Rising magma causes small earthquakes (seismometers), swells the ground (tiltmeters, GPS) and releases gas (sulfur dioxide sensors). Rain can tilt the ground too, so observatories keep rain gauges. No single sign is proof; scientists weigh them together. USGS alert levels: Normal, Advisory, Watch, Warning (aviation colors Green, Yellow, Orange, Red; Orange means increased potential of eruption, timing uncertain). The Watch Desk's colors are labeled Old Ash's own. Forecasts give likelihoods over weeks to months, not a day; one that ends calmly is not a failure. The **Vesuvius Observatory** (1841) is the world's oldest volcano observatory, now part of INGV. Station ZVGAR near Garrotxa's Fageda d'en Jordà (owner IGN, per ICGC) has recorded since August 2021; a 2021 report said it recorded little.

**Tsunamis.** A series of very long waves made when the sea floor suddenly moves (usually an undersea earthquake; also landslides and eruptions). At sea it is a few feet high or less (NOAA), unnoticed by ships, and fast: speed ≈ √(gravity × depth), about 200 m/s (over 700 km/h) at 4,000 m. Near shore it slows and grows, arriving as several waves; later ones can be larger. "Tidal wave" is a misleading old name; tides do not cause tsunamis. NOAA's DART sea-floor pressure sensors notice 1 cm of extra water in 6,000 m and relay it by buoy and satellite. Since 2005 UNESCO has coordinated a warning system for the North-eastern Atlantic and Mediterranean (NEAMTWS), with providers in France, Greece, Italy, Portugal and Türkiye.

**Building and preparing.** Braces and shear walls stop a frame racking (triangles keep their shape); a wide base resists tipping; *base isolation* puts flexible bearings under the building so less shaking reaches it; a heavy damper counters sway (Taipei 101's 660-tonne pendulum, mainly for wind). Spain's seismic building code (NCSE-02) is mandatory. Beyond buildings: warning sensors, practice and plans, where to build. Each reduces harm, none removes the hazard, each costs something.

**Safety.** No bench, model or quiz gives safety advice. Spec 139 holds the earthquake card; this pack adds only a Grown-up note pointing to official tsunami guidance and the local authority.

## 5. The game

### 5.1 Volcano cutaway
One labeled cross-section. Tap the glowing blob up chamber → vent → crater; at the rim its label changes from *magma* to *lava*. Tap a part to name it.

### 5.2 Honey and Water bench (`magma-v1`, a simplified picture)
Two switches: **Runny / Sticky** and **Little gas / Lots of gas**. **Erupt** plays a short animation; five eruptions build a shape.

| Magma | Gas | Eruption (model) | Shape after 5 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Runny | Little | Lava pours and spreads far | Wide, low shield |
| Runny | Lots | Lava fountains; cinders fall near the vent | Small cinder cone |
| Sticky | Little | Thick lava oozes and piles up | Steep dome |
| Sticky | Lots | Gas bursts out; ash and rock | Steep layered cone |

Label: *A simplified picture. Real volcanoes mix these, and some change style over time.* The honey panel is on-screen only.

### 5.3 Plate Edge Board
Buttons **Apart**, **Together**, **Slide past** play the motion; the child taps result chips (new crust, gentle volcanoes, mountains, explosive volcanoes, earthquakes, no volcanoes; earthquakes fit every edge) onto each, then places Iceland, the Andes, the Pyrenees and the San Andreas Fault. Garrotxa, "Off the edge," comes last.

### 5.4 Shiver Book (`seis-v1`, a made-up model)
A weight on a spring with a pen over moving paper: **Shake the table** moves frame and paper while the weight stays nearly still and draws the trace. A **staircase** shows magnitudes 2 to 5 at the same distance, each wiggle ten times taller (drawn as rows of boxes, since the top would not fit). A **three-town map** shows one made-up quake with one magnitude and three intensities. Keeper's Desk adds **wave gap**: P waves 6 km/s and S waves 3 km/s (round model numbers), so the distance is the gap in seconds × 6 km. Label: *A made-up model with round numbers.*

### 5.5 Watch Desk (`watch-v1`, a made-up volcano)
"Old Ash," a fictional volcano on a fictional island. Weekly cards show three meters: small earthquakes a day, ground bulge (mm), gas (low/medium/high), plus a rain gauge. The child chooses a color (Green, Yellow, Orange, Red) each week and taps **Why** chips (one sign / several signs / rain). Then one of two authored endings plays: *Old Ash calms down* or *a small eruption on the empty upper slope*. Both praise careful watching; colors are never scored. Label: *A made-up volcano. Real observatories use many more instruments and years of records.*

### 5.6 Tsunami Tank (side view, a simplified picture)
**Sea floor slips** lifts the whole water column; the wave crosses deep water low and fast, grows on the shelf and arrives as three waves. A wind-wave button moves only the surface. Older depths add a depth slider (speed √(9.8 × depth)). No people, homes or boats.

### 5.7 Shake Table (`shake-v1`, a made-up model)
A block tower on a table. Budget 5 coins. Features, each at most once:

Plain tower steadiness 1. Cross braces +2 (2 coins); wide base +1 (1 coin); rubber feet, base isolation, +2 (3 coins); swing weight, a damper, +1 (2 coins). Each shows a "Why it helps" line (companion).

Shakes: gentle 1, moderate 2, strong 3, big 4, **What if?** 6 (bigger than any in the old ledger). Rule: steadiness ≥ shake → *stands*; steadiness one less → *stands but cracks, needs mending*; otherwise the blocks topple softly. **Change one thing** locks all features but one. By enumeration: 12 designs fit the budget, 6 stand in the big shake, and none stands undamaged in What if?: harm is reduced, never removed. Label: *A made-up model. Real buildings are tested by engineers with real codes.*

### 5.8 Plan Grid (for Nuria's market hall)
Four solution cards (Stronger building, Warning sensors, Practice and plans, Choose firmer ground) each show three made-up marks: protects the building, helps people move in time, cost. The child picks one of Vadoalto's priorities ("money is short," "the hall must stay open," "lots of visitors"), chooses two solutions and says why to a grown-up. No single right pair; feedback names the trade-off. A grayed "Stop the earthquake" card answers: *Nobody can stop an earthquake. You can make it do less harm.*
