## The missing action

At optional **`#cafe/portions`**, Moss joins two portions of a pretend loaf for the café's shared table. The learner moves **one already-cut piece** from the waiting portion to the serving tray, then can return it. The amount **on the tray** increases or decreases; each piece's size and the whole-loaf unit remain fixed. All food across the waiting place and tray is conserved. This is a small **M3-fractions step 6** encounter in the canonical strand map, not M2's whole-number operations or a whole fraction-operations course.

Ribbon Recut changes cuts while its pictured amount stays fixed. Fraction Trail moves one flag along a supplied/constructed partition. Same Amount, New Cuts reconstructs the same amount in another unit; Tenths supplies fraction/decimal names; Sharing Counter partitions one loaf or packs a selected part. Here **two existing fractional quantities are joined in a third place**, preserving the identities and sizes of the pieces. The learner can see why a count of quarter-size pieces changes while the denominator still names quarters. A prompt with a new answer would not supply this action.

This first bench is transient and unscored. No input field, Check, saved source, Keep, history, stock reward, guest completion, mastery, film, prerequisite lock or automatic question selection is needed. Native movement is visual practice, not evidence of independently adding fractions. It neither changes café ingredients/orders nor consumes real food. ## One declared whole and twelve finite states

**One whole means one uniform model loaf of this fixed size.** Both starting portions use that **same-sized loaf unit**, even if they came from two loaves. Equal model pieces have the same width, height and area, not just the same count. The serving tray is a container; its capacity is **not** the whole in the fraction's denominator. Each outlined tray row is room for **one** loaf of the declared size. A second row never halves or rescales the first row or its pieces. The unit guide is a reference drawing, not extra food.

Each case owns fixed integers `(a,b,d)`: A has `a` pieces already on the tray; B has `b` pieces waiting; each piece is `1/d` loaf. A's pieces `A1…Aa` never leave the tray in this first pack. Session integer `k` is the number of B pieces moved, `0≤k≤b`. B1 through Bk occupy the next tray slots, in order; B(k+1)…Bb remain in their original waiting slots. Return moves Bk back to its original waiting slot. No piece is copied, lost, recut, rotated, resized or changed into another unit.

| ID / title | Exact original request | `(a,b,d)` | Every possible tray amount as k increases | Original sum when all B is moved |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `quarter-supper` / Quarters for supper | “One quarter is on the tray. Two more quarters are waiting. Bring both waiting pieces over, then try returning one.” | `(1,2,4)` | `1/4 → 2/4 → 3/4` | `1/4 + 2/4 = 3/4` |
| `third-picnic` / Thirds for the picnic | “One third is on the tray. Another third is waiting. Join them without changing the size of either piece.” | `(1,1,3)` | `1/3 → 2/3` | `1/3 + 1/3 = 2/3` |
| `sixth-tasters` / Sixths for the tasters | “Two sixths are on the tray. Three sixths are waiting. Move the waiting pieces over one at a time.” | `(2,3,6)` | `2/6 → 3/6 → 4/6 → 5/6` | `2/6 + 3/6 = 5/6` |
| `past-one` / A little more than one loaf | “Three quarters are on the tray. Two quarters are waiting. Use the next same-size loaf space when the first is full.” | `(3,2,4)` | `3/4 → 4/4 → 5/4` | `3/4 + 2/4 = 5/4` |

All four cases are open in any order. Choosing a case starts k=0 and opens Learn. Quarter supper is the first suggestion, not a gate. The final case crosses one whole by the visible step `3/4 → 4/4 → 5/4`: four quarter-size pieces fill the first loaf space; the fifth starts the equally sized next space. Supply **“4/4 is one whole loaf”** and **“5/4 is one whole loaf and one quarter of the same-size loaf”** when those actual tray amounts are displayed. These names are offered, not an assessed conversion. Do not automatically simplify other supplied labels: a tray of two quarters still counts quarters as `2/4` here.

Exact quantities at every state are `trayCount=a+k`, `waitingCount=b−k`, `allCount=a+b`; corresponding loaf amounts have denominator d. The displayed addition is **`a/d + k/d = (a+k)/d`**, with the terms labelled **Started on tray / Moved from B / Now on tray**. Thus a partly moved case never falsely displays the full original sum as the current tray amount. A separate waiting caption names `(b−k)/d`. At k=b the equation is the original sum in the table. At k=0, say **“No B pieces have moved yet”** beside the supplied zero term. All numerators, denominators, mixed names and explanations are model readouts; no independent response or exposure score follows.

## Complete teaching and interaction copy

Learn starts with the exact idea **“The bottom number names the size of our pieces. The top number counts those pieces. Joining quarter-size pieces gives more quarters on the tray; it does not make the pieces smaller.”** Then **“A whole loaf here is always this same size. Empty outlines show room, not extra food. Moving food changes what is on the tray, but not how much food is here altogether.”** It shows the whole guide and a **separate supplied example** with `(a,b,d)=(1,1,4)`, independent of the live case. Three freely selectable native **Example picture 1/2/3** buttons show:

2. k=1: **“Move the waiting quarter beside the first. Two quarter-size pieces are on the tray: 1/4 + 1/4 = 2/4.”** Waiting `0/4`.
3. k=0: **“Return that same piece. The tray has one quarter again. The piece has moved; its size has not changed.”** Same geometry/counts as picture 1.

Return it whenever you like.”** The two actions are **“Move one {unit} to the tray”** and **“Return one {unit} to waiting”**, where singular unit is quarter, third or sixth.
