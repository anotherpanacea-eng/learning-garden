# Rain Paths: a storm-water tray investigation

## Purpose and first slice

The storm left the garden's low path wet. At Water Lab, help the gardeners ask: **what can change the amount of rain that travels over a surface?** Add an original manually operated two-tray model, `#science/rain`, beside the existing molecular Water Lab visits. Predict, move rain through the visible apparatus, compare what changed, then try a tiny runoff-holding plan. This is a new manipulation loop, not another text quiz.

The first slice is deliberately an **invented closed-tray token model**. It illustrates cover, available storage and accounting; it is not a soil simulator, physical experiment, hydrology dataset, infiltration-rate model, flood forecast or drainage recommendation. Every rain token represents the same arbitrary amount of model water, **not a molecule, a raindrop, a millimetre, a litre, or a unit of time**. No actual floodwater activity or equipment is needed. No claim about whether the real path is ready follows from playing.

Use four compact pages: **choose and predict → rain and follow → compare → plan and keep**. Every case/page is open; predictions and all questions are optional, unscored practice. Back and breaks retain the exact apparatus state. The relevant trays, current step, changed factor, supplied controls and water ledger stay beside the immediate action. No timer, waiting for real days, score, access lock, inventory reward or learner identity.

## Scientific grounding and the boundary of the analogy

Primary sources checked 2026-10-20 local (2026-10-21 UTC):

- [USGS Water Science School: Infiltration and the Water Cycle](https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/infiltration-and-water-cycle): infiltration depends on rainfall characteristics, material, moisture, cover and slope. Earlier wetness can leave less room for new water. Ground water can also move later; storage is not permanent disappearance.
- [USGS: Surface Runoff and the Water Cycle](https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/surface-runoff-and-water-cycle): runoff and infiltration depend on several conditions. Urban cover and drainage alter pathways; even stormwater-management features have limits during large events.
- [EPA CADDIS: Urbanization—Stormwater Runoff](https://www.epa.gov/caddis/urbanization-stormwater-runoff): sealed surfaces commonly reduce infiltration and increase surface runoff. The document includes third-party copyrighted figures; use no copied artwork or percentages.
- [EPA research: Infiltration Models](https://www.epa.gov/water-research/infiltration-models): the relation between incoming rate and the ground's ability to admit water matters, so empty storage alone is insufficient for a real prediction. This supports the explicit rate limitation of our model.
- [USGS Unsaturated Zone Flow Project: drought](https://wwwrcamnl.wr.usgs.gov/uzf/investigations/drought.html): water repellency and preferential paths complicate dry-ground behaviour. This supports the warning against a universal dry-soil rule.
- [EPA education: Streams in the City](https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2015-10/documents/streams_city_activity.pdf), activities I–IV: studied its sequence from water pathways to a neighbourhood design. Its older school calculations make strong simplifying assumptions linking area to infiltration. **Do not copy those assumptions, numerical fractions, worksheets or diagrams.** This is a design reference, not validation of our token capacities.

Keep one short child-facing line beside the current machine: **“Our pretend machine holds water in spaces. Real ground has more paths, and water can keep moving.”** Put the fuller caveats and source links in clearly labelled Details: these are rules we chose, real runoff can begin before storage fills, wet ground can transmit water, and this model omits deep drainage, evaporation, plants and changing rain speed. Also explain there that very dry ground does not always absorb readily: texture, compaction and water-repellent dry surfaces can change the outcome. Do not teach “dry soil always absorbs,” “wet ground never lets water through,” “all paving behaves identically,” or “more storage prevents flooding.” The optional drier/wetter analogy concerns **different starting water in the same machine**, not a universal ranking of soils.

The source-backed ideas are qualitative. All capacities, routes and exact counts below are original arbitrary rules, not extracted measurements or calibrated estimates. The visible rule of filling available slots before runoff is specific to this toy; it cannot establish real rate-limited infiltration. No water-quality, drinking-safety, ecological health or whole-water-cycle competency claim is introduced.

## Apparatus and four open cases

Two matched **tabletop water-path machines** sit side by side. Each has the same clear storage chamber with **12 numbered slots in a 3 × 4 array**, an entrance, an outlet and a collector; their geometry and input are matched. Both receive the **same number of new rain tokens each**. Keep those controls visible; no hidden change in chamber size or rain. Use a mechanical cutaway with a clearly visible entrance, solid lid and outlet arrows. Immediate controls and tray titles say **open entrance / sealed lid** and **0 already stored / 8 already stored**. Do not draw realistic soil, roots, calibrated pores or terrain, and do not label either machine dry/wet. Permeability and prior rain in real ground belong in the short optional analogy in Details, not the machine's operating labels. This is an original storage/cover apparatus, not a study of actual soil or paving products.

Four stable case IDs, with supplied controls and no random result generation:

| Case | Tray A | Tray B | New rain on EACH tray | Only factor changed within the pair |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `cover-12` | Open entrance, 0 prior tokens | Sealed lid, 0 prior tokens | 12 | Cover |
| `cover-24` | Open entrance, 0 prior tokens | Sealed lid, 0 prior tokens | 24 | Cover |
| `space-12` | Open entrance, 0 prior tokens | Same open entrance, 8 prior tokens | 12 | Starting stored water |
| `space-24` | Open entrance, 0 prior tokens | Same open entrance, 8 prior tokens | 24 | Starting stored water |

The 8 earlier tokens are visible from setup, patterned differently and labelled **already here before this rain**. New tokens have a different shape/fill and a persistent legend. Colour alone is insufficient. Prior tokens remain where they started in this closed model; this does not claim real old and new water stay unmixed or cannot drain. Call the comparison “more empty storage / less empty storage”; mention drier/wetter only as an explicitly limited analogy in Details, never in tray titles or immediate controls. Do not call the 8-of-12 tray fully saturated.

The input is **12 or 24 per tray**, not a shared packet divided between trays. Pair totals are 24 or 48 new tokens respectively. Per-tray ledgers are primary so no diagram silently doubles or halves rain. Choosing a case restores its pending draft; “Restart this case” explicitly resets it to its authored starting water, leaving earlier checked/kept snapshots. A replay is familiar practice, not a second independent experiment.

## Predict and manually move the water

Prediction: “Which tray might send more of this new rain to the outlet?” A / B / same / not sure. Record first and latest choice, separate support and whether any result from **this model pack** had already been available. Do not require a prediction. Exposed initial controls/capacities are context, not an outcome; narration explaining an actual result, a worked example, completed/pending result picture or a kept result counts as result exposure.

The manipulation is a repeatable three-step mechanism, applied in sync to both matched trays:

1. **Bring down four new rain tokens on each tray.** Move exactly four from each cloud reserve to that tray's surface. Nothing is deleted; the reserve and surface counts update. A text equivalent announces both trays' positions. A batch is four arbitrary water tokens, not a time interval or rainfall-intensity unit.
2. **Follow the opening.** With the entrance open, move up to four surface tokens into available slots; with the solid lid sealed, show that none can enter. Keep excess visibly on the surface. The selected entrance/lid, empty-slot count and reason are visible before and after. Never imply a sealed chamber admits tokens simply because its slots are empty.
3. **Follow the surface water to the outlet.** Move all remaining surface tokens into that tray's labelled runoff collector. The next batch is then available. A button can still show the explanation when zero tokens move; it does not manufacture a runoff token or wrong-answer mark.

Each button makes one explicit visible change with no automatic animation. The learner can pause, inspect exact counts or read an accessible equivalent after every move. They choose the case and operate the paths; the toy's cover/storage rules constrain where water goes. Do not turn a blocked physical path into a punitive question or make a correct answer necessary to continue. Helpful “Why here?” explanations and a different worked example are optional.

A “Replay these steps” view can inspect earlier completed phase snapshots without changing the active apparatus, adding water or duplicating results. If implementing step replay adds more machinery than its benefit, retain the existing current-step view plus explicit restart; it is a suggestion, not a release requirement. Resetting must never refill an already-running tray silently.

### Exact accounting

For each tray, preserve separate quantities:

- `priorStored` = 0 or 8; `newRainTotal` = 12 or 24.
- `cloudRemaining`, `surfaceNew`, `storedNew`, `runoffNew`, all nonnegative integers.
- At every phase: **newRainTotal = cloudRemaining + surfaceNew + storedNew + runoffNew**.
- Also show: **priorStored + newRainTotal = priorStored + cloudRemaining + surfaceNew + storedNew + runoffNew**.
- `storedNew <= 12 - priorStored`; a sealed cover always has `storedNew = 0`.
- Prior tokens are not counted as new rainfall, newly infiltrated water, or runoff in this model.

At a completed run (`cloudRemaining=surfaceNew=0`), expected **newly stored / new runoff** per tray:

| Case | Tray A | Tray B |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `cover-12` | 12 / 0 | 0 / 12 |
| `cover-24` | 12 / 12 | 0 / 24 |
| `space-12` | 12 / 0 | 4 / 8 |
| `space-24` | 12 / 12 | 4 / 20 |

All equalities and pictures must hold at intermediate phases too, including zero-movement steps. A current partial ledger is labelled **so far**, not a completed experimental result. No outcome row exists merely from visiting the page or making a prediction.

## A small runoff plan, with overflow

After any completed tray result, choose that source snapshot and try two **fictional outlet paths** on a separate tabletop diagram:

- **Straight channel:** all of this tray's new runoff reaches the low-path collector.
- **Holding cup:** first 12 runoff tokens stay in a labelled empty holding cup; any excess reaches the same low-path collector. The cup has no leak or outlet during this toy demonstration.

The source runoff remains a frozen observed model value. Trying a plan replays a **copy** of those tokens; it does not change the source trial, rain/storage totals, another tray or game inventory. Show “Trying this result again on a planning board,” so no one thinks the water duplicated within the same physical run. Let the learner manually send groups of up to four along the chosen route, filling cup spaces and then visibly overflowing. Total source tokens = planning reserve + cup tokens + low-path tokens at every step. A straight route has cup zero. With zero source runoff, the board honestly says no runoff arrived; both routes have no new tokens, with no false failure.

Both routes remain selectable and keepable, even with overflow. Feedback uses exact counts: “In this closed toy, the cup held 12 and 8 continued onward,” not “the village is safe.” Changing a route explicitly restarts that copy from the same source result; never teleport water out of the low-path collector in the ongoing plan. One current draft per source tray/case is enough; retain the last kept plan snapshot when experimenting anew. No multi-cell terrain routing, flow rates, slope changes, real rain-garden sizing or flood defeat mechanic in this slice. Those need their own source-reviewed spec.
