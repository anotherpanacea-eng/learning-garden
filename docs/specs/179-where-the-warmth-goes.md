## Purpose

The Energy domain is the largest science gap in the map ([six-act strands](../six-act-strands.md), S6). Drafts cover energy stores, friction and sources ([147](https://github.com/anotherpanacea-eng/learning-garden/pull/139)), insulation ([153](https://github.com/anotherpanacea-eng/learning-garden/pull/140)), thermal conductor as a property word ([167](https://github.com/anotherpanacea-eng/learning-garden/pull/147)), particles ([168](https://github.com/anotherpanacea-eng/learning-garden/pull/147)), sea breezes ([169](https://github.com/anotherpanacea-eng/learning-garden/pull/148)) and circuits ([163](https://github.com/anotherpanacea-eng/learning-garden/pull/144)). Nobody yet teaches sun and shade, the seasons as a pattern, collisions, the three ways heat moves, or power.

A child comes to understand that sunlight warms things and shade **blocks** the warming (it makes no cold); that a shade works by where it sits and what it is made of, shown by a test; that the seasons repeat, with long warm days and short cold ones; that in a bump energy moves from one thing to the other and some leaves as sound and warmth; that warmth moves from hotter to cooler by **conduction**, **convection** or **radiation**; and, at the Keeper's Desk, that **power** is how fast energy moves (watts), a different question from how much.

## The knowledge

**Sunlight warms things (Sprout).** Things in sunlight get warmer than the same things in shade at the same time. Shade is cooler because it is not being warmed. Water and see-through things warm too, and warmed things cool again in shade or evening. Relative only at Sprout. Dark, dull surfaces take in more sunlight (a side note).

**A shade that works (Sprout to Seedling).** Awnings and umbrellas work by getting **between the Sun and the spot**. What the cover is made of matters (canvas blocks, a net lets some through, a clear sheet nearly all), and so does where it hangs, since the Sun moves through the day. The test compares the shaded spot with an unshaded one at the same time; looks are not evidence.

**The year's pattern (Sprout).** Four seasons repeat; in Willowbank's northern valley summer days are long and warm, winter days short and cold. At about 42.5° N (the Pyrenees) the shortest day has about 9 hours of daylight and the longest about 15 (computed). Earth is closest to the Sun in early January, in the northern winter, so closeness is not the cause. Tilt is spec 141's; the poles are spec 153's.

**Bumps and clacks (Seedling to Sapling).** When a moving thing hits a still one, energy moves across: the striker slows, the target speeds off, and some leaves as sound and a little warming. A faster striker hands on more. Outcomes depend on speeds and masses together: equal balls, the striker nearly stops; a heavier target moves off slowly as the striker bounces back; a lighter one shoots away. Speed and energy are spec 147's. Qualitative; no momentum.

**Hotter to cooler (Sapling).** One rule first: energy moves from the hotter thing to the cooler thing until they are the same temperature. Cold is not a thing that flows; a cold window takes warmth from the room. The bigger the difference, the faster the transfer, so a hot drink cools quickly at first and then more slowly. Three ways, each answered by "what moves?":

- **Conduction:** energy passed along through a material that stays put (a spoon in hot tea warms to the handle). Metals conduct well. A metal latch and a wooden shutter in the same cold morning are the same temperature; the metal feels colder because it carries warmth from the hand faster. (Particles are spec 168's; property words spec 167's.)
- **Convection:** warm air or water expands and rises, cooler fluid takes its place, and the moving fluid carries the energy in a loop. The café stove and a radiator warm a room partly this way, partly by radiation; the share varies. "Heat rises" is loose: warm air rises. (Spec 169's sea breeze is the outdoor loop.)
- **Radiation:** light carries energy without needing air. Warm everyday objects mainly emit invisible infrared; sunlight carries energy in visible and invisible light across space. Dark, dull surfaces absorb and emit it better than pale, shiny ones. Nothing nuclear is meant.

**Insulators slow it; they make no warmth.** A tea cozy slows the teapot's cooling and would slow an iced drink warming. Insulation is spec 153's; the teapot bench revisits it once.

**The baker's oven (real practice, simplified).** Wood-fired masonry ovens are fired until the bricks are hot, the embers raked out, and bread bakes in the stored heat (radiation from the walls, conduction from the floor, help from the air). The bricks stay hot enough to bake for hours (one museum account says up to six; it depends on the oven).

**Power (Keeper's Desk).** Power = energy ÷ time. One watt is one joule each second; a kilowatt is 1,000 watts. Higher power does the same job faster; total energy depends on power **and** time (energy = power × time), so a small lantern on all night can move more energy than a kettle used for two minutes. Warming 1 kg of water by 1 °C takes about 4,200 J (4,184, rounded); desk items ignore losses and say so. The kilowatt-hour is an energy unit (glossary only).

**History cards (real, checked).**
- **Rumford's cannon (1798).** Benjamin Thompson, Count Rumford, saw cannon and chips grow very hot during boring at the Munich arsenal. A blunt borer in a water-filled box boiled the water in about two and a half hours; friction seemed to give heat without end. He argued heat is motion, not a fluid (caloric). Read to the Royal Society, 25 January 1798. His 1796 fireplace essay gave a shallow fireplace with angled sides that sends more warmth into the room.
- **Herschel's thermometers (1800).** William Herschel put blackened thermometers in the colors of prism-split sunlight; one beyond the red, where no light showed, read warmer still. He had found infrared.
- **Joule's paddle (1845).** James Prescott Joule's falling weights turned a paddle in water and warmed it a tiny amount: the same work always gave the same warming. A tale, told decades later, has him carrying a thermometer to an Alpine waterfall on his 1847 honeymoon; no measurement came of it, and the card labels it a story.
- **The watt (1882, 1889).** William Siemens proposed the name in 1882 and the British Association approved it; an international electrical congress adopted it in 1889.
- **Dewar's flask (1892, Keeper's note).** A vacuum between double walls slowed conduction and convection; silvering, added later (Royal Institution record), slowed radiation.

## The game or activity

**(a) Sun, Shade and Seasons.** The market square at morning, midday and evening. Five objects (stone step, water trough, slate tile, pale linen, apples), each with a sunny and a shaded twin. Tap the hand on a twin for a warm, a little warm or cool face. Seedling adds spec 140's thermometer as an unscaled bar. **Season wheel:** drag clue cards (blossom, awning up, apple baskets, lanterns lit early) to festival quarters and set the lamplighter's day bar (short, middle, long). Placements can change; feedback asks a question.

**(b) Awning Builder, model `shade-v1`.** *A made-up model; the rule is on screen.*

- Inputs: Sun `morning` (low in the east), `midday` (high), `afternoon` (low in the west); cover `canvas`, `net`, `clear sheet`; place `overhead`, `east side`, `west side`.
- Rule: the cover shades the stall if place matches the Sun (overhead with midday, east side with morning, west side with afternoon). Shaded fraction = 1 for canvas, ½ for net, 0 for clear sheet; otherwise 0.
- Apples show **cool** (1), **a little warm** (½) or **warm** (0). A comparison crate always sits in full sun and always shows warm.
- 27 settings; the child predicts, places, then taps **Check the apples** to compare the two crates.
- Model line: "Real shade depends on the hour, the season, the cover's size and the breeze. This bench uses three Sun spots and three covers."

**(c) Clack on the Green, model `clack-v1`.** Roll `slow`, `medium` or `fast` into a target `same size`, `heavier` or `lighter`. The child predicts **both** balls, then rolls; authored before-and-after speed arrows show the outcomes in The knowledge, longer arrows and a louder clack for faster rolls. A ten-token **energy tally**: same size 9 to the target, 0 kept, 1 to sound and warming; heavier or lighter 8, 1, 1. The total always shows ten. Model line: "A made-up tally, rounded. Real balls lose different amounts, and grass slows them." 

**(d) Warmth Trail.** Three parts.

1. **What moves?** Eight scene cards (spoon in the teapot; stove air rising; sunlight through the window; oven floor under a loaf; warm oven walls; hand on a cold latch; steam rising from tea; a wrapped warm brick) sort into *passed along through stuff that stays put*, *warm air or water moves*, *light/radiation, no air needed*. Cards with two honest answers say so.
2. **Arrow board.** Draw the warmth arrow between tea and room, hand and latch, window and room on a frosty night. Only hotter-to-cooler arrows are accepted; a reverse arrow asks "Which one is warmer?"
3. **Teapot bench, model `teapot-v1`.** *A made-up model.* Room 20 °C, tea 84 °C. Each 5-minute step keeps half the difference bare, three quarters with a cozy: 84, 52, 36, 28 and 84, 68, 56, 47 at 0, 5, 10, 15 minutes. Model line: "Real teapots cool in a similar pattern, but the amounts depend on the pot, the room and the lid."

**(e) The Watts Desk (Keeper's Desk).** Set a rate dial (watts) and a clock (seconds), predict the joules, then run it (instantly). Presets: lantern 6 W, heater 1,000 W, kettle 2,000 W. A **same-job panel** shows two kettles warming the same water: one finishes sooner; both move the same energy. Model line: "Ignores energy lost to the room and the kettle itself."

No timers, scores, streaks, medals or locks. Every activity is open from the first visit.
