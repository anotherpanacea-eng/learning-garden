# 319 · The market list: colons and semicolons at the Post Office

Proposed 2026-10-31. A bounded first slice of [278](278-exchange-manifest.md), not approval of that whole pack. It is the slice 278 suggests (The Manifest List with set A), narrowed to the **market list version** only, because the manifest version opens at Act VI chapter 2 and Act VI is not built. It closes one E2-V bridge: a colon opens a list after a whole sentence, and semicolons split list items that have commas of their own. Supplied tiles make this supported punctuation practice, not free writing, dictation or placement. PR254 (the unrelated mill-film PR) remains held.

## Why this slice

Nothing built teaches the colon or the semicolon. Spec 90 teaches one capital and one end mark, Sentence Bridges (109) supplies commas, and Instructions for a Reader (54) orders steps without punctuating a list. 278 is a reviewed proposal with every child-facing line written, a source audit and an independent fresh-context check (its "Checks for implementation"). The market list version is open from the start, never names Vadoalto, Nuria or Hollis and reads no page, so it needs no story adapter, no Spanish reader and no Act VI state.

Add one Post Office page, **The Exchange Manifest**, at `#post/manifest`, with an entry card on the Post Office hub and a back link reading **← Post Office** (the glossary's short-name rule). Extend the existing host; no framework, new top-level destination, story unlock, chapter contribution, page reveal, mutation of another root, or audio. Future 278 work (the manifest version, benches b to e, sets B to F) must extend this route, controller and `exchangeManifest` root, not build a second manifest page beside it.

**Grown-up panel, exact text** (companion text with out-of-slice clauses and sentences omitted):

> **What this teaches:** a colon to introduce a list after a whole sentence; semicolons between list items that contain commas.
>
> **Terms:** England's national curriculum teaches these in Year 6 and calls a period a full stop; the child text uses American terms. In the Common Core, colons and semicolons for lists and clauses appear in grades 9 to 10, so this pack runs ahead of the US grade order for some children.
>
> **What it does not claim:** style guides differ. The colon is keyed only in running sentences: Chicago leaves it out after *We need*, but some house styles (Microsoft's, for one) allow a colon after a short lead-in such as "You can:" above a bulleted list. The comma before "and" in a list (the serial comma) is a choice and is never marked.

## The bench: exact model

Each market line is a lead-in, slot 1, and the list. Lines 1 to 3 have list slots 2 and 3; line 4 has slot 1 only.

| Line | Text with slots | Fixed boundaries in the list | Fits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| m1 | The miller sells three things [1] flour [2] oats [3] and bran. | "and" before *bran* | [1] colon · [2] comma · [3] comma or empty |
| m2 | The baker needs three loads [1] flour, two sacks [2] eggs, one dozen [3] and butter, one block. | inner commas after *flour*, *eggs*, *butter*; "and" | [1] colon · [2] semicolon · [3] semicolon |
| m3 | The cart stops at three places [1] the mill, at nine [2] the bakery, at ten [3] and the café, at noon. | inner commas after *mill*, *bakery*, *café*; "and" | [1] colon · [2] semicolon · [3] semicolon |
| m4 | We need [1] flour, eggs and oats. | list comma after *flour*; "and" | [1] empty |

Tiles in fixed order: **colon ( : )**, **semicolon ( ; )**, **comma ( , )**, **leave it empty**. Every slot starts unchosen, drawn as a visible gap, and **leave it empty** is a deliberate choice. **Load it** is enabled once every slot on the line holds a tile. Tiles are buttons of at least 44 px; tapping a slot then a tile, or a tile then a slot, both work, and there is no drag requirement.

**How Pip reads a line (deterministic, for the crate picture).** Pip loads one crate per piece and writes each piece on its crate label, reading only the marks:

1. Semicolon in slot 1: no crates. The line is two sentences.
2. Colon in slot 1 on m4: no crates. Pip stops after "We need."
3. Otherwise, choose the split rule. If slot 2 or slot 3 holds a semicolon, Pip splits **only at semicolons**. If not, he splits at **every comma, placed or fixed, and at "and"**.
4. Slot 1: a colon starts the list. On a lead-in line, an empty slot 1 joins the lead-in to the first piece. A comma in slot 1 is a split point under the comma rule, so the lead-in becomes its own crate, and is ignored under the semicolon rule, so the lead-in joins the first piece. On m4, an empty slot 1 starts the list after "We need".
5. A leading "and" is dropped from a crate label. Trim surrounding whitespace and the final sentence period; discard empty pieces (including the piece between a comma and the following "and"). Lowercase the remaining labels, for example "eggs, one dozen" or "one dozen".

Examples a test table must include (all slots not explicitly changed hold fitting tiles): m2 with all fits gives three crates; m2 with commas in slots 2 and 3 gives six (flour · two sacks · eggs · one dozen · butter · one block), which is the scene's "one dozen" joke; m2 with a semicolon in slot 2 and slot 3 empty gives two crates, the second reading "eggs, one dozen and butter, one block"; m1 with slot 3 empty gives three crates; m4 empty gives three. At most seven crates are ever drawn. The picture is original inline SVG, each crate with a text label, and the figure has a text equivalent ("Pip loads {n} crates: {labels}"). Its alt text never states which marks fit.

## Saved work

One optional per-explorer root, the 278 shape trimmed to what this slice writes:

```
exchangeManifest: {
  v: 1,
  cardsSeen: bool,
  lists: { [lineId]: { variant: 'market', shown: [tileId], slots: { [slotId]: tileId },
                       load: loadCode, text: string, crates: [string], said: string,
                       supported: bool, at } },
  items: { [itemId]: { shown: [choiceId | tileId], answer: choiceId | { [slotId]: tileId },
                       prompt: string, offered: [string], answerText: string, said: [string],
                       outcome: 'fits' | 'other', supported: bool, guided: bool, at } }
}
```

The root is absent until the first Load it, item answer or support exposure described below. Opening the page, reading the story or a card without its worked example, and choosing tiles write nothing; support exposure writes its flag before display. `lineId` is `m1` to `m4`, `slotId` is `s1` to `s3`, and `tileId` is `colon`, `semicolon`, `comma` or `empty`. Item choice ids are `a`, `b` and `c` in the order shown. A1, A2 and A4 store choice ids in `shown`; A3 stores its tile ids in the displayed order (`colon`, `semicolon`, `comma`). `at` is an ISO time written by the host clock, as elsewhere in the notebook; no field holds anything about the child, and there is no typed text.

Each Load it replaces that line's entry with the current slots, the shown tile order, the code from the table and the time, **plus a frozen copy of what was shown**: `text` is the line as punctuated by the learner's tiles (empty slots leave no mark), `crates` the crate labels Pip drew, and `said` the Load it line shown. Freeze the displayed line template, item prompt, offered texts and order in the active view when first rendered; a submit uses that snapshot with the current selected tiles, never a fresh catalog lookup. Compute the result strings from that snapshot in the submit candidate and reveal them only after a successful commit, so the saved and shown result agree. Each item answer replaces that item's entry the same way, freezing `prompt` (the item's card and prompt as shown), `offered` (the shown choice texts, or for A3 the tile names in shown order), `answerText` (the chosen choice text, or A3's card punctuated by the chosen tiles) and `said` (the feedback lines and explanation shown). `load`, `outcome` and every frozen string are written at that moment and never recomputed or compared with today's content. Historical readouts use only these saved strings, so a future wording change never rewrites old work. A resumed completed item shows its saved `prompt`, `offered`, `shown` order, answer and `said` as the earlier attempt, read-only. Answering again never scores those saved choices: **Try again** starts today's complete question snapshot (prompt, choices, order and answer rule, frozen in the active view as above), and the saved entry stays exact and on show until the new answer commits and replaces it. An earlier `outcome` is never rescored. Pending entries have no saved question yet and display the current authored question. Each frozen string is at most 400 Unicode code points and each frozen-string array at most 8 entries.

**Support is captured before it is shown.** `supported` becomes true for a line or item, and is saved before the support appears, when the learner opens any stuck-ladder step for that item, or has already seen feedback or a What fits panel for that line or item. The attempt whose result first shows that feedback keeps the `supported` value it was committed with; only later attempts on that line or item carry the flag. Teach cards are page-level: opening any card's worked example first writes `cardsSeen: true` on the root, and every subsequent Load it or item answer copies `supported: true` into its entry. A newly created root has `v: 1`, `cardsSeen: false`, and empty `lists` and `items` maps before the triggering change is applied; opening a worked example sets `cardsSeen` to true in that same candidate, and it never returns to false. Set A sits in a native disclosure, **Practice: colons and lists**, closed by default; opening it is what displays A1, so that open event creates A1's pending entry only if A1 is absent, before A1's steps render. If A1 is already completed, preserve its answer and frozen strings and only set its support/guided flags if needed. Never replace an unreadable A1 entry; keep A1 read-only while the other items remain available. Keep guided/support content hidden until its required candidate has committed, including during a native disclosure toggle; opening the disclosure must not paint that content first. If the pre-display save is refused, keep the disclosure closed; the same refusal rule prevents any worked example or ladder support from appearing before its required flag is saved. In saving-off mode, a successful in-memory commit suffices. A1 always records `guided: true` and `supported: true`, captured before its steps render, even with no ladder use. Once true, a flag stays true for that line or item. A false flag means no recorded support, not an independent attempt, and nothing is labeled independent. Opening support before any answer creates a minimal pending entry so the flag survives reload; this, and `cardsSeen`, are the only writes that precede an answer.

**Entry shapes.** Considering recognized fields only, a *pending* line is `{ supported: true }`; a pending item is `{ supported: true, guided: bool }`. Unknown additive fields are allowed on either shape and retained unchanged. A *completed* line has all of `variant`, `shown` (4 tile ids), `slots` (one tile id for every slot the line has: 3 for m1 to m3, 1 for m4), `load`, `text`, `crates`, `said`, `supported` and `at`. A completed item has all of `shown`, `answer`, `prompt`, `offered`, `answerText`, `said`, `outcome`, `supported`, `guided` and `at`, with `answer` a choice id for A1, A2 and A4 and a full three-slot map for A3. Any other mix of recognized fields, for example `{ supported: true, load: 'fits' }` with no slots, is unreadable: it stays exact and that line or item turns read-only.

**Validation is structural.** Check the ids, codes and counts the save holds against bounds (at most 4 lines, 3 slots per line, 4 shown tiles, 8 items, 3 shown choices), never against today's lines, answer key or wording. Validate the frozen fields explicitly: `text`, line `said`, `prompt` and `answerText` are strings; `crates`, `offered` and item `said` are arrays of strings; enforce the 400-code-point limit on every frozen string (including array elements) and the 8-entry limit on each frozen-string array. `offered` has the same length as its saved `shown`, and a choice `answer` must occur in that saved `shown`; do not compare any saved text with the current catalog or regenerate it to validate it. Root `cardsSeen`, entry `supported` and item `guided` are booleans; a present root must have object maps `lists` and `items`. A malformed root header or maps makes this page read-only while keeping the root exact and exportable, using the unreadable notice. An unknown `v` keeps the whole root exact and makes the whole page read-only, with the unknown-version notice and links labeled Grown-up notes and Post Office. This includes support/disclosure callbacks: no pending entry or `cardsSeen` write may occur, and support requiring a new flag stays hidden. In a `v: 1` root, an unreadable entry stays exact and exportable, and only that line or item turns read-only, with the unreadable notice. Additive fields written by a future 278 bench stay exact; no loader normalization, default injection, deletion or migration. Other `#post/*` roots, the story root and every other extension stay untouched.

Every mutation clones a candidate, changes only `exchangeManifest`, passes a local shape check of the root header/maps and the entries being changed, retaining existing unreadable sibling entries as opaque unchanged values, and the host's `canExport(candidate)` (the actual Notes JSON/import preflight), then commits. A refusal changes nothing and says so. Readers (render, Notes) write nothing. Notes gains a "Market list marks" section showing, from the saved strings only, each completed line's `text`, crates and `said` with its support flag, and each answered item's `prompt`, `answerText`, `said`, outcome and flags. Pending entries contribute only their saved support/guided flags to the adult readout, with no invented answer or frozen strings. Render every frozen string as text, never as HTML; retain line breaks and use a fixed card/prompt line-break separator when building `prompt`. Verify the actual export, `prepareProgressImport`, explorer import and reopen.
