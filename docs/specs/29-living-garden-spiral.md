# 29 — Living Garden: familiar trail, changing questions

**Status: Implemented after independent spec/science and implementation review.**

## Purpose and reusable teaching direction

Turn inspect → compare → explain → find → pack into a small renewable game. Keep its controls familiar while changing the organism, observed conditions, reading clue and parcel quantities. A spiral revisits an idea in a changed context and extends it: transfer damp-shelter reasoning from a land snail to a woodlouse; contrast shelter with a nectar-seeking adult butterfly's food; use a short delivery note and equal groups to carry café supplies. Do not equate repetition with mastery, add unrelated toll questions, or turn the loop into a generic multi-subject engine.

For future increments, preserve a successful play action and vary the learning purpose, representation and context deliberately. Revisit with support, then offer another setting. This is a design direction, not a claim of measured progress or a mandatory sequence.

## Content and changing visits

Ship three animals (garden land snail, woodlouse, adult butterfly seeking nectar), three authored case settings per animal (nine cases), and renewable combinations of those cases, card positions, starter café ingredients and parcel quantities. Mixed exploration cycles the three animals; a manual animal choice stays open. Within each animal, cycle its three cases before repeating. Keep a bounded list of offered cases so a repeat is labelled familiar; honestly state nine authored settings, not infinite new biology or live generation. Fresh parcel quantities can accompany familiar settings.

Shelter cases place damp covered leaves beneath a log, stone or pot, contrasting with explicitly dry/exposed cover or underwater. The correct location must vary: a dry log is not automatically suitable. Butterfly cases show open nectar-bearing flowers, contrasting with bare stems, closed buds or pond water. Adult butterflies and caterpillars have different food needs; this animal is explicitly looking for nectar. Each case has one defensible best pictured location and reason. Conditions are supplied by original drawings/text, not real measurements, guarantees of an animal's presence, or claims that all logs/flowers/ponds are equivalent.

Three visible location cards have large inspect controls. Each reveals its condition illustration and a short notebook observation. Choose a location and a reason; feedback refers to the supplied clues, not a penalty. Inspect the target and at least one contrasting place, then either match place/reason or open “Think it through together.” A short delivery note describes the same conditions, so reading/listening guides the search. Vocabulary help defines damp/shelter/nectar and is recorded as support. A specific “Look beside [chosen habitat]” button finds a sealed parcel near the animal without collecting or feeding wildlife. A short optional “What if these conditions changed?” talking prompt connects to the next visit; it is not scored or simulated.

## Meaningful parcel math and supplies

Each new visit freezes one of the eight starter ingredients and an equal-groups quantity. Default small parcels use ordered 2–5 × 2–5 pairs, with all 16 pairs cycling; an open all-facts option cycles every ordered 1×1 through 12×12 using the app's existing coprime-walk approach. Controls affect the next search only. Choose any starter ingredient; a “For our waiting table” default targets a current recipe shortage, otherwise a low-stock starter jar. No ingredient is an actual plant/animal diet; the sealed parcel is fictional café food.

After finding it, show countable packs and bites, the expression and a number input. A correct count or explicit “Count with Moss” explanation enables carrying. The latter shows the total and records help; no mandatory independent arithmetic gate. Record parcel attempts and help separately from ecological reasoning; pictures are always available and neither arithmetic nor habitat selections certify recall/mastery. Narration, vocabulary, explanation and prior familiarity stay distinct. Register newly visible multiplication pairs through the existing shared fact-exposure mechanism so later math cannot treat an already-seen fact as fresh; do not insert practice observations into other destinations or change recommendations.

Only an explicit carry changes stock, exactly once per visit, by packs×bites, under the existing 180-bite cap. If full, keep the parcel waiting and link to café. To avoid trapping exploration, an explicit “Leave this parcel behind and try another search” closes it without stock and keeps its notes; it is not a resumable stock claim. Collected visits offer “Another search.” No start, inspect, correct answer, help, render, reload or rereading adds inventory. Other gathering games remain available.
