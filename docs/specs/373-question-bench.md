## New action and story

Fair Test already compares ramp setups, reveals authored trials, separates claims and observations, and revisits confounds. Research Corner370 selects answerable supplied questions and organises source-linked notes. Reference Scales371 and Compare Shelf369 own their statistical/measurement contrasts. Those are built, not missing.

This increment adds **constructing a more precise question from supplied pieces or writing your own wording**, then exploring what different approaches could and could not establish. A good question need not be a controlled test. No single method is graded universally correct. This is structured inquiry planning, not demonstrated independent question generation, a conducted investigation, biological knowledge coverage or a completed Act V project.

Place: existing `#workshop/fair`, an optional **Question Bench** button beside the old Fair Test surface. No new destination. In Act IV/V the town is growing beyond repair: neighbours bring vague wonders for the next Keeper to sharpen. S1-IV/V inquiry and E3-IV/V precise wording connect to existing evidence/record work. Everything stays open without age, gender, chapter, achievement or prerequisite locks.

## Four finite story situations

Each source supplies a broad wonder, two focus pieces and two context pieces, four fully authored question sentences (every pair), four method cards with an affordance AND a limit, three manually opened example parts, and a small glossary. The companion supplies all strings.

| Source | Focus pieces | Context pieces | Distinct return |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Seedlings: what counts as doing well? | Stem length / leaf spread | Light versus dim on day6 / change from day0 to6 in the light setup | Some existing Field Notebook rows are relevant; its current task may ask a different question. |
| Ramps: what does better mean? | Stopping distance / time until stopping | Change height, same surface / change surface, same height | Fair Test supplies distance examples, not measured stopping times. No new time data is invented. |
| Frogs: what would we need to look up? | Adult food / usual habitat | Common frog / common toad | Plan a species-specific source search. No diet/habitat answer is supplied or biological attainment claimed. |
| Flowers: whose favourite? | Count choices / collect reasons | Sunflower versus poppy / daisy versus iris | Reports can describe preferences; they do not establish an objectively prettiest flower. No real respondents/data collected. |

All situations are fictional plans, even where they name real species. The frog questions deliberately ask for information, not assert answers. These examples do not categorise questions into mutually exclusive methods. Watching, setting up a fair comparison, looking up sources and asking people can work together; what they establish depends on the question, records and design. Comparing two places observationally is not automatically a controlled test; a reference guide cannot give today's local readings. Preference surveys can yield empirical reports without deciding what everyone should value. Repeats and further inquiry may still be needed.

## Manual turns and teaching encounter

The board shows four pictorial wonder slips. Explicit selection opens/resumes its visit-local draft, freezing the source at that time; rendering does not initialise saved work.

1. **Sharpen the wonder.** A compact pinned question card beside two tap/keyboard chip trays: **What to notice** and **Which context**, with source-owned labels where those differ. Each chosen chip changes the assembled sentence; pieces remain freely revisable. Missing pieces produce an unfinished card, not an error. Alternative combinations are different questions, not mistakes. Optional **My own words** switches to a labelled multiline field; the piece-built wording stays retained. Own wording is never auto-corrected, classified, evaluated or sent to a service. Switching modes preserves both forms.
2. **Choose a way.** The frozen question remains pinned above four illustrated method cards. Select any, or none. Selection reveals the source-owned example's **What it could help with** and **What it would not establish**; no correct-answer key, grading, tally or penalty. If using own wording, label these as **Notes for the story example** and explicitly ask an adult how they apply to the written question. Never present them as an analysis of that custom question. Changing a piece does not clear the chosen method; it is a plan to reconsider, not a verified match.
3. **Keep the question.** Read the wording and method, then optional **Questions to talk over**: what would count as an answer, which record/measure would be needed, and what remains unresolved? These prompts are supplied help; spoken/paper explanations are ungraded and unstored. Keep can retain unfinished parts, no method or an empty own-wording field. No forced procedure or required prediction.

**An example in parts** opens one authored step per request. It demonstrates broad wonder → named measure/context → question/method limitation using a fixed authored example kept separate from and never applied to the learner draft. It never changes the child's selections. Glossary and model notes are manual reference help. ExampleSeen/reflectionShown retain disclosure state, not evidence of reading or understanding. The second situation is a new application. No arithmetic answer is supplied, and no multiplication familiarity/attempt/exposure roots change.

**Purposeful later return:** after a card is kept, a later `#living/notebook` visit offers **Card {rawIndex+1} · {frozen source title}** with its frozen wording under a manual disclosure and the exact path **Fair Test → Question Bench → Kept cards**. Ask whether the current notebook question and its pretend seedling rows answer that card, or whether different observations, a source, or reports would be needed. Some seedling questions can use available model rows; frog/preference/ramp-time questions cannot be answered by those rows. Do not force every question into a chart, claim the existing rows answer it, copy numbers/wording into Field Notebook, or write a completion flag. No readable kept card means no invitation. Keep the ordinary Question Bench entry open. The named artifact/context, rather than navigation alone, is the later use.

## Source/work and save contracts

Original modules: `question-bench-content.mjs`, `question-bench-core.mjs`, `question-bench-ui.mjs`, `question-bench.css`. The core reads/writes only new optional root `questionBench`. It is an append-only array, cap12, absent until explicit successful Keep. No migration, eviction, daily clock or global assessment write.

Each card is `{id:'question-card-N',source,work}`. N is canonical decimal1…999999 without leading zeros. Allocate one above the maximum syntactically valid ID across ALL raw entries including unreadable siblings; refuse at exhaustion/cap. Duplicate IDs are unavailable as open targets. A malformed/non-array root is retained unavailable and refuses Keep, while the rest of the app stays open. Unreadable items stay raw in Notes and in shelf numbering. Unknown own fields survive source/work transitions, Keep, copy, reload and Notes transfer. No current catalogue ID/count/wording/key is used to validate older cards. Unsupported source versions remain unavailable, never replaced by today's source.

`source:{v:1,model:'question-bench-v1',id,title,wonder,origin,notice,focusLabel,contextLabel,focus:[{id,label}],contexts:[{id,label}],questions:[{focusId,contextId,text}],ways:[{id,label,icon,can,limit}],teaching:[string],glossary:[{term,meaning}]}`. Plain own-property objects, dense arrays, ASCII IDs1…80 excluding prototype names, unique IDs within each list. Nonempty strings bounded in Unicode code points: title/labels≤160, wonder/origin/notice/questions/can/limit/teaching/definitions≤1000, glossary term≤80. Focus/contexts each1…4; questions exactly their OWN source's full cross-product with unique valid pairs (maximum16), not today's choices. Ways1…6, icons from `watch,compare,research,ask,card` (purely decorative; no semantic grading). Teaching1…5, glossary1…6. These are structural readers, not scientific-truth validators. No requirement to match current four cases or their counts.

`work:{v:1,wording:'pieces'|'own',focusId:null|<source id>,contextId:null|<source id>,wayId:null|<source id>,customQuestion:string,exampleSeen:0…source.teaching.length,reflectionShown:boolean}`. `customQuestion` can be empty and is at most600 Unicode code points. A computed sentence comes from the frozen matrix only when both pieces are selected; own wording is the exact saved text and does not imply independent authorship/understanding. No separate saved answer/key/fit result. Unknown fields retained, no regrading on load. Kept views may not reveal numerical/example/reflection teaching beyond saved flags; ordinary glossary and source reference remain openable as help. **Work on a copy** clones the entire frozen source/work without altering the earlier card.

Editing text stays visit-local until Keep. Do not silently truncate, trim, normalise or overwrite an overlength input: retain its raw text visibly in local pending input, show a concise length message, and refuse Keep until it is shortened or cleared. Re-render/back/page/mode/board/shelf navigation retain that pending text. Other drafts and kept work remain open. Raw overlength text is never committed to the notebook. The UI counter and core guard use the same Unicode code-point length. No name, school, home, body or family prompts. State plainly that written questions remain in this browser and in Notes files the parent downloads; no app data is sent for analysis. Source/tests/PRs use invented examples only.

Keep clones a disposable candidate, appends the full source/work proposal, applies existing `recoveryFitsProgressLimits` and actual pretty Notes canExport callback (`prepareProgressImport(JSON.stringify(notesReport(candidate),null,2)).ok`), and only then updates `questionBench` and invokes the app's save callback.
