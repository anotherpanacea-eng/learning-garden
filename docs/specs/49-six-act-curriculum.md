# Six acts, continuous learning: curriculum programme specification

Status: programme-planning/accounting specification independently reviewed by Claude; see the review receipt. Topic-level lesson designs remain unfinished. No runtime change or release is authorised by this document. Individual activity builds require their own bounded specifications and reviews.

## 1. Problem, outcome and honesty about completeness

The six-act story outline did not account for all intermediate instruction. The current app has useful footholds, but jumping from equal groups and short messages to algebra, research and independent projects would omit substantial teaching. This specification provides a complete source-topic inventory beside proposed longitudinal teaching sequences and six dramatic acts. At this planning baseline, **0 of 1,590 topics has a reviewed link to a specific numbered teaching step**. Those links are a required per-unit deliverable, not something the strand labels establish.

Baseline checked against this branch: the unchanged pinned Marble graph contains 1,590 topics, 3,221 edges and 54 subject/domain pairs across eight subjects. The current authoring atlas has 118 references across 62 distinct topics, leaving 1,528 without declared references. All 265 topics whose source starting-age metadata is at least 11 have no declared reference. A reference is a limited activity connection, not full instruction or evidence of mastery. Bell Studio is a separate unpublished draft, not a new live coverage claim.

**Completeness has three different meanings:**

1. **Inventory completeness:** every pinned topic has an explicit row, owning strand and outstanding design status; every source dependency remains visible for review in the unchanged source graph. Retention can be checked now; semantic per-edge decisions remain a unit-authoring task.
2. **Sequence completeness:** the intermediate conceptual changes have named teaching units, representations, contrasts and revisits. The strand companion proposes these; source-topic placements within units still need semantic review.
3. **Lesson completeness:** every in-scope topic has an authored teaching encounter, meaningful practice, response interpretation, support, fresh application and revisit. This is NOT complete. Neither the ledger nor this programme spec claims it is.

The deliverable is a reviewable plan for finishing those lessons, not a declaration that all 1,590 lessons exist or that the six-act programme is ready for wholesale implementation. No subject may disappear merely because its topics do not fit the town metaphor.

## 2. Scope and the roughly-thirteen horizon

Use all pinned topics for the inventory. Preserve source IDs, names, ages and relationships unchanged; match descriptions/evidence before designing lessons because several names are misleading. The topic ledger links to the existing coverage index rather than replacing its narrower declarations.

The ledger copies source age bands as reference metadata and assigns no topic to a first unit yet. It does not use age-named workload buckets. Source ages may help identify review workload but do not decide a child's route, a first teaching act, readiness or access. A topic beginning at 13 and extending to 15, such as trigonometry, remains an optional stretch candidate. Advanced maths is not required to see the story ending. A foundation may still need explicit teaching late in a child's journey.

The eight source subjects are all represented. Geography/cartography, French, music, visual art, keyboarding/older transcription and broader programming receive separately identified extension strands; they are not falsely inserted into Marble's subject taxonomy. French has its own language progression and observations. Scientific knowledge of space, ancient history or the human body can use specialist expeditions and authored source packs rather than being relabelled café arithmetic. Physical handwriting, speaking/listening, social interaction and field observation retain adult/off-screen routes.

Out of scope for this planning increment: new games, new child records, generated live lessons, age placement, timetable promises, a mastery engine, a whole-curriculum efficacy claim, or replacing the pinned graph. The programme is a long authoring horizon, not six quests or six mandatory school years.

## 3. Two structures that must remain distinct

**Story acts** change relationships, opportunities and the community. **Teaching sequences** change what a learner can notice, represent, reason about or make. They overlap but are not identical. Every act’s main tools must teach their required operations inline first or supply explicit support; an activity’s place in the story is never proof those prerequisites were taught.

The strand map's columns indicate useful introductions/revisits within the story, not enforced prerequisites. A child can visit a later act with accessible material, narration, a companion or an earlier representation. A confident algebra learner may still use a short French sentence; a strong reader may use counters. Act completion never certifies a subject level.

Source hard/soft edges are authoring prompts, not child access locks. The [original bridge checklist](../six-act-teaching-bridges.md) records instructional links the source omits; these are not additional Marble edges. The audit found cross-domain and cross-subject dependencies, misleading names, empty evidence arrays and source age inversions. For each implemented unit, inspect prerequisite descriptions and edge reasons, decide which abilities the actual task requires, and provide an accessible support route. Do not assume a topological sort is a sufficient pedagogy. Original teaching bridges must be labelled separately from source edges. Unresolved or questionable source relations remain visible authoring issues; do not silently rewrite them.

## 4. Dramatic spine: six acts

### I. After the Storm — participation

Purpose: make a welcoming place usable again. Main chapters: find and feed; deliver and explain; investigate and plant; prepare the welcome-back supper. Neighbors supply bounded requests, while the learner can choose where to start. Current loops supply partial footholds. The supper closes the recovery story even when optional investigations remain unfinished. Rebuilt amenities persist.

Learning changes: count/compose quantities, distinguish groups and unknowns, make equal shares, read a clear instruction, separate an observation from a guess, make and revise a small representation. Systematic foundational doors remain available and must not be inferred from playing these games.

### II. A Town of Our Own — belonging and deliberate making

Purpose: decide how a recovered town lives. Main chapters: a weekly market; a garden across seasons; a dragon's birthday gathering; the town's first original show. Requests overlap: the same room, materials, time or ingredients may be wanted twice. Playful inventiveness remains central.

Learning changes: multi-digit place value/calculation, division and remainders, fraction equivalence, time/money/units, purposeful paragraphs, story causality, explicit word reading/spelling routes, repeated observations and an initially supplied fair comparison. The learner makes choices within a supplied plan, checks quantities and instructions, then revises.

Ending: a celebration with a lasting learner-designed contribution. The town thrives; no second disaster resets it. A visiting courier brings an invitation from beyond Willow Bridge.

### III. Beyond Willow Bridge — connection and transfer

Purpose: discover that other communities have lives, knowledge and needs of their own. Main chapters: a floating market; a seed exchange; a route through unfamiliar ground; an extended Paris correspondence. Trade and cooperation are reciprocal, not a savior story.

Learning changes: fractions/decimals/percentages and the bridge to ratios, area/volume/scale/coordinates, rates and timetables, interpreting several documents, inference across a longer text, mechanisms/circuits/material properties, food relationships and choosing useful data. The task increasingly asks the learner to choose a tool rather than naming the required operation.

Ending: complete an exchange, return with a friendship and a question. The new route stays open. An unexpected observation or conflicting account introduces Act IV.

### IV. The Town Has Two Stories — evidence and disagreement

Purpose: encounter sincere disagreement and check claims. Main chapters: an old map and a returning resident's account; a proposal for shared land; a puzzling change in the garden; a newspaper's competing explanations. Bramble sometimes has the better argument; a trusted guide can revise a belief without becoming incompetent.

Learning changes: proportional versus additive relations, variables and graphs, probability and variation, comparisons of distributions, controls/repeats, source credibility, narrative perspective, historical gaps, argument and fair counterargument. Explicit lessons teach these operations before the larger dilemma relies on them.

Facts, model assumptions and values remain distinct. False factual claims can be corrected; preferences need not converge on an official moral answer. Effects shown by a toy model must be labelled as model effects. The learner may recommend several feasible plans and explain trade-offs.

Ending: a consequential proposal changes because someone investigated, listened or reconsidered. The resolution acknowledges what remains uncertain; it need not make everyone agree. A new opportunity leaves room for the learner's own initiative.

### V. A Question of Your Own — authorship and inquiry

Purpose: originate a worthwhile project. Offer authored project paths such as a night garden, a neighborhood newspaper, an observatory visit, a traveling theatre or a new courier service. The learner can change the question or select a smaller project; a generic blank page is insufficient scaffolding.

Project chapters: choose an intention; identify a knowledge gap; plan an inquiry/design; make a first version; receive evidence or an audience response; revise and communicate. The familiar games become tools for a purpose the learner helped set.

Learning changes: connected tables/graphs/equations, rates and constraints, justified geometric design, data limitations, scientific reporting, sustained research, developed writing and revision. Each subject strand retains explicit teaching and examples alongside projects. Companions advise; asking for help remains capable work.

Ending: a contribution with a visible revision history and an explanation of its limits. It can be a partial investigation or a deliberately small design. Optional original writing is human-read; quality is not inferred from length or keywords. Community trust is expressed only in authored story events, never a numeric participation counter, ability score, virtue rank or access condition.

### VI. The Opening of the Ways — shared agency

Purpose: establish a sustained exchange beyond the valley. Chapters: choose a partner and purpose; compare routes and obligations; investigate one consequential uncertainty; propose and revise a workable plan; make the first journey; welcome someone new. Different communities contribute, question and benefit.

The café supplies provisions; cartographers examine routes; garden investigators check habitat effects; designers test tools/signals; archive readers question an account; writers prepare invitations/instructions and explain the plan. No single branch must teach every topic. A specialist astronomy/history project can contribute an exhibition, navigation question or source comparison without claiming that cameo teaches its whole subject.

Completion is a story action: explicitly keep a chosen plan and conduct the first exchange with help available. Mechanical constraints may check a supplied plan's arithmetic and stated conditions, but a smaller companion-supported plan can complete the same story. Optional stretch includes simultaneous equations, quadratic behavior or surveying trigonometry where the authored model actually calls for them. No 100% facts gate, failure lock, perfection requirement or child virtue score.

Final scene: a new apprentice arrives with an unfamiliar parcel. The familiar guide remains alongside the learner, who can now help someone else begin. The town remains intact. Continued play adds partnerships, investigations and compositions; it never re-breaks the town to manufacture a new recovery grind.

## 5. Intermediate teaching contract

The companion [strand sequences](../six-act-strands.md) names the intermediate ideas and conceptual contrasts. Every future authored lesson bundle must supply:

1. **Target and prerequisite check:** actual topic descriptions and the narrow task ability, with needed supporting ideas and any questionable source relation resolved or flagged.
2. **A teaching encounter:** a concrete/visual/spoken example whose changes are under learner control; explain one idea at a time. A long introductory paragraph is not enough.
3. **A contrast:** an adjacent case that makes the boundary visible (equal piece count/unequal size; additive/multiplicative change; current reading/recipe target; observation/explanation; disagreement/contradiction).
4. **Supported practice:** an actionable hint, a different worked example and a way to return to the exact draft. Supply grouped moves for large quantities.
5. **A new application:** a changed representation, unknown, reader, dataset or setting that requires the same idea. Track support and familiarity separately; access never depends on a successful unaided attempt.
6. **A purposeful revisit:** later use in another act/game, carrying a frozen artifact or context rather than awarding a copied answer.
7. **Data and audience boundary:** prompts use fictional people/situations and do not solicit identifying names/places, private family, health or emotional disclosures. List every stored free-text/observation field, its save-off behavior, local notes/export and raw-recovery copies, and what explicit deletion removes. Human audience means a co-present chosen person or a local performance; no automatic sharing, upload, public posting or model transmission. Existing optional local JSON export remains available. An author cannot guarantee that a child will never type private text; it must remain local and never be reused as public content.

These are authoring requirements, not six mandatory screens for the child. Several small targets may share a well-designed encounter. Conversely, an open project cannot stand in for unprovided lessons in decoding, calculation, grammar or scientific method. Each bundle documents what remains unobserved.

Modes include tap/keyboard manipulation, diagrams, listening, reading, original composition and adult/off-screen activity. Mathematical depth, reading load and response mode are independent choices. Optional narration must not claim independent decoding; an interpreted statement must not claim independently written prose. Use two separate distinctions: source kind (supplied/model, learner measurement, optional adult-reported offscreen observation) and response context (supported/independent/familiar/unobserved). Do not turn an adult report into an on-screen independent attempt or combine these into a global level. Offscreen work may go unrecorded. A future activity spec names its exact narrow representation; this programme adds no observation fields or new learner schema. Real investigation and a supplied simulation remain distinct sources.

## 7. New capabilities and reuse boundaries

Extend café/recipes into resource and proportion planning; Post Office into mapmaking and schedules; Garden/Workshop into chosen investigations; Notebook into learner-selected representations and uncertainty; Archive into a multi-source dossier; Loft into sustained audience-aware work. Two substantial new toolsets are a Cartographer's Table and a variable/constraint planning bench. A council/dossier activity can reuse literal message choices and exact human-read writing, but needs authored objections and multiple defensible plans.

Use the existing frozen-source, pending/applied input, explicit keep/replace, explorer, local narration and raw-recovery patterns. Do not introduce a universal simulation engine, learner model or cross-game score for this programme. When a particular dataset/geometry/systems model is needed, specify its units, assumptions, conservation rules and failure cases in that activity's own spec.

Jane's reviewed quest packs, stable task, mixed response formats, requested examples and glossary are design references. Its age gating, mastery inference, rewards, timed formats, creative lexical scoring and learner export workflow are not adopted. No new Jane code, lesson prose or assets are incorporated. Marble supplies source IDs/relationships and candidate ideas; original story acts and teaching bridges are not source edges. Recheck relevant upstream files when implementing a unit.
