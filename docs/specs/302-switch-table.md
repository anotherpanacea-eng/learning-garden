## Learner action and exact model

Moss is preparing pretend battery lanterns for a town gathering, not asking a child to repair outdoor electrical equipment. **Can you make the path complete, then break it somewhere else?** A compact original connection sketch and real44px Open/Close switch controls form the main surface. One filament bulb, one cell and ideal connecting wires form one series loop. No automatic propagation animation or sequential bulb lighting; state is a static steady-state explanation, not a measured result. No real equipment is needed.

Visit-local state: layout in `lamp|cell|left|two`, A and B Boolean closed states, representation in `sketch|symbols`, trace Boolean. Initially lamp, both open, sketch, trace off. One-switch layouts place A beside the lamp (right side), near the cell (bottom left wire), or on the left side; changing among them preserves A and therefore lamp state. The two-switch layout puts A on the left and B on the right. It uses both retained Boolean states; changing back to one switch leaves B retained but disconnected from the model: plain wire occupies the former B position, not a newly introduced unfilled gap. Explain when B is added/omitted; its hidden value is not a physical bypass claim. Start again resets all five fields. Choices and trace survive opening/returning from the film; route departure/explorer/import resets them. No notebook reads, writes or normalization.

Exact rule: one-switch lamp on iff A closed; two-switch lamp on iff A AND B closed. A switch connects its two distinct contacts with a conducting bridge; open means a visible gap. A gap at any position stops the sustained current throughout this one loop. Opening/closing switches creates no charge; the cell supplies energy and charges already exist throughout the circuit. State text says **Model bulb on: complete path** or **Model bulb off: open path** and names the actual open switch(es). For two, explain that both must close. No “everything stops” claim about molecular thermal movement. No score, answer entry, time limit, completion, prerequisite or inferred independent understanding.
