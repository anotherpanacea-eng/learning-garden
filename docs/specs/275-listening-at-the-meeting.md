## The knowledge

Sourced in the [audit](../source-audits/275-listening-at-the-meeting.md).

**Claim, reason, evidence.** A spoken argument has a claim (what the speaker wants believed or done), reasons (why), and evidence (what is counted, seen or recorded that makes a reason credible). This is a classroom simplification of Toulmin's claim, data and warrant (1958) and of the claim-evidence-reasoning frame. Common Core asks grade 4 to identify a speaker's reasons and evidence (SL.4.3) and grade 5 to summarize a speaker's points and explain how each claim is supported (SL.5.3). Speakers also say things that support nothing (jokes, asides, boasts); sorting them out is part of the skill.

**Judging support.** Two plain checks suit ages 9 to 11: relevance (is this about the claim?) and fit (does the support match how strong the claim is?). Absolute words such as "never", "always" and "all" need far more evidence than "slows" or "most". Who said something is not evidence for it. A careful listener also avoids the opposite error: dismissing everything said, or refusing any conclusion the evidence does allow.

**Paraphrase and summary.** A paraphrase restates a passage in one's own words with the meaning kept; copying the words is quotation, and changing the meaning is an error. A summary is shorter and gives only the main points of a whole. Common Core asks grade 4 to paraphrase what is read aloud or presented in media (SL.4.2) and grade 5 to summarize it, "including visually, quantitatively, and orally" (SL.5.2). The English national curriculum (spoken language, years 1 to 6) asks pupils to listen and respond, ask relevant questions, and "consider and evaluate different viewpoints, attending to and building on the contributions of others."

**Building on and concluding.** Classroom talk research describes "talk moves" that make discussion productive: revoicing ("So you're saying…"), asking someone to restate, adding on, and asking for evidence (Michaels and O'Connor). Mercer, Wegerif and Dawes (1999) taught 9- and 10-year-olds explicit ground rules for "exploratory talk" (reasons given, challenges answered with reasons, ideas pooled), and the taught classes' individual reasoning scores improved more than matched controls'. Common Core asks grade 5 to pose and respond to specific questions and elaborate on others' remarks (SL.5.1c), and to review key ideas and draw conclusions from a discussion (SL.5.1d); grade 4 adds coming prepared (SL.4.1a) and explaining one's own understanding in light of the discussion (SL.4.1d). The EEF's Toolkit gives oral language interventions an average of about six months' additional progress, and its Key Stage 2 literacy guidance makes developing pupils' language capability its first recommendation.

**Pictures in a presentation.** Mayer's coherence principle: people learn better when interesting but irrelevant pictures, words and sounds are left out; the signaling principle: cues that show structure help. Common Core asks grade 5 to include multimedia components and visual displays "when appropriate to enhance the development of main ideas or themes" (SL.5.5).

**Facts inside the speeches** are kept to spec 120 §9's modest list and sourced: a marsh holds water and lets it go slowly (US EPA), and how much it helps varies; a wall protects what is behind it and can send water elsewhere; a new apple tree takes years to bear a good crop (Iowa State and Minnesota extension). Counts in the market speeches and Nuria's rain table are labeled made-up models.

## The lesson

Jane's **teach -> guided -> practice**: nine teach cards, one idea each with a worked example revealed a step per tap; each set's first item shows 1-3 visible steps. **"I'm stuck" ladder** on every item: hint -> an easier case plus how to start (Ember's cake, the late bus, the hot soup, the muddy path, "all cats hate water", three friends' games, Pip's lunch) -> skip for now, no penalty. Every speech has **Hear it again**, **Show the words** and **Hear one sentence**; with sound off, the words carry the bench.

**Contrasts:** claim vs reason vs evidence vs aside; a reason vs a cost the speaker admits; paraphrase vs copy vs changed meaning; building on vs repeating vs changing the subject; a clear question vs a vague one; relevant vs off the point; "slows" vs "never"; who said it vs what shows it; summary vs one detail vs opinion; a supported conclusion vs deciding for the town vs concluding nothing; a helpful picture vs a fun or too-broad one.

## The game or activity

Every listening screen shows its words. No score, timer, lock or mastery label. Drags have tap equivalents. Sentence ids in the scripts are for the build, not shown.

- **(a) Before the Bell.** Pick two useful facts and one question from supplied cards before a set; off-topic cards get a gentle "does this help?". After the speeches, a spoken talk card: *Before, I thought… Now I think…, because…*
- **(b) Find the Reasons.** Tap each sentence of a speech and sort it: claim, reason, evidence, problem (a cost the speaker admits) or something else (jokes, boasts, "here's why"). Only the baker's speech (M1, B3) is sorted; its one problem sentence follows a signpost ("I'll be honest about the problem"). A speaker map fills as you sort.
- **(c) Say It Your Way.** Replay one or two sentences; choose the paraphrase; then say one aloud to a grown-up.
- **(d) Add to It.** Choose the reply that builds on a point, or the clearest question. Talk cards and talk rules for a grown-up or sibling, spoken only.
- **(e) Does It Hold Up?** Three tabs: is it about this claim, is there evidence, does it go too far. The overclaims are balanced across sides: Pip for the Sponge (V1), Hob for the Wall (V2).
- **(f) What We Heard.** Choose a summary and a supported conclusion; at Keeper's Desk, **Nuria's Picture Talk** (five pictures, her rain table and her voice) is summarized as a media presentation.
- **(g) The Picture Show.** Pick a picture for each caption, order the slides, optionally one sound, then play it full screen to a grown-up, who hears the child read each caption. **Three Places for One Bench** (Sapling) and **What the Meeting Heard, for Pip** (Keeper's Desk). The meeting show asks for every plan with its cost; no supplied caption ranks a plan. Nothing leaves the device (build-out question 8).

## Saved work

Optional root `meeting` per explorer. An explicit Start freezes only the selected visit's complete working source; Keep freezes that source if no visit has started. New visits retain earlier visits. No snapshot of every catalog item is required.

The sketch uses `Saved<T>` only as shorthand for an inline first/latest record, not a shared runtime helper or a source registry. `FullSource` is the complete selected authored source: passages and provenance labels; instructions, teaching and examples; models/data and captions; every selected question, ordered choice text, key and feedback; help and read-back text or asset references. It contains authored prose; only the response payload `T` uses codes.

```
ObservedHelp = { observed: true, concept: [{ forId, kind }], reading: [{ forId, kind }] }
Snapshot<T> = {
  source: FullSource,
  shown: { question: text, choices: [{ id, text }], key, feedback },
  answer: T | null,
  outcome: 'fit' | 'not-yet' | 'shown' | 'skipped' | 'kept',
  helpBefore?: ObservedHelp,
  taskBefore?: { initialStimulusRequested: [{ forId, clipId }] },
  at
}
Saved<T> = { first: Snapshot<T>, latest?: Snapshot<T> }

meeting: {
  v: 1,
  visits: { [visitId]: {
    working: { source: FullSource, help?: ObservedHelp, task?: { initialStimulusRequested: [{ forId, clipId }] } },
    prep: { [setId]: Saved<{ facts: [cardId], question: cardId }> },
    sorts: { [speechId]: Saved<{ [sentenceId]: 'claim' | 'reason' | 'evidence' | 'problem' | 'other' }> },
    shows: { [showId]: Saved<{ slides: [{ picture: cardId, caption: cardId }], sound: cardId | null }> },
    answers: { [itemId]: {
      first: Snapshot<choiceId>,

```

**Frozen source and first response.** Every `Snapshot` above embeds its own full source, shown question/instruction, ordered choices, key, feedback, response and original outcome. For a bench with no supplied choices or key, preserve its instruction and use an empty choice list and null key; an ungraded keep uses `kept`. The working source is frozen before the visit begins and is never replaced by today's catalog. Waiting work and saved responses render only from their own sources. `first` is write-once at the first recorded response/keep; later attempts have a separate `latest`, and later skips/help never change `first`. Explicitly starting revised content creates another visit and retains earlier sources and work. `answer: null` is allowed only when no selection exists and the outcome is `skipped` or `shown`; otherwise retain the actual response. No original spoken or written explanation is graded.

**Listening task and help.** Before playing an initial stimulus clip, append its `{ forId, clipId }` request to `task.initialStimulusRequested`, unique by that pair and bounded by the visit’s selected frozen source. Requesting another item’s clip retains earlier requests. Copy the exact requests relevant to the response’s `forId` into its `taskBefore`; the initial clip is the task, not conceptual or reading support. Replays, transcript reveals and prompt read-aloud are distinct `reading` codes, recorded before their reveal/playback. A request never proves the clip was heard or establishes independent listening.

**Observed help before reveal.** During a newly tracked visit, `observed: true` with empty arrays means no help request was recorded in that visit so far, not demonstrated independent performance. Commit concept-help codes (hint, guided step, example, shown answer, meaning or slot scaffold), with the affected task/item id, to working help before their first reveal. Commit read-aloud/prompt-audio codes and the affected task/item id separately to `reading` before playback. Copy the observation into each response's `helpBefore` at the response time. A later request changes working help and the next response only. Missing older observations remain unobserved: never infer them from a later response or turn them into an empty list, false or “none.” Help requests are not proof that help was used; reading/audio data establish neither independent reading nor independent listening.

**Validation and preservation.** Validate structural bounds, ids, counts, outcome codes and consistency with the save's own source/choices; never require today's catalog membership, bench sizes, wording or answer key. A missing item is absent. Never recompute a saved result. Unknown versions and unreadable entries remain exact and exportable; only the affected visit/bench becomes read-only, and one bad item never closes the pack. Response payloads contain no free text; say why is spoken and unstored. No field holds a real learner's identity, age, grade or coursework. No child audio, transcript, free text or playback duration is recorded. Spoken tasks store nothing. The `story.plan` root (spec 120) is never read or written here.

**Atomic saves and capacity.** Every mutation, including source copies and help-before records, must pass the actual host's pretty-notes serialization/import preflight and shared limits before committing only this root. Refusal preserves the whole earlier state and visible pending work; do not rerender, navigate or change explorer in a way that silently loses unsaved words/selections. Offer the existing notes download and an explicit confirmed restore of saved words where needed. No silent truncation, history eviction or replacement of saved writing. Export, import, reset and save-off use the shared store; preview/render/suggestion reads are save-neutral. Existing source/language/cultural/rights checks remain open until separately cleared.
