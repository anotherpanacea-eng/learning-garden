## The small addition

Tenths Table already makes 0/10 through 10/10 on one fixed repair strip. It records eight ten-part cases under `tenthsTable` version 1. Add an open **Hundredths Bridge** at `#numbers/hundredths`: the same conceptual whole unit redrawn as a square with ten rows of ten equal small parts. The strip and square are different diagrams of one unit; no physical equality of their shape or dimensions is claimed. A learner makes a supplied amount with reversible tenth and hundredth steps, then checks it, or compares two supplied pictures of that same whole. The finite pack makes `0.3 = 0.30`, `0.35 = 0.30 + 0.05`, and same-whole comparisons visible. It does not add a general decimal calculator, typed parser, money, percentages, independent decimal writing, or attainment.

The 100-part board is a subdivision of the **same conceptual whole**, not 100 whole counters. A broad row is one tenth (ten hundredths); one small square is one hundredth. Its exact saved and visible `wholeLabel` is **“One whole repair-material unit, shown as a square with 100 equal small parts.”** Say visibly: **“One row is one tenth of this whole square. One small part is one hundredth. A whole counter is a separate object, not one of these parts.”** Use `wholeId='repair-whole-one'` in new sources only; this names the conceptual unit without changing Tenths Table's saved `repair-strip-one` key or claiming matching physical geometry. Keep the square's whole bracket and dimensions identical on every bridge card, and link openly from both Number Garden and Tenths Table. Tenths Table, Fraction Trail, Bundle Exchange, their saved records, and their existing routes stay unchanged.

The tempting small adaptor is to put 100-part cases into `tenthsTable.cases`. Its admitted source requires `partCount:10`, selection indices 0–9, and `shownValues` 0–10; its UI draws ten buttons from the current case. Mixing a 100-part case into that root would require a new discriminator and mixed renderer in every older read path. A separate, single optional `hundredthsBridge` root is the smaller compatible seam. It is absent until explicit Start and never migrates, rewrites, or renames `tenthsTable` version 1. Use its local source/check/keep/recovery pattern, not a generic decimal state framework.
