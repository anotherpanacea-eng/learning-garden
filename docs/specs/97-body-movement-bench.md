# Living Garden: how two bodies move

Status: **independently reviewed specification and art; implementation candidate awaits separate review**. [Source and overlap audit](../source-audits/97-body-movement-bench.md); [original six-frame art mock](assets/97-body-movement-frames.svg). No atlas change, merge or deployment is claimed.

## Purpose and boundary

Body Clues already lets a child follow a two-feature key for pictured adult animals. The land snail and earthworm both have **zero jointed walking legs**, but the key stops after identifying them. This small, optional bench lets the child **step and compare two different ways to move**: a land snail creeps on a broad muscular foot; an earthworm changes its segmented body's shape and uses tiny bristles to grip a surface. The useful contrast is *same need to move, different body structures*, not “zero legs means no movement.” It is an original simplified model, not a real animal observation, species identification, speed or force experiment, account of every snail/earthworm gait, or a whole-body-systems lesson.

This is guided teaching, not a quiz. There is no answer key, score, attempt, achievement, adaptive suggestion, reward, required prior Body Clues visit, real specimen handling, photo, location, microphone or learner-data upload. A person may point, speak, sign or simply step the models. The existing picture key, saved visits, checks and frozen version-1 Body Clues renderer remain untouched. Current model captions never purport to describe the child’s older saved drawing or regrade an old trail.

## Complete finite teaching copy

The invitation on the Body Clues page reads: **“How can two animals move without jointed walking legs? Step a snail and an earthworm model together.”** It links to a separate optional `#living/movement` view, with a visible return link to `#living/bodies`; the Living Garden hub may also link directly to it. `adaptivePage(state, slug)` handles this sibling route before habitat state guards. The teaching view does not need a Body Clues record. Its title is **“Two ways to move”**; opening text: **“Our pictured land snail and earthworm have no jointed walking legs. Try each model. The drawings show parts helping movement, not a real animal moving at this speed or distance.”**

The two model cards are co-present at every width, stacked on a narrow phone. They never require one another to advance. Each has a native **Previous picture** and **Next picture** button, cycling through exactly three frozen authored frames in order; Previous at frame 1 and Next at frame 3 are disabled, with an explicit **Start this model again** button at frame 3. There is no autoplay, drag target, hidden timer or automatic “correct” reveal. The child changes the visible picture and caption, then can reverse and compare. Each card states “Picture 1 of 3,” etc. An `aria-live=polite` status announces the new card label and complete caption once after a deliberate step; the static figure has the same complete text equivalent. Local **Hear this picture** reads only the currently visible label and caption using the app’s existing optional narration control. Speech request is not saved or evidence of playback/understanding.

| Card / picture | Exact visible caption and narrated text | Simplified visible change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Land snail 1 | **“The snail's broad muscular foot rests against a surface. The shell sits above its soft body.”** | Body, coiled shell and broad foot; no walking legs. Foot touches a plain surface. |
| Land snail 2 | **“Muscles make a wave along the foot. The foot helps the snail creep along the surface.”** | A shaded band in the foot shifts; a small arrow marks forward direction. No lifting alternate feet. |
| Land snail 3 | **“The broad foot has carried the snail a little farther. It did not use jointed walking legs.”** | Same animal and foot against surface at another illustrative position. |
| Earthworm 1 | **“The earthworm has a long body with ring-like segments. Tiny bristles are enlarged in this model.”** | Ringed body on a surface; a callout enlarges several tiny bristles. No legs or foot drawn. |
| Earthworm 2 | **“Part of its body lengthens and narrows. Some tiny bristles help it grip the surface.”** | The labelled front extends right while a rear region stays wider; the same illustrated ring boundaries remain present, and bristles touch the surface. |
| Earthworm 3 | **“Part of its body shortens and widens, pulling the body forward. Bristles help it hold on.”** | The front stays forward while the rear catches up and a region widens; the same ring boundaries remain present, and bristles still touch the surface. |

The initial co-present prompt is **“Before you step: which part might change shape for each animal? Point to it, tell someone, or just wonder.”** It stays visible; the app does not capture a prediction. A below-card compare fold is openable at any time, titled **“Compare the two models”**, with this complete text: **“In these pictures, neither animal uses jointed walking legs. The snail creeps with a broad muscular foot. The earthworm changes the shape of its ringed body and uses tiny bristles for grip. These diagrams show one simple movement contrast; real animals can move in more than one situation.”** Its optional talk prompt is **“What changed shape? What touched the surface? Go back a picture if you want to look again.”** Opening this fold or requesting speech is not recorded as help on a Body Clues visit. No branch depends on these actions.

An always-available **“About the drawings”** fold says: **“Original teaching diagrams, not real observation or a speed scale. The snail’s foot wave, the worm’s ring shape change and the bristle callout are enlarged so we can compare. The drawings do not show every muscle, all contact points, real timing or a measured distance. Tiny bristles are not jointed walking legs. A person can read the words instead of seeing the diagrams.”** A small source link for grown-ups points to the two primary pages recorded in the audit. Do not claim direct field identification, accuracy of a particular specimen's motion, or that one body type is faster or stronger.

## Art and control contract

The linked SVG is an **original review contact sheet**, not an imported runtime image. It shows all six planned states at phone width, including the snail's persistent broad foot, a shifting underfoot band, the worm's local shape changes and a separately magnified tiny-bristle callout. The worm is one model animal in all three frames: keep the same nine *illustrated* ring boundaries as its shape changes, label the front to the right, show the front extending first and the rear catching up without the front retreating, and connect small underside bristles to the surface line. Nine drawn boundaries are a visual tracking choice, not the real animal's exact segment count; the callout enlarges bristles, not a jointed leg. The actual view shows only one frame per card and keeps both card headings, captions and controls in ordinary HTML. The background surface lines and small forward arrows are not scales; no equal arrow length or spacing is a comparison of speed. Do not animate automatically or rely on color alone: caption, texture/shape and accessible figure description carry the change. Keep labels readable and native controls at least 44 px tall at 320 px; no page-wide horizontal scrolling. Text equivalents are the exact frame captions above plus each figure's visible-change sentence, not a hidden fact or an answer to a scored question. Child-facing copy uses American spelling and no em dash.

Use a narrow new `body-movement` view/controller/stylesheet (or equivalent local functions) and the app’s existing local speech button. Current frame indices `{snail:0..2, worm:0..2}` and compare-fold state live only in the mounted view. Navigating away, reloading or switching explorer starts at picture 1 and does not change the other explorer's work. This is sufficient because there is no learner-authored draft, checked response or later decision that needs the frame index. Persisting it would manufacture a progress record and require unnecessary source migration. The static complete source can change in a later release only as *current teaching*; old `bodyClues` source/checks never read it. On route change, detached controls do nothing; local narration stops through the existing route-change path.

The bench itself makes **no state mutation**, including `bodyClues`, habitat, Life Stages, Food Links/Fork, suggestions or notes. The host may append its existing numerical “Carry an idea here” panel on `#living/*` routes and register genuinely displayed incoming arithmetic pairs. Treat that as the already documented host exception, not as movement learning. A movement visit creates no science atlas result or familiarity signal. If the route stays under `#living/*`, compare the host's exposure behavior with another Living Garden route rather than asserting whole-app state immutability.
