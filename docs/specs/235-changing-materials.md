## The knowledge

**Mixtures and pure substances (supported inline).** In science, **pure** means one substance only. A **mixture** has two or more, not joined. A pure substance melts and boils at one sharp temperature (pure water boils at 100 °C at sea level); a mixture such as salty water boils higher and over a range. Formulations are named once at Keeper's Desk.

**Separating mixtures.** Choose from the property that differs, and from which part you want. **Sieve:** solid bits of different sizes (145). **Filter:** an undissolved solid from a liquid; a dissolved solid passes straight through. **Evaporate to crystals:** keeps a dissolved solid; the liquid is lost to the air. **Distil:** keeps the liquid: heat, the liquid boils off, the vapor **condenses** in a cooled tube, and pure liquid is collected; the solid stays behind. Liquids with different boiling points separate the same way (Keeper's note only). **Chromatography:** a spot of ink on paper above the solvent line; as solvent rises, dyes travel different distances; the number of spots shows the least number of dyes. The paper adds no color. Some mixtures need steps in order: sand, salt and water are filtered, then evaporated. Nothing made on a bench is tasted.

**Four families of reaction.** A reaction makes new substances; atoms are rearranged, never made or lost, so total mass stays the same when every gas is counted. Word equations name them: *magnesium + oxygen → magnesium oxide*. **Combustion:** a fuel burns in oxygen, giving heat and light (*methane + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water*). **Oxidation:** a substance gains oxygen; rusting is slow, burning fast (rust needs oxygen and water). **Thermal decomposition:** heating splits a single compound into simpler substances (*copper carbonate → copper oxide + carbon dioxide*, green to black; *calcium carbonate → calcium oxide + carbon dioxide*, a lime kiln). Melting is not this: no new substance. **Displacement:** a more reactive metal takes a less reactive one's place (*iron + copper sulfate → iron sulfate + copper*); the bench ladder runs magnesium, zinc, iron, copper, silver, and a metal never displaces one above it. The families overlap: burning magnesium is combustion **and** oxidation.

**Acids and salts.** Spec 168 owns pH and indicators. Three routes, at word-equation level: *acid + metal → salt + hydrogen* (magnesium, zinc, iron with dilute hydrochloric or sulfuric acid; copper does not react); *acid + alkali → salt + water* (**neutralization**, indicator added first, stopping at pH 7); *acid + metal oxide or hydroxide → salt + water*. The salt's name: the metal gives the first word, the acid the second (chloride from hydrochloric, sulfate from sulfuric, nitrate from nitric). **Salt** is a whole family; table salt (sodium chloride) is one member. Hydrogen is shown by the squeaky pop at a lit splint (on screen). Evaporating the neutral solution leaves salt crystals: the products were there all along. Chemical symbols in equations, and moles, wait for later study.

**Ceramics, polymers, composites.** **Ceramics** (fired clay, porcelain, brick) are hard, strong when squashed, heat-resistant, and **brittle**: they snap rather than bend. Brittle is not weak or soft. **Polymers** are long-chain materials, natural (rubber, wool, cotton, wood's cellulose) or made (polythene, nylon); their properties are tuned in making, from cling film to bullet-resistant fiber. **Composites** combine a **matrix** and a **reinforcement** so the whole has a mix of properties neither part gives alone: straw in clay (cob) stops cracking; steel bars in concrete take the bending and pulling concrete cannot; glass or carbon fibers in resin are stiff and light. Materials are chosen for the job; none is best at everything. Qualitative only.

**Stretching, squashing, heat and depth.** **Elastic** change springs back; a permanent (inelastic or "plastic") change stays. Past its **elastic limit** a spring stays longer. Heated solids **expand** because particles vibrate more and sit further apart; particles keep their size. A heated ring's hole grows too, since every particle moves away from its neighbors. Gaps in rails and bridges leave room. Steel grows about 12 millionths of its length per °C. **Fluid pressure** pushes in every direction, at right angles to each surface, and grows with depth, from the weight of fluid above: water adds about one atmosphere every 10 m. Air pressure falls with height (a Keeper's note).

**Investigating forces.** A **newton meter** reads force in newtons (N); mass is in kilograms, and on Earth 100 g weighs about 1 N. In a spring test, the force changes (independent), the **extension** is measured (dependent, length now minus length at rest), the spring stays the same. Equal steps, a pause, readings repeated on unloading. Force against extension is a straight line through zero until it reaches the **limit of proportionality** (Hooke's pattern: the same extension added per newton). A best-fit line averages the scatter; it does not join the dots. At Keeper's Desk a cart test compares prediction (applied force ÷ mass) with measurement; friction makes the resultant smaller, so the cart speeds up less. F = ke and gradients are deferred.

**The motor effect.** A wire carrying current across a magnet's field is pushed **at right angles to both**: not toward a pole. Reverse the current or the magnets and the push reverses; reverse both and it is back as it was. A coil on an axle, pushed up on one side and down on the other, turns: a **motor**. In a **loudspeaker** current flipping back and forth pushes a coil and its paper cone in and out, making sound. Why the fields push is left for later; the left-hand rule is a Keeper's note naming the pattern after it is found. Spec 163 owns "a current makes magnetism".

**History cards (real, checked).** Mary the Jewess (Alexandria, first to third century CE; a still and the water bath credited to her by Zosimos, details uncertain); Mikhail Tsvet's chalk column (plant pigments, early 1900s; named chromatography in 1906); the salt spring of Salies-de-Béarn (brine still evaporated in pans; the boar legend labeled a story); Shen Kuo's bitter spring (*Dream Pool Essays*, about 1088: heating the water gave crystals, and an iron pan used to heat them "turned to copper"); Lavoisier's sealed tin (1774, published 1777); Mesoamerican rubber (latex and morning-glory juice, by about 1600 BCE); composite bows of the Eurasian steppe (horn, wood, sinew); Joseph Monier's flowerpots (patent 16 July 1867); Stephanie Kwolek (Kevlar, DuPont, 1964-65); Hooke's hidden law (1676, answered 1678); Faraday's rotating wire (3 September 1821, often called the first electric motor); the moving-coil loudspeaker (Rice and Kellogg, 1925).

## The game or activity

Every bench shows its model line and the safety line. No timers, scores, streaks, medals or locks.

**(a) Clean-Up Bench, model `separate-v1`.** Five supplied mixtures with property tags: gravel and sand; muddy water; salty spring water; sand, salt and water; a black ink spot. A **Which part do you want?** toggle, then one tool or two in order: sieve, filter, evaporating dish (sunny sill), still, paper strip. A tool works only on the property it uses; a mis-chosen tool fails visibly (salty water through the filter dries to a white crust on a dish; the open dish loses the water as vapor). The still takes clear solutions only. Enumerated by script: 10 offered (mixture, wanted part) pairs × 25 plans; every pair has a working plan; the water from salty water has exactly one (the still); every working plan for salt from the three-part mixture starts with the filter. The ink strip shows three spots (blue, red, yellow). *A made-up model; real separations are messier. In real labs, heating and glassware need a grown-up, and nothing is tasted.*

**(b) Reaction Shelf, model `reaction-cards-v1`.** Eleven cards (candle, stove gas, magnesium ribbon, nail rusting, copper heated in air, green copper carbonate heated, limestone in a kiln, iron in copper sulfate, magnesium in copper sulfate, copper in magnesium sulfate, ice melting) sort into four family bins plus **No new substance**. Three cards belong in two bins and say so. Each card turns over to its word equation and **mass bars** (magnesium 2.4 g + oxygen 1.6 g → 4.0 g oxide; copper carbonate 12.4 g → copper oxide 8.0 g + carbon dioxide 4.4 g). A **displacement ladder** of five metals: 20 metal-and-solution pairs, 10 react. *Pictures of real reactions with rounded masses. Flames and heating stay on the screen.*

**(c) Salt Maker, model `salt-namer-v1`.** Pick an acid (hydrochloric, sulfuric, nitric) and a partner: four metals (magnesium, zinc, iron, copper; with the first two acids only), three alkalis (sodium, potassium and calcium hydroxide) or two metal oxides (copper, magnesium). The child builds the word equation from tiles **before** the bench shows it: 23 pairings, 21 salts, 2 "no reaction" (copper). Neutralization shows an indicator strip reaching green at pH 7 (168's colors); **Evaporate** grows crystals. *Pictures only. Acids stay on the screen.*

**(d) Right-Stuff Rig, model `rig-v1`.** Eight samples (clay tile, porcelain cup, polythene sheet, nylon rope, natural rubber, cob block, reinforced concrete, glass-fiber pole) on three test icons (bend, oven heat, drop) and a light/heavy bar: 32 authored cells. **Composite builder:** clay alone cracks as it dries, straw alone flops, cob holds. **Job cards** (oven tile, waterproof bag, tow rope, bridge beam, light springy pole) each offer three samples; the script confirms exactly one fits each. The child says why a fit fits before the next card. *Typical behavior in pictures, not lab measurements.*

**(e) Spring Bench, model `spring-v1`, and Pull Cart, model `cart-v1`.**

- **Bend or bounce** (Sprout): rubber band, sponge, paperclip, play dough; squeeze or bend, predict *springs back* or *stays*.
- **Spring test:** a 10.0 cm spring, a ruler, a newton meter hook, loads 0-7 N in 1 N steps. The ruler shows **length**; the child records **extension**. Authored extension readings (cm, length minus 10.0) with scatter: 0, 2.1, 3.9, 6.1, 8.0, 10.0, 13.5, 18.0. Points drop onto a force-extension graph; the best-fit tool never forces the line through each dot (slope through zero from 0-5 N: 2.0 cm per N; every point within 0.15 cm). After 5 N the line bends (the 6 N point sits 1.5 cm off it); unloaded from 7 N the spring reads 12.0 cm long, a leftover extension of 2.0 cm. The market scale's story spring is this spring.
- **Pull Cart** (Keeper's Desk): a 2 kg or 4 kg cart pulled with 2, 4 or 6 N; predicted speed gain each second = force ÷ mass; measured shows the 0.4 N friction (2 kg, 6 N: predicted 3.0, measured 2.8 m/s each second). Starts from rest. *A made-up model with tidy numbers.*

**(f) Gap and Depth, models `gap-v1` and `tank-v1`.** **Bridge gap:** a 10 m steel handrail section with a 6 mm gap at 15 °C; a temperature dial from −10 to 40 °C; the gap reads 9.0 mm at −10 and 3.0 mm at 40 and never closes. **No gap** shows the rail bowing on a hot day. A particle window keeps every dot the **same size**; only spacing grows. A ring-and-hole picture. **Tank:** the miller's 1.0 m barrel on a 1.0 m ledge, holes at 0.2, 0.5 and 0.8 m deep; pressure arrows of 2, 5 and 8 units at right angles to every wall and the base; jets land furthest from the bottom hole for this supplied geometry. The free surface is 2.0 m above the ground; horizontal landing distance = 2√(depth below the surface × drop to the ground), with negligible resistance. The explicit table fixes each distance to its hole:

| Depth below surface (m) | Hole height/drop to ground (m) | Horizontal landing distance (m) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0.2 | 1.8 | 1.20 |
| 0.5 | 1.5 | 1.73 |
| 0.8 | 1.2 | 1.96 |

Diver panel at Keeper's Desk: about one more atmosphere per 10 m. *Made-up sizes; steel's 12 millionths per °C and water's one atmosphere per 10 m are real rounded values.*

**(g) Wire Jump, model `motor-v1`.** A stiff wire across the gap between two magnets, a small cell in the picture, a switch. Controls: current **toward you / away**, magnets **N left / N right**. Only after T19 does the child predict up, down, toward a pole or no push. Rule (computed as current × field): toward you with N left → up; one reversal → down; both → up. Then **Coil**: two sides pushed opposite ways, the coil turns; **Cone**: current flipping back and forth moves the cone in and out. *A picture model. It shows which way the push goes, not why or how strong. On screen only.*
