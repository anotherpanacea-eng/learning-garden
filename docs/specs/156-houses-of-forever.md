## Purpose

H2 owns 29 Ancient Egypt topics in the pinned Marble catalog. Spec 134, *The Nile Year* (draft PR #137), takes the river: geography, flood and seasons, nilometers, the calendar, scribes, numerals, the Rosetta Stone and archaeology ethics; spec 95 holds two writing objects. Spec 134's Grown-up note leaves "gods, mummification and pharaohs" to other specs, and its crosswalk lists *Building the Pyramids* as untaught. This pack takes Egypt away from the riverbank: monuments, beliefs and people. It repeats none of 134's content and uses the same dates.

A child comes to understand that:

- The pyramids were kings' tombs. The Great Pyramid was built for Khufu about 2550 BCE; the Sphinx has a lion's body and a human head.
- Tomb shapes changed: mastaba, step pyramid, bent, smooth, then hidden valley tombs.
- Organized crews of Egyptian workers, fed and housed nearby, built them; most were probably called up in turns, owing labor to the state. Enslaved masses do not fit the evidence, and "not enslaved" is taught without implying "free to say no".
- Some of the "how" is known (sledges, wet sand, ramps, boats) and some is still argued.
- Egyptians believed in many gods. The game labels every god story as a belief and gives Greek and Egyptian names.
- Belief in a life after death explains tomb goods, mummification and the weighing of the heart.
- The "social pyramid" is a modern drawing with limits: most people farmed, women had legal rights, a few rose, workers once struck.
- Surplus grain freed people for other work, and trade brought cedar from Lebanon and incense from Punt.
- How a museum shows the dead is a choice about respect.

## The knowledge

Checked through search excerpts of the audited sources (page fetches were blocked). No `[check]` items remain.

**Pyramids and the Sphinx.** The Great Pyramid was built for Khufu (4th Dynasty) about 2550 BCE, the date specs 133 and 134 use. Base sides about 230 m; about 146 m tall when new, about 138 m now that most of its casing is gone (Britannica). "More than two million blocks, by most estimates." Britannica counts the Giza pyramids as the only Ancient Wonder still standing. The Sphinx, a reclining lion with a human head, was carved from bedrock and later patched: about 73 m long, 20 m high; its face probably Khafre's, about 2500 BCE.

**Tomb shapes.** Flat-topped **mastabas** ("bench") came first. Djoser's step pyramid at Saqqara (about 2650 BCE; architect traditionally Imhotep) stacked six levels. Sneferu's Bent Pyramid changed angle partway up; his Red Pyramid is usually counted the first true smooth-sided pyramid. Royal pyramids ended with Ahmose I. From Thutmose I (reigned about 1506 to 1493 BCE), kings used rock-cut tombs in the **Valley of the Kings**, for secrecy against robbers (Ineni wrote the digging was done unseen), changing beliefs and the landscape. Most were robbed anyway.

**Who built them.** Lehner's team (AERA) excavated the workers' town south of the pyramids, with bakeries, storerooms and houses; Hawass's team, the workers' cemetery with job titles (Britannica; PBS NOVA). Current reading: a skilled year-round core plus larger groups called up in rotating shifts; service was owed to the state. Herodotus (5th century BCE) wrote of gangs of 100,000 men in turns; archaeologists distrust the number. Workforce estimates vary widely, so the game gives none. Slavery existed in Egypt, but the evidence does not show enslaved masses building pyramids. Child text: "organized crews, fed and housed by the king", with "archaeologists think" on the rotation; "paid" only for skilled crews, as in 134. The Grown-up panel says the labor was owed, so "fed and organized" is not read as "volunteer". Spec 134's Library reading and its item already teach the workers' town and the `slaves-built-pyramids` contrast; this page revisits that briefly (B1 adds the underclaim) and adds rotation, Merer and Herodotus.

**Merer's logbook.** Found at Wadi al-Jarf in 2013 (Pierre Tallet's team), the oldest known inscribed papyri. Merer, an inspector over a unit of about 40 boatmen (part of a gang of about 160), logged limestone trips from Tura to Giza around Khufu's 26th or 27th year. Paraphrased only.

**Moving and raising stone.** Djehutihotep's tomb painting (12th Dynasty, about 1900 BCE) shows 172 men hauling a statue about 6.8 m tall on a sledge while one pours water ahead. A 2014 University of Amsterdam study (*Physical Review Letters*) found the right amount of water roughly halves the pull; too much undoes it. Egyptologists long read the pouring as ritual, so the game says "one good explanation". Ramps lead as the explanation for raising blocks; their shape is argued. A 2018 Liverpool and IFAO mission found a steep Khufu-era ramp at the Hatnub quarry with side stairs and post holes. It is at a quarry, so the game calls it a clue.

**Gods, as beliefs.** Many gods, temples as their homes, animal heads standing for qualities. Seven tiles labeled *A story Egyptians told*: **Ra** (sun; sailing a boat across the sky by day and through the underworld by night), **Osiris** (ruler of the dead; crook and flail), **Isis** (magic and protection; sun disk between cow horns), **Horus** (falcon; linked to the king), **Anubis** (jackal; embalming and tombs), **Thoth** (ibis; writing), **Bastet** (cat; protection). **Ma'at**: goddess and idea of truth and order, shown by a feather. Osiris, Isis, Horus, Anubis and Thoth are Greek forms; Egyptian forms (*Wsir, Iset, Hor, Inpu, Djehuty*) vary by scholar because vowels were not written. Contrast: Khufu's real boat of Lebanese cedar, about 43 m long, now at the Grand Egyptian Museum. The king was believed to stand between gods and people; officials led by the **vizier** governed.

**Afterlife and mummification.** Belief that life went on after death explains tomb goods. Tombs were "houses of eternity" (Getty; Australian Museum); stone tombs were costly and rare. Embalmers dried the body in **natron** about 40 days, wrapped it in linen with **amulets**, and coffined it: about 70 days in all. Liver, lungs, stomach and intestines went into four **canopic jars**; the heart stayed, believed the seat of thought. Depth rule: tomb goods and simple graves for all; drying, wrapping, the four jars and "the heart stayed in the body" from Seedling (the weighing needs the heart); removal of the brain, amulets and the later practice of returning organs (after about 1000 BCE) only at Keeper's depth. No image of a body.

**The weighing of the heart.** *Book of the Dead* chapter 125. The Papyrus of Hunefer (British Museum EA 9901; the museum's catalog dates it about 1285 BCE, and other sources give 1275 or 1310) shows Anubis weighing the heart against Ma'at's feather, Thoth recording, Ammit waiting. A balanced heart passed to Osiris. Every line says "people believed" or "in the story"; no item asks about a child's own heart. Ammit appears only at Keeper's depth, in one gentle line: people believed she swallowed a heart that failed, and Hunefer's papyrus shows his heart passing.

**Who did what.** The textbook pyramid: king; vizier, officials, priests; scribes; craftworkers and traders; farmers, the majority. Limits taught: the drawing is modern; a few rose; few could read; women's formal legal rights were close to men's (Janet Johnson, University of Chicago), with class mattering; enslaved people existed. About 1157 BCE (year 29 of Ramesses III) Deir el-Medina's tomb-builders stopped work over late grain rations, recorded by the scribe Amennakhte (Museo Egizio).

**Trade and plenty.** Surplus grain fed non-farmers. For most of its history Egypt had no coins; values were counted in copper weights. A New Kingdom **deben** was about 91 g (UCL Digital Egypt; earlier standards were lighter), and a plain linen garment about 5 deben (Janssen's Deir el-Medina prices, via secondary sources). The Palermo Stone records 40 ships of wood, probably cedar, under Sneferu. Hatshepsut, a female pharaoh, sent an expedition to **Punt** that brought back incense trees, myrrh, ebony and gold (Deir el-Bahari reliefs). Punt was probably around the southern Red Sea; its exact place is argued. Lapis lazuli came from Badakhshan in Afghanistan (Keeper's note only).

**Looking with care.** Applies 134's ethics card to one choice: museums show wrapped remains only, remove them, or label them "mummified person" (Undark; The Art Newspaper). No verdict, no image of remains.

- **Tomb timeline**: five drawings to order (drag, tap-to-place, or Earlier/Later buttons). *Simplified drawings. Not to scale.*
- **Size strip**: the pyramid (146 m new) beside a made-up 10 m mill; about 15 mills. *Made up for comparing.*
- **Sledge bench** (Who built them): side view of block, sledge, rope and haulers; three sand trays. **Pull** shows haulers needed: dry 20, damp 10, soaked 16. A forward arrow shows the pull, a backward arrow the sand's push-back. Only the sand changes. It builds on the built Workshop ramp ("Fix the parcel ramp", smooth and rough landings) and reuses its friction words; it does not re-teach friction, and proposed spec 154 (Ramps, Levers and the Hoist, wave 2) owns how a ramp trades force for distance. *A made-up model based on a real lab test with small sledges. - **Evidence board**: find cards (town, bakeries, tombs, Merer, Hatnub ramp, Herodotus) sorted into **What it shows** and **Still argued**.
- **Gods shelf**: seven tiles (symbol, role, both names, **Say it**), each *A story Egyptians told*.
