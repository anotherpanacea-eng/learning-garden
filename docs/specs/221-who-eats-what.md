## The knowledge

Sourced in the audit. Willowbank, its scenes and the Osprey Ledger are fiction or model, labeled so.

**What animals need (supported, no claim).** Animals need food, water, air and a safe place; they cannot make their own food, so they eat plants or other animals. (Spec 149 has plants; "right amounts" of food is left to families.)

**Herbivores, carnivores and omnivores (Sprout).** A **herbivore** eats plants; a **carnivore** eats animals; an **omnivore** eats both. Diet decides: the elephant and beaver are large or big-toothed herbivores, the shrew and ladybug small carnivores, and the bearded vulture a carnivore that eats bones of animals already dead (a **scavenger**). The omnivore is central and richly shown: red fox (berries, beetles, worms, mice), badger, hen, wild boar, magpie. Teeth are a Seedling clue: wide flat back teeth grind plants, pointed ones hold and cut. People are not sorted; the Grown-up panel says humans are omnivores as a species if a child asks.

**Living things change their habitats (Sprout to Seedling).** A beaver's dam backs water into a pond whose depth keeps its lodge door underwater; a bird's nest shelters eggs; a rabbit's burrow gives safety; earthworms pull leaves down and leave casts, and their tunnels let air and water in; tree roots lift stones as they grow toward water; people build houses. Every change is paired with the need it meets (food, water, shelter, safety), and argued as "the ___ changed its home because ___". Changes are framed as meeting needs, not as harm. Causation runs from organism to place: the pond was not there first.

**Structures for survival (Sapling).** A **structure** is a part; its **function** is its job; the job helps the organism survive, grow, behave (find food) or make seeds and young. Outside: owl feathers with soft, fringed edges quiet its flight; hedgehog spines; holly prickles; apple petals and scent bring bees; an eggshell protects the growing chick; a heron's long legs let it wade deeper; a beaver's front teeth keep growing and are worn down by gnawing. Inside: a hare's heart pumps blood carrying food and oxygen; a stem's water tubes; a reed's air spaces carry air to roots in airless mud. Organisms do not choose or want their structures; the feature works. Arguments use claim, evidence, reasoning, and broken arguments are fixed.

**Energy from food and the Sun (Sapling).** Plants (and algae) use sunlight to make food that stores the Sun's energy; soil gives water and minerals, not energy. In every chain in this pack, food energy traces back link by link ("where did its energy come from?") to plants and then the Sun, on land (Sun → clover → rabbit → fox; Sun → beech seeds → wood mouse → tawny owl) and in water (Sun → algae → mayfly young → brown trout → heron). A night hunter is no exception. Animals use food energy to move, grow, mend and keep warm; most leaves as warmth. Energy flows one way; matter cycles (151's rot log). Sunshine can warm an animal directly, but its food energy still came through plants. Energy-flow level only: no photosynthesis or respiration chemistry. The sunlight claim is scoped to the chains shown ("every chain in this activity"), because spec 171 teaches deep-sea vent chains that start with chemical energy; the Grown-up panel says so, and child cards never say "every food chain".

**Toxins building up (Keeper's Desk).** Two mechanisms, kept apart. **Within one animal:** a persistent poison is taken in faster than it is removed, so it accumulates over many meals. **Across levels:** a persistent poison is kept as it passes up, but only a small share of each level's living stuff (biomass) passes up to build the next, the rest leaving as waste and breathed-out gas (the energy leaves mostly as warmth, a separate point), so the same poison sits in less living stuff and its **concentration** (poison per unit of body) rises at each step. Total amount alone is never the evidence: a bigger animal can hold more poison without holding it more densely, and harm is shown by real evidence (thin eggshells), not by the model. Persistence is the pivot: a poison that breaks down does not build up. The poison travels mainly through food, not drinking water. The mirror: energy shrinks up the chain while a persistent poison's concentration grows. Real case: DDT, sprayed against insects from the 1940s, breaks down very slowly; its product DDE thinned eggshells of fish- and bird-eating birds, eggs broke and fewer chicks hatched. In a 1967 Long Island salt-marsh study, plankton held about 0.04 ppm and a ring-billed gull 75 ppm. After the 1972 US decision, ospreys and eagles recovered.

**History cards (real, checked).**
- **al-Jahiz, *Book of Animals* (Basra, 9th century).** Mosquitoes seek blood, flies hunt mosquitoes, other animals eat flies: some historians call it an early written food chain.
- **Darwin's worms (1881).** Years of garden observation: earthworms mix soil, make topsoil and slowly bury stones.
- **Elton's *Animal Ecology* (1927).** Food chains, with many small animals below and few large hunters above.
- **Rachel Carson, *Silent Spring* (1962),** and the 1972 US decision stopping most DDT use.
- **Ratcliffe's eggshells (1967, Keeper's note).** Peregrine, sparrowhawk and golden eagle eggshells in museum and private collections, laid since the late 1940s, were lighter and thinner; later work tied this to DDT.

## The game or activity

**(a) Who Eats What?** Fourteen animal cards, each with a drawn food plate as evidence, sort into **Herbivore (plants)**, **Carnivore (animals)** and **Omnivore (both)**. Cards can move freely; the reply asks the child to check the plate, and three surprise cards (elephant, shrew, beaver) speak a line. Ember's card is set aside: a made-up dragon has no real diet. Seedling adds a **teeth tray** of skull drawings (flat, pointed, both) as a clue, always followed by "check the plate".

**(b) Who Changed This Place?** Six before-and-after pairs (beaver pond, nest, burrow, worm casts, root-lifted stone, a house). Pick the changer, then the need (food, water, shelter, safety). Replies ask the child to look again, never mark. A spoken sentence frame turns it into a claim with a because.

**(c) Built to Survive.** Ten structure cards fill a three-slot chain (structure → function → how it helps), then an inside/outside tray. The **argument builder** offers three claim, three evidence and three reasoning tiles per organism; the child joins one of each. Three broken arguments (wants-to-be-safe, prickles-and-green, feathers-for-swimming) are fixed by choosing what the part does.

**(d) Trace It Back.** Start at a hungry animal and press **Where did its energy come from?** to add the next link, food → eater, until the Sun. Three chains (field, night, stream). A reversed arrow reads back "This says the fox is food for the rabbit. Is that right?" An energy-jobs panel (move, grow, mend, keep warm; most leaves as warmth) and a one-way line contrasting matter's loop (151).

**(e) The Osprey Ledger (Keeper's Desk), model `chain-v1`.** *A made-up model; the rule is on screen.*

- Inputs: poison `stays` or `breaks down`; share of living stuff passed up each step `a tenth` (default, 151's rule) or `a fifth` (151's slider: the rule is rough).
- Rule, per **whole level** (all the plankton, all the small fish, and so on), never per animal: the plankton level holds 1,000 units of living stuff. Each level up holds the share of the level below (tenth: 1,000, 100, 10, 1; fifth: 1,000, 200, 40, 8). The plankton level picks up 1,000 poison units, 1 per unit of living stuff. If the poison stays, all of it is eaten and kept, so every level's total stays 1,000. If it breaks down, it breaks down before anything eats it, so only the plankton hold any: whole-level poison 1,000, 0, 0, 0. No fresh poison is added at higher levels.
- Three meters per level: **living stuff**, **poison in the whole level**, **poison in each unit of living stuff** (the concentration, the meter the child reads). Concentration = poison ÷ living stuff.
- Readouts (4 settings), concentration: stays, tenth: 1, 10, 100, 1,000; stays, fifth: 1, 5, 25, 125; breaks down (either share): 1, 0, 0, 0. The whole-level poison meter stays at 1,000 when the poison stays, so a rising total is never what the child is shown as evidence.
- **Show energy too:** energy in the whole level, from the selected share, 1 energy unit per unit of living stuff (tenth: 1,000, 100, 10, 1; fifth: 1,000, 200, 40, 8). It always matches the living-stuff meter, so the overlay and readouts cannot drift apart.
- Model line: "The same share passes up each step. In \"stays\" mode, every bit of poison is eaten and kept. Real chains are messier. The meters show amounts, not harm." The "kept" sentence applies to "stays" only.
- A real-figures card sets the model beside the 1967 marsh numbers (0.04 ppm and 75 ppm).

No timers, scores, streaks, medals or locks. Every activity is open from the first visit.
