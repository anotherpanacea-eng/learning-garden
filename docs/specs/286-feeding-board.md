## Purpose and scope

At exact **`#living/who-eats`**, make a picture board of fourteen animals in three food baskets, then apply the idea to paired-animal evidence comparisons. The target is sorting a named animal using supplied information about diet. Size, teeth, cuteness and hunting are unreliable substitutes. Names and food evidence are supplied. A fitting check is evidence of this supported task response, never independent animal identification, reading, dietary expertise, recall or mastery.

All fourteen proposal animals are in scope: four herbivores, four carnivores and six omnivores after source correction. A new four-case **Looks and food** contrast replaces a detached recall quiz; no extra Set1 quiz. Teach cards1–3 become a manipulable example and optional help. No teeth tray, habitats, structure/function, energy, DDT, history, later proposal benches, story adapters, ActIII route change, atlas expansion or curriculum closure. No child/family food questions, human sorting, diet advice, free text, microphone, recording, network service, timer, grade, rewards or access locks.

The seven-spot ladybird correction is substantive: proposal232 labelled an aphid-only ladybug a carnivore. Verified primary research includes pollen feeding, so this board includes aphids+pollen and groups it as an omnivore. This does not establish a wild pollen frequency, equal amounts, both foods at every meal or every ladybird species' diet. Other general labels are pinned to the named organisms in the companion. Food pictures illustrate supplied species information; a single pictured meal cannot prove an entire diet.

Original named-animal and food drawings are required, not emoji substitutes or fourteen paragraph cards. Sources provide biological facts, not licenses for pictures. No source illustration, upstream code or content is incorporated without an applicable verified license; current reuse is design study only.
