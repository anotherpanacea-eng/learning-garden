A child learns: some common words are read at a glance, and most of each works the usual way, with one odd part; a root word sits under -s, -es, -ing, -ed, -er, -est and un-, and one letter or two before the ending is a clue to the root; compound words are two words joined, whose parts often give the meaning but not always, and some words only look like two; every syllable has a vowel sound, so a long word splits into chunks, and a failed split gets a second try; rereading a familiar book makes it smoother, and the end marks say which voice to use; and an unusual word can be opened by a word inside it, then fixed to a word already heard.

## The knowledge

Sourced in the [audit](../source-audits/252-pips-library-cushions.md). DfE is England's national curriculum for English (2013) and its Appendix 1; CCSS is the Common Core ELA standards.

**Sight words.** The DfE calls words with unusual correspondences "common exception words" and asks pupils to read them, "noting unusual correspondences between spelling and sound and where these occur in the word" (Year 1 and Year 2); its Year 1 list includes the, said, was, you, they, of, are, come, some, one and there, and its Year 2 list includes again, water, half and only. CCSS RF.K.3c asks children to read common high-frequency words by sight, and RF.1.3g and RF.2.3f irregularly spelled words. Ehri's orthographic-mapping account explains sight-word learning as bonding a word's letters to its sounds, not memorizing its shape, which is why the cards name the regular parts and the one odd part. Sight words here come from the DfE lists, plus three common words (about, all, from) that also appear among Dolch's 1936 service words. Dolch's public-domain status is unconfirmed `[check]`, so the words are used but no Dolch list is presented or reproduced as a list. Fry's Instant Words are not used.

**Endings.** DfE Year 1: read words with -s, -es, -ing, -ed, -er and -est endings, and the prefix un-, which is added "without any change to the spelling of the root word"; its notes add that pupils read suffixed words by building on root words they can already read. CCSS RF.1.3f: read words with inflectional endings. When a root's last consonant doubles (big, biggest) or its final e drops (hope, hoping), the letters before the ending are a clue to the root.

**Compound words.** DfE Year 1: two words joined, each part spelled as it would be on its own (football, playground, bedroom). CCSS L.2.4d: use the meaning of the parts to predict a compound's meaning. In most English compounds the second element names the kind of thing (a raincoat is a coat), but meanings can drift from the parts (butterfly, pineapple). Some words contain two shorter words without being compounds: *carpet* came into English from Middle French and Old Italian, and *season* from Anglo-French.

**Syllables.** CCSS RF.1.3d: every syllable must have a vowel sound; RF.1.3e: decode two-syllable words by breaking them into syllables; RF.3.3c: decode multisyllable words. DfE Year 1: read words of more than one syllable; Years 3-4: pupils reading longer words "should be supported to test out different pronunciations". Child text uses the letter form: each chunk usually has a vowel letter (a, e, i, o, u or y). With two middle consonants, usually split between them (rab | bit); with one, try before it first (ti | ger), then after it (rob | in). In every tag the keyed split is the only offered split with a vowel letter in each chunk, and it matches the dictionary division `[check]`. Words with a beat but no vowel letter (*rhythm*) and syllabic consonants (the last beat of *little*) are left out of every item; the grown-up panel says why the cards say "usually".

**Rereading and fluency.** DfE Years 1 and 2: re-read books "consistent with their developing phonic knowledge" to build fluency and confidence. CCSS RF.1.4b and RF.2.4b: read on-level text orally with accuracy, appropriate rate and expression on successive readings; RF.1.4c: use context to confirm or self-correct, rereading as necessary. The US National Reading Panel (2000) found guided repeated oral reading improved word recognition, fluency and comprehension; the EEF's fluency guidance names guided oral reading and repeated reading. This pack takes the shape (adult model, read together, read alone, reread another night) and none of the measurement: rate and accuracy are left to the grown-up's ear.

**Unfamiliar words.** DfE Years 3-4: apply knowledge of root words, prefixes and suffixes "both to read aloud and to understand the meaning of new words", and read further exception words. Worked words: *eighth* (eight + h); *knowledge* (built from know, with the k once sounded in Old and Middle English); *business* (busy + ness, y to i; the DfE's own example); *island* (Old English *īgland*; the s was added in the 1500s by association with the unrelated *isle*, and is not said); *heard* (hear + d, rhyming with bird); *cupboard* (cup + board, the p not said); *breakfast* (break + fast, where fast means going without food).

## The lesson

Jane's **teach -> guided -> practice**: seventeen teach cards, one idea each with a worked example revealed a step per tap; each set's first item shows 1-3 visible steps. **"I'm stuck" ladder** on every item: hint -> an easier case plus how to start (I sat on the rug; sunset; hops; sun and set; Quack!; sleepy) -> skip for now, no penalty.

**Contrasts:** said vs rain; saw vs was; a compound vs a word that only looks like two (sunset, carpet); the thing vs the kind (rain-coat); parts that give the meaning vs parts that do not (snowflake, butterfly); -er vs -est; un- vs re-; hoping vs hopping; a chunk with a vowel letter vs one without; ti | ger works vs ro | bin fails; first read vs reread; question mark vs exclamation point vs period; close enough vs matching the page; letters inside a word vs the word it was built from (now and know).

**Misconceptions tagged on choices:** `look-alike`, `same-letters-any-order`, `reversed-letters`, `ai-always-rain`, `split-anywhere`, `first-word-is-thing`, `ignore-a-part`, `missed-compound`, `parts-always-give-meaning`, `wrong-cut`, `ending-is-root`, `er-for-est`, `ending-reverses`, `un-is-again`, `ignore-un`, `double-ignored`, `ending-ignored`, `chunk-without-vowel`, `vowel-missed`, `ignore-end-mark`, `keep-going`, `skip-page`, `fast-is-fluent`, `letters-not-root`, `every-letter-sounds`, `same-letters-same-sound`, `one-meaning-only`.

## The game or activity

Every bench screen: *Nothing here listens or times you. Read to a grown-up if you like.* No score, timer, lock or mastery label. Taps only; nothing is dragged. A grown-up may read any sentence; the child reads the word tiles.

- **The bedtime shelf.** Three books, each page one or two lines, with **Read it again**: listen (a grown-up, or later a cleared narration), read together, read alone, and another night read the whole book again. Nothing is saved: no bookmark, count or date.
- **(a) Sight-Word Lamps (Sprout).** Twelve lamps, each a sentence with a gap and three word tiles; only one tile makes sense. A lit lamp shows its **tricky part**, compared with a known word, never with a symbol.
- **(b) Cushion Covers (Seedling).** **Split:** six covers, tap where two patches meet (three seams offered). **Build:** four labels, pick two of four patches. **Only looks like two:** carpet, sunset, season, bedtime.
- **(c) Ending Lamps (Seedling).** Eight words; tap the root under the ending (unfolded peels both ends).
- **(d) Lantern Tags (Sapling).** Eight tags, three splits each, only one with a vowel letter in every chunk; then *robin* and *tiger* show the second try. **Clap it** with a grown-up, unrecorded.
- **(e) Read It Like You Mean It (Seedling).** Six lines from the books; choose the voice the end mark asks for, then read it to a grown-up.
- **(f) The Puzzle-Word Lamp (Sapling, Keeper's Desk).** Six words with a four-step ladder: a word you know inside, the parts you can read, another sound for the odd part, a word you have heard.

Bench tiles vary the fitting position: lamps A 4 / B 4 / C 4; covers 2 / 2 / 2; endings 3 / 2 / 3; tags 3 / 3 / 2; marks 2 / 2 / 2; puzzle 2 / 2 / 2.
