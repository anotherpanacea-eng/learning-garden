## Purpose and bounded scope

After the storm, Moss wants room for wildlife as well as new planting beds. A delivery note says a sealed café parcel is beside a damp, sheltered place on land. Compare three original pictured places: a sunny dry patch, a shady log with damp leaves, and the water inside a pond. Investigate which pictured place could shelter a garden land snail, and explain using clues. This is one authored, replayable investigation, not the three-investigation/food-chain release originally outlined in spec 23. Other organisms, real field observations, food chains, biological growth and a dynamic ecosystem remain later work.

Connect the opening and ending to the existing 24-place planting plan: a drawing of equal spaces does not tell us what real organisms need; a garden can include planting beds and undisturbed shelter. Return links keep that plan intact. The parcel is fictional café food, never an organism's diet or a harvest from the log.

## Play and science content

All three places are open immediately. Each has an original illustration and a large keyboard/tap inspection control. Inspection reveals an enlarged/cutaway drawing with a short supplied observation, adds that observation to a small notebook, and can be repeated. Sunny patch: sun falls on exposed ground; its surface is drawn dry. Log: underside is shaded, with damp fallen leaves on land. Pond: the pictured spot is underwater, not the bank. These are authored scene conditions, not measurements or rules that every sunny place is dry/every log is damp. No need to lift a real log, collect an animal or approach water.

A persistent comparison asks which pictured place offers a land snail damp shelter. Choose a place and then a reason (damp and sheltered / brightest sunshine / underwater). Keep attempts separate from observations. Useful response is log + damp shelter; another response gives specific feedback and invites comparison, with no penalty or forced unrelated question. An always-available “Think it through together” explanation supplies the reasoning and records support. Within a started visit, narration requests are recorded separately from explanation help; playback/understanding is not inferred. Welcome narration before the explicit start is not recorded. Ordinary inspection is the learning activity, not an independent assessment.

The clue is resolved after viewing the log and at least one contrasting place, plus either a matching reasoned choice or the explanation. This is a small story search, not a skill/access lock; all locations, other destinations, help and pause/return remain open. Explain the two observations still needed if search is incomplete. Show the sealed parcel only after this search and an explicit “Look beside the log” action. Reveal an illustrated snail on land nearby as a story detail, with text that the clues suggest a suitable place but cannot guarantee an animal is present.

The parcel holds exactly three sealed packs of four Moon crunch bites (12). A countable picture and 3×4 text are an optional mathematical connection, with no additional scored arithmetic toll. An explicit “Carry 12 bites to the café” adds exactly 12 to the existing moon-crunch stock once per explorer. Inspecting, answering or following links never fills jars. Stock cap is the existing 180; if adding 12 would overflow, retain the found parcel and offer to use stock first. Repeated visits/reloads/rereads do not create another parcel. Existing gathering trips are unchanged and remain the renewable supply game. Replaying this investigation revisits the same authored scenes and retained work, with no mastery label or claim of unfamiliar transfer.
