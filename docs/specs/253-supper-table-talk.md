**Speaking rules (binding):** talk cards are spoken with a grown-up, a sibling or a friend. Nothing is recorded or stored, no microphone permission is ever requested, and the game never knows whether a card was used (build-out question 5). No card asks about the child's own body, family, feelings, home, school or location: prompts are about the town, the story, things on the table or made-up situations ("describe Ember's cake", never "describe your house"). On-screen practice uses supplied choices only. Opinions are never keyed as right or wrong; only whether a reason fits an opinion is keyed, and only where the card's stated facts decide it.

## Purpose

**Speaking & Listening** has 21 pinned topics and three named on `main`; all six targets below are "not yet mapped" in `curriculum/topic-coverage.csv` and named in no spec. Strand **E5-I** asks for "listen, ask questions, explain and retell with an adult" and **E5-II** for "collaborative talk" ([six-act strands](../six-act-strands.md)); the listener and viewpoint bench reaches into **E5-III** (audience awareness, build on others), and the Act VI welcome into **E5-VI**. Built work has no talk activity: the café's welcome-back supper (spec 22) is a serving goal, the gathering (spec 50) is recipe math, and Sentence Bridges (109) builds one spoken-style sentence. Nothing gives a young child a reason to describe, wonder or say "because" out loud.

A child learns: a description gives a listener size, shape, color, material and sound or smell, in an order; a wondering tries an idea before you are sure, and a new word sticks when you use it; an opinion needs a reason, and a reason has to fit (saying the opinion again, or a point for the other side, does not; a point against the other side can); one talker at a time, staying on topic and linking a turn to the last one; a speaker watches the listener and can win them back, and another view gets its reason said back before yours; a retelling keeps its order, and a short report names its topic, gives facts about it and wraps up.

## The knowledge

Sourced in the [audit](../source-audits/253-supper-table-talk.md).

**What the curricula ask.** The English national curriculum's spoken-language programme (years 1 to 6, statutory) asks pupils to listen and respond, articulate and justify answers and opinions, give well-structured descriptions and narratives, take part in collaborative conversations staying on topic, use talk to speculate, hypothesize, imagine and explore ideas, use strategies to build vocabulary, gain, maintain and monitor the interest of listeners, and consider different viewpoints, building on the contributions of others; its guidance adds taking turns. Common Core asks kindergartners to describe familiar people, places, things and events with extra detail (SL.K.4), follow agreed rules such as listening and taking turns, and continue a conversation through multiple exchanges (SL.K.1); first graders to describe with relevant details (SL.1.4) and build on others' talk through multiple exchanges (SL.1.1b); second graders to recount an experience with facts and descriptive detail (SL.2.4) and link comments to others' remarks (SL.2.1b); third graders to report or recount at an understandable pace (SL.3.4); fourth and fifth graders to report or recount in an organized, logically sequenced way (SL.4.4, SL.5.4).

**Talk as thinking.** Mercer, Wegerif and Dawes taught 9- and 10-year-olds explicit ground rules for "exploratory talk" (share ideas, give reasons, consider alternatives) and found their reasoning improved more than matched classes'. The Oracy Framework, devised by Voice 21 and Oracy Cambridge, names four strands of talk: physical, linguistic, cognitive, and social and emotional. The EEF Toolkit rates oral language interventions at about six months' additional progress on average. These support teaching talk explicitly, with sentence frames and agreed turn rules.

**A reason that fits.** A reason supports an opinion when it says why that choice makes sense. Two common non-reasons for young children are restating the opinion ("because it's the best") and "because I just do"; a third is a true fact that supports a different choice. Whether a reason fits is checkable against stated facts; whether an opinion is right is not, and the pack never asks.

**Describing, retelling, reporting.** A description gains detail from the senses and from an order (top to bottom, big to small). A retelling keeps time order, signaled by time words (first, then, after that, at last) and causes ("so"). A short report names its topic, groups facts on that topic, and closes; speaking at a pace a listener can follow is checked by the listener, not a clock.

## The lesson

Jane's **teach -> guided -> practice**: twelve teach cards, one idea each with a worked example revealed a step per tap; the first item of sets A to E shows 1-3 visible steps. **"I'm stuck" ladder** on every item: hint -> an easier case plus how to start (a red ball; wet paw prints; a hat that keeps rain off; "Yes, and rolls!"; a story about socks; Pip woke, ate, slept) -> skip for now, no penalty.

**Contrasts:** a description vs a location or an opinion; a new detail vs the same word again; one clue vs all clues; ordered vs jumbled; a wondering vs a telling; a maybe that uses the clues vs one that ignores them; a fitting reason vs saying it again vs a reason for the other side vs "just because"; a liking vs a fact on the card; waiting vs interrupting; building on vs repeating vs changing the subject; louder vs more interesting; naming another's reason vs brushing it off vs giving up your own; a closing vs a new topic.

**Misconceptions tagged on choices:** `no-picture-words`, `opinion-not-description`, `repeat-not-detail`, `off-topic`, `not-every-clue`, `jumbled-order`, `telling-not-wondering`, `ignores-clue`, `word-wrong-sense`, `word-no-meaning`, `shuts-down-idea`, `change-subject`, `other-side-reason`, `no-reason`, `says-it-again`, `one-right-opinion`, `missed-reason`, `unchecked-reason`, `interrupt`, `grab-turn`, `repeat-not-build`, `already-told`, `dull-start`, `no-start`, `ignore-listener`, `louder-not-better`, `dismiss-view`, `drop-own-view`, `agree-no-add`, `disagree-not-asked`, `drop-on-topic-fact`, `abrupt-end`, `new-topic-at-end`.

## The game or activity

Every talk card shows: *Say it out loud with a grown-up, a sibling or a friend. Nothing is recorded.* Cards, choices and feedback have read-aloud through the café's existing optional narration (spec 22). For ages 4 to 6, every Sprout and Seedling card shows a drawing of its scene, and choices that name a thing show it. No score, timer, lock or mastery label. Drags have tap equivalents.

- **(a) Describe It.** Six talk cards (Ember's cake, Dot's hat, the café lantern, the miller's cat, the baker's loaf, a made-up teapot) and the five-finger noticing hand; then swap roles. **Guess the Thing:** three rounds of three clues and three pictures (fitting picture second, third, first).
- **(b) I Wonder.** Six wonder cards with starters ("I wonder…", "What if…", "Maybe it's because…") and three new words from the scenes: crumbly, steaming, flicker.
- **(c) Because.** Six opinion cards about town things, the "I think… because…" frame, then **Which reason fits?** (Set C), where each card states its facts.
- **(d) Pass the Spoon.** Ember's talking spoon, a four-line talk-rules card, linking starters, and four topic cards to keep going for three turns or more.
- **(e) Keep Them Listening.** Drifting-off and bring-them-back picture cards ("sometimes a yawn just means someone is sleepy"), Hob's goat story, and a disagree-kindly card.
- **(f) Tell It in Order.** Puff's five market-day cards, time words, and retelling cards; the Act VI card after ch. 4. At Keeper's Desk, **A Short Talk About the Café**: pick an opening, three of five facts and a closing (fitting cards at varied positions), then give the talk aloud; the grown-up checks pace by saying a fact back.

Codes only. No audio, no transcript, no free text, and no field records whether a talk card was pulled, used or for how long. Every entry keeps `shown` (the ids offered, in order) and its own outcome code, so it restores without today's content. Validation uses the save's own entries and structural bounds: `shown` holds 2 to 6 distinct ids, `pick` is in `shown`, `placed` is a permutation of `shown`, `picked` holds up to three distinct ids from `shown`, outcomes are in their enums, `at` is a finite timestamp. Kept results are never recomputed against today's items, wording or key on load; a missing item reads as absent. Support (a guided step or the stuck ladder) is a separate flag per kept item, never merged with an independent attempt. An unknown `v` or unreadable entry stays exact and exportable, only that bench turns read-only, and one bad item never closes the corner. Export, import, reset and save-off follow the shared store and `prepareProgressImport` limits. `cafe` and `story` are never read or written here.
