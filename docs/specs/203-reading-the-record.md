## Purpose

Ten pinned Data & Statistics and Measurement topics have no reference and no claim: organizing and interpreting categorical data, reading tables and timetables, scaled bar graphs and line graphs for change, the full family of displays (pictograms with many-to-one keys, tallies, grouped tables, pie charts) and choosing among them, line plots of measured lengths in whole units and eighths, comparing two data sets, and plotting scatter graphs. Draft spec 116 (#128) already builds a tally, a one-to-one pictogram and a bar graph from the child's own weather log; draft spec 119 (#126) and draft spec 140 (#138) already teach the mean, and 119 names correlation. Nobody yet asks a child to **read and argue from a record someone else kept**.

A child comes to understand that sorting needs a rule and an honest Other group; that a table is a tool for answering a question, with totals that check against the whole; that "how many more" is a gap; that a line plot's axis is a ruler where position means length and stack height means how many; that a table cell lives where one row meets one column, and an hour has 60 minutes; that a bar is read on its scale, and a picture on its key; that a line joins readings in time order, its steepest part is the biggest change and its in-between values are estimates; that a pie slice is a share of one whole; that comparing two sets takes a typical value **and** a spread, and one far value pulls the mean but barely the median; and that a scatter graph puts one dot per pair, a line of best fit balances the dots, reading far outside them is unsafe, and two things rising together need not cause each other.

**Builds on, never re-teaches:** spec 116's tally, one-picture-per-day pictogram, bar graph and "how many more" questions (here: scales of 5, keys of 10, half pictures, grouped classes and two data sets); spec 42/47's two-bar drafts (here: scaled axes and many bars); spec 84's one-rule if/otherwise machine (here: data categories with an Other group, then a second rule); sibling spec 183's Carroll grid for shapes (here: the same two-rule grid holding counts, as the doorway to two-way tables); spec 119's mean, median and positive correlation and spec 140's leveled mean (here: one recap line; the pack teaches median, mode, range and outliers for comparing); spec 140's thermometer reading between marks (here: reading a line graph between points); spec 178's distance-time graph (here: a quantity other than distance over time); spec 189's flood post and spec 120's ledger (here: the same numbers on a chart). **Not targeted:** Venn diagrams and set overlaps (sibling spec 202); probability (202); telling time itself (sibling spec 207; durations are counted on inline); the mean as a first teaching (119, 140); correlation strength in numbers; a line of best fit's equation (spec 195's linear graphs).

## The knowledge

**Sorting and organizing.** Choose a rule before sorting ("what kind is it?"). Each thing goes in exactly one group; a thing that fits none goes in **Other**, never forced into a group or dropped. Count each group, write each name beside its count, check that the counts add to the number of things sorted (6 + 4 + 3 + 2 = 15), then order most to fewest; the most common group is the **mode**. A second rule makes a grid (kind down, size across), where each thing lands in one box: the doorway to two-way tables. Sibling 202 owns overlapping sets (Venn diagrams); the grid here never overlaps.

**Interpreting categories.** Three moves: read one count, total all counts (never count the groups), compare two counts. "How many more" is the gap: pair one to one or subtract (6 shells, 3 feathers: 3 more, not 6 and not 9). A total that does not match the whole means something was lost or counted twice.

**Line plots.** The horizontal axis is a ruler: every whole number present and evenly spaced, even where no X sits (8 inches stays, empty). One X per object, stacked when lengths match. Two questions: how many? (count Xs) and how long? (read position). Measure from zero. **In eighths** (inches split into 8): 2/8 = 1/4, 4/8 = 1/2, 6/8 = 3/4 sit on the same marks, so 1/2 − 3/8 = 4/8 − 3/8 = 1/8. Totals add every X: three Xs at 5/8 give 15/8 = 1 7/8 inches (not 3, the count; not 15/24, adding bottoms). Inches are used because US rulers mark eighths; the flood post and river charts stay in centimeters.

**Tables.** A value lives where one row meets one column (finger on each). Timetables: services in columns, stops in rows. **Durations** are counted on across the hour, because an hour is 60 minutes: 9:30 to 10:20 is 30 + 20 = 50 minutes, not 90 (1020 − 930) and not 0.90 hours; 8:45 to 10:20 is 15 + 60 + 20 = 95 minutes, 1 hour 35 minutes, not "1 hour 75". Two-way tables: each box is one pair (shells found at the mill yard); end numbers are row or column totals, never a single box or a difference. A hidden box is found from its row or its column, and both ways agree.

**Bar charts, pictograms, tallies, classes.** Read the scale first: if each square is 5, nine squares are 45, and a gap of five squares is 25 finds. Pictogram keys can stand for many (one sack = 10, half a sack = 5: 3½ sacks = 35, not 3½ and not 31). Tallies bundle in fives (four strokes and one across). Two data sets on one chart (two colors) support a conclusion about a **difference in change**: floods per decade 1, 1, 1, 4 against big rains 6, 5, 6, 8. Continuous readings are grouped into classes that do not overlap and leave no gaps, written 10 ≤ h < 20, so a boundary value (20) belongs to exactly one class (20 ≤ h < 30); bars for classes touch, while bars for categories have gaps. An axis that does not start at zero must say so: Bramble's axis from 8 makes 15 look seven times 9.

**Line graphs for change over time.** Time runs along the bottom; each point is a reading, traced down to its time and across to its value. Height is not change: the brook is highest on Friday (32 cm) but rises most from Wednesday to Thursday (12 to 26, up 14), the steepest climb. A difference is two readings subtracted (Friday − Monday = 28 cm), read on the scale (each square 2 cm), not counted in squares. Between readings the line is an **estimate** under a steady-change assumption (19 cm halfway between Wednesday and Thursday), neither a measurement nor worthless. A line is the wrong choice when the values are not ordered in time: kinds of finds take bars. The line graph is not a picture of the river (spec 178's graph-as-picture caution, revisited).

**Pie charts.** The whole circle is the whole data set, 360°. Each slice is its count's share of the total: angle = count ÷ total × 360°. The poll: Wall 9, Sponge 15, Some of Each 12 of 36 give 90°, 150°, 120°, summing to 360°. Wrong methods named: 360 ÷ count (40° for the Wall; the three slices would fill only 94°) and count × total. A slice shows a proportion; a count comes back only with the total (90° of 36 = 9; 60° of 24 letters = 4). **Which chart?** kinds → bar chart; readings over time → line graph; shares of one whole → pie chart; measured lengths → line plot; two measures on the same days → scatter graph; ungrouped whole-number counts can also take a vertical line chart.

**Comparing two data sets.** Give a typical value and a spread. **Median**: the middle value in order. **Range**: highest − lowest, one number. **Mean** (recap from 119/140 only): the total shared out evenly. Courier trips by two roads (minutes): bridge 38, 40, 41, 42, 43, 44, 46 (median 42, range 8, mean 42); ford 31, 32, 33, 34, 36, 38, 76 (median 34, range 45, mean 40). The ford is usually 8 minutes faster, the bridge far more reliable; the means (42 and 40) hide both facts. Hiding the 76 moves the ford's mean from 40 to 34 and its median from 34 to 33.5. The 76 was a real flooded day: an outlier is explained, not deleted to tidy the data.

**Scatter graphs.** Each dot is one day's pair: rain at Vadoalto (mm, along the bottom) and the next morning's rise at the mill post (cm, up the side), on labeled axes with even scales. Nine usual days: (4, 6), (8, 8), (15, 15), (20, 17), (22, 20), (28, 25), (30, 25), (35, 30), (40, 33); one outlier (12, 34) carries the note *ice jammed below the mill*. The pattern rises (positive, named in 119); a Three patterns card recaps uphill, downhill and no pattern, and says no line is drawn when there is no pattern, as the research asks. A **line of best fit** is one straight line in the trend's direction with dots balanced either side (the drawn line, rise = 0.8 × rain + 2, has four above, four below, one on; least squares on the usual days gives a slope of about 0.77), not a dot-to-dot zig-zag, not forced through (0, 0), and not bent by the outlier. Read inside the data (25 mm → about 22 cm); outside it (80 mm → "66 cm") the river may spill and behave differently, so the line cannot be trusted there. **Goes with is not causes** (recap from 119): market umbrellas rise with the brook because rain moves both.

**Real anchors (see audit).**
- **The Babylonian Astronomical Diaries.** Cuneiform tablets written in Babylon from about 652 BCE to 61 BCE: observers recorded the sky, and each month also the weather, prices of commodities such as barley and dates, and the level of the Euphrates. Most surviving tablets are fragments (standard edition: Sachs and Hunger). Child text: "about 600 years, ending about 2,000 years ago". Chosen over the Nilometer, which draft spec 134 (#137) already owns.
- **William Playfair's charts.** *The Commercial and Political Atlas* (London, 1786) printed line charts of England's trade over the 1700s and a bar chart of Scotland's imports and exports with 17 trading partners for one year (1780 to 1781), drawn as bars because he lacked a run of years. His *Statistical Breviary* (1801) printed what is generally credited as the first pie chart.
- **Florence Nightingale's diagram.** Her *Notes on Matters Affecting the Health, Efficiency, and Hospital Administration of the British Army* (1858), sent to Queen Victoria, included a diagram of the causes of army deaths in the East (Crimean War): one wedge per month, blue for preventable disease, red for wounds, black for other causes, wedge **area** standing for deaths. It is a polar-area diagram, not a pie chart; child text says wedges.

## The game or activity

**(a) Sorting Tray, model `sort-tray-v1`.** Fifteen picture cards (6 shells, 4 buttons, 3 feathers, a spoon, a brass key) and baskets. *A made-up model: fifteen authored finds; every find goes in exactly one basket.* Sprout: choose the rule *What kind is it?*, drag finds, add an Other basket; line rows up one to one to see which has more (no numbers). Seedling: count, fill a two-column table, see the check line (15), order the rows; **Add two feathers** updates the record (17). **Add a second rule** (*Big or small?*) turns two kinds into a 2 × 2 grid (shells 2 big, 4 small; buttons 3 big, 1 small).

**(b) Line Plot Bench, model `line-plot-v1`.** A picture ruler under a number line. *A made-up model: authored lengths, no real measuring.* Whole inches (Seedling): ten feathers, 3 to 9 inches with 8 empty; **Show the ruler under the line** aligns the scale. Eighths (Sapling): twelve snail shells, 3/8 to 1 1/8 inch, with 1/4, 1/2 and 3/4 labels shown on the eighth marks; tapping a stack shows "how many" and "how long" separately, and **Add them up** lists each X's length.

**(c) Table Desk, model `table-desk-v1`.** A morning timetable for the upstream market boat (three boats × four stops, orchard landing to the high ford, legs of 30, 15 and 50 minutes, all before noon; Vadoalto is half a day by boat in spec 120, so it is not a stop) and a two-way table of where 13 finds were picked up (mill yard 4/1/2, footbridge 2/3/1, with totals). **Row finger** and **Column finger** highlight and meet; **Count on** draws jumps on a minute line to the next o'clock; **Hide a box** blanks one cell for C4. *A made-up model: the boats run the same times every day.*

**(d) Chart Wall, model `chart-wall-v1`.** Finds by street on a bar chart where each square is 5 (35, 20, 45, 15), the same data as a pictogram (one sack = 10, half sacks) and Market Square's tally. Two-color bars by decade from the story ledger and Nuria's table. Keeper's Desk: twenty authored morning readings (0 to 36 cm) dropped into classes with inequality labels (counts 4, 6, 6, 4) and drawn as touching bars; a boundary reading asks where it belongs. *A made-up model: invented counts; flood counts from spec 120's ledger.*

**(e) River Line, model `river-line-v1`.** One spring week at the mill post, 8 a.m. (4, 6, 12, 26, 32, 20, 14 cm; grid 2 cm). The child plots points, joins them in time order, and uses **Trace down and across**; **Estimate between** drops a dashed marker labeled as an estimate. A **When a line doesn't fit** card joins kinds of finds and asks whether that shows change. *A made-up model: invented readings, below the ledger's 40 cm flood cut.*

**(f) Gazette Press, model `gazette-pie-v1`.** The 36-neighbor poll. The child takes each count's fraction of the whole, multiplies by 360, and draws the slice on a protractor ring; **Check the circle** reports any gap or overlap and points to the slice that differs. **Fix the axis** shows Bramble's bar chart from 8 and from 0 side by side. **Which chart?** sorts five data cards to five chart types. *A made-up model: nobody real was asked.*

**(g) Compare Shelf, model `compare-v1`.** Two rows of trip cards. **Line them up**, **Mark the middle** and **Show the range** draw on a shared number line; **Hide the 76** grays the outlier and redraws mean and median markers so the child sees which moves. *A made-up model: invented trips; the 76 is the flooded-ford day.*

**(h) Scatter Desk, model `scatter-v1`.** A made-up river chart table (not the old Keeper's: spec 120's page 4 says Hollis had only their own post). The child plots dots on labeled axes, then drags a straight line; **Count above and below** tallies dots each side (the outlier marked and left out by choice, with its note shown); **Shade the data range** marks 4 to 40 mm, and a read-off outside it shows Wren Halloway's caution card. An umbrellas card shows the same rise against market umbrellas. *A made-up model: invented days; the ice jam is story.*

No timers, scores, streaks, medals or locks. Every bench is open from the first visit. Feedback names the row, box, bar or slice that differs and never says "wrong".
