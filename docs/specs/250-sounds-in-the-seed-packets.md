Strand E1 ([six-act strands](../six-act-strands.md)) asks for explicit sound-letter blending (**E1-I**) and alternative graphemes (**E1-II**). Nothing teaches a letter team.

A child learns: two letters can make one sound (sh, ch, th, ck, tch, ng, ph, wh); two vowels can team up (ai, ay, ee, ea, oa, oe, oo, oi, oy), and some teams have two sounds; an e at the end is quiet but changes the vowel before it, with an honest shelf where it doesn't; some teams sound two ways and the sentence decides (ow, ie, final y), and others share a sound (ou and ow, ew and ue, aw and au); r after a vowel changes it, and er, ir and ur sound alike; igh is three letters for one sound; and some sounds have other spellings (soft c and g, ge, dge, kn, gn, wr, s in treasure), read by trying the usual sound, then the other.

## The knowledge

Sourced in the [audit](../source-audits/250-sounds-in-the-seed-packets.md), mainly English Appendix 1 (DfE, Year 1 and Year 2) and Common Core RF.1.3 and RF.2.3.

**Declared reading model (adults only).** General American (GA), rhotic, broad phonemic, no length marks, as in brief 55: short /æ ɛ ɪ ɑ ʌ ʊ/; /i/, /u/, /eɪ/, /oʊ/, /aɪ/, /aʊ/, /ɔɪ/, /ju/; /ɔ/ kept distinct in aw and au (unmerged), with the merged /ɑ/ named as equally standard and never contrasted in an item; stressed /ɝ/ for er, ir, ur; /ɑr/, /ɔr/ (horse and hoarse merged), /ɛr/ (Mary, marry, merry merged), /ɪr/; /w/ for wh (wine and whine merged); /u/ after j, r, l and after t, d, n (tube); /ju/ after c, f, m, p (cube, few). A human linguist and the eventual speaker settle a narrower description before any recording, as brief 55 requires.

**Consonant digraphs (Year 1; RF.1.3a).** sh /ʃ/ (ship, shed, fish); ch /tʃ/ (chip, chick, chest); th /θ/ (thin, bath) and /ð/ (this); ck /k/ straight after a single short vowel letter (duck); tch /tʃ/ after a single vowel letter (patch; exceptions rich, which, much, such); ng /ŋ/ (ring); ph /f/ (photo); wh /w/ (wheat; some speakers keep /hw/); k for /k/ before e, i, y (kit). ch is also /k/ (school) and /ʃ/ (chef) in other words, which are not used here.

**Vowel digraphs (Year 1; RF.1.3c, RF.2.3b).** ai and ay /eɪ/ (ai virtually never word-final; ay at the end); ee /i/; ea /i/ (leaf) and /ɛ/ (bread, thread); oa and oe /oʊ/ (boat, hoe); oo /u/ (moon) and /ʊ/ (book, wood); oi and oy /ɔɪ/. Words whose oo varies across American speakers (room, roof, root, broom) are avoided.

**Split digraphs (Year 1; RF.1.3c, RF.2.3a).** a-e /eɪ/, e-e /i/ (these), i-e /aɪ/, o-e /oʊ/, u-e /ju/ (cube) and /u/ (June). Minimal pairs: cap, cape; kit, kite; pin, pine; cub, cube; hop, hope; mat, mate. Exceptions shown: give, have, come (the e changes nothing). "Magic e" and "the vowel says its name" are not used: letter names stay separate from sounds.

**Diphthongs and complex vowels (Year 1).** ow /aʊ/ (cow) and /oʊ/ (snow), so sow and row read two ways; ou /aʊ/ (out, house; you, soup and touch differ and are not used); ew and ue /u/ (grew, blue) and /ju/ (few); ie /aɪ/ (pie) and /i/ (field); aw and au /ɔ/ (saw, haul); final y /i/ (happy) and /aɪ/ (my), with a stated "often": short words often take /aɪ/, longer words /i/, but not always (July).

**R-controlled vowels (Year 1).** ar /ɑr/ (car, barn), or and ore /ɔr/ (fork, more), er, ir, ur /ɝ/ (her, bird, turn); unstressed er is /ɚ/ (better). In GA the /r/ is said; in non-rhotic varieties (much of England, Australia, some older US Eastern and Southern speech) these are long vowels without /r/ unless a vowel follows. Either way the vowel changes (cat, cart), which is what the child text says.

**Trigraphs (Year 1).** igh /aɪ/ (night) is three letters for one phoneme in every variety. Appendix 1 lists air, ear (/ɪə/ and /ɛə/) and are (/ɛə/) with RP's non-rhotic vowels; in GA they are /ɛr/, /ɪr/, /ɛr/, a vowel plus a pronounced /r/, so this pack calls them teams with r. ear also reads /ɝ/ (learn), used only as a distractor.

**Alternative spellings for known sounds (Year 2; RF.2.3e).** /dʒ/: g often (not always) before e, i, y (giant, ginger), -ge after other sounds (cage, sage), -dge straight after a short vowel (badge, hedge); hard g stays in get, give, girl. /s/: c before e, i, y (city, celery). /n/: kn and gn (knot, knife, gnat, gnaw); /r/: wr (wren, write); the k, g and w were sounded long ago (Appendix 1; the w of wr fell silent in the 1600s). /ʒ/: s in treasure, measure. Reading strategy: try the usual sound, then the other, and keep the word that fits ("set for variability", Steacy and colleagues 2022).

## The game or activity

Every screen: *People say some words in different ways. All of them are fine.* Until reviewed audio exists, also: *No sounds play yet. A grown-up reads the words and sounds aloud.* The grown-up reads prompts, never the answer choices. No score, timer, lock or mastery label. Taps only. Pictures name the target; they do not stand in for reading the word.

- **(a) Two Letters, One Sound (Sprout, Seedling; first slice: sh, ch, th).** *Find the team* (tap the pair that makes one sound), *Name the packet* (picture and three word cards) and *Fix the packet* (a washed-off team; three tiles for three different sounds, so one fits; a real-word slip such as bash gets picture-specific feedback). Later packets add ck, tch, ng, ph, wh and k.
- **(b) Vowel Teams (Seedling).** Team trays for ai, ay, ee, oa, oe, oi, oy; two-way shelves for oo and ea.
- **(c) The End e (Seedling).** Slide an e onto cap, kit, pin, cub, hop, mat and read the new word; an odd-ones shelf (give, have, come).
- **(d) Two-Way Teams (Sapling).** ow trays; sentence packets where the sentence decides (sow in spring; a row of beans); ie and final-y trays; same-sound pairs (out and cow, grew and blue, saw and haul).
- **(e) Teams with r, Teams of Three (Seedling, Sapling).** r packets; the er, ir, ur tray; igh, air, ear, are; an ear shelf (hear or bear).
- **(f) The Odd Packets (Sapling).** Soft c and g with an always-shown honest shelf (get, give, girl); quiet letters; dge and s in treasure; the try-both card.
