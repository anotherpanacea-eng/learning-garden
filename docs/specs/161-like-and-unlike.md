## The knowledge

Sourced in the [audit](../source-audits/161-like-and-unlike.md); unconfirmed claims were cut.

**One backbone, many shapes.** Every life cycle has a start (seed, birth or hatching), growth, making young, and the end of that life; reproduction restarts the ring. **Plant (broad bean):** seed, seedling, flowering plant, pods with seeds; the bean plant dies back after seeding. **Mammal (sheep):** a ewe carries a lamb for about 147 days (roughly five months, range about 142 to 152); lambs are born live, usually stand within the first hour, drink milk and grow without changing form. **Bird (hen):** eggs hatch after about 21 days of incubation; the chick grows into a hen or rooster without changing form. **Amphibian (common frog):** frogspawn in late winter or spring, timing set by warmth; tadpoles live in water and breathe with gills; back legs grow first, then front legs, lungs develop, the tail is absorbed, and a froglet leaves the water, a change of form called metamorphosis. Pyrenean common frogs breed up to about 2,500 m (one survey of 325 waters); spawning follows the thaw, early April near 1,500 m to June near 2,330 m.

**Like, but not the same.** Young are the same kind as their parents and share many features, yet are not identical to either parent or to each other. In most white sheep breeds white wool is dominant and black recessive, so two white sheep that both carry black can have a black lamb, though it is uncommon (genes not named on screen). Apple seeds do not grow true (draft 149), so named apples are grafted. Variation within a kind is real, not measuring error, and can be shown as a spread on a dot plot.

**Born with or picked up.** Inherited: a sheep's natural wool color, a plant's usual flower color. Picked up from surroundings: a shorn fleece, a painted farm mark, a bean long and pale in dim light (spec 41), smaller apples on a shaded tree. Both: an animal's size (inherited makeup and food). Picked-up traits are not passed on: a shorn ewe's lamb is born woolly. The cleanest evidence uses genetically identical organisms in different places: two trees grafted from one tree onto the same kind of rootstock are clones above the graft (rootstock alone changes vigor and yield), so a difference between them comes from where they grow. Older depth: bigleaf hydrangea flowers are blue on acid soil (aluminum available) and pink on alkaline soil.

**Fit for the place.** An adaptation is an inherited feature that helps a living thing survive and have young where it lives. Valley examples: a frog's webbed back feet for swimming; the alpine marmot (introduced to the Pyrenees from 1948; it had vanished there after the Ice Age) hibernates in its burrow for about half the year or more, when food is under snow; a heron's long legs for wading. A feature is a good fit only for a place: a pale moth is hidden on pale, lichen-covered bark and easy to spot on sooty bark. An animal does not choose its adaptations. Wild sheep such as the Asiatic mouflon, the domestic sheep's likely ancestor, shed their coat; many breeds were bred by people for fleece that keeps growing and must be shorn: selection by people, a contrast with natural selection. In one 2015 common-garden study, tadpoles from a higher Pyrenean population grew faster than lower-population tadpoles in the same conditions, which suggests an inherited fit to a short summer (a single study, framed so).

**Natural selection: the peppered moth (Keeper's Desk).** Peppered moths (*Biston betularia*) are usually pale and speckled. A dark form (carbonaria) was recorded in Manchester in 1848 (a specimen had been collected by 1811); by 1895 about 98% of Manchester's peppered moths were dark. Soot had killed pale lichens and darkened tree bark, and birds found the moths that stood out. After clean-air laws (the Clean Air Act of 1956, later 1968), bark lightened and the dark form declined. Bernard Kettlewell's release experiments (1953, 1955, Birmingham and Dorset) supported bird predation but were criticized for method (moth densities, daytime release, trunk placement). Michael Majerus's redesign released 4,864 moths over six years, mostly onto branches; birds took more dark moths. Colleagues published it after his 2009 death (Cook et al. 2012, *Biology Letters*). In 2016 van't Hof and colleagues (*Nature*) found the dark form came from one change in a gene called *cortex*, dated to about 1819. A 2018 Exeter study confirmed pale moths are better hidden on lichen, to bird eyes. Four conditions: variation, inheritance, more young than survive, better-suited variants leaving more young. No moth changed; the population's share changed. British peppered moths have one generation a year, so 1848 to 1895 is about 47 generations.

**Evolution, plainly.** Evolution is change in the inherited features of populations over many generations; natural selection is one cause. Charles Darwin and Alfred Russel Wallace described natural selection in papers read at the Linnean Society on 1 July 1858, and Darwin's *On the Origin of Species* appeared on 24 November 1859. Evidence includes fossils (spec 106, draft 142), comparisons of living things, and changes watched happening, such as the peppered moth. Stated as science, without debate framing.

## The game or activity

Every screen: *These are simplified pictures. Real animals and plants vary.* Models say "A made-up model." No score, timer, lock or mastery label. Drags have tap equivalents.

- **(a) Four Rings.** Place 4-5 stage cards on each of four rings (bean, sheep, hen, frog). **Line them up:** each ring unrolls under a backbone strip (*start, grow, make young, life ends*). **Shape or size?** sorts the four. A read-only butterfly ring links to `#living/stages`. The make-young arrow is dashed and labeled *offspring*, as in spec 65.
- **(b) Whose Young?** Match six young to six adults (feedback names the shared feature); **Spot one difference** on three pairs (three highlighted spots each); the kitten line-up: tap two kittens and a difference.
- **(c) Bean Pod Count.** Twenty made-up pods (14 to 21 cm); each tap measures one and drops a dot on a dot plot. Then mode, range and a fraction.
- **(d) Born With or Picked Up?** Eight cards into three trays with per-card feedback; **Pass it on?** for each picked-up card. **Twig Twins:** two *Lantern Red* trees grafted from one tree onto one kind of rootstock, sunny top and shady low corner; count a made-up harvest (120 and 75); choose why they differ.
- **(e) Fit for the Place.** Tap a feature, then the problem it solves (three valley creatures). **Wrong place:** move a frog to dry rock and a pale moth to sooty bark.
- **(f) The Moth on the Mill Wall (Keeper's Desk).** **Bird's-eye Bark**, a made-up model: ten pale and ten dark moths on pale bark; the child taps the eight a bird would notice first (no clock). The authored run then plays the same whatever was tapped: birds find 2 pale and 6 dark; each moth left leaves two young like itself (16 pale, 8 dark). **Sooty bark** runs the mirror (8 pale, 16 dark). Then the real file (dated cards, Kettlewell and his critics, Majerus's redesign, the *cortex* finding), the **Four conditions** checklist, and **Two stories**: Bramble's "they changed themselves" against "more dark ones survived," and the evidence that separates them.
