## Purpose

Spec 49's Act VI asks the explorer to "propose and revise a plan"; spec 120 folds that into chapter 2, *Compare routes and keep a plan*, as the town readies its first exchange with Vadoalto. Nothing yet teaches the habits that job needs: criteria and limits, one yardstick for several ideas, test, improve, and a report that says how sure.

In pinned Marble's **Scientific Inquiry** domain (30 topics, 4 with scoped references), the design topics and the upper evidence and reporting topics have no reference and no claim in any spec or draft. A child comes to understand that:

1. A sketch is a thinking tool that shows how a **shape** helps a thing work. It need not look finished.
2. Two designs are compared by testing them **the same way**. Each usually has strengths and weaknesses.
3. A design problem is defined before building: **need**, **who it is for**, **criteria** (checkable success) and **constraints** (limits).
4. Choosing starts with **at least three ideas**. Constraints are pass or fail; criteria go on one shared grid. The best choice is often a trade-off, then an improvement.
5. Results can suggest a **new prediction**; a prediction beyond what was tested must be **tested too**.
6. A report says what you found, what caused it and **how sure** you are. An odd result is kept and explained.
7. Evidence can **support** an idea, **refute** it or **not bear on it**, and some evidence is far stronger than other evidence.

**Builds on, never re-teaches:** the **Fair Test Bench** (`#workshop/fair`, specs 56 and 60: one factor, confounded pairs, three trials, claims apart from observations) and draft **spec 155**, *The Question Bench* (questions, change/measure/keep, repeats and spread, changing your mind). Station 5 reuses 155's change/measure/keep tokens; station 6 its spread language. Drafts **124** and **170** (budgeted bank, Plan Grid) are revisits; this pack uses calm, everyday problems and never touches the Act IV flood plan. `#workshop/repeats` (spec 71) is a programming loop, not repeated testing.

## The knowledge

Facts are checked in the [source audit](../source-audits/176-design-table.md).

**Design words.** US standards for ages about 8 to 11 (NGSS 3-5-ETS1-1) ask learners to define a design problem with **criteria for success** and **constraints on materials, time or cost**. Many classrooms use the Museum of Science, Boston's Engineering is Elementary cycle, **Ask, Imagine, Plan, Create, Improve**, which repeats. The pack uses those step names.

**Shape and job.** A sloped lid sheds rain; a wide base resists tipping; ridges give grip. Everyday worked examples; no physics is claimed beyond "this shape helps because...".

**Mary Anderson's window wiper (true).** On a snowy streetcar ride in New York (soon after 1900; the year is undocumented) she saw the driver lean out to clear the glass. She designed a hand lever inside that swung a rubber blade across the window, patented 10 November 1903 (US 743,801). Wipers became standard car equipment years later; she did not profit. Reading it as a defined need is labeled ours.

**The Wrights' wind tunnel (true).** Their 1901 glider made only about a third of the lift that published tables predicted. That autumn they built a box wind tunnel with a fan and tested about 200 small model wings the same way. The data showed the long-used Smeaton coefficient was too high and Lilienthal's table did not fit their wings; they designed the 1902 glider from their own numbers. On 17 December 1903 Orville flew the powered Flyer 120 feet (about 37 m) in 12 seconds. Fair comparison, and evidence that **refuted** a trusted number.

**Sharing what you found (true).** *Philosophical Transactions*, begun by the Royal Society in London in March 1665, is one of the first science journals and is still published (not "oldest continuous"). Peer review came much later; the card says so.

**Made-up models** (every screen says *A made-up model*):

- **Two bags** (station 2). Ten letters, same watering can, same time, three tries each. Damp letters: oilcloth satchel 0, 1, 0 (1 of 30); willow basket 3, 4, 2 (9 of 30). Parcels that fit: satchel 6, basket 10.
- **The Ways crate** (stations 3, 4). Criteria: 12 letters dry in the rain test; 6 jars through the bump test; 5 kg or less empty. Constraints: shed materials; 10 coins or less; ready in 3 days.

| Idea | Coins | Days | Dry (of 12) | Jars | Empty weight |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Willow basket, oilcloth lid | 7 | 2 | 11 | 6 | 3 kg |
| Oak chest | 12 | 3 | 12 | 8 | 9 kg |
| Oilcloth sack | 3 | 1 | 12 | 2 | 1 kg |
| Yoke with two baskets | 9 | 3 | 8 | 8 | 6 kg |

The chest fails the coin limit. Of the rest, none meets every must: basket and sack meet two of three (the basket misses by one letter, the sack by four jars), the yoke one. An oilcloth letter packet (1 coin, 8 in all) improves the basket; the re-test keeps 12 of 12 dry.
- **Paper footbridge** (station 5). Coins held, middle of three tries: 0 folds 2, 4 folds 6, 8 folds 10, 12 folds 14: one more coin per fold. Predicting 6 folds (between tested values) gives 8; the test agrees. Predicting 20 (beyond them) gives 22; the test gives 15, as tight pleats squash. The leveling point is invented.
- **Trust** (station 6). Pip tries the improved crate once: 12 dry, "perfect!". Wren tries five times: 12, 12, 12, 11, 12 (59 of 60). An extra try kept 6 dry, noted "lid left open"; it stays in the record and is repeated.

## The game or activity

- **1 Shape for a job.** Three problem cards (post box lid lets rain in; café lantern tips; hen-house ramp is slippery). Tap a card, a shape tile per part (flat or sloped lid; narrow or wide base; smooth or ridged ramp), then **How does it help?** and a label chip. The sketch board shows plain labeled lines: *A sketch shows the idea. It doesn't have to look finished.* Shapes swap freely.
- **2 Two bags, one test.** **Run the rain test** reveals one authored try per bag at a time (three each, no fourth). The table fills; **Show totals**. **Strength** and **Weakness** tags; a frame ("Better for ___ because the data showed ___") from chips; **Improve it** offers three changes. A side door shows an unfair test (basket watered twice as long).
- **3 Write the brief.** Four slots: **Need**, **Who it's for**, **Musts**, **Limits**. Statement tiles are tapped into slots; vague tiles ("strong", "nice") flip to **Can we check it?** with checkable rewrites and number chips. The brief is read back. Authoring by tile assembly, not free writing; the spec claims no more.
- **4 Three ideas, one yardstick.** Four idea cards pass a **constraint gate** (coins, days, shed: pass or fail lights). Survivors go on a grid against three musts (met, missed by a little, missed). **Pick the most promising** needs a because-chip pointing at cells. **Improve it** adds the packet, checks the coin limit, and re-tests.
- **5 Test, predict, test again.** A supplied dot chart (folds across, coins up; no plotting). **Make a prediction**: folds (6, 10 or 20), a value chip, a reason chip. **Plan the check** uses 155's tokens. **Run the check** reveals the authored result. A band marks the tested range; predictions beyond it say "less sure: test it".
- **6 How sure? Whose evidence?** A trust scale (**not sure yet**, **fairly sure**, **very sure**) moved only by reason chips (repeats, close results, how many tried, odd result explained), never a score. Then an evidence board: Bramble's claim and four cards sorted **Supports**, **Refutes**, **Doesn't bear on it**; kept cards go on a **strength ladder** (someone said so; one try; repeated fair tests). Keeper's Desk adds the Wright card.
- **7 The report board.** Six slots: **Our question**, **What we did**, **What we found**, **What it means**, **How sure we are**, **What we'd try next**, each with three tiles (one fits). The report is read aloud and pinned; telling it to a grown-up is speech. Nothing typed.

## Saved work

Optional root `designTable`, per explorer, absent until the first explicit keep. Sketch for review:

```
designTable: {
  version: 1,
  sketches: [{ id, source, problem: code, parts: [code], labels: [code], at }],      // latest 12
  briefs:   [{ id, source, need: code, user: code, musts: [code], limits: [code], at }], // 12
  picks:    [{ id, source, grid: code, pick: code, because: [code], improve?: code, at }], // 12
  predictions: [{ id, source, caseId: code, guess: number, reason: code,
                  shownResult: number, at }],                                         // 12
  reports:  [{ id, source, about: code, tiles: [code], trust: code, at }],           // 12
  attempts: [{ itemId, source, choice?: code, entry?: number,
               support: 'none'|'hint'|'example', skipped: boolean, at }]             // 60
}
```

- Codes, numbers and stable ids only; **no free text is stored**. "Say why" answers and told reports are spoken. `source` freezes the content version.
- Support, skips and attempts are separate; nothing derives mastery or a score. A prediction is saved with its shown result and never edited.
- Never rewrite a saved entry on load. An unknown version or malformed entry stays exact, exportable and read-only; one unreadable item never closes the table.
- Saves follow an explicit action; at a cap the oldest readable entry is replaced only after a disclosed notice; unreadable entries are never evicted. Export, import, reset and explorer switching use shared behavior.
