## A useful new language action

**One home: `#loft/bridges`.** A small link from Story Loft and a secondary Post Office link both reach this same bench; there is no second Post route or duplicate notebook. The helpers need a short note about a community scene. Start with two complete supplied sentence strips; keep their words and scene in view, join their strips with **and / because / but**, reverse which clause comes first, separate them again, and hear how the changed sentence frames the relation. A purpose is an invitation to compare choices, never a one-right-answer selector or completion gate.

This changes the relationship inside a sentence, rather than choosing one of several complete prewritten messages or answering a question about grammar. The output ribbon is assembled from two fixed clause strips and one word bridge; moving the bridge updates the sentence and a separately labeled, supplied meaning note. Two true source sentences do not make every joined causal claim true. Unexpected trials stay available without changing the source scene or being celebrated as supported facts. A reader may imagine another context; the app interprets only these finite scenes.

## Three original, fixed source scenes

A heading **“Our scene”** and the complete scene below stay visible above every trial, including after swapping clauses and during speech. Names, events and reasons are invented for this activity, not real observations or claims about earlier saved town events. The supplied purpose is shown directly below it. Every case is open from the start; default `supper`, original A→B order, no bridge.

### `supper` · A note about supper

**Our scene:** Rain was falling. The helpers moved supper indoors to keep rain off the tables. Moving supper did not change the weather.

**Purpose:** Tell the gathering helpers why the supper moved.

**Sentence A:** The helpers moved supper indoors. **Sentence B:** Rain was falling.

### `lamp` · A note about the lamp

**Our scene:** The lamp was lit. A thick screen blocked its light, so the room stayed dark. The helpers had expected the lamp to brighten the room. This note does not say why anyone lit it.

**Purpose:** Tell the stage helpers what was unexpected about the room.

**Sentence A:** The room stayed dark. **Sentence B:** The lamp was lit.

### `jobs` · A note about two jobs

**Our scene:** Pip folded the banner while Dot packed the cups. The jobs happened at the same time. Neither job caused the other. The note gives no reason to expect the same job from both helpers.

**Purpose:** Tell the next helpers about the two jobs, without inventing a cause.

**Sentence A:** Pip folded the banner. **Sentence B:** Dot packed the cups.

## Exact finite trials and meaning notes

The following 18 outputs and notes are the complete joined content. Do not generate interpretations, infer new reasons, run a language model, or grade the learner. In the six separated states, show A and B as complete sentences in the current order and say **“Two sentences. No joining word is selected.”** Use the exact American child-facing punctuation below: a supplied comma before and/but between these independent clauses, no comma before the final because clause. We provide punctuation and sentence casing; this is not punctuation practice or an assertion that every writer must punctuate every short join this way. Proper names stay capitalized.

The output label is always **“Sentence we are trying”**, never “What happened.” The accompanying heading is **“Meaning to notice”**; its note appears immediately with every joined trial, without needing Check, Reveal or success. It says what the sentence would communicate and what this scene supports. Speech always includes this boundary, including for reversed/unsupported because.

## Teaching and exact controls

Opening: **“Two sentences can tell different kinds of connections. Keep our scene in view. Change the joining word and listen to what your sentence would say.”**

A freely open **“What do these joining words do?”** disclosure gives this original teaching: **“And adds one idea to another. Because gives the second part as a reason for the first. But sets up a contrast: something different or unexpected. A joining word changes what we ask our reader to connect. It does not change what happened in our scene. But does not make either idea untrue.”** This describes these middle-bridge trials; it is not a complete account of conjunctions, all sentence positions or all kinds of reasons. Optional word note: **“A clause is a group of words with a verb. Here each starting strip is a complete sentence.”** No grammar terminology is required to use the bench.

- **Choose a scene**: three native buttons, always available. Choosing a different case starts it at A→B and separate sentences; selecting the current case is a no-op. Adjacent copy: **“Another scene starts its two separate sentences. This visit is not saved.”**
- **and / because / but**: three fixed-position, equally styled native buttons under **“Choose a joining word”**, with `aria-pressed`. Choosing one visibly places its labeled bridge between the clause strips and shows the exact joined trial/note. Selecting the already active word is a no-op. These are operations, not three answer choices; do not shuffle them or imply that the leftmost is correct.
- **Swap sentence order**: swaps the two whole clause strips, retains the bridge and updates the exact output/note. Swapping twice restores the original order. Before a bridge is selected it swaps the two complete sentences. Visible A/B identifiers move with the clauses. No drag-only interaction.
- **Separate sentences**: removes the bridge, restores each strip's supplied capital/start and full stop in current order, and clears the meaning note. It does not reset order or change the source scene. Disabled only when already separate, with adjacent text explaining that state.
- **Undo last change**: one previous `{order,bridge}` snapshot for this case, not a history. Each real bridge/order/separate change replaces it; one Undo restores it and clears Undo. Disabled before a change and after Undo. Switching case clears Undo. No confirmation dialog or retained event list.
- **Hear this trial**: reads **“Our scene: [complete context]. Sentence we are trying: [current exact trial, or the current two full sentences]. Meaning to notice: [current exact note, or the separate-state sentence].”** No automatic speech or audio recording. Existing local speech is optional, not evidence of listening, pronunciation or independent reading.

There is no Check, right/wrong color, score, progress, reward, answer input, Keep, print/export, automatic next case, free-text evaluation or current-purpose completion flag. The purpose remains visible and the learner may prefer a different supported emphasis. Optional Talk together: **“Which words came from our scene? What connection does your joining word add? What would need to change in the scene for another reading to fit?”** A human's discussion is unrecorded.
