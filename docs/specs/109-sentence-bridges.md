## A useful new language action

**One home: `#loft/bridges`.** A small link from Story Loft and a secondary Post Office link both reach this same bench; there is no second Post route or duplicate notebook. The helpers need a short note about a community scene. Start with two complete supplied sentence strips; keep their words and scene in view, join their strips with **and / because / but**, reverse which clause comes first, separate them again, and hear how the changed sentence frames the relation. A purpose is an invitation to compare choices, never a one-right-answer selector or completion gate.

This changes the relationship inside a sentence, rather than choosing one of several complete prewritten messages or answering a question about grammar. The output ribbon is assembled from two fixed clause strips and one word bridge; moving the bridge updates the sentence and a separately labeled, supplied meaning note. Two true source sentences do not make every joined causal claim true. Unexpected trials stay available without changing the source scene or being celebrated as supported facts. A reader may imagine another context; the app interprets only these finite scenes.

## Three original, fixed source scenes

A heading **“Our scene”** and the complete scene below stay visible above every trial, including after swapping clauses and during speech. Names, events and reasons are invented for this activity, not real observations or claims about earlier saved town events. The supplied purpose is shown directly below it. Every case is open from the start; default `supper`, original A→B order, no bridge.

### `supper` · A note about supper

**Our scene:** Rain was falling. The helpers moved supper indoors to keep rain off the tables. Moving supper did not change the weather.

**Purpose:** Tell the gathering helpers why the supper moved.

**Sentence A:** The helpers moved supper indoors. **Sentence B:** Rain was falling.

### `lamp` · A note about the lamp

**Our scene:** The lamp was lit. A thick screen blocked its light, so the room was dark. The helpers had expected the lamp to brighten the room. This note does not say why anyone lit it.

**Purpose:** Tell the stage helpers what was unexpected about the room.

**Sentence A:** The room was dark. **Sentence B:** The lamp was lit.

### `jobs` · A note about two jobs

**Our scene:** Pip folded the banner while Dot packed the cups. The jobs happened at the same time. Neither job caused the other. The note gives no reason to expect the same job from both helpers.

**Purpose:** Tell the next helpers about the two jobs, without inventing a cause.

**Sentence A:** Pip folded the banner. **Sentence B:** Dot packed the cups.

## Exact finite trials and meaning notes

The following 18 outputs and notes are the complete joined content. Do not generate interpretations, infer new reasons, run a language model, or grade the learner. In the six separated states, show A and B as complete sentences in the current order and say **“Two sentences. No joining word is selected.”** Use the exact American child-facing punctuation below: a supplied comma before and/but between these independent clauses, no comma before the final because clause. We provide punctuation and sentence casing; this is not punctuation practice or an assertion that every writer must punctuate every short join this way. Proper names stay capitalized.

Strip rendering is exact and independent of the output paragraph. In a separated state each strip shows its original complete sentence, with original initial capitalization and one final full stop, in the current order; no bridge or joining comma appears. In a joined state remove that single final full stop from each strip. The first strip retains its original capitalization. In the second strip change only an initial **The → the** or **Rain → rain**; preserve **Pip** and **Dot** and every other character. Put no comma or full stop inside either joined strip: the selected word occupies the intervening bridge, and the one correctly punctuated complete joined sentence appears only in the output paragraph using the exact table. A/B labels identify original source sentences and move with them; they are not spoken as sentence words. Separating restores both original full stops/capitals even after a swap. The separated output paragraph repeats the two original complete sentences in their current order, separated by one space. Hear reads this output once, not duplicate strip text.

The output label is always **“Sentence we are trying”**, never “What happened.” The accompanying heading is **“Meaning to notice”**; its note appears immediately with every joined trial, without needing Check, Reveal or success. It says what the sentence would communicate and what this scene supports. Speech always includes this boundary, including for reversed/unsupported because.

| `supper` | BA / **but** | Rain was falling, but the helpers moved supper indoors. | This offers the indoor move as a contrast with the rain. Our scene gives a reason for moving, not a surprise about it. What expectation might your speaker have? |

| `jobs` | BA / **but** | Dot packed the cups, but Pip folded the banner. | This contrasts Dot’s job with Pip’s. Both jobs are supplied; the speaker chooses to make the difference stand out. |

## Teaching and exact controls

Opening: **“Two sentences can tell different kinds of connections. Keep our scene in view. Change the joining word and listen to what your sentence would say.”**

A freely open **“What do these joining words do?”** disclosure gives this original teaching: **“And adds one idea to another. Because gives the second part as a reason for the first. But sets up a contrast: something different or unexpected. A joining word changes what we ask our reader to connect. It does not change what happened in our scene. But does not make either idea untrue.”** This describes these middle-bridge trials; it is not a complete account of conjunctions, all sentence positions or all kinds of reasons. Optional word note: **“Here each starting strip has a subject and a verb.”** No grammar terminology is required to use the bench.

- **Choose a scene**: three native buttons labeled **“Supper”**, **“Lamp”** and **“Two jobs”**, always available. The current case has `aria-pressed="true"`, a thick outline and a visible check mark; the native pressed state announces selection once; the visible check mark is hidden from the accessible name. Choosing a different case starts it at A→B and separate sentences; selecting the current case is a no-op. Adjacent copy: **“Another scene starts its two separate sentences. This visit is not saved.”**
- **and / because / but**: three fixed-position, equally styled native buttons under **“Choose a joining word”**, with `aria-pressed`, a thick outline and a visible check mark on the selected word; the native pressed state announces selection once; the visible check mark is hidden from the accessible name. Choosing one visibly places its labeled bridge between the clause strips and shows the exact joined trial/note. Selecting the already active word is a no-op. These are operations, not three answer choices; do not shuffle them or imply that the leftmost is correct.
- **Swap sentence order**: swaps the two whole clause strips, retains the bridge and updates the exact output/note. Swapping twice restores the original order. Before a bridge is selected it swaps the two complete sentences. Visible A/B identifiers move with the clauses. No drag-only interaction.
- **Separate sentences**: removes the bridge, restores each strip's supplied capital/start and full stop in current order, and replaces the joined meaning note with the separate-state sentence. It does not reset order or change the source scene. Disabled only when already separate. Its adjacent disabled explanation is exactly **“The sentences are already separate.”** Otherwise this explanation is absent.
- **Undo last change**: one previous `{order,bridge}` snapshot for this case, not a history. Each real bridge/order/separate change replaces it; one Undo restores it and clears Undo. Disabled before a change and after Undo, with the adjacent exact explanation **“No change to undo.”** Otherwise this explanation is absent. Switching case clears Undo. No confirmation dialog or retained event list.
- **Hear this trial**: reads **“Our scene: [complete context]. Purpose: [exact supplied purpose]. Sentence we are trying: [current exact trial, or the current two full sentences]. Meaning to notice: [current exact note, or the separate-state sentence].”** No automatic speech or audio recording. Existing local speech is optional, not evidence of listening, pronunciation or independent reading.

After every real word/order/separate/Undo/case edit, deliberately focus the following target after rendering: word choice → the activated word button; swap → **Swap sentence order**; Separate → the **Sentence we are trying** heading (the activated Separate button is now disabled); Undo → that same heading (Undo is now disabled); different scene → that scene’s still-enabled selected button. Give the trial heading a stable `id="bridges-trial-title"` and `tabindex="-1"`; do not focus a hidden, disabled or empty status element. Hear does not rerender and retains focus. Selecting the current word/case or another no-op performs no render, status change or focus move. Native teaching-disclosure toggles retain summary focus and do not rebuild the page.

Provide one initially empty, persistent `role="status" aria-live="polite"` area. Following the edit render, set exactly: word choice → **“Joining word: {word}. Trial and meaning updated.”**; swap → **“Sentence order: {A then B|B then A}. Trial and meaning updated.”**; Separate → **“Two sentences. No joining word is selected.”**; Undo → **“Last change undone.”**, then **“Sentence order: {A then B|B then A}.”**, then one of **“Joining word: and.”**, **“Joining word: because.”**, **“Joining word: but.”** or **“Two sentences. No joining word is selected.”**, joined with single spaces; different scene → **“{A note about supper|A note about the lamp|A note about two jobs}. Two sentences. No joining word is selected.”** Substitute only those finite current values, not source-derived or learner text. The exact full current meaning note remains visible beside the trial. Do not autoplay speech or use the live region to announce a score.

There is no Check, right/wrong color, score, progress, reward, answer input, Keep, print/export, automatic next case, free-text evaluation or current-purpose completion flag. The purpose remains visible and the learner may prefer a different supported emphasis. Optional Talk together: **“Which words came from our scene? What connection does your joining word add? What would need to change in the scene for another reading to fit?”** A human's discussion is unrecorded.
