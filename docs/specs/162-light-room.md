## The knowledge

**Seeing needs light.** Dark means no light. In a sealed, unlit room you see nothing however long you wait; eyes adjust to dim light, never to none.

**Sources and reflectors.** A source makes its own light: the Sun, a flame, a lamp, a firefly. Everything else is seen by light bouncing off it. The Moon reflects sunlight; its gray rock returns only about a tenth (albedo about 0.12, roughly like old asphalt), scattered, not like a mirror. Moonlight takes about 1.3 s to arrive (spec 141 owns light time). A cat's eyes glow in a beam because a reflective layer behind the retina (*tapetum lucidum*) sends light back out; cats make no light.

**How we see.** Light goes source → object → eye. Many, Euclid and Ptolemy among them, held that eyes send out rays. Ibn al-Haytham (born Basra about 965, died Cairo about 1040) argued in his *Book of Optics* (*Kitab al-Manazir*) that light enters the eye; one argument: very bright light injures the eye. It also describes the camera obscura, and in Latin it shaped European optics. Historians date it to about 1011-1021 or about 1028-1038; the game teaches the range.

**Reflection.** All surfaces reflect some light. Smooth ones (mirror, still water) send a beam off in one direction, so you see a picture; rough ones scatter it, so you see the surface. Dark surfaces absorb more. **Law of reflection** (Sapling up): the angle between incoming beam and *normal* (the line at right angles to the mirror) equals the angle between outgoing beam and normal.

**Straight lines and mirrors.** Light passes three holed cards only when the holes line up. Shadow Theatre already shows straight paths grazing a card's edges; this spec uses it and never re-teaches shadow size. A ray is a drawn line with an arrow, not a thin piece of light. A mirror at 45 degrees turns a beam through a right angle. A **periscope** has two parallel 45-degree mirrors; light leaves going the way it came, lower down. Johannes Hevelius described one, his "polemoscope", in *Selenographia* (1647); the Royal Society archive dates the invention to the 1630s.

**Refraction, a simple picture.** Light travels about 300,000 km/s in air, 225,000 km/s in water and 200,000 km/s in glass. Crossing at a slant, it changes direction at the boundary and then goes straight. A straw looks broken because light from the underwater part changes direction leaving the water. A curved **lens** bends light toward one spot. Augustin Fresnel's stepped lens, first lit at the Cordouan lighthouse (Gironde estuary) on 25 July 1823, gathered a lamp's light into a far-seen beam.

**White light and color.** A prism spreads white light because each color changes direction by a slightly different amount (violet most, red least). Isaac Newton worked on prisms from early 1666, sent his "New Theory about Light and Colours" to the Royal Society in February 1672, and published *Opticks* in 1704. In his *experimentum crucis* one color from a first prism went through a second: it bent again but kept its color, so prisms separate colors rather than make them. His early lectures treated the spectrum as endless gradations; by 1704 he named seven, adding orange and indigo partly to match the half steps of a musical scale. The spectrum is smooth; seven is a choice.

**Rainbows.** Sunlight from behind you, raindrops in front: light bends into each drop, reflects off the back and bends out, spreading the colors. The main bow is about 42 degrees from the point opposite the Sun (where your head's shadow falls), red outside. Its top is about 42 degrees minus the Sun's height, so from level ground no main bow shows when the Sun is above about 42 degrees. Theodoric of Freiberg modeled this with a water-filled globe (about 1304-1310; sources differ) and Descartes worked out its angle in 1637.

**The eye, simplified.** Light passes the cornea and the **pupil**, a hole the **iris** makes smaller in bright light and bigger in dim; the cornea and **lens** focus it on the **retina**, which makes signals; the **optic nerve** carries them to the **brain**, which makes what we see. The retinal picture is upside down; vision scientists say the brain never needs it "flipped". A generic drawn eye and the miller's cat only; never the child's eyes.

**The Sun rule** (fixed, not a model result). Never look at the Sun: not with sunglasses, not through thin cloud, never through binoculars, a telescope, a camera or a lens. It can permanently damage the retina, often without pain. To watch the Sun, look at its pinhole picture on paper, back to the Sun, with a grown-up. The rule shows wherever the Sun is drawn and is never a slider, model or "what if".

## The game or activity

**(a) Dark Box.** A lidded box with a lamp switch, holding a lantern, spoon, red apple, mirror, Dot's spare hat. Lamp off: the window stays black; **Wait** changes nothing and says why. Lamp on: objects appear. A **Sort** tray: *Makes its own light* / *Light bounces off it* (Sun, Moon, candle flame, firefly, lantern, mirror, spoon, cat's eyes); any card can move. *A simplified picture: a real room is rarely perfectly dark.*

**(b) Eye Path board.** Scenes: lamp-apple-eye; Sun-Moon-eye; lamp-cat-Bramble's eye. The child places arrows between pieces. The object shows as *seen* only when arrows run source → object → eye. An arrow out of an eye draws faintly with *Nothing comes out of your eyes.* A chain stopping at the object lights it but leaves the eye blank. A **Smooth or rough** toggle swaps the apple for a mirror tile: one neat arrow versus a fan.

**(c) Ray Bench, model `light-grid-v1`.** A 7 × 5 grid from above (R3 is a side view). One fixed lamp sends a beam along a row or column. At most three pieces: **mirror** `/` or `\` (turns the beam 90 degrees); **paper** (beam stops, square glows softly, target unlit); **card** (blocks). The star lights when the beam reaches it; readout: squares traveled. Layouts (columns 1-7 west to east, rows 1-5 south to north; `/` turns east to north, `\` east to south): **R1** lamp (1,2) east, star (5,5), solution `/` at (5,2), 7 squares; **R2** lamp (1,2) east, cupboard cards (4,1) and (4,2), star (7,4), solution `/` at (2,2) and (2,4), 8 squares; **R3** Pip's periscope, light entering (1,5) going east, crowd cards (3,1)-(3,3), eye (7,1), solution `\` at (5,5) and (5,1), 10 squares; **R4** R1 with paper at (5,2), star dark; **R5** open stage. Other working solutions count. **Angle Table** (Sapling up): one mirror turning in 15-degree steps, dashed normal, fixed incoming beam, outgoing beam drawn by the rule with both angles labeled. *A simplified picture: perfect flat mirrors, beams as lines, squares not to scale.*

**(d) Bend and Split.** **Straw:** *Show the light's path* draws one ray from the underwater end changing direction at the surface; *Empty the glass* shows the straw straight. **Lens:** lamp, lens, card; slide the card to the smallest spot; lamp only, with a no-Sun caption. **Prism:** white beam to a band; *Second prism (one color)* bends one color without changing it. Colors carry names and numbers, never hue alone.

**(e) Eye Card.** A generic cross-section with tap-to-reveal parts and a brain icon. **Lamp bright or dim** shows the drawn cat's pupils narrow or widen. No camera, no mirror, no question about the child's eyes.

No timers, scores, streaks, medals or locks; all benches open.
