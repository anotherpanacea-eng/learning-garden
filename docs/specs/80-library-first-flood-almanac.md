## Learner turn and boundary

At Lucie’s existing bookstall, offer **Three dates beside the Seine** as an openly selectable, text-only Paris story. An adult may read it aloud; the learner may also use existing optional local narration, sentence focus, and reading-support controls. The learner chooses any of three passage lengths or accepts the existing gentle suggestion. Length is a choice, not a measured level or gate. The passage remains visible while the learner selects the sentence giving the river’s highest-mark date, then arranges three undated event cards by rereading dates in that passage. Variant 3 mentions March before January so mention order cannot stand in for date order. Both questions may be skipped or revisited under existing Paris behavior. The two smaller fictional examples, hints, explanations, and talk prompts are exactly the reviewed authored pack.

This is one nonfiction reading opportunity, not an independent-reading, listening, fluency, comprehension-gain, flood-science, safety, or historical-mastery claim. A reported adult read-aloud, requested audio, sentence focus, helper use, attempts, and familiarity retain their existing separate meanings. Talk remains ungraded and unsaved. The real 1910 Paris facts and the imagined Learning Garden town are distinguished in **every** passage length; no image or historical voice is added.

The bundled Marble pin and existing attribution remain unchanged. The pack’s partial sequence reference is only for relating three dated pieces of nonfiction information; retelling and central-message work remain unobserved. No new historical or educational-efficacy assertion is introduced by the installation.
