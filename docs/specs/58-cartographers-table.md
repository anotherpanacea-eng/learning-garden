## Purpose and boundary

After the storm, the town is preparing a weekly market. Its old signs name destinations, but visitors also need to know where things are and how far a route travels. Make an original practice map, locate its landmarks with two coordinates, then construct and compare two walking routes. This is a distinct loop: the Post Office jumps between named places, Orchard Gathering finds covered grid cells, and Number Garden arrays model quantities. Here the learner authors landmark positions at **intersections** and measures **travelled line segments**. Explain that Orchard numbers identify covered plots (cells), while these pairs identify meeting points of grid lines; five intervals lie between six successive points0–5. No real location, GPS, physical map measurement, journey time, money, obstacles or movement speed is involved.

Use a first-quadrant grid of 36 points: x and y are integers0–5 inclusive. The lower-left point is(0,0), the origin. First go across to the right(x), then up(y). Axes and every coordinate remain labelled; north is up **on this practice map**, east right, south down, west left. Four original redundant symbol/name keys: Post Office, Café, Garden and Bridge. One mark per point; select a mark, then tap a point to place/move it. Refuse an occupied point with a specific message and keep both original positions. Tapping a mark’s own point is a harmless no-op. Landmarks may be unplaced. The map is a fictional schematic, not a reconstruction of the current named-place flight map.

Each adjacent horizontal or vertical segment represents **one model metre**. Count intervals travelled, not dots visited or the area of cells. The equal segment length is supplied, not measured from device pixels. Going out and back travels two metres even though the endpoint returns to its start. Diagonals are outside this slice. Coordinates give position from the origin; route length measures travel from the chosen start. A shorter screen drawing or a smaller endpoint displacement does not automatically mean fewer travelled intervals.
