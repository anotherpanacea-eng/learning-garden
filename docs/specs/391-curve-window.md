## Known mathematical family and exact representation

Omit k=0, show “+k” for positive k and “−abs(k)” for negative k, never an ambiguous doubled minus. Written notation also says “multiply (x−h) by itself, then add k”; square is not double. Format a negative h with parentheses (“x−(−1)”), and narrate it as subtracting a negative number; no ambiguous doubled minus. All real inputs belong to the rule; a finite graph WINDOW is shown. Do not draw straight joins as if they were the known continuous curve. A finite sample table is labelled selected inputs, not all possible inputs.

The model fold is x=h and vertex is(h,k). For k<0 there are two real zeroes; k=0 gives one distinct zero touching the x-axis; k>0 gives no real zeroes anywhere because a real square cannot be negative. The known rule justifies that last statement; the visible vertex helps illustrate it, “none of our dots is zero” does not. Say “zero” or “x-intercept” includes touching; do not call every zero a crossing.

Reference intervals use integer comparisons, not Math.sqrt rounding. For k<0, choose integer a with a*a≤16*(-k)<(a+1)*(a+1); if a*a==16*(-k), both roots are exact quarter coordinates4*h±a (in this family exact roots are integers). Otherwise reference bands are [4*h-a-1,4*h-a] and [4*h+a,4*h+a+1], interpreted in quarters. Show endpoint function values and signs using exact fractions. They bracket each root in a monotone side of this known continuous quadratic. Do not claim the root equals the midpoint, introduce unjustified decimal digits, or claim sign-change is necessary for touching. For k=0 display[h,h], encoded as {lo4:4*h,hi4:4*h}; for k>0 show no root markers and the square/negative-number reasoning. Counts are model descriptions, not grades for the learner's idea/bands.
