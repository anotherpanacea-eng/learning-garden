## Sources and historical boundaries

A: selected public-domain wording from the Declaration of Independence, adopted July4,1776, using the National Archives transcription of the Stone engraving. B: selected public-domain wording from the January13,1777 petition to the Massachusetts Council and House of Representatives, signed by Black petitioners including Prince Hall; use Document11 of Grant Stanton's2024 transcription collection (PDF pages16–17, numbered from1), cross-checked against the Massachusetts Archives item and Gilder Lehrman's transcript. Do NOT mix in Document10, a document Stanton labels a draft and the MHS citation labels a manuscript copy, with materially different spelling. These are selected excerpts, not the complete documents and not transcriptions made by this app.

Keep wording/capitalization/punctuation from the named transcription; normalize line wrapping only. Each excerpt is a continuous fragment and labelled as selected, with no invisible internal omission or silent modernization. Context, glossary and guided paraphrases are original present-day prose, visually/textually labelled apart from primary words. Quotation marks in a child's response are their own text; no automatic quotation or interpretation grading. No document scan, portrait, modern editorial prose, external image/audio or contemporary source's page design is incorporated.

Do not claim that all signers were enslaved, that Hall was the only author, or that a disputed signer's biography/military service is established. Do not give a signatory count. The packet demonstrates Black political agency through the actual petition's argument. It never treats enslaved people as passive recipients of someone else's ideas. Similar words do not prove direct copying or that every petitioner read the Declaration. A request is not an enacted law or a record that its authors gained freedom. These two excerpts do not establish every person's beliefs or later legal outcomes. Retain the petition's actual qualified request concerning children after age21 rather than rewriting it as immediate freedom for every child.

No roleplay of enslavement, defense of enslavement, guessing a real author's emotions or questions about a child's family/body/identity. Brief factual notice: these documents discuss people being held in slavery and arguing for freedom. Optional read-together mode and existing Hear remain available. The packet does not become a full Revolution course or legal account of abolition.
