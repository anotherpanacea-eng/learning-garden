## Geometry and two sources

Free frame: for i0..n−1, mathematical raw vertex v_i=(s cos(90°+360°i/n),sin(90°+360°i/n)); s=1 for regular,1.6 for stretched. Stretching is an invertible affine map, preserving strict convexity and vertex order while making these frames nonregular. It does not mean every side or every angle differs. No dragging constraint solver needed. In either free shape, subtract the arithmetic vertex centroid, use one positive uniform scale so max vertex radius is160 for Large or120 for Small, then rotate by shared r and invert y to screen center(320,280), viewBox640×560. Changing size or rotation changes no angle relationships.

Panel frame raw vertices in counterclockwise order are (0,0),(width,0),(width,height),(0,height). Consume only the actual frozen **copy** width/height/caseTitle from captureCrossingPanel; no original-dimension substitution, title lookup or free fallback. Use the same centroid normalization with radius160. n=4; all four angles90°; regular iff width=height. A non-square rectangle is irregular **but still has equal angles**. Dividing any polygon total by n gives an average; it equals every corner only when equal angles are known. Regularity guarantees that condition but is not necessary: the panel's four given right angles establish it independently. This clarifies182's shorthand “regular only”; do not teach that an irregular polygon can never have equal angles or that all irregular corners must differ.

## Split the frame

Let a be the active chosen vertex, called **A** in the drawing. Vertices are accessed cyclically. There are n−3 possible diagonals from A to vertices a+2 through a+n−2, in that order. Show the first k, where k is active draw. Chosen adjacent sides already exist and are not diagonals. Completed triangles for k<n−3 are (a,a+j,a+j+1) for j1..k; the remaining unsplit polygon has at least four corners. At k=n−3 show all n−2 triangles, j1..n−2. This also handles n3,k0: one triangle, no diagonal. Use faint alternating triangle fills without equality claims, solid boundary, dashed geometric diagonals and a distinct thinner dotted label leader.

Before the final diagonal, report **k+1 pieces; some are not triangles yet** and **k completed triangles**. At completion report **n−2 triangles; each contributes180°; total=(n−2)×180°**. Do not call all partial regions triangles. A pentagon progresses: k0,0 triangles+one pentagon; k1,1 triangle+one quadrilateral; k2,3 triangles. Undo removes the latest diagonal. Choosing another corner starts a fresh fan at that corner. A different fan proves the same total; it is not new mastery evidence.

General reason at completion: all n−3 non-neighbor vertices join to A without crossing or leaving a strictly convex outline. Each new diagonal adds one region:1+(n−3)=n−2. Those regions are triangles. They fill the original polygon with no gaps/overlap; their corner angles partition the original boundary angles without introducing an interior vertex. Therefore the polygon angle sum is(n−2)×180°, whatever its size or regularity. This is a geometric argument, not a formula inferred only from examples. Explicitly distinguish the sum of all corners from one corner.

## One corner

Keep the outline, no fan/marker. Highlight only the selected interior corner at A. A regular free frame has equal sides AND equal angles, so each interior is total÷n. Show **T° ÷ n** exactly, where T=(n−2)180; if T/n is an integer also show that integer degree value. For n7 and11 retain the exact division expression, explicitly **This division is exact; no rounded angle is shown.** Do not append a decimal. For stretched free frames show symbolic selected angle a_{index+1}, the total and **Total÷n is an average here. The corners are not all equal, so it does not tell us this corner.** No approximate angle value. The default stretched square is a rhombus with unequal angles: equal sides alone do not make a regular polygon. Changing selected corner changes the highlighted angle, never the source facts. Panel: each angle90° independently of equal side lengths, and360°÷4=90° agrees with that fact; explicitly distinguish square/regular from non-square rectangle/irregular. No automatic wrong/right response.

For the selected original interior sector, incoming vector u=V_i−V_(i−1), outgoing v=V_(i+1)−V_i. In mathematical coordinates exterior e=atan2(cross(u,v),dot(u,v)) in(0,180°); interior I=180°−e. If incoming bearing is alpha, use exterior startalpha/sweep e and interior startalpha+e/sweep I, then shared rotation and y inversion. For original interior radius rho=min(24,0.35×shorter incident side,0.7×minimum distance to a nonincident edge's supporting line). Strict convexity makes that distance positive. Known90° cases (panel and regular n4) use a right-square with leg rho/sqrt2; other selected corners use an arc. Never widen a tiny sector for visibility.

## Walk the edge

Walk uses the active chosen vertex as start. At step0 the marker is at A, pointing along its outgoing side; no counted turn, angle wedge or incoming reference extension is shown. At each next step j1..n, move to vertex(a+j)%n and show the left turn from the incoming side's forward direction to the outgoing side. Include a30-unit incoming continuation past the vertex and a30-unit outgoing direction ray, with exterior start/sweep as above and radius min(24,0.35×shorter incident side). No opaque point label at the walking corner: marker, directional arrow and HTML **Corner {index+1}; turn j of n** identify it without hiding the short extension. All mathematical endpoints stay within200 units of center. Leaders/tags may use outer stage corners; no equality inferred from colour. Only Split/One corner has a point label A, placed55 drawing units beyond its chosen vertex along the outward ray from the centroid, with a leader to the vertex. Walk has no point-text labels. Angle/turn names are plain text a1/e1 etc., not raw TeX or underscore-brace syntax.

At stepn the marker returns to A and its outgoing direction matches the start. Caption explicitly distinguishes one accumulated360° turn from zero change of final direction. For any allowed convex frame every turn is positive. Each inside corner plus its adjacent outside turn is180°. Thus sum of outside turns=n×180°−(n−2)×180°=360°. This derivation supports the visible full lap; repeatedly observing an arrow is not the proof. Never sum reflex outside regions or claim the positive-turn rule applies to concave/crossed shapes.

Regular frame: each turn=360°÷n, integer shortcut when exact. Stretched: use symbolic turn e_{index+1} and j-of-n counter, never a rounded running degree sum. Panel: each turn90°; at a partial step j one may state j×90° accumulated, and at4 the total360°. At0 say zero turns counted, not a zero-size geometric angle. Backward walk reduces j, with current explanation/marker recomputed; no timer, automatic lap or multiple loops.
