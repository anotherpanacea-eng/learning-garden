## Learner experience and bounded model

Pip is planning a gathering call. **Can you change its pitch without changing the signal size?** Main tablet surface pairs a large computer-signal picture with two independent44px control groups: **Lower pitch / Higher pitch** and **Smaller signal / Bigger signal**. Exactly four steady sine settings: frequency220 or440Hz, relative amplitude1 or2. Steady waveform is `u(t)=A×sin(2πft)`, phase0 at the left rising from zero. Marker labels round to at most two decimals, trim trailing decimal zeros and normalize negative zero to0; screen negatives use “−”, accessible labels use “minus”. Fixed graph time window `1/110` second (about9.09ms), so exactly2 or4 cycles. Graph is the **steady part of a computer signal**, not a drawing of air's trajectory, speaker displacement, a measured pressure trace or the complete0.6s playback envelope. Both axes/scales stay fixed for all settings and held/current comparisons: time0…1/110s, labelled relative-signal ticks−2,0,+2 within a fixed drawn domain−2.4…+2.4, leaving headroom around size-2 peaks. Never auto-normalize the height or compare unequal windows. Frequency changes cycle count without changing signal peak; size changes peak without changing count. Explain: faster repetition raises a simple tone's pitch; larger amplitude at the same frequency/setup is the loudness comparison. Equal amplitude at different frequencies need not sound equally loud, especially on small speakers. No decibel, calibrated output level or doubled-perceived-loudness claim.

Visit-local model: `{pitch:'low'|'high',size:1|2,held:null|{pitch,size},marker:false|true,tick:0…32}`; defaultlow,1,noheld,markerfalse,tick0. Replace held setting is explicit, Clear held setting removes it. Changing pitch/size never starts audio. Start again resets all model fields. Held/current figures have equal dimensions/scales, distinct titles and independent labels; no overlay/superposition. Plain comparison copy identifies whether frequency, amplitude, both or neither changed, never right/wrong. This small compare loop may be revisited indefinitely; it does not adapt or infer mastery.
