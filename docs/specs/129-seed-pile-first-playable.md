## A playable sitting

Setup offers the three rules, a pile5–30 (13 default), who starts, and Bramble sleepy/paying attention/has a plan. Default paying attention. A child can begin immediately. One compact board shows seeds, count, rule, whose turn and last moves. Three large Take buttons remove a positive allowed number. Then **Let Bramble play** advances her move explicitly, at the child's pace. No autoplay/timer; old/stale controls cannot advance another turn or explorer. A finished game announces the actual winner under its rule, then offers Play again, change the game, optional note, or leave. New games need no completed old game. No win/loss totals.

Sleepy chooses uniformly from legal takes. Paying attention takes an immediate winning move if any, else uniform legal take. In last-loses the immediate winning move leaves exactly1 seed; if none exists, a random legal take may intentionally be a losing move, including taking the last seed. Has a plan chooses the first ascending legal take that leaves the opponent a losing position; if none, take1. Winning/losing is finite backward reasoning: zero is losing in normal play and winning in last-loses; positive n wins if some legal take leaves a losing position. Taking the last seed loses in last-loses. Stored moves include Bramble's actual random choices, so resume never rolls again.

Optional **Bramble explains** is a separate view with one worked thought per Next, concrete seed diagrams, Back, and Return to game; normal play teaches end positions1–3, then4, then restoring multiples of4. Changed rules explicitly explain multiples of3 or one-more-than-multiples-of4. A Try it button opens an in-memory practice board against her plan. It never replaces or archives the waiting current game; Return to game restores that same waiting board, and leaving/reloading clears only the temporary practice. Explanation is recorded as supplied strategy/familiarity, never independent reasoning. No correctness test or outcome implies skill mastery.
