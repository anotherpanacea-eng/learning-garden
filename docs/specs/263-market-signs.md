A child learns: some spellings come in families you learn together (walk, talk; Monday, honey; after w, warm and word); two words can share a sound and differ in meaning (here, hear); a prefix adds a meaning at the front and usually changes no letter, so mis + spell keeps both s's; -ly, -ous and -ation each have a few root changes, and some words just keep their own spelling; long word lists are easier in families and with helpers; and a dictionary is searched by first letters, guide words and ABC order, with another start tried when a word is not where you looked.

## The knowledge

Sourced in the [audit](../source-audits/263-market-signs.md), mainly English Appendix 1 (DfE) and Common Core L.2-L.3.

**Other spellings for known sounds (Year 2).** /ɔː/ spelled a before l and ll (all, ball, call, walk, talk, always); /ʌ/ spelled o (other, nothing, Monday); /iː/ spelled -ey (key, donkey, valley; plurals just add -s); /ɒ/ spelled a after w and qu (want, watch, wander, squash); /ɜː/ spelled or after w (word, work, world, worm); /ɔː/ spelled ar after w (war, warm, towards); -tion (station, fiction, motion, section). Homophones are chosen by meaning (here/hear, to/too/two, be/bee, blue/blew are on the Year 2 list). On screen these are families with anchor words, never sounds. Rules stated by sound with no letter equivalent (-dge after a short vowel, c before e, i, y for /s/) are left out rather than restated wrongly. *Money* and *honey* are not on the Year 2 example list but have the same o; *swarm* and *swan* follow the after-w patterns.

**Prefixes (Years 3-4).** Appendix 1: most prefixes join with no change in spelling (in- is the exception, and is not taught here); dis- and mis- have negative meanings, mis + spell = misspell; re- means again or back; super- above; anti- against; auto- self or own. Pre- (before) is in the Marble description, not the Appendix. Common Core L.2.4b asks for the meaning of a known prefix on a known word; RF.3.3a for the most common prefixes. Taught joins: reopen, rewrite, preheat, prepay, preview, unsold, unwrapped, unnamed, unnoticed, dislike, disobey, misprint, mislabel, misspell, antifreeze, autograph, autopilot, superstar, supermarket. Double letters at a join (misspell, unnamed, unnoticed, dissatisfied) follow from "no change".

**Suffixes (Years 3-4).** -ly is added to an adjective to make an adverb and usually just added (sadly, completely, usually, finally); a consonant + y root of more than one syllable changes y to i (happily); a root ending -le changes -le to -ly (gently, simply); -ic adds -ally (basically, dramatically), except *publicly*; *truly*, *duly*, *wholly* are exceptions. On screen: "a consonant and le", which excludes *pale* and *sole*, and "usually" for y, which covers *shyly*. -ous: usual vowel-suffix rules (famous, various, glorious), some with no obvious root (enormous, jealous), *courageous* keeps its e. -ation is added to verbs to make nouns, rules still apply (information, preparation, admiration, invitation). Less common spellings: y in myth and gym, ou in young and touch, -sure (measure, treasure), -ture (picture, nature). *Explanation* and *exclamation*, which drop a letter, are not used. British *humour* drops its u before -ous; US *humor* needs no change (grown-up note only).

**Word list (Years 3-4).** The statutory list is a reference only (owner question 2). Words are grouped by shared letters: eigh (eight, eighth, weight, height), ough (enough, though, although, thought, through), ear (early, earth, heard, learn), double letters (address, appear, different, difficult, possible, opposite); helpers for separate, business, island and believe. British forms on the list (centre, favourite) are not used.

**Dictionary use.** Lower key stage 2: use the first two or three letters of a word to check its spelling in a dictionary; upper key stage 2: three or four letters. Common Core L.2.2e and L.3.2g: consult beginning dictionaries to check and correct spellings. Guide words are the first and last entries on a page. The Stall Dictionary's five pages and 34 definitions are newly written; ordering is checked by script.

## The lesson

## The game or activity

Every screen: *Spelling rules usually work. A dictionary has the last word.* No score, timer, lock or mastery label. Taps only; every drag has a tap equivalent. Choices are shown, not read aloud.

- **(a) The Pattern Stall (Sprout, Seedling).** Gapped signs and five families (ball, Monday, key, after-w, -tion); pick the family, then fill the gap. A two-words shelf puts here, hear; two, too, to; bee, be; blue, blew into picture sentences.
- **(b) The Prefix Signs (Seedling, Sapling; first slice).** Eight prefix tiles and an authored list of nineteen joins, each with its meaning. Prefix and root show in two colors, and a doubled letter glows at the join. Any other pair is "a word to wonder about", with a check-the-dictionary invitation.
- **(c) The Ending Signs (Seedling to Keeper's Desk).** -ly, -ous and -ation on 22 roots, with four tools (just add, drop the e, y to i, le to ly) and, at Keeper's Desk, ic to ically. A no-root shelf (enormous, jealous) and an odd-ones shelf (truly, publicly, courageous).
- **(d) The Word-List Crate (Seedling, Sapling).** Sort list words into eigh, ough, ear and double-letter crates; four helper cards for tricky parts.
- **(e) The Stall Dictionary (Seedling to Keeper's Desk; first slice).** A made-up five-page dictionary with guide words. Find a page; put words in ABC order by the second, third or fourth letter; check one of Pip's spellings letter by letter; try another start (kn, wr).
