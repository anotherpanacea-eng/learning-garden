## The knowledge

Sourced in the [audit](../source-audits/255-puppets-sentence-cards.md). Child text uses American terms; the grown-up panel names England's.

**Nouns and verbs.** The Common Core asks kindergartners to use frequently occurring nouns and verbs (L.K.1b). England's grammar glossary warns that "naming word" and "doing word" are starting points, not tests: prepositions can name places, nouns can name actions, and many verbs (is, likes, knew) name states. Its surer tests: most nouns fit the frame "The ___ matters"; a verb can usually have a present and a past tense. So Sprout uses naming and doing words inside a line, and Sapling shows one word in two jobs (*Pip waves* against *Pip gives a wave*).

**Determiners.** England's glossary defines a determiner as specifying a noun as known or unknown; the articles *the* (definite) and *a* or *an* (indefinite) are the commonest, with demonstratives (this, those) and possessives (my, your). Common Core L.1.1h: use determiners (articles, demonstratives). This and these usually point near the speaker, that and those farther away; this and that are singular, these and those plural.

**A or an.** England's Year 3 content: a or an by whether the next word begins with a consonant or a vowel (a rock, an open box); its glossary defines consonants and vowels as speech sounds. Merriam-Webster and other dictionaries say the sound, not the letter, decides: an hour (silent h), a unicorn and a uniform (a "y" sound), an umbrella. The article goes with the very next word, so a big apple but an old drum. Before an h in a weakly stressed syllable both are used ("a historic" or "an historic"), and *herb* differs between American and British speech; the pack keeps those to the grown-up panel and uses only *hour* as its silent-h word.

**Pronouns.** Common Core L.1.1d: personal, possessive and indefinite pronouns (I, me, my; they, them, their; anyone, everything). England's glossary shows pronouns standing in for a noun once it is known; it lists possessives (my, your) among determiners and calls *his* in *his obituary* a possessive pronoun that "may act as a determiner." So this pack, like the DfE and the Common Core, calls my, his, her and their possessive pronouns; many grammars call them possessive determiners before a noun, which the grown-up panel says, and no item asks for the term. I is the subject form and me the object form in Standard English; the cover-the-other-name test checks a pair. Each character's own pronouns come from spec 120; Puff takes none, so the name repeats. Singular *they* for a known person takes a plural verb (*Chicago*: "takes a plural verb").

**Agreement.** Common Core L.1.1c: singular and plural nouns with matching verbs (He hops; We hop). In Standard English present tense, he, she, it, one named thing and indefinite pronouns such as everyone take -s; I, you, we and they (including singular they) do not. England's glossary notes the subject can determine the verb's form (I am, you are) and that Standard English is a small set of forms, not an accent. Many varieties of English differ here: the eWAVE atlas codes zero third-person -s ("she show up") as present in 53 of 77 varieties and -s on all persons ("I sees") in 27. Wheeler and Swords' contrastive approach treats such forms as patterns, not errors, which is the owner's default.

**Expanded noun phrases.** England's Year 2: expanded noun phrases for description and specification (the blue butterfly, plain flour). A noun phrase has a noun as its head; modifiers before it can be adjectives or nouns (*paper* in *the tiny paper moon*). Size before color is the usual order and is simply supplied.

**Year 2 terms.** England's Year 2 terminology for pupils: noun, noun phrase; statement, question, exclamation, command; compound, suffix; adjective, adverb, verb; tense (past, present); apostrophe, comma. Its glossary separates sentence jobs by the main clause's form (*Be my friend!* a command, *What a good friend you are!* an exclamation) but does not define the exclamation further. The narrow rule, that an exclamation begins with What or How, is the Standards and Testing Agency's key stage 1 grammar test framework (§4.4.2). Everyday usage and the Common Core's "exclamatory sentences" (L.1.1j) are wider and include commands such as *Sit down now!* So card 15 calls What-or-How "a strict rule" used here, and G2, G3 and the sentence-jobs sort, which file a ! command as a command, say on screen that many people would call it an exclamation.

## The game or activity

Every screen: *Any line you build is a real try. Pip will read it just as it stands.* No score, timer, lock or mastery label. Drags have tap equivalents; cards are at least 44 px and every picture has a text name.

- **(a) The Line Builder (first slice).** Five puppet cards (rabbit, kitten, dragon, owl, unicorn) and five doing cards (hops, sings, waves, sleeps, spins). Sprout cards carry *the*; Seedling adds an a / an / the box. All 25 lines and any article are accepted; Pip reads exactly what was built (voiced audio for the 75 article lines), and authored notes cover a owl and an before a consonant sound. **Naming or doing?** taps each word's job. **Two-line scene:** meet a puppet, then use the.
- **(b) This or That?** A two-by-two picture grid (near or far, one or many) for Pip's speech bubble.
- **(c) Say It First.** Twelve voiced word cards: vowel or consonant sound, then a or an; then **Fix the poster** from scene 5.
- **(d) The Program.** **Name Swap** on a seven-line program (Puff's line takes no swap), then **He hops, we hop**: seven lines, each a choice of two verb forms. The bench says it practices the printed-book way and never marks anyone's speech.
- **(e) Grow the Noun.** Size and color cards before *dragon*, *drum* or *hat* (2 × 3 = 6 phrases each, size first); the picture changes to match. **Which one?** shows item F2.
- **(f) The Word Box.** Label noun, verb, adjective and adverb in one line; sort four lines by job; toggle present and past; item G6 matches compound, suffix, apostrophe and comma to examples, linked to the sibling benches.

Each kept item stores `ver`, the authored item-version id that fixes its wording, choice ids and shown order (any edit to an item gets a new `ver`), and `outcome`, the code kept when it was answered. A saved item is restored from its own `ver`, `answer` and `outcome`; if that `ver` is no longer today's, it shows as kept and is never re-marked. Validate the ids, counts and codes the save holds against structural bounds (at most 12 kept lines, tap indexes inside the kept line's own words) and the save's own entries; never require today's card lists, item lists or counts, and never check against today's wording or answer key. A missing item reads as absent. `supported` is set when the item was answered after opening the stuck ladder and is never merged with an independent attempt. An unknown `v` or unreadable entry stays exact and exportable, only that bench turns read-only, and one bad item never closes the page. Export, import, reset and save-off follow the shared store and `prepareProgressImport` limits. `loft`, `puppetNotices` and the bridges root are untouched.
