# A message for a reader

Second curriculum spiral increment, 2026-10-20. Connect the Post Office’s literal instructions to the Story Loft’s audience and revision. A small optional rehearsal sits beside both existing games; it never changes a pending parcel, route, rival round, show draft or shelf.

## Experience and learning purpose

Three freely selectable fictional storm-recovery notes ask for clarification of **which object**, **which destination**, and **which action**. Each has a pictured scene, accessible scene description, short visible request/purpose and an ambiguous note. The missing information cannot be recovered from the note alone. The reader asks a specific question; the learner selects a relevant scene clue and a supplied revised sentence. Alternate choices produce their literal meaning and a request-specific response, without penalties or access locks. A clue selection receives feedback about whether it answers this question, separately from whether the sentence communicates the request. Multiple useful descriptions are welcome; this finite activity does not judge general writing.

Post Office entry opens `#post/messages`; Story Loft entry opens `#loft/messages`. Case chooser → inspect source/reader question → revise/test → before/after playback. The original-note playback pauses and asks the specific missing-information question; it never silently chooses one possible interpretation. Back and next controls preserve every case’s exact work. No compulsory successful answer is required to enter the Loft or choose another case. A fresh note revisits the same habit with a different missing detail. The source scene and original note remain visible on every activity page.

Optional original message wording is kept exactly, at most 600 characters, and labelled “Read this with a person.” The finite runner **only runs supplied cards**; it never pretends to understand free text. Every supplied-card test and playback visibly names the exact supplied sentence being run. A separate “Read my words together” action opens an original-message reading view that presents the exact text to the accompanying adult; editing it cannot appear to change the finite runner. It receives no automatic correctness, spelling or independence label.

The Post Office invitation carries the current note and revised card/free text to a **separate Story Loft reader rehearsal**. In two manual scene turns, a puppet has a reason to ask, then responds to the message. The child may give the sender a reason and the reader a reply, in exact optional text (400 characters each), and edit the message/replay to discuss the effect. Supplied example dialogue is available as support and cannot overwrite authored text. The stage labels supplied literal actions versus human-read original words. The ordinary 1–12-scene Story Loft remains a separate link, with its existing draft and shelf intact; this slice does not automatically insert, shelve or evict shows.

Each case has independent Post Office work and Loft work. First crossing clones a snapshot, including selected clue, sentence, exact optional original text and support/read-report context. On a later crossing reopen the existing destination draft without replacing it, explicitly explaining that choice. The original source is never overwritten by destination edits. Direct Loft entry starts its own draft. A carried sentence is familiar, not a new independent success. Playback itself adds no attempt; testing an explicit supplied revision records only that specific selection and current context. Authored reasons/replies are unscored. Reading report unknown / read myself / read together is optional; narration and help remain separate sticky flags even if the report later changes. No grade, reading level, mastery, reward, timer, required length or automated recommendation.
