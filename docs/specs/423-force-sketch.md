## 3. Teaching encounter and exact finite physics packet

Always label diagrams as model diagrams: **“Invented model, with only the described interactions.”** Force measures use N, newtons, a unit of force; no mass or weight conversion. Up/down/left/right describe page directions. Model objects translate without rotation or deformation. The reference includes every force described by this finite model on the selected object, not a claim that real objects have no other influences.

### Separate worked teaching example

Five manually stepped pages, Previous/Next and **Back to my sketch**; no automatic advance. Example is a block initially still on a horizontal support, weight4N down and support4N up. It uses its own example constants, not practice state, choices or keys. All teaching steps available directly through Previous/Next.

- **T1 Choose one object.** “A force is a push or pull that one object gives another. Our object is the block. We draw forces acting on the block.” Show block with no arrows.
- **T2 Add a force.** Earth→block4N down: “Earth pulls the block downward. This is its weight. The arrow points the way the force pushes or pulls. A longer arrow means a bigger force. Arrows start at the edge of the object’s box; they do not show where it is touched. N means newtons, a unit of force.” Show one arrow; “Contact forces need touching; gravity and magnetic forces can act across a gap.”
- **T3 Compare both forces.** Add support→block4N up: “The support pushes up. These forces act on the same block. They balance: their net force is zero. The net force is all the forces on one object added with their directions. A still block stays still; an already steadily moving object would keep the same velocity—its speed and direction—while the net force stays zero.” Explain bigger arrow=stronger force; equal opposing sizes cancel; a leftover points in the direction velocity changes. Friction: “When a different box slides left on a rough floor, the floor’s friction pushes right. It is a contact force.”
- **T4 Follow one interaction.** Show support→block4N up and block→support4N down in separate partial views: “One interaction, two objects. The block also pushes down on the support with an equal force at the same time. These two arrows do not balance the forces on the block: they act on different objects. Earth’s pull and the support’s push balance on the block, but are different interactions.” Partial-view warning from §5.
- **T5 Try another setting.** Two repelling model magnets across a gap. “Each magnet pushes the other away. Equal opposite partners act on different objects. To predict one object’s motion, collect the forces on that object.” No numeric practice key, answer or copied diagram. Back returns exact prior scene/body/page/draft.

Practice flow: **P1 Build**, **P2 Compare and revise**, **P3 inspect source net**, **P4 Motion**, **P5 Interaction pair**. These are open pages/actions, not required stages. P1’s hint: **“Name the object receiving each force. Choose its direction and size from the scene clues.”** Teach link supplies the different worked block. - `arrows`:0..6 objects `{forceId,direction,amount}`; unique candidateforce IDs, cardinal direction, amount one of2/4/6. Including other receivers, absent candidates or wrong size/direction is a **valid proposal**, not an accepted physical diagram.
- `tool`:`{forceId,direction,amount,editIndex}`; candidate ID, cardinal,2/4/6; editIndex null or valid arrow index. Initial tool first candidate/up/2N, not an answer.
- `selected`: null or valid arrow index; selects a row/visual emphasis, not a force assertion.

- `prediction`: null or one of the four IDs; `predictionChecked`:boolean, initiallyfalse. A changed choice clears checked only; reveal flag retained.
