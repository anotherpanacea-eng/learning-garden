# Field Notebook: choose, chart, question, revise

## Problem and bounded outcome

Plant Study keeps exact model observations, but copying a supplied row is not choosing evidence or making a graph. The Storm Workshop already displays correct zero-based bars automatically; it does not ask the learner to construct them. Add a small, repeatable Field Notebook beside Living Garden: choose observations that answer a particular question, build a two-bar draft, read it, compare it with its source, and revise. This is supported practice with invented model data, not independent measurement, a real experiment, a growth forecast or a placement instrument.

Entry: `#living/notebook`. Its invitation follows the current Living Garden task and the Plant Study kept-observations section; it adds no prerequisite or pre-task wall. Every comparison, page and support is open. The source packet and current question remain beside the immediate action. Use four compact pages: **question and data → build → read and check → keep or revise**. Returning, changing a question or taking a break preserves its draft.

## Source packet and repeatable questions

Reuse `illustrative-cress-light-v1` and exact immutable observations from `plant-study-content.mjs`. Do not invent a second biological dataset, crop yield or ecological claim. The source notice appears beside the table, chart and kept chart: “These pictures and numbers were invented for comparing. They are not measured cress results or a growth forecast.” Relative model day, adequate/too-little light, specimen A/B and stem millimetres remain explicit. Stem length is not a health score. References, controls and leaf/stem descriptions stay available in a compact details panel.

Eight finite cases: each of days 0, 2, 4, 6 crossed with specimen A or B. Stable IDs `light-day-0-A` through `light-day-6-B`. Each asks: “In our invented model, on day D, how do specimen S's stem lengths compare in adequate light and too little light?” This is a same-day category comparison, not a time-series graph. Day 0 deliberately has matching lengths and a zero difference. Both day-0 comparisons match; later pairs differ in this invented pack.

Source browser has four manually selected day packets; each contains the four accurate, individually labelled observation rows for that day. A small “kept in Plant Study” mark identifies rows the learner previously copied; other rows remain available as **supplied model rows**. An empty, absent or unavailable Plant Study does not lock this activity. Selecting a supplied row here does not pretend it was previously kept or physically measured and does not add it to Plant Study's notebook. No random regeneration; explicitly choose any case, or “Another comparison” cycles through the eight and labels a replay as familiar. No endless-content claim.

**Cross-loop exposure is required:** any actually displayed later-day model row here must prevent a later Plant Study prediction from being described as before-looking. Count every visible source: the browsed day packet, selected rows displayed on any page, the requested pair on the read page, a different-case worked example, and source rows in a kept chart beside the planting corner. Opening a day selector does not expose its unshown packets. A small pure `fieldNotebookDisplayExposure(state, view)` helper returns the unique known displayed day set and an uncertainty flag for the actual rendered view; it neither records an answer nor mutates either root. The app applies this through narrow `notePlantExposure(state, day)` and `notePlantExposureUnknown(state)` actions that preserve selected day/condition/specimen/page, prior first prediction and unrelated work. Do not infer only `sourceDay` while overlooking displayed selected or carried rows.

Prediction timing has three outcomes: **after-later-days** if any validated later day is known shown; otherwise **unknown** if prior exposure is uncertain; otherwise **before-later-days**. Known later exposure wins over uncertainty. Both the notebook and Plant Study retain a sticky `exposureUnknown` flag; Plant Study's flag is optional for backwards-compatible version-1 records, and absent means false. A newly recorded first prediction or revision uses the current three-way timing, while an already recorded first prediction remains exact. An unavailable, malformed or future notebook means prior source views cannot be reconstructed: conservatively mark exposure unknown through the narrow Plant Study action rather than claiming before-looking. Explicit notebook recovery retains its raw original and sets its own sticky unknown flag, even if new validated exposures are later recorded. Explicit Plant Study recovery likewise sets its sticky unknown flag. After recovery, replay only validated notebook known days and its uncertainty flag through the same narrow actions before a new prediction. Never inspect arbitrary malformed payloads as trusted exposure data. An unavailable/future Plant Study root stays exact and unavailable; the integration must not normalize it to record either known exposure or uncertainty. Do not introduce a general learning-profile service.

## 1. Choose useful data

Choose up to two source rows. Checkboxes or two explicit selection buttons work by touch and keyboard; at the limit, explain “Remove a row to choose another” rather than silently replacing it. A review strip shows the selected rows with full day, condition, specimen, exact source value and units. Both selection and removal are reversible. Changing the browsed packet leaves the selected pair intact.

“Check my data” records the selected IDs and distinguishes **missing a row**, **mixed days**, **wrong requested day/specimen**, **same condition twice**, and **the requested matching pair**. Check the actual IDs and field values, not the order or wording of labels. Feedback points to the question and labels. It does not replace rows, erase an unusual choice, or award a score. A nonmatching pair can still be charted and investigated, plainly labelled “Your selected rows do not yet match this question.” Empty/single-row drafts are allowed; do not fabricate a zero bar for a missing row.

Support can model a different case, identify what the question asks, narrate it or invite working together. Revealing the target pair is supplied help, never an independent data-selection result.

## 2. Build a chart

Show two bars for the selected rows, in selection order, with each category automatically labelled by the accurate source day/condition/specimen. The learner chooses the quantity label from **stem length / leaf spread / number of plants**, and its unit from **millimetres (mm) / centimetres (cm) / plants**. The reference table always says **stem length in mm** for this task. The chart title is supplied “My comparison draft”; choosing title prose is not part of this increment.

Each bar value starts **blank**, not zero or the source value. Bounded raw input drafts (up to 16 characters) preserve unfinished/invalid text separately from applied integer values, including through help, label changes, pages, cases and reload. A Set bar action applies a valid entry; Apply entries and check validates/applies the visible entries together or explains an invalid entry without recording a check. Model-choice and optional-difference drafts also persist, including after submission. Checks/kept snapshots retain pending text separately; unapplied text never silently becomes a chart value or answer. A labelled integer input accepts 0–70 inclusive; touch controls add/subtract 1 or 10 without exceeding that range. Keyboard entry and buttons are equally supported; no dragging is required. Zero is a real entered value and remains visibly different from blank. Both bars use the same fixed zero-based 0–70 numerical scale with labelled 10-unit major ticks; the current chosen unit and measure label are visible. Bars are categorical, separate and unjoined. Length is proportional to the entered number. Never truncate the baseline, autoscale the pair differently, or snap a value to its source automatically.

Incorrect labels and values remain editable as the learner's draft. Prominently distinguish **Your draft chart** from **Supplied model table**; an entered 40 or a chosen cm unit never replaces the canonical 38 mm source. Provide exact text equivalents of the draft labels/values and the canonical table. Colour is supplementary. Changing the pair retains entered values only for rows that remain selected, initializing newly selected rows blank; it must not relabel an old number as a new specimen. Earlier checked snapshots remain frozen.

“Check my chart” gives separate feedback for data relevance, quantity label, unit and each numerical bar. It must not collapse these into a single success score. Retain mismatches and allow editing; all later pages remain open. Optional help uses an openly labelled different-case worked example. One-tap copying the answer is not the main construction interaction; if a full example reveals any target information, retain that support.

## 3. Read, compare and revise

Keep both the exact source pair and current draft available. Use three small, separate prompts:

1. **Read this draft:** when two draft values exist, which labelled bar is longer, or are they equal? Evaluate against the current entered values, even when they were copied incorrectly. Correctly reading an inaccurate draft is distinct from representing the source accurately. If a bar is blank, explain what is missing; no synthetic attempt or wrong answer is recorded.
2. **Check against the source:** “Do both numerical bars and their labels agree with the supplied table?” Choices **yes / something differs / not sure**. Feedback checks current canonical values, quantity and unit independently of whether the pair answers the original question. A faithfully copied but irrelevant pair can agree with its rows while failing question relevance. Keep these outcomes distinct.
3. **Answer the original question from the model table:** choose **adequate-light longer / too-little-light longer / same length / not sure**, with an optional whole-millimetre difference entry. This refers to the authored requested pair even if the draft currently uses other rows. Show that requested pair explicitly beside this prompt, labelled as supplied support; do not silently switch the learner's chart. Difference feedback uses the absolute difference; day 0 is zero. A correct numerical answer alone is not experimental reasoning or transfer.

These are practice with visible source material. Any answer can be revised; no score, timer, gate, or success prerequisite. Check feedback names the actual rows/values and never says longer means healthier or infers real causation. An optional unrecorded “Tell someone: I chose these rows because… / I would change…” invitation has no keyword, length or grammar grading. Narration does not establish independent reading.

## 4. Keep and revisit

“Keep this draft” freezes the selected source observations, labels, entered values (including blanks), source model ID/notice/references, case ID/question, support metadata, and most recent separate checks. It works with incomplete or mismatching work and calls it a draft. Keep one latest snapshot per authored case plus that case's bounded check history; do not create an unbounded journal or delete other cases on revision. Later edits never alter an earlier check or kept snapshot until the learner explicitly keeps a new draft. Mark it revised, rather than erasing the fact earlier work was familiar or supported.

A small Field Notebook panel beside the planting corner can display the currently kept draft and link back to its question. It carries evidence and a question, never a pass label, inventory reward or biological outcome. It does not replace Plant Study's separate frozen observation plan. Existing planting grids, café orders/stock, rivals, letter rounds and stories stay untouched. Further Workshop datasets, chart choice, time/line plots, averages, independent scale choice, real measurements and uploads are later increments.
