## Purpose

**Animals of the World** has 28 pinned topics and no declared reference in `curriculum/topic-coverage.csv`. Draft 144 claims Animals Everywhere and Migration; drafts 150, 151 and 161 own insects and the waggle dance, food webs, and life cycles. Seven untouched topics fit one valley story: **Animal Homes**, **Nocturnal Animals**, **Animal Communication**, **Wild, Farm & Pet Animals**, **Endangered & Extinct Species**, **Protecting Endangered Animals** and **Invasive Species**. Strand S4 (living things and their places, human impact) has no conservation content at all, and S3 has no behavior.

A child comes to understand that a **home** is a shelter with a job, made or found, and smaller than a **habitat**; that **nocturnal** animals swap the day and use features that gather scarce light or work by sound, smell and touch; that a **signal** works only when another animal receives it; that **wild, farm and pet** describe a relationship with people; that **endangered** (still here) differs from **extinct** (none left), ranked from evidence on the **IUCN Red List**; that **protection** matches help to threat and keeps the habitat; and that an **invasive** species was moved by people and spreads and harms, often because nothing there holds it back.

## The knowledge

**Homes.** Made from moss, mud, twigs, wax or silk, or found. The **white-throated dipper** builds a moss dome on a ledge, under a bridge or behind a waterfall (S14); the **barn swallow** a mud cup (draft 144); **Alpine marmots** dig burrows where family groups of up to about 20 sleep six to seven months of winter (S10, S11); the **badger** digs a sett; **honeybees** build wax comb (150); a **garden spider** spins a web. Finders: a **hermit crab** takes an empty shell; a **tawny owl** nests in a tree hollow it did not dig (S22). A habitat is the whole place; a home is the animal's own shelter in it.

**Night.** Diurnal: marmot, dipper, swallow, bearded vulture (it soars on daytime rising air). Nocturnal: tawny owl, hedgehog, pipistrelle bats, badger, many moths, female glow-worms. The **tawny owl's** eyes are tube-shaped like telescopes, good in low light but narrow in view, so it turns its head; its ear openings sit at different heights, letting it pinpoint a sound (S19). Claims of far better night vision than ours may be overstated (S19, weaker source). **Bats** echolocate. The **Pyrenean desman** is mainly nocturnal (S3) and feels for food with its long, touch-sensitive snout (S23). Real foxes often hunt at dusk; Pip is a story fox who likes naps.

**Signals.** Sender, channel, receiver, response, and a purpose (danger, mate, food, territory). **Sound:** marmots whistle at a predator and the colony dives, even marmots that cannot see the danger (S11). Tawny owls call to hold a territory. **Light:** the wingless female glow-worm glows yellow-green on summer nights; winged males fly to her, and brighter females attract more (S20). Daylight washes the glow out. **Dance:** draft 150's honeybees. **Scent:** foxes and badgers scent-mark territory. **Body language:** the miller's cat lashes her tail.

**Wild, farm, pet.** Wild animals find their own food and shelter; farm animals are kept for food, wool, milk, eggs or work; pets for company. One kind can be in several groups: a rabbit can be wild, farmed or a pet; domestic pigs come from wild boar (S24). The **Pyrenean mountain dog** (*patou*) is a livestock guardian raised among the sheep that deters bears and wolves without being trained to attack (S18). The miller's cat (a pet who chases mice) is the "it depends" case. Wild animals are watched from a distance and never fed; bear and park rules are a grown-up card pointing to the local authority.

**Endangered and extinct.** The IUCN Red List has nine categories (version 3.1, 2001). Seven rate risk: Least Concern, Near Threatened, Vulnerable, Endangered, Critically Endangered, Extinct in the Wild, Extinct; the middle three are "threatened". Data Deficient and Not Evaluated are not threat ratings (S17). Case files:

- **Pyrenean desman:** a small river mammal of the Pyrenees and northern Iberia, moved to **Endangered** in 2021; range losses of more than 60% are reported in the French Pyrenees (S1). Threats: dams and hydro plants breaking up rivers, pollution, water taken out (S1, S2), and possibly escaped American mink (S2 says "might"; S3).
- **Pyrenean ibex (bucardo):** extinct; the last one was found dead in Ordesa National Park in January 2000 (S12). Another Iberian ibex subspecies was released in the French Pyrenees from 2014 (S12).
- **Dodo:** Dutch sailors reached Mauritius in 1598; last widely accepted sighting 1662 (S21). **Thylacine:** last known one died at Hobart Zoo in 1936 (S21; day and cause disputed).

Extinction also happens naturally (spec 142), but people have raised today's rate; said plainly, without counts.

**Protecting.** Match the help to the threat, and keep the habitat:

- **Too few to breed in the wild:** captive breeding and release. All 27 **California condors** were in captivity by April 1987; the first captive chick hatched in 1988, releases began in 1992, and in 2022 there were 561, 347 in the wild (S15). The **bearded vulture** eats mostly bone, dropping big bones on rocks to break them (about 85% bone by one estimate, S4, S5). It was gone from the Alps by about 1913 while a small population held on in the Pyrenees; zoo-bred birds were released in the Alps from 1986, and the first wild Alpine chick came in 1997 (S6).
- **Too few and too related:** bring animals from elsewhere. A handful of **brown bears** were left in the Pyrenees by the mid-1990s; three from Slovenia were released in 1996–1997 (S7). In 2024 the French Biodiversity Agency (OFB) detected **at least 96**, model estimate 104 (likely 97 to 123); 46% were detected only in France, 26% only in Spain or Andorra, 28% on both sides (S8). OFB's 2025 report raised the 2024 minimum to 107 as more bears turned up (S8): a minimum is below the true number because not every bear is detected. One male, Pyros, fathered more than 85% of cubs born since 1996 (S7).
- **Losses to livestock:** shepherds, guardian dogs and night pens, still debated on steep ground (S18).
- **Broken rivers (desman):** clean, flowing water and eased barriers.
- **Brought back by people:** the **Alpine marmot** vanished from the Pyrenees at the end of the Ice Age; about 400 to 500 were released there from 1948 to 1988, and sources call it both an introduction and a reintroduction (S10). It is the Keeper's Desk "which word fits?" case, spoken, not graded.

**Invasive.** Moved by people, then spreads and harms. Non-native is not automatically invasive; spreading on its own is not an introduction. Invaders often thrive because the predators, parasites or diseases that held them back at home are missing. The **eastern gray squirrel** came to Britain from North America (reliable record 1876); it carries squirrelpox without falling ill, the virus is often fatal to reds, and 1990s studies found it a major cause of their decline, alongside competition and habitat loss (S16). Also **American mink** and draft 151's **Himalayan balsam**. Released pet goldfish can spread and harm lakes (S25), so pets are never let go. The language stays about animals and plants, never people.

## The game or activity

**(a) Home Finder.** Eight animal cards, eight home cutaways (Sprout set: four). Tap an animal, then a home. A match opens the cutaway (animal inside, young where that home raises them) and asks *What does this home do?* with three job chips; a mismatch shows the animal politely not fitting and invites another try. Then a **Make or Find?** two-tray sort and one **Home or habitat?** pair.

**(b) Night Watch.** One valley scene with a **Day / Night** switch; night dims the scene (never black). **Who is awake?** (four of seven), then **Feature to job**: tap a feature (owl's tube eyes, owl's uneven ears, bat calls, desman snout) and pick its job from three.

**(c) Signal Board.** Five tiles (whistle, glow, dance, scent mark, tail lash). For each, tap four chips in order (sender, channel, receiver, response), then a purpose. A **Daylight** toggle on the glow tile washes it out: *A signal needs a channel the receiver can sense.* The dance tile is read-only and links to draft 150.

**(d) Wild, Farm or Pet?** Nine cards into three trays, each with a **because** chip. The miller's cat accepts Pet or Farm with a matching because ("it depends"). A tenth card: *Ember is from a story. Real animals only on this board.* Ends with **Watch from a distance**; bear safety is a grown-up card.

**(e) The Red List Shelf.** Six case files (desman, bucardo, bearded vulture, brown bear, condor, gray squirrel), each with **What the evidence shows**, **What people think**, **What nobody knows yet**. Tasks: a **Red List ladder** (simplified picture: *the real ladder uses population size, range and decline*); **Threat to help** (four threats, five helps, one fitting none; "breeding only" shows a flat line labeled *made-up model: young bred, nowhere safe to release*, no real numbers); **Invasive or not?** with because chips.
