All seven targets below, in Marble's *Organisms & Life Processes* domain, are unclaimed on main and every `pj161v` draft branch.

A child comes to understand that particles wander at random, so more spread from crowded to less crowded places (**diffusion**), with no push; that air sacs in the lungs let oxygen diffuse into blood and carbon dioxide out, and **breathing**, **gas exchange** and **respiration** are three different things; that the heart is two pumps with four rooms and one-way doors, sending blood through itself twice per full trip; that most plant and animal cells release energy from glucose using oxygen; and that birds and mammals hold a steady warm temperature with responses that **oppose** a change, while a lizard borrows heat from outside.

**Never the child's body.** Facts are about "a person", a cast animal or a model. No screen asks the explorer to breathe, count, feel, measure or describe their own body; there is no pulse, breath or temperature activity on screen or off (spec 148's optional grown-up-led pulse card stays its only one). Out of scope: illness, fever, medicine, exercise or health advice, smoking, altitude, the immune system (white cells only in the grown-up panel, as in 148), anaerobic respiration and ATP.

## The knowledge

Sources in the audit. "About" marks ranges.

**Diffusion.** Particles in liquids and gases move constantly in random directions. Where there are more, more wander out than in, so the **net** movement is from higher to lower concentration until even; motion continues after that with no net change. Nothing pushes them and cells spend no energy (passive). Faster with a steeper difference, a larger surface, a shorter distance and a higher temperature Smells crossing a room ride mostly on air currents, so the pack uses still water.

**Gas exchange.** Windpipe (trachea) → two bronchi → bronchioles → clusters of air sacs (**alveoli**). A study of six adult lung pairs found a mean of about **480 million** alveoli (range about 274 to 790 million). Surface estimates vary with method, roughly **50 to 100 m²**; the pack uses 70 m² as a labeled example. The right lung (three lobes) is a little larger than the left (two lobes). Walls are extremely thin and flowing blood carries oxygen away, keeping the difference steep. Oxygen diffuses in, carbon dioxide out, at the sacs, not along the tubes. Spec 159 owns the diaphragm; one Sapling card adds pressure: a bigger chest space lowers inside pressure, so outside air is pushed in (lungs do not "suck").

**Other animals.** Insects have no lungs: air enters small holes (spiracles) and branching tubes (tracheae) carry it near cells; their blood does little oxygen carrying. Earthworms take oxygen through moist skin and have five pairs of pumping aortic arches ("five" or "ten hearts", depending on counting). Fish use gills.

**The circulatory system.** Heart, vessels, blood, in a loop; blood is never used up. **Arteries** carry blood away from the heart (thick, stretchy walls); **veins** bring it back (thinner, with valves); **capillaries** are about 5 to 10 µm wide with walls one cell thick, and red cells (about 7.5 µm) pass almost single file. Blood is about 55% plasma (American Red Cross); red cells carry oxygen out and help bring carbon dioxide back; plasma is about 92% water and carries dissolved glucose and other nutrients; platelets help clotting. Vessels are named by **direction**: the pulmonary artery carries oxygen-poor blood.

**Heart and double circulation.** Two **atria** receive, two **ventricles** push; four valves (tricuspid, pulmonary, mitral, aortic) allow one way only. Route: body → vena cava → right atrium → right ventricle → pulmonary artery → lungs → pulmonary vein → left atrium → left ventricle → aorta → body. The left ventricle pushes blood all round the body at higher pressure, so its wall is much thicker. The heart pumps; the lungs add oxygen. **Why double:** blood loses pressure in the lung capillaries; a second push sends it to the body at high pressure. Contrast: a fish heart has one atrium and one ventricle and one loop (heart → gills → body); frogs have three chambers; birds, mammals and crocodiles four. At rest an adult heart moves about 5 to 6 L a minute, close to the blood volume (about 5 L), so blood goes round in about a minute on average; hard exercise can reach about 20 to 25 L a minute. The pack uses 5 L and 20 L as labeled examples. History is a revisit: one Keeper's card points to 148's Ibn al-Nafis (died 1288; the lung route) and Harvey (1628).

**Aerobic respiration.** In most plant and animal cells, mainly in mitochondria: glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water, with energy **released**, not made. Rates of breathing and heartbeat rise in activity to supply working muscles. A breath cloud on a cold day is water vapor in warm breath condensing into droplets; most of that water comes from the damp lining of the airways. **Stove or cell:** both use oxygen, make carbon dioxide and water, release heat; respiration has no flame and runs at body temperature in many small steps. **Lavoisier and Laplace (winter 1782-83, reported 1783):** a guinea pig in an ice calorimeter; heat measured by melted ice, carbon dioxide compared with burning charcoal; they called respiration a slow combustion, an idea later refined. Only the setup is told.

**Body temperature.** Birds and mammals hold a steady core temperature. **Keeper's Desk history only, as a typical value scientists measure:** Carl Wunderlich published 37 °C (98.6 °F) in 1868 from a huge set of armpit readings; a 2017 study of over 35,000 people (mostly mouth readings) found a mean of about 36.6 °C, and later work suggests averages have drifted down. No line invites checking anyone; the dial measures nobody. A brain center (hypothalamus) compares with a set point and triggers responses that **oppose** the change. Too hot: sweat **evaporates**, carrying heat away (wetness alone does little); skin vessels widen in place (they do not move). Too cold: shivering releases heat from working muscles; skin vessels narrow. Dogs cool mainly by panting; foxes pant too and shed heat from ears and legs. A lizard makes little heat; it basks and seeks shade, and a basking lizard's blood can be warm, so "cold-blooded" misleads. Insulation is spec 153's.

## The game or activity

Native buttons, 44 px targets, keyboard operable, tap equivalents for every drag, no timers, no score.

**(a) Spreading Glass, `spread-v1`.** The glass as a 10 × 6 grid split by a dashed line with gaps; 12 dots start left. **Step** moves each dot one cell at random (seeded; blocked moves stay); **Run 10** repeats. Bars count dots per side; arrows show crossings each way this step. Sapling toggles, one at a time: 24 dots, wider gaps, warmer glass (two moves per step). Model line: *A made-up model. Real particles are far too small to see. Each run is a little different.*

**(b) Air Sac.** Four zoom tiles (windpipe, bronchi, bronchioles, air sacs) ordered either way. A close-up air sac beside a capillary: **Step** uses the same random walk; **Blood flows** carries the capillary's dots away and brings fresh blood. Then **Who breathes how?** (four animals to four surfaces) and a three-basket sort (Breathing, Gas exchange, Respiration). Model line: *A simplified picture. A real air sac is tiny and has millions of neighbors.*

**(c) Four-Room Heart.** The miller's pump first: **Push handle** / **Lift handle**; **Take out the flap** shows water sloshing back. Then a front-view cutaway heart (labeled: the person's right is on the viewer's left). **Squeeze top rooms** / **Squeeze bottom rooms** move a drop; valves draw open or shut; the drop turns bright red at the lungs; a **diagram code** toggle adds blue/red tags. Names appear as the drop passes. **Vessel trio** and **Fish compare** cards follow. Model line: *A simplified diagram. Both sides of a real heart squeeze together.*

**(d) Slow Fire, Steady Warm.** **Equation builder:** tiles glucose, oxygen, carbon dioxide, water, energy released and flame; the flame fits nowhere and says why. **Stove or cell?** sorts six statements into Stove, Cell, Both. **Hold at 37, `hold37-v1`:** a dial from 35 to 39 °C in 0.5 °C made-up steps; event cards (Pip's parcel race, the cold bridge) nudge the needle; four response cards push it back only if they oppose the change, and a wrong-way card says so. A lizard panel uses a basking rock and shade. Model line: *A made-up model, not a thermometer. It measures nobody.*

**Off-screen card, optional, adult-led:** colored water in a cold glass and a warm glass (warm tap water, a grown-up pours). Nothing is entered or recorded.
