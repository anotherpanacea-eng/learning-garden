## The knowledge

Sourced in the [audit](../source-audits/266-grafting-bench.md); unconfirmed etymologies were cut.

**Word families.** A word family shares a root and part of its meaning: grow, grower, growth, regrow, overgrow, outgrow; plant, planter, replant, transplant; act, actor, action, active, react. Shared letters are not enough: *growl*, *planet*, *cactus* and *tractor* are not in these families. The family relation is taught as present-day English structure.

**Verb prefixes.** The English national curriculum (Year 5, Appendix 2) names dis-, de-, mis-, over- and re- as verb prefixes. Taught senses: re- again or back (refill, react); over- too much (overcook, overwater); dis- the opposite of or not (disappear, disagree, disconnect); de- undo or take away (defrost, decode); mis- wrongly (misplace, miscount, misheard). Prefixes also join nouns and adjectives (overcoat, dishonest), and even on verbs over- need not mean too much (overhear, overlook), so child text says over- "often" means too much and re- and de- "can" mean their senses. Spec 34's un- is a contrast only.

**Verb-making suffixes.** -ate, -ify and -ize (UK -ise) form verbs from nouns and adjectives: pollinate, activate; simplify, purify, solidify, beautify; memorize, fossilize, magnetize. Spelling can shift (simpl-, beauti-, memor-, pollin-). The pack shows the relation, not a claim that each verb was first made in English. Marble's example *advert* to *advertise* is not used: dictionaries trace *advertise* to French. *Advertise*, *surprise* and *exercise* take -ise in every variety; Oxford spelling uses -ize (grown-up note only).

**Latin and Greek roots, with cousins.** Latin *portare*, to carry: transport (trans- across), export, import, report (re- back), portable; Spanish *portar*, *transportar*, *exportar*, *portátil*; French *porter*, *transporter*, *exporter*. Several of the cousins were borrowed from Latin later rather than handed down in speech (Spanish *portar*, *transportar*, *exportar*, *portátil*, per Wiktionary), while French *porter* came down in speech. So child text says Spanish and French grew from Latin and English borrowed Latin words, never that each cousin "grew from" the root. English *port* "harbor" is from a different Latin word, *portus*, and is never put in the carry family. Latin *scribere*, to write: describe, script, manuscript; Spanish *escribir*, French *écrire*. Latin *vox* (stem *voc-*), voice: vocal, voice (English *voice* came through Old French); Spanish *voz*, French *voix*. Latin *dicere*, to say: predict, contradict; Spanish *decir*, French *dire*. Greek *graphein*, to write: autograph, photograph (usually credited to John Herschel, 1839; spec only); *tele* far and *phone* sound: telephone. **Graft** comes through Old French from Latin *graphium*, a writing stick, from Greek *graphein*: the pointed scion probably looked like a stylus. **Drift:** Latin *liber* (book, first the inner bark of a tree) gave *library* and Spanish *librería* and French *librairie*, which mean bookshop; a library is *biblioteca*, *bibliothèque*. A second Latin *liber*, free, gave *liberty*.

**Vocabulary strategies.** Readers combine context clues, word parts and reference works, and then check an inferred meaning (Common Core L.6.4 to L.8.4; KS3 reading). Research supports explicit morphology teaching, with small to moderate effects that are largest when it is integrated with other literacy work (Bowers, Kirby and Deacon 2010), and teaching how to derive meaning from context (Fukkink and de Glopper 1998). Multiple-meaning words: *current*, *bank*, *stock* (the rooted part in grafting). *Portage* (Merriam-Webster): noun, carrying boats or goods overland between waters, or the route; verb, to carry over a portage (Léon's "we portage" is the verb, so the dictionary card shows it); from *porter*, to carry. *Benevolent*: Latin *bene* well + *velle* to wish.

## The lesson

**Contrasts:** family vs matching letters (grow, growl); re- again vs back (refill, react); over- too much vs over- position (overcook, overcoat); prefixed verb vs prefixed adjective (overcook, dishonest); noun vs verb (memory, memorize); Latin vs Greek root for one meaning (*scrib*, *graph*); same root vs same meaning (vocal, louder); cousin vs drifted cousin (*transportar*, *librería*); first meaning vs fitting meaning (*current*).

## The game or activity

Every screen: *Word parts are clues. A dictionary has the last word.* No score, timer, lock or mastery label. Taps only; every join has a button equivalent.

- **(a) One Root, Many Shoots (Sprout, Seedling).** Three rootstocks (grow, plant, act); the child taps shoots to join them; a non-family shoot (growl, planet, cactus) goes to a "not this family" pot with a one-line reason. Feedback gives each joined word's meaning.
- **(b) Prefix Cuttings (Seedling, Sapling).** Twelve verbs, five prefix cuttings; an authored list of real results, each with its meaning. Any other pair is "a word to wonder about," spec 34's discovery treatment, never a rejection. An overcoat card shows over- on a noun.
- **(c) Verb Makers (Sapling).** Nine words, three endings; the authored verb, its spelling shift and a sentence. A word-job card shows the class change.
- **(d) The Root Family Bench (Sapling; first slice).** One Latin root on the rootstock (*port*); five English twigs with meanings; Spanish and French cousin tags hung beside them. Later stocks *scrib*, *vox*, *dict* and a Greek corner reuse the same frame.
- **(e) Cousin Letters (Sapling).** Lines from Nuria's and Léon's letters, each with its English line; the child taps the highlighted word, then its English family member; *librería* opens the drift card.
- **(f) The Unknown Word (Keeper's Desk).** Léon's portage note and four tools in order, with a two-sense dictionary card; a "two meanings" shelf (current, bank, stock).

## Saved work

Optional root `grafting` per explorer. An explicit Start freezes only the selected visit's complete working source; Keep freezes that source if no visit has started. New visits retain earlier visits. No snapshot of every catalog item is required.

The sketch uses `Saved<T>` only as shorthand for an inline first/latest record, not a shared runtime helper or a source registry. `FullSource` is the complete selected authored source: passages and provenance labels; instructions, teaching and examples; models/data and captions; every selected question, ordered choice text, key and feedback; help and read-back text or asset references. It contains authored prose; only the response payload `T` uses codes.

```
ObservedHelp = { observed: true, concept: [{ forId, kind }], reading: [{ forId, kind }] }
Snapshot<T> = {
  source: FullSource,
  shown: { question: text, choices: [{ id, text }], key, feedback },
  answer: T | null,
  outcome: 'fit' | 'not-yet' | 'shown' | 'skipped' | 'kept',
  helpBefore?: ObservedHelp,
  at
}
Saved<T> = { first: Snapshot<T>, latest?: Snapshot<T> }

grafting: {
  v: 1,
  visits: { [visitId]: {
    working: { source: FullSource, help?: ObservedHelp },
    shoots: { [rootId]: Saved<{ [wordId]: 'family' | 'pot' }> },
    cuttings: { [verbId]: Saved<prefixId> },
    makers: { [wordId]: Saved<endingId> },
    bench: { [stockId]: Saved<{ twigs: [wordId], cousins: { [cousinId]: wordId } }> },
    letters: { [lineId]: Saved<wordId> },
    items: { [itemId]: {
      first: Snapshot<choiceId | [pairId]>,
      latest?: Snapshot<choiceId | [pairId]>
    } }
  } }
}
```

**Frozen source and first response.** Every `Snapshot` above embeds its own full source, shown question/instruction, ordered choices, key, feedback, response and original outcome. For a bench with no supplied choices or key, preserve its instruction and use an empty choice list and null key; an ungraded keep uses `kept`. The working source is frozen before the visit begins and is never replaced by today's catalog. Waiting work and saved responses render only from their own sources. `first` is write-once at the first recorded response/keep; later attempts have a separate `latest`, and later skips/help never change `first`. Explicitly starting revised content creates another visit and retains earlier sources and work. `answer: null` is allowed only when no selection exists and the outcome is `skipped` or `shown`; otherwise retain the actual response. No original spoken or written explanation is graded.

**Observed help before reveal.** During a newly tracked visit, `observed: true` with empty arrays means no help request was recorded in that visit so far, not demonstrated independent performance. Commit concept-help codes (hint, guided step, example, shown answer, meaning or slot scaffold), with the affected task/item id, to working help before their first reveal. Commit read-aloud/prompt-audio codes and the affected task/item id separately to `reading` before playback. Copy the observation into each response's `helpBefore` at the response time. A later request changes working help and the next response only. Missing older observations remain unobserved: never infer them from a later response or turn them into an empty list, false or “none.” Help requests are not proof that help was used; reading/audio data establish neither independent reading nor independent listening.

**Validation and preservation.** Validate structural bounds, ids, counts, outcome codes and consistency with the save's own source/choices; never require today's catalog membership, bench sizes, wording or answer key. A missing item is absent. Never recompute a saved result. Unknown versions and unreadable entries remain exact and exportable; only the affected visit/bench becomes read-only, and one bad item never closes the pack. Response payloads contain no free text; say why is spoken and unstored. No field holds a real learner's identity, age, grade or coursework. Spec 34's `words` extension is untouched.

**Atomic saves and capacity.** Every mutation, including source copies and help-before records, must pass the actual host's pretty-notes serialization/import preflight and shared limits before committing only this root. Refusal preserves the whole earlier state and visible pending work; do not rerender, navigate or change explorer in a way that silently loses unsaved words/selections. Offer the existing notes download and an explicit confirmed restore of saved words where needed. No silent truncation, history eviction or replacement of saved writing. Export, import, reset and save-off use the shared store; preview/render/suggestion reads are save-neutral. Existing source/language/cultural/rights checks remain open until separately cleared.
