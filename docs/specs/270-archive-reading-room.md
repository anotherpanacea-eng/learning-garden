## Purpose

**Reading Comprehension** has 72 pinned topics; the 11 targets below are "not yet mapped" in `curriculum/topic-coverage.csv` and named nowhere on `main`. Strand E3 asks for main ideas, causal links and text features (E3-II), summary, comparison and quoted evidence (E3-III), and evaluating reasoning and relevant, sufficient evidence (E3-IV); H1 asks for checking accounts against one another (H1-II), evidence against interpretation (H1-III) and causal claims with limits (H1-IV).

Built nonfiction reading is narrow: the flood almanac (three dated Paris sentences, ordered by date), the Paris archive visit (spec 46: one photograph, one chronology, source kinds) and Follow the Source Trail (spec 69: whether two cards are independent). Nothing teaches how an information text is organized, why one structure suits a purpose, how a writer backs a point, fact against opinion, a summary without opinion, or how to combine two texts on one topic.

A child learns: features (headings, captions, bold, charts, timelines) help a reader find and understand; information texts have shapes (time order, cause and effect, problem and solution, compare and contrast) signaled by words and headings; two texts on one flood can share facts, add different facts or disagree; a point needs evidence that fits and is enough; a fact can be checked and an opinion cannot, however sure it sounds; a summary gives main ideas without opinion; and combined sources keep their tags.

## The knowledge

Sourced in the [audit](../source-audits/270-archive-reading-room.md); unconfirmed claims were cut.

**How information texts work** (DfE KS2 reading programme of study; Common Core RI.4 and RI.5). Presentational features (headings, captions, bold, diagrams, timelines) help a reader find and understand information. Information texts have recognizable overall structures, commonly chronology, comparison, cause and effect, and problem and solution, signaled by headings and signal words; one topic can be written in more than one structure, and each foregrounds different relations. Writers support points with reasons and evidence, which can be judged for relevance and sufficiency. Facts are checkable; opinions are judgments, and confident wording can present an opinion as a fact. A summary states main ideas without the reader's opinion. Integrating two texts means noting what both say, what each adds and where they conflict, and reporting in one's own words with sources credited.

**Paris, 1910.** A very wet autumn in 1909 filled the ground; heavy rain fell from 18 to 21 January 1910 on soaked and frozen ground (DRIEAT: *sols saturés, gelés*; the text softens this to "in places"); tributaries including the Yonne and the Marne rose together; a second, lighter spell of rain from 23 to 25 January kept the rivers high; the Seine peaked at 8.62 m on the Austerlitz gauge on 28 January. A commission was formed on 9 February to study causes and ways to lessen future floods; the Seine was back in its bed on 15 March (Archives de Paris; DRIEAT dates it 16 March, by its 2.50 m threshold).

**Valencia, 1957.** After days of heavy rain in the city and upstream, the Turia overflowed into Valencia on 14 October 1957; many people died. Law 81/1961 of 23 December 1961 made the Plan Sur official: a new channel round the south of the city, designed 12,692 m long and 200 m wide. A city plan proposed a road along the dry bed; neighbors campaigned for a park (slogan *El riu és nostre i el volem verd*); the Turia Gardens were inaugurated in 1986 and run about 9 km. The two waves of 14 October, the death toll and the park-decision year are kept out (sources differ; spec 118 owns them).

**Room for the River.** High water on the Rhine and Meuse in 1993 and 1995; in 1995 more than 250,000 people were evacuated (Rijkswaterstaat; the text says "more than 200,000"). The Dutch response did not only raise dikes: it lowered floodplains, moved dikes back and dug side channels, which needs land (at Nijmegen, land was expropriated with compensation). At Nijmegen a dike was moved back about 350 m and a side channel about 3 km long was dug beside the Waal, making an island; work finished around 2016. Project counts (30 or 34) and costs are kept out.

**Flood marks in France.** The law of 30 July 2003 (Code de l'environnement, art. L563-3) asks mayors in flood-exposed towns to inventory existing flood marks and set marks for historic and new great floods; towns put up, maintain and protect them, visible from the public road. Their purpose is memory: great floods are rare, and newcomers may not know. Toulouse 1875 stays with spec 118.

**The Nile.** From spec 134's audit: the yearly flood brought soil farmers needed; nilometers such as the Elephantine stair read its height; too low could mean hunger, too high could do damage.

## The lesson

Jane's **teach -> guided -> practice**: twelve teach cards, one idea each with a worked example revealed a step per tap; each set's first item shows 1-3 visible steps. **"I'm stuck" ladder** on every item: hint -> an easier worked case plus how to start this one (a recipe card; a hen chart; a soaked lawn; *first, then, finally*; the café's opening time against its buns; two notes about Dot's hat) -> skip for now, no penalty.

**Contrasts:** words against the picture that adds a number; "first" against "four hours first"; one flood in time order against the same flood as problem and solution; a true fact against a fact that fits the point; a checkable sentence against a sure-sounding opinion; after against because; a summary against a retelling or a review; copying against own words with a source.

**Misconceptions tagged on choices:** `features-are-decoration`, `heading-skipped`, `picture-explains-cause`, `picture-shows-everything`, `picture-repeats-words`, `not-in-text`, `town-story-transfer`, `after-means-because`, `signal-word-only`, `structure-is-topic`, `same-topic-same-structure`, `structure-is-truth`, `compare-means-winner`, `confident-is-fact`, `evidence-any-fact`, `opinion-as-evidence`, `checkable-is-opinion`, `disagree-means-wrong`, `popular-is-evidence`, `one-text-has-all`, `summary-adds-opinion`, `detail-as-main-idea`, `copy-is-synthesis`, `changed-meaning`.
