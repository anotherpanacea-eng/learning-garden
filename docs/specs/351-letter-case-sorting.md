## Problem and boundary

The Prop Box now supplies selected lowercase letter/sound encounters. A child can still meet an unfamiliar capital form on a sign without needing a harder phonics task. Add a visual letter-case encounter, then use case choices in four labels. This is recognition and adult-led letter naming, not letter formation, pronunciation, decoding, an alphabet test, capitalization rules or a complete course. A larger printed lowercase letter stays lowercase. Similar-looking b and d are different letters, not two cases of one letter.

One new exact route, `#words/letterpress`, with discovery from Word Garden. Dot is arranging theater labels after the storm. This act-agnostic encounter names no late chapter, Keeper's Page, reward or story completion. Every letter, label and tool stays open. No timers, points, answer penalties, age/gender gate or inferred level. No new audio, speech capture, phonics playback, API, font download or network service. An adult may say letter names; no sound sample is implied. Existing host cancellation of prior narration is allowed.

## Three views, one visit

**Drawer.** Initial view. A compact alphabet chooser, initially folded behind **Choose a letter**, exposes all26 buttons when opened. Start at `a` in Model. Show only one current pair and mat. Use Georgia, serif for glyphs, with inherited body type. Capitals and lowercase use the same48px nominal size; their natural heights differ. Do not transform one with CSS `text-transform` while pretending the underlying text changed. The actual letters and accessible text must agree. The selected pair and every changed control have an explicit accessible name from the companion. No decorative animation or drag interaction.

- Model shows the capital/lowercase pair, the partner-naming prompt and **Try the other case**.
- Try shows the target form, three native choice tiles, **Show the pair**, and **Turn the card over**. Show the pair sets this letter's `tool` to `model`, preserves direction/choice, and focuses the Model heading. Try the other case sets `tool` to `try` without clearing those fields. Initially target the capital and offer three lowercase forms. Turning the card over targets the lowercase and offers the corresponding capitals; it clears the current choice. The target form remains visible, and the adult can name it. Model was available before Try; this is supported practice, never a hidden-answer or unfamiliarity claim.
- The three choices are a finite deterministic window: the selected letter plus the next two letters cyclically in the26-letter table, sorted by alphabet index, then rotate left by `selectedIndex % 3`. The same ordering applies in either direction, with the opposite-case glyphs. Do not shuffle on render. This spreads fitting positions; enumerate every resulting case in content review. The companion gives the independent26-row expected ordering. At y the window wraps to a; at z it wraps to a/b. The b/d and p/q foils are intentional, not case pairs. A selection stays in the visit, shows both literal chosen forms and the authored comparison (the Other comparison always names the target letter's pair, never the chosen distractor's pair), and leaves all choices available. There is no automatic next item or lock. **Another letter** advances cyclically by one; the chooser can jump anywhere.
- Choosing a letter or Another letter selects its own stored work. An unvisited letter starts in Model, capital-target direction, with no choice; returning to a visited letter resumes its exact tool, direction and choice. Turning a card changes only that letter's direction, stays in Try and clears its choice. Opening Model preserves the direction and choice for the next Try; returning from Look closely or Print changes none of them.

**Look closely.** A local sub-view with a three-button selector (**A and a**, **C and c**, **b and d**), showing exactly one of three authored contrasts: `Aa`, `Cc`, and `b/d`. Aa shows A and a; Cc shows C and c; the b/d example shows B b beside D d, never b paired with d. Each also has the finite explanation in the companion. Only the Aa view also shows the size example: lowercase a at48px and96px, both explicitly named lowercase a. These are examples in this installed font, not a universal visual rule for other fonts. No handwriting stroke demonstration or mirror transformation. Selecting a contrast updates only `contrast`, focuses its named contrast heading and persists it for the next Look closely visit. **Back to my letter** restores the exact selected pair, direction, tool and choice.

**Print a label.** Four label choices: cat, pin, mop, bat; all original Prop Box320 pictures/senses. Start with cat, all lowercase. Show the supplied lowercase word and meaning beside the existing picture, and an enlarged strip of3 place buttons. Each place cycles only its own letter between uppercase and lowercase. It cannot substitute another letter. This makes `cAt` a different printing of the same supplied label here, without generating accidental new words or implying case never changes meaning elsewhere. Name each place with its actual form and case. Only one label is expanded.

- **All capitals**, **All lowercase** and each place toggle update the actual literal strip; they do not grade it.
- **Compare with Dot's poster** opens the uppercase reference only by choice. It does not overwrite the child's strip. The comparison says both are ways of printing this supplied word; this is not teaching when capitals are required in sentences or proper nouns.
- **Try on the wall** puts the current pattern into that label's single visit-local wall slot, replacing only that slot. A four-slot wall can show the four supplied pictures and chosen patterns, with blanks for untried labels. An occupied wall slot describes its actual pinned pattern; clicking it selects that label and its current editing strip without loading the older pinned pattern into the strip. Empty slots are non-interactive text. Placing a label opens the wall disclosure before focusing its result; its open/closed state is visit-local. No record of earlier patterns, completion banner, score or inferred evidence.
- cat and pin initially show their pair reference as supported practice; mop and bat are the new-use invitation. All four start in the same ungraded tool; mop/bat do not automatically reveal uppercase pairs or Dot's poster. A **Show these pairs** disclosure is optional for every label. It displays only that label's3 case pairs. On label change, close both references for the label being left, without erasing its strip or wall slot. The selected label uses its own reference state: cat/pin start with pairs open on first selection; mop/bat start closed. Once a label has been left, its references stay closed until deliberately opened again. For mop/bat the new-use prompt follows the ordinary label prompt. **Back to my label** appears only in Dot's poster comparison: it closes that comparison, preserves the pair disclosure and strip/wall, and focuses the strip description.
- **Back to my letter** restores the drawer exactly. Drawer/Look closely/Print are sub-views of one route, preserving all letter work, label patterns, wall slots and current label during the visit.

The companion supplies all static child copy and finite templates; ordinary route/back links may reuse established host wording. No whole-prose membership test is required. Review wording substantively, then test finite case mappings, transitions, semantic announcements and boundaries.

## Learning contract and honest limits

Spec49 §5: teaching = one pair plus partner naming; contrast = same letter in two cases, b/d different letters and size versus case; supported practice = bidirectional3-choice mat with optional reference and nonpunitive literal comparison; new use = printing mop/bat without automatically showing the case key; in-visit return = another pair/direction or another label in the familiar drawer. A later-act/cross-game purposeful revisit still needs its own bridge; this increment does not claim to supply it. E1-I is the candidate strand family, not coverage claims. This does not teach all sounds, independent letter naming, handwriting, alphabet recitation, reading the four words independently, or capitalization conventions. Full Knowing all letters remains unfinished. Older arrivals can choose it without a lower-age skin or being assigned a level.
