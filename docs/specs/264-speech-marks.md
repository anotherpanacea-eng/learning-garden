A child learns: quotation marks hug a speaker's exact words, with the comma, question mark or exclamation mark inside; a speaker can come first, last or in the middle; each new speaker starts a new line; telling what someone said needs no marks. Then commas: after an opener (a fronted adverbial, then any introductory word, phrase or clause), with yes, no, tag questions and the name of someone spoken to, and wherever a reader would otherwise misread. At Sapling and Keeper's Desk: brackets, dashes and commas around extra information, chosen for how loud it should be; and hyphens that change meaning (*re-cover* and *recover*, *a sock-chasing cat*, *twenty-odd puppets*).

## The knowledge

Sourced in the [audit](../source-audits/264-speech-marks.md). Child text uses American conventions; the grown-up panel names England's terms.

**Direct speech.** Quotation marks enclose a speaker's exact words; reported (indirect) speech tells the content in other words and takes none. When the speaker tag follows the words, the spoken part ends with a comma, question mark or exclamation mark inside the closing mark ("Let's begin," said Dot.). When it comes first, a comma follows the tag and the quote begins with a capital (Dot called, "Places, everyone!"). A quote split by its tag resumes in lower case when it is one sentence ("When the bell rings," said Dot, "open the curtain."). A new speaker starts a new paragraph. In American style commas and periods go inside closing quotation marks; British practice places them by sense outside dialogue (dialogue keeps them inside), often prints single marks, and calls them inverted commas (England's Year 3 terminology adds "or 'speech marks'"). England teaches this in Years 3 and 4; the Common Core in grades 3 (L.3.2c) and 4 (L.4.2b). The Real Academia Española advises angle quotes « » first in printed Spanish and puts the period after the closing mark; French uses « » with a space inside. Spanish and French novels often mark dialogue with a dash instead, so the pack shows « » only for quoted words in letters. A play script sets the speaker's name first and uses no quotation marks.

**Commas after openers.** England's Year 4 asks for commas after fronted adverbials ("Later that day, I heard the bad news."). Common Core L.5.2b extends this to any introductory element: a word (However), a phrase (Before the show) or a subordinate clause (When the lights go down). Adult style guides such as the *Chicago Manual of Style* let writers drop the comma after a short opener unless misreading is likely, and an inverted sentence takes none (Under the bed lay a cat). The child text says openers "usually" take a comma, items that need one ask for it, and the grown-up panel names the option.

**Commas with yes, no, tags and names.** Common Core L.5.2c: a comma sets off yes and no (Yes, thank you), a tag question (It's true, isn't it?) and a name in direct address (Is that you, Steve?); a name in mid-sentence takes a comma on each side. England's Year 6 lists question tags as a feature of informal speech, not a punctuation rule.

**Commas against misreading.** England's Year 5: "use of commas to clarify meaning or avoid ambiguity." A missing comma can make a reader attach words to the wrong part ("While Dot painted the puppet watched"; "Inside the box was full of hats"; "Let's eat Pip").

**Parenthesis.** England's Year 5: brackets, dashes or commas to indicate parenthesis; Common Core L.6.2a: commas, parentheses and dashes to set off parenthetical elements. Parenthetical material can be removed and the sentence stays grammatical; the marks come in pairs (except where the aside ends the sentence). Many usage guides describe the effect the same way: dashes emphasize, brackets play down, commas sit between; the child text says "usually". Printers differ on the dash: a long closed-up dash in most American books, a spaced shorter dash in British ones and in England's own curriculum documents. The child text uses the spaced form, so no long dash appears anywhere.

**Hyphens.** England's Year 6: hyphens to avoid ambiguity ("man eating shark versus man-eating shark, or recover versus re-cover"). Merriam-Webster enters *re-cover* (to cover again) separately from *recover*; *odd* after a number with a hyphen means "somewhat more than" (twenty-odd). A compound modifier before a noun is usually hyphenated (a two-hour show) and usually open after it (the show is two hours long). Spelling-appendix pairs such as *co-ordinate* and *re-enter* are hyphenated in British spelling but usually closed in American dictionaries, so the pack keeps them out of child items. The shark pair is replaced by *a sock-chasing cat* and *a sock chasing cat*, and the Hyphen Hook adds *a small-puppet show* (very small puppets) beside *a small puppet show* (a small show).

## The game or activity

Every screen: *Marks tell a reader's voice where to go. Try any mark. Nothing here is scored.* No score, timer, lock or mastery label. Drags have tap equivalents; tiles are at least 44 px.

- **(a) The Show Book.** Six authored lines with fixed slots between words. A tray of five tiles (quotation marks, comma, period, question mark, exclamation mark) fills the slots; capitals are supplied. **Read it** plays Pip reading exactly the marks placed (authored text and optional voiced audio for each authored pattern). Every acceptable pattern is authored (for example, a comma or ! inside "Let's begin"); common errors get the feedback in the companion; any other pattern gets the neutral line "Pip reads it just as it stands," never "wrong." Sprout job: tap the spoken words. Line 5 adds the new-line move.
- **(b) Pip Reads It Wrong.** Six lines; Pip voices the misreading; the child places one comma (two for a mid-sentence name) and Pip rereads. Covers names, yes, tags and misreading.
- **(c) Opener Cards.** Drag a bracketed opener to the front, then add its comma. Leaving it at the end is accepted with "Openers at the end need no comma."
- **(d) Whisper or Shout.** Put a pair of marks around extra information; **Take it out** hides the part to test the sentence; **Choose the volume** tries brackets, dashes and commas on one line.
- **(e) The Hyphen Hook.** Five pairs; toggling the hyphen swaps an authored picture and caption (the cat chases the sock, or the sock chases the cat).

## Saved work

Optional root `speechMarks` per explorer. An explicit Start freezes only the selected visit's complete working source; Keep freezes that source if no visit has started. New visits retain earlier visits. No snapshot of every catalog item is required.

The sketch uses `Saved<T>` only as shorthand for an inline first/latest record, not a shared runtime helper or a source registry. `FullSource` is the complete selected authored source: passages and provenance labels; instructions, teaching and examples; models/data and captions; every selected question, ordered choice text, key and feedback; help and read-back text or asset references. It contains authored prose; only the response payload `T` uses codes.

```
ObservedHelp = { observed: true, concept: [{ forId, kind }], reading: [{ forId, kind }] }
Snapshot<T> = {
  source: FullSource,
  shown: { question: text, choices: [{ id, text }], key, feedback },
  answer: T,

  helpBefore?: ObservedHelp,
  at
}
Saved<T> = { first: Snapshot<T>, latest?: Snapshot<T> }

speechMarks: {
  v: 1,
  visits: { [visitId]: {
    working: { source: FullSource, help?: ObservedHelp },
```

**Frozen source and first response.** Every `Snapshot` above embeds its own full source, shown question/instruction, ordered choices, key, feedback, response and original outcome. For a bench with no supplied choices or key, preserve its instruction and use an empty choice list and null key; an ungraded keep uses `kept`. The working source is frozen before the visit begins and is never replaced by today's catalog. Waiting work and saved responses render only from their own sources. `first` is write-once at the first recorded response/keep; later attempts have a separate `latest`, and later skips/help never change `first`. Explicitly starting revised content creates another visit and retains earlier sources and work. No original spoken or written explanation is graded.

**Observed help before reveal.** During a newly tracked visit, `observed: true` with empty arrays means no help request was recorded in that visit so far, not demonstrated independent performance. Commit concept-help codes (hint, guided step, example, shown answer, meaning or slot scaffold), with the affected task/item id, to working help before their first reveal. Commit read-aloud/prompt-audio codes and the affected task/item id separately to `reading` before playback. Copy the observation into each response's `helpBefore` at the response time. A later request changes working help and the next response only. Missing older observations remain unobserved: never infer them from a later response or turn them into an empty list, false or “none.” Help requests are not proof that help was used; reading/audio data establish neither independent reading nor independent listening.

**Validation and preservation.** Validate structural bounds, ids, counts, outcome codes and consistency with the save's own source/choices; never require today's catalog membership, bench sizes, wording or answer key. A missing item is absent. Never recompute a saved result. Unknown versions and unreadable entries remain exact and exportable; only the affected visit/bench becomes read-only, and one bad item never closes the pack. Response payloads contain no free text; say why is spoken and unstored. No field holds a real learner's identity, age, grade or coursework. The `loft`, printer and bridges roots are untouched.

**Atomic saves and capacity.** Every mutation, including source copies and help-before records, must pass the actual host's pretty-notes serialization/import preflight and shared limits before committing only this root. Refusal preserves the whole earlier state and visible pending work; do not rerender, navigate or change explorer in a way that silently loses unsaved words/selections. Offer the existing notes download and an explicit confirmed restore of saved words where needed. No silent truncation, history eviction or replacement of saved writing. Export, import, reset and save-off use the shared store; preview/render/suggestion reads are save-neutral. Existing source/language/cultural/rights checks remain open until separately cleared.
