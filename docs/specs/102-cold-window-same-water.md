## Purpose and boundary

The Water Lab has three saved guided visits: melting, warmer liquid, and one molecule evaporating from a liquid surface. The Water Evidence Shelf compares visible clues with possible routes. Neither lets the learner reverse the gas-to-liquid direction at a cool surface. Add one optional **transient teaching bench** at `#science/window`, linked from the Water Lab hub near the existing evaporation visit. The learner changes an illustrated pane from ordinary to cool, compares a whole-view pane with an explicitly imagined tiny view, and may let it warm again. The new action teaches this narrow contrast: **in the pictured conditions, some already-present invisible water vapor in the air becomes visible liquid drops on the air-facing side of a sufficiently cool pane; the water has not leaked through the pane.** Warming the model pane offers a reversible return to evaporation. This is a local water-state bridge, not the entire water cycle.

This is a made-up screen model. It does not infer a child's understanding, record an answer, verify a real pane, prescribe a home experiment, or claim that every cool window develops drops. No temperature, humidity, rate, amount, dew point, cloud formation, precipitation, runoff, energy balance, molecule count, or real flood-safety result is measured or simulated. No prerequisite lock or score. Existing Water Lab visits, external Marble links, Science notes, Water Evidence Shelf, Rain Paths, and imports/exports remain untouched. A compact link back to the evaporation visit invites comparison without rewriting that visit or implying that it was completed.

## Route, interaction, and exact copy

One page has a short opening and a single active frame, not three full frames stacked. Native buttons are at least 44 CSS pixels high. The page keeps the whole-view picture and imagined tiny view together. A brief change statement, one optional talk prompt, controls, and a closed **How this drawing works / sources** disclosure follow the paired pictures. The pane image has text alternatives that convey the current state and the same science as its visible caption. The macro picture contains no visible-vapor symbol. Small round dots occur **only** in the labelled imagined tiny view, stay the same size across frames, and depict a sample rather than measured molecule numbers. Blue drops appear only on the air-facing surface in the cooled frame. The opposite side of the pane remains dry in the model. A pane boundary is continuous in all frames; no arrow crosses it.

The page opens at frame 0 every time the route is entered or reloaded; changing explorer also resets the local frame. Within the live page, **Cool the pane** moves 0→1, **Let the pane warm** moves 1→2, **Cool the pane again** moves 2→1, and **Back to before cooling** moves 1 or 2→0. These are deliberate reversible model actions. Disabled/unavailable controls are not rendered as dead buttons. No auto animation or timeout advances the frame. A stale detached control, old route, or changed explorer cannot change the current page or start a visit. If the app rerenders this route for an unrelated reason, returning to frame 0 is acceptable and announced as a new look; no claim that a saved frame resumed.

**Opening title:** “Cold Window, Same Water”

**Opening text:** “There is invisible water vapor in the air around this made-up window. Cool the pane and compare what is on the air-facing side. The drops do not come through from the other side.” The mock shows a sealed, clear pane with an air-facing side and a dry other side; it does **not** depict a liquid-filled window. If the illustration uses a blue tint beyond the pane, that tint means background, not a water reservoir.

**Before-action talk prompt:** “Where could drops on the air-facing side come from? Tell someone or point to the part of the picture you mean. You do not need to enter an answer.” This remains visible but does not gate the first action. No new Hear control is part of this small bench; existing Water Lab narration and reading observations stay with their own visits.

The transition announcement after **Cool** is: “The cooled pane has drops on the air-facing side. Some invisible water vapor became liquid here: condensation.” After **Let the pane warm**: “The model drops have evaporated into invisible water vapor. This return is a model, not a timed observation.” After **Cool the pane again**, use the first announcement. After **Back to before cooling**: “Back before cooling. The pane is dry; water vapor is still in the air.” Put the active frame heading in the visual reading order with id `window-frame-title` and `tabindex=-1`; after each action focus that heading. A separate polite `role=status` announces only the one-sentence transition above, not the full captions. No new speech playback, narration state or recording is added.

**Always-visible comparison line:** “Evaporation: liquid → invisible water vapor. Condensation: water vapor → liquid drops. In both, it is still water.” Use text and direction words, not arrow color alone. This line supports the specific contrast but does not assert a complete cycle.

**After-action talk prompt:** “Which side has drops, and where was that water just before? What does the tiny drawing show that your eyes could not see?” An optional **Compare with the evaporation visit** link returns to the Water Lab hub or opens the existing evaporation mission entry according to its current navigation behavior; it must not create/overwrite a mission until the existing explicit start behavior occurs. The link remains accessible at every frame.

**Grown-up/model disclosure, exact core text:** “This is an original explanatory drawing, not an observation or experiment. The dots are imagined water molecules and are not visible vapor. The two views are not to scale, and the dot count is not a measured amount. Condensation depends on the air and surface conditions; not every cooled pane will make drops. The model skips airflow, humidity thresholds, heat flow, cloud formation, and time. It shows drops on one side of an unbroken pane, not water leaking through it. There is no need for real cold glass, water, heating, or equipment.” Follow with primary USGS condensation/evaporation and NOAA water-cycle links from the audit. Do not suggest a physical trial.

## Original art and layout contract

The three proposed SVGs use one identical upright pane and side labels. The macro area is a simplified **cross-section of an air-facing surface**, not a realistic architectural window. The cool frame adds drops adjacent to that surface only. The tiny area is headed **“An imagined tiny view”**, with seven same-radius dots in the mock: before, they are spread in air; cooled, three form one close-packed liquid sample against the air-facing surface while four remain spread in air; warmed again, they spread back. The macro cool frame deliberately has two visible drops while the tiny sample has three dots, so no one-dot-per-drop correspondence is implied. Seven is an illustrative drawing choice, never a schema constant, displayed quantity or measured conservation claim. Keep all seven identical circles through the three static mocks. If implementation refines the arrangement, retain the same molecule identity/size, no through-pane path, and no visible gas in the macro view. Draw a continuous pane, a clear air-facing label, a dry other-side label and no gas cloud, steam, leak or water reservoir. Drops have their own shape and label in the whole view; a single faint patch behind the close-packed sample distinguishes liquid in the imagined tiny view. Neither view depicts a visible drop as one molecule.

At 320 CSS pixels the active drawing remains approximately 280 pixels wide, with body text at least 16px and native control targets at least 44px; no page-level horizontal scroll. At 820 pixels the opening and art may sit beside a short explanation, but both macro/tiny representations remain paired and their captions remain adjacent. The static [responsive mock](assets/102-cold-window-mock.html) shows frame 1 so the crucial condensation state can be reviewed on phone and tablet. It is design material, not shippable UI or an interaction test. The three SVGs are original local teaching diagrams, not USGS/NOAA/Marble images or a claim of scientifically precise particle spacing, motion, count or droplet geometry. Art review must inspect the rendered 320 and 820 mock and all three SVG frames before runtime build.

Exclude the new route from any generic footer that would stack unrelated invitations below this short teaching bench; use its own small back/compare links.
