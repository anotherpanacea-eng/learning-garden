## Source kinds and language access

Label both accounts **original fiction** on every story page and in notes. No historical eyewitness, authenticated testimony, source corroboration, reliable-narrator generalization, empathy/personality judgment or real-world event claim. Pictures are schematic reminders; the numbered passages are the source for the supplied statement tasks. Artwork must not supply an absent count/time/distance used by a not-enough-information question.

Each teller has3 short core lines; an expanded form can elaborate those same facts and add up to2 decorative lines. Stable core line roles and shared event/claim meanings must survive both forms. No added sentence can quietly state an intended inference, resolve an unknown quantity/time/distance or contradict the other teller. Choose either form openly; neither is a level, readiness test or unlock. Expanded form changes language load, not the taught comparison. Changing form explicitly starts a separate retained visit; familiarity follows the event across forms.

Optional adult reading, local narration and supplied picture reminders support access. Reading reports are unobserved/read-together/reported-independent and are separate from comprehension-card outcomes and help/audio. Reports do not assess decoding, fluency, listening, spelling or independent composition. Short text can carry deep reasoning; longer text alone is not advancement.

## Four relationships, within both accounts together

Every case has four supplied statements, one of each kind:

- **Directly said** (`stated`): a literal statement or faithful paraphrase in a passage.
- **Worked out from clues** (`inferred`): not directly stated, but supported by combining details. An inference can follow strongly from those details; it need not be a guess. Other interpretations may be provisional and must be qualified in their wording/feedback.
- **Text says otherwise** (`contradicted`): an explicit detail conflicts with the statement. Mere omission is insufficient.
- **Not enough information** (`unknown`): the passages leave a specific fact unresolved. This does not mean false, and selecting an irrelevant line cannot prove absence.

The task scope is **the two displayed accounts together**, not whichever teller was opened last. Labels explain that scope near the question. For the first three kinds, content supplies every defensible minimal supporting line set in context. A selection containing such a set includes useful evidence; extra selected lines do not erase it, and feedback identifies which matched lines actually help rather than praising every selected line. A supported alternative interpretation remains welcome in ungraded discussion/own words; the finite supplied-card check is not a general truth classifier.

For `unknown`, require no arbitrary proof-of-absence selection. Evidence outcome is null/not automatically assessed; feedback names the missing fact and invites rereading both passages. Keep selected lines if any, without reporting them as proof. Classification and evidence outcomes stay separate. A correct category with insufficient selected evidence says so; a useful line with another category is not described as wholly wrong.
