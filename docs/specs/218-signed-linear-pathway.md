# 218 — Signed rates to linear equations

A six-lesson, freely open math miniunit at `#learn/math`, alongside the original Courier’s Price. The user requested a complete short slope→linear-equations sequence with signed/rational bridges. This is original supported practice, not a whole algebra course or a mastery/placement assessment. No locks, timers, age labels, real learner information, automatic advancement or story ending.

## Teaching contract and finite content

Each lesson has four freely reachable pages: **Explore** (one idea and worked example), **Together** (guided inputs with explicit steps), **Your turn** (a different item, with optional per-item hint/different worked example/skip), **Return** (a fresh-context numeric prompt and optional ungraded explanation/recommendation). All pages and lessons remain open; returning restores each lesson’s page, model control, drafts and support. Check does not move the learner. Keep marks a visit, with first/latest snapshots; it never claims completion/mastery. Skip records a skipped item and leaves editing/navigation open.

1. **Across zero.** A horizontal number line from −6 to 8 connects zero to a chosen signed height. Positive means above a reference platform, negative below; never negative water. Pair (x,y) means time first, height relative to platform second; x≥0 in this context. At x=0 height is 6; every second moves down 2: 6,4,2,0,−2,−4,−6. Teach 0−2=−2 as direction before `y=6−2x`. Together: height at x=4 (−2). Your turn: height at x=5 (−4). Return: an elevator at +3 goes down 5 floors (−2), typed exact number, with interpretation in words.
2. **Half a step at a time.** Physical half-unit ticks and two half steps make a whole before notation `1/2`. A marker starts 1 unit above a datum and rises 1/2 unit per second: `y=(1/2)x+1`, x=0…6. Together fields: y at x=2 (2), signed change for one second (1/2). Your turn: y at x=5 (7/2). Return: start −1/2, rise 1/2 per second for 2 seconds (1/2). Explain slope is change per one x, not total height.
3. **Three views, one rule.** Net token balance (negative means owed): `y=2x−3`, x=0…6. Start −3 and add 2 each turn, table −3,−1,1,3,5,7,9. Name x/y and rate m/start b explicitly, including commutativity from b+mx to mx+b. Together m=2,b=−3. Your turn y at x=4 (5) and b=−3. Return new net balance `y=(3/2)x−1/2`, ask m=3/2,b=−1/2; show concrete two-turn change of 3. Graph/table share exact values and units; no angle inference.
4. **Keep both sides equal.** Begin inverse operations using `2x−3=5`: add 3 to BOTH sides →`2x=8`, divide BOTH sides by 2 →x=4, substitute 2×4−3=5. Together asks right side after adding 3 (8), x (4). Then both sides: `2x−3=(1/2)x+3`. Subtract `(1/2)x` from BOTH sides →`(3/2)x−3=3`; add 3 to both →`(3/2)x=6`; divide both by 3/2 →x=4; substitution gives 5=5. Your turn asks remaining x coefficient (3/2), right side after adding 3 (6), x (4). Return `−2x+6=0`, x=3, with signed inverse explanation. Fixed authored transformations only, no equation parser.
5. **Two fee plans.** Invented fees A=`3+(3/2)x`, B=`7+(1/2)x`, x=0…6; positive distances and fees. Shared graph/table. Together difference in start B−A=4 and difference in rate A−B=1; subtract 1/2 x and 3 on both sides to get x=4. Your turn crossover x=4, common fee=9; compare x=2, A=6 B=8, and x=6, A=12 B=10 in displayed examples. Return a changed plan pair `2+x` and `5+(1/2)x`, crossover x=6. Explain cheaper choice depends on distance; no claim that higher slope always costs more.
6. **Carry it somewhere new.** Original model net stall balance `y=(3/2)x−3`, x=0…6, with debts distinguished from physical token counts. Explore restates rate/intercept, table/graph and solve. Together m=3/2,b=−3. Your turn solve balance 3: subtract −3 via adding 3 to both sides, `(3/2)x=6`, x=4. Return compare new net balance `x−1` against `(1/2)x+1`, tie x=4. Optional own explanation/question and a self-selected next step: revisit signed numbers, fractional rates, equations, comparing plans, or discuss a new model. This recommendation is ungraded and never auto-inferred as a placement decision.

Every item freezes id, prompt, fields/labels, exact rational expected values, hint, a different-number worked example, guidance and feedback when its lesson starts. Lessons have dedicated model metadata and finite narrative text. Shown instructions/table/graph are support on every item; per-item hints/examples seen before each check are separately recorded. Return items are fresh contexts, not claims of unaided transfer. They can also be skipped. No supplied multiple-choice answers.

## Exact number and graph contract

Numeric answers accept trimmed ASCII signed integers, finite decimal forms with at most 3 decimal places, or numerator/denominator (`-3/2`, positive denominator). Optional leading `+`, trailing decimal zeros and unreduced fractions are accepted. No expressions, exponent notation, units, Unicode minus, mixed numbers, negative/zero denominator, NaN or Infinity. Whole numerator ≤9999, denominator≤1000; final absolute value≤1000. Decimal whole digits≤4. Normalize to safe integer `{n,d}` with gcd; arithmetic and equality use cross multiplication of bounded integers, no tolerance/eval. Raw input is retained verbatim (≤40 chars). Empty/unsupported syntax gives an input instruction without a checked attempt. Mathematically valid wrong values get an explanatory different outcome. A wrong x is checked by frozen substitution where applicable; partial multi-field matches identify fitting field labels, then point to the guided operation.

The interactive control is integer x=0…6 with buttons and labeled select; its exact y may be negative or fractional. Tables show all seven points. SVG has fixed axes x=0…6, y=−6…16, origin, labeled ticks and current point; table exact values are authoritative, SVG geometry uses numeric conversion only for drawing. Lesson 1 shows a number line; lesson 2 shows half ticks. Both fee lines stay on common axes. Models are linear between shown points in the invented model; examples are bounded to their stated domain. Lesson 4’s graph shows both sides of its equation; lesson 6 changes context on Return with explicit item wording and existing reference model clearly labeled.
