## The missing action

At an optional `#workshop/recut` table, Moss wants a colored section of a pretend ribbon to stay the same length while its drawn dividing marks change. The learner presses **Split every equal space in two** and **Join neighboring pairs**. These actions add or remove marks; they never physically cut, join, add, or remove cloth. Both the count of colored parts and the count of all parts change; the ribbon's whole, colored endpoint, and fixed flag do not. A second uncut reference strip stays aligned above the working strip. This is a small M3-II equivalence encounter, not a general conversion rule or a measured craft project.

The overlap matters. Fraction Trail already constructs cuts, moves a flag, accepts equivalent readings, and compares a kept half with a later fourths or sixths route. Same Amount, New Cuts already asks for a matching endpoint under a specified different partition, including thirds and sixths in both directions. Lantern Line already places two fractions on one whole number line. This table adds **direct, reversible repartition of the same pictured amount without moving or reconstructing its endpoint**. It shows which old boundaries survive and which new cuts create smaller units. No old source, validator, saved work, or outcome is reinterpreted.

## Five complete ribbons

The whole is the same 0–1 model ribbon at every stage of a case, not a real length. The colored portion always begins at 0 and ends at one fixed, visibly flagged point. All initial fractions and every later name are supplied on screen; no independent fraction recall, drawing, writing, or explanation is inferred.

These are five small fictional jobs, freely selectable in any order. Start initializes only an in-memory visit to that case's first stage. Opening the chooser creates nothing. Switching cases starts the newly chosen case at its first stage and discards the old **unsaved** screen state; say that plainly beside the chooser. Every case remains open. The table neither spends Workshop stock nor completes any other story.

One whole has 24 exact internal positions; this is only a rendering coordinate, not a claim of 24 physical inches or 24 visible pieces. For a stage with denominator `d`, the current equal boundaries are `j × 24/d` for `j=0..d`. The colored endpoint `e=24 × initialNumerator/initialDenominator` is constant. The colored-part count is `e × d/24`, an integer for every authored stage. A split doubles `d`; a join halves it by undoing the most recent split. The five cases admit only the listed stages, no arbitrary denominators or partial/unequal cut action. At all stages, the uncut reference and working strip share the same left edge, total width, and shaded endpoint. Changing a cut never changes colored area or flags. Do not animate stretching, move the flag, add shading, or treat extra boundary marks as extra ribbon.

## One compact, open turn

The page keeps the case request, **one whole from 0 to 1**, the uncut reference, and the working strip together above the two action buttons. The reference has a continuous colored prefix and no interior cuts. The working strip shows all current equal spaces and that identical colored prefix. A vertical brass-flag marker aligns both endpoints. Pattern plus text distinguishes color from uncolored area; solid **older boundary** marks and dashed **new cut** marks distinguish changes without color alone. On Start, the initial boundaries are older marks. Immediately after Split, every previous boundary remains solid while only inserted midpoints are dashed. A second Split makes the prior stage's cuts old and marks only its newly inserted midpoints dashed. After Join, show only the surviving larger-space boundaries; the removed cuts are absent, not misleading ghost divisions. State this change in visible text. Labels and plain-text picture alternatives give the complete counts and fixed endpoint; no fact depends on recognizing a color or counting tiny lines.

Native **Split every equal space in two** adds midpoint marks and advances one listed stage. Native **Join neighboring pairs** removes only those midpoint marks and returns one stage. The former is disabled at the last stage; the latter at the first. Do not clamp a requested split to a different action. When a clicked control becomes disabled after rerender, focus its enabled reverse control or the board heading, never the document body. Both actions are freely reversible and available without Check, a prediction, Help, or a correct response. All action targets are at least 44 CSS pixels, keyboard-operable, and do not require dragging or tapping a cut mark. The current case title and stage name stay visible while exploring. Earlier stages are revisited through Join, not saved as a history.

Exact stable chrome: heading **“Ribbon Recut Table”**; intro **“One whole ribbon. The colored end stays at the brass flag while we change the dividing marks.”** Reference label **“Uncut reference: the same colored length”**; working label **“Our ribbon with equal spaces”**; endpoint label **“Colored end stays here”**. On first stage: **“Count spaces, not dividing marks. Try splitting every equal space in two.”** After Split: **“We added a dividing mark in every equal space. The whole and colored end stayed still. Now {coloredCount} of {denominator} smaller equal spaces are colored.”** After Join: **“We joined neighboring pairs by removing the extra dividing marks. The colored end did not move. Now {coloredCount} of {denominator} larger equal spaces are colored.”** Always follow with **“Each space is one {unitName} of this whole. The colored length is still {initialFraction} of the same whole.”** The five case fractions above supply exact `initialFraction`; unit names at stages are half, third, quarter, sixth, eighth, or twelfth, singular after “one.” Show the actual current fraction `coloredCount/denominator` alongside this sentence, so both changed numbers are visible. Do not say that a greater numerator or denominator alone means more ribbon.

Optional **How can both numbers change?** reveals: **“Each old equal space becomes two smaller equal spaces. The whole has twice as many spaces, and the same colored stretch covers twice as many spaces. Joining neighboring pairs reverses that cut. The colored length never moved.”** Optional **Talk together** reveals: **“Which old boundaries stayed? Which new cuts appeared? After joining pairs, where is the colored end?”** These are supplied explanations, not a grade or observation of a spoken answer. An always-visible model note says **“A made-up screen ribbon, not a measured cloth length. The labels and picture show these five cases; they do not prove a rule for every fraction.”** The grown-up note adds that optional help, local speech, and correct button use establish no independent reasoning or attainment.

The optional worked example is separate from the five live ribbons and never changes the working case. Its own three native picture buttons show **1/6 → 2/12 → 1/6** on another aligned 0–1 strip with a fixed endpoint at internal 4. Its exact captions are: (1) **“One of six equal spaces is colored. The flag marks its end.”** (2) **“Split all six spaces. Two of twelve smaller spaces cover the same colored length; the flag stays still.”** (3) **“Join each neighboring pair. One of six larger spaces is colored again.”** Reopening the example returns to picture 1. This demonstration is supplied teaching, not hidden learner work, a sixth case, or an adaptive baseline. A local **Hear this picture** control, if offered, reads only the currently visible case counts/caption and never autoplays or records listening.

## Review and acceptance

- Enumerate all listed stage fractions using exact integers. Split then Join returns the same denominator, colored count, cut positions and endpoint for every nonterminal stage; dash styling may change because Join removes the newest cuts. Activating a disabled bound control does nothing. Quarter cases stop after one split; half/third/two-thirds stop after two. Zero and one remain fixed labels, not selectable answers.
- Review the [mock](assets/100-ribbon-recut-table.svg) and phone/tablet art: the reference/working wholes align; 1/2, 2/4, 4/8 and the joined 2/4 share one colored endpoint; old/new marks are distinguishable; no row stretches or shade appears after a split. Verify a third and a non-unit case too. Picture alternatives convey all counts and the unchanged amount.
- In a real browser at 320 and 820 CSS pixels, exercise each case's full Split/Join sequence with keyboard and touch. Controls, focus, optional example and help stay reachable without horizontal overflow or a long detour between picture and action. Leaving and returning, reload, explorer change, import and reset restart at the chooser without changing saved notes or an unfinished Fraction Trail/Matches/Lantern visit. A stale detached control is inert.
- Independent spec/math/art review precedes runtime. Independent implementation review then checks the actual geometry, current narration, lifecycle and unchanged old saves. These checks support only finite visual practice, not real-world measuring, general fraction equivalence, independent explanation, score, mastery, or a new atlas mapping.
