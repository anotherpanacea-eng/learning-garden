# 77 — Joining Supply Orders

**Status: author proposal; independent specification and content review required before implementation.** This is one optional M1 addition-with-regrouping turn in Bundle Burrow, not a written-algorithm course or a claim of calculation fluency. The [source audit](../source-audits/77-joining-supply-orders.md) identifies the precise overlap and limits.

## The missing action

The existing [Bundle Exchange](53-bundle-exchange.md) preserves one fixed collection while ten ones become a ten and back again. Number Garden also asks a small addition question and adds whole tens in its general playground. Neither asks a child to **join two separately identified two-digit collections**, retain which order supplied each counter, make a ten from their combined ones, and compare that action with a no-regroup addition. This proposal adds exactly that turn at `#numbers/join`, linked from the existing Exchange Table and Bundle Burrow. Both destinations remain open. It reuses the Exchange Table's countable ten-bundle and ten-frame visual language and pure drawing helpers where their existing meaning fits. Its optional `joiningOrders` save root is separate, so Bundle Exchange's version-1 source, records, shown-example bridge and recovery path are untouched. There is no inventory, purchase, grade, route gate, new world or answer-key change in another game.

The supplied story concerns **imaginary seed counters**, not actual seed stock. Pip and Dot each have a labeled order card. A child may start either case, join the cards, move individual ones into the frame, wrap ten, type a total, ask for help, keep an unfinished draft, or leave. Pages and cases do not unlock. The numbers, pictures, captions, optional narration and feedback are supplied information; a fitting response records one pictured practice attempt, not an independent written method, fact recall or mastery. A typed number does not establish handwriting or who operated the keyboard.

## Complete finite pack

Version-1 case IDs and operands are mathematical identities. A future operand change needs a new case ID or version; wording may change while old readable visits keep their frozen title and prompt. Both cases use two two-digit positive quantities and totals below 100.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | ---: |
| `steady-orders` | “Pip's order has 24 seed counters. Dot's order has 13 seed counters. Join both orders. How many individual counters are there altogether?” | Pip: 2 tens and 4 ones. Dot: 1 ten and 3 ones. | 3 wrapped tens, 7 loose ones. A ten cannot be wrapped from these ones. | 37 |
| `full-frame-orders` | “Pip's order has 24 seed counters. Dot's order has 17 seed counters. Join both orders. How many individual counters are there altogether?” | Pip: 2 tens and 4 ones. The pair holds Pip's order and both tens counts fixed while Dot's ones change from three to seven. It makes the contrast visible without suggesting that adding always requires a carry. A case chooser labels the two cards **“Dot brings 3 ones”** and **“Dot brings 7 ones”** and states both addends. It never labels one case easier or requires the first. Initial separate cards and the joined table may show each constituent counter; all three original tens bundles, two Pip and one Dot, retain their origin's color or pattern label. A wrapped bundle shows ten constituent marks and counts as ten individual counters, not a separate eleventh object. At narrow widths, a compact count and a scroll-free, countable ten-mark drawing may be stacked; no mark is duplicated in a summary.

The always available help is original: **“Count the tens from both orders. Then count the ones. If ten ones fill the frame, wrap those same ten as one more ten. The number of counters stays the same.”** Its separate supplied example says: **“Example: 12 and 18 join. Two tens and ten ones are on the table. Wrap those ten ones. Three tens and zero loose ones make 30.”** The example has its own label and cannot fill, answer or change either live order. Opening it records `example` support. After `steady-orders`, ask **“What changed when Pip's and Dot's orders joined? Did you need a new ten?”** After `full-frame-orders`, ask **“How did ten loose ones become one new ten without changing the total? How did that differ from the other pair of orders?”** The other pair is optional and available, never a prerequisite. No response to either talk prompt is graded or stored.

## One small interaction

The initial view shows two source cards with their numbers and distinct pictures, the equation `24 + 13 = ?` or `24 + 17 = ?`, an empty joined-table destination and a native **Join these orders** button. Join explicitly places both fixed collections on one table. It does not answer or wrap anything. The joined model starts with the original three wrapped tens and separate loose counts `{pip:4,dot:3|7}`. A visible source strip continues to show both exact operands after joining. `Separate and start again` explicitly restores the initial working model, without changing the source, raw typed total, support, checks or kept copy; it announces that the working grouping reset.

On the joined table, **Move one Pip counter to the frame** and **Move one Dot counter to the frame** transfer one available loose counter of that origin. **Return one Pip/Dot counter from the frame** reverses a chosen origin's transfer. The ten-frame has ten two-row positions, and every placed mark retains its origin's color/pattern and text label. A full frame may mix origins. **Wrap these ten** is enabled only when the frame contains exactly ten marks; it moves those same ten into a new ten bundle whose ten constituent marks retain their origin labels. **Unwrap the new ten** restores exactly that origin mix to an empty frame. Since the source has at most eleven combined loose ones, no more than one new bundle can exist. An original Pip or Dot bundle is never silently broken or relabeled. Refused moves make no change and provide a specific visible reason. There is no free add/remove counter control and no automatic regrouping.

For a joined case, `loosePip + framePip + newTenPip = 4` and `looseDot + frameDot + newTenDot = 3|7`; `newTenPip + newTenDot` is either zero or ten. Original wrapped tens remain `{pip:2,dot:1}`. These equations are implementation invariants, not child-facing algebra. The child-facing count says **“Pip's counters: 24. Dot's counters: 13 [or 17]. Joined table: the same counters together.”** After a valid wrap, it may say **“Ten ones moved into one new ten; none were added or lost.”** The figure's countable marks and accessible description must agree with these statements. The total source is never changed by a grouping action.

A single native text input **“Our proposed total, in individual counters”** retains up to 24 raw characters, including whitespace and leading zeroes. It accepts a checked response only when trimmed text contains ASCII digits alone and parses to an integer from 0 to 99. Blank, signed, decimal, exponent, non-ASCII and out-of-range drafts stay editable with an attached syntax message; they do not become wrong-answer checks. An in-range wrong total is a valid checked response. Editing preserves the input node and caret, clears only current feedback/error, and never alters earlier checks. The expression above the input remains a question; while editing or after a wrong entry, do not render a false equality. **Check our total** may be used before joining, after joining, or after wrapping. It compares the typed number with the frozen operands' sum and reports number match separately from what the model showed. A fitting number never implies that regrouping occurred. Feedback may supply `24 + 13 = 37` or `24 + 17 = 41`, clearly labeled as the source result and recorded as feedback exposure, not the learner's method.

The exact numeric feedback is **“Your proposed number matches the supplied total: 24 + 13 = 37 individual counters.”** or **“Your proposed number differs from the supplied total: 24 + 13 = 37 individual counters. You can revise or keep it.”** Substitute `24 + 17 = 41` for the second case. A separate model note is selected purely from the *current grouping*, never from the last action or a historic check. In the separate state it says **“The orders are separate right now.”** In the joined state with an empty frame, it says **“Three original tens and seven loose ones are together. Seven ones cannot make a new ten.”** or **“Three original tens and eleven loose ones are together. Ten of those ones can become one new ten.”** A partial frame states its actual count and Pip/Dot origins. A full frame says **“The full frame has ten unwrapped ones.”** A wrapped state says **“The same ten ones are one new wrapped ten. One unwrapped one remains on the table.”** or **“The same ten ones are one new wrapped ten. One unwrapped one remains in the frame.”** according to the remaining one's location. Historic feedback remains the historic wording.

**Keep this draft** works with a blank, wrong, correct or syntactically invalid text draft and any valid working grouping. It freezes the exact pictured source, current separate/joined grouping, raw text, support, latest check if any, and prior-context qualification. It does not mark an unfilled ten-frame as wrong. The user may revise and keep a later copy explicitly; old checks are immutable. A history label distinguishes **“earlier grouping”** when the pictured model differs. An optional Hear button follows the existing Bundle Exchange click hook. Its `narration` support means the child explicitly requested local narration, not that speech actually played; a failed request still records the request, while the separate voice preview does not.

## Save, exposure and evidence boundary

Use one optional `joiningOrders` version-1 root with current known case ID, at most two known-ID case drafts, and optional opaque `recovered` data after an explicit recovery action. No root is allocated on app load, home, notes, chooser hover, or route entry alone; explicit **Start this order** allocates the first case. A started case owns its full frozen source, phase, origin-aware grouping, raw text, sticky support, current error/feedback selector, bounded checks and one latest kept draft. Rendering and import are pure. If the root is absent, notes stay absent. As in Bundle Exchange, an unknown, future or invalid root is read-only, escaped in notes and retained exactly by generic export/import; a visible **Keep the original and start fresh** action retains the entire raw payload. Do not normalize, regrade or delete an imported record just to render it.

Each newly made check freezes its **own** source, grouping, raw input, pre-feedback support, typed-number outcome, joined/regrouped observation flags and prior-check/familiarity status. Its source math comes from the frozen known case ID. On read, accept only the two fixed operand pairs for these version-1 IDs; require bounded, renderable frozen strings and raw input; validate every current, checked and kept grouping against the per-origin conservation, phase, frame-capacity and one-new-ten invariants. Check IDs must be unique safe positive integers below the next ID. Each check's numeric match must agree with its valid parsed raw value and the fixed sum; its joined/regrouped observations must agree with its frozen grouping; its pre-feedback support must be a subset of the case's sticky support. The current feedback pointer is null or references only the latest check whose raw text still equals the current raw text. These are fixed version-1 record-consistency checks, not a regrading of historical wording or later changed catalog rules. Reject a malformed known root as read-only and retain its exact raw payload. Historical display wording remains frozen, not rewritten against today's catalog. The latest **numeric** feedback stays current while the frozen operands and raw input are unchanged; moving counters does not change their sum. Its historical picture is labeled **“earlier grouping”** after a model change, and the separate live model note describes the new picture. Editing the raw text clears only current numeric feedback. Limit to eight stored/readable checks per case and one kept copy; before a ninth check, disclose that the oldest check will be removed from this case's local history when the new check is made. An earlier export may still hold it; the app does not archive it elsewhere. An oversized or future imported root is retained raw, not trimmed during reading. Check IDs increase monotonically within each playable case; refuse safe-integer exhaustion without mutating work. A fresh check after recovery or unknown prior history is conservatively familiar/unknown, never a new independent-first attempt.

`picturedSource`, `joined`, `handling`, `help`, `example`, `narration`, `together`, and `feedback` are separate support/context facts. `picturedSource` is supplied context added when **Start this order** creates the case, not a learner response. `joined` is added by a successful Join; `handling` by a successful counter move, Wrap or Unwrap; `help` when the help is opened; `example` when the separate example is opened; `narration` on a Hear click request; `feedback` after a successful numeric check, so that check's pre-feedback snapshot excludes newly supplied feedback. Provide an optional **We worked together** button beside help; it records `together` without changing the model, raw input or check. No support is added by a refused action, passive render, voice preview or route visit. Neither joining nor wrapping creates an arithmetic check or any Number Garden baseline answer. This mode passes `facts:[]` to the existing `exposeStoryFacts` hook: its count captions do not state or claim a multiplication fact. No new shared exact-example signature is emitted: these two-addend prompts are not the same task as the existing single-bundle total questions. Do not mark broad drafts, café/Bramble, Bundle Exchange, atlas progress or other saved roots as familiar through a guessed relation. Parent notes call this **“pictured joining and one entered total”**; they do not assert an independent addition strategy, written algorithm, grade, fluent calculation, or handwriting. Optional adult help and prior encounters remain visible qualifiers.

Keep the mode inside Bundle Burrow with a reciprocal link to `#numbers/exchange`, and connect it to existing explorer/save-off/reload/export/reset/notes flow. Use native buttons and input; keyboard, touch, 44px targets, focus restoration and local error descriptions must work at phone and tablet widths. Origin must have a non-color cue. No dragging, timed turn, autoplay, analytics, accounts, child identity, real supplies, server storage or new image license is needed. The source cards, countable counter art, compact summary and total entry must fit without sideways overflow.
