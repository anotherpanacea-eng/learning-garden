**Writing rule (binding; owner question 6 default):** the child's own writing is spoken or on paper and read by a person. Nothing the child writes or says is typed, recorded, stored or graded. On screen the child works only with supplied notices: orders a recount, matches reasons to an opinion, picks a closing line, picks the linking word, and chooses which supplied sentence to say aloud before writing. **Opinion rule (binding):** no opinion is ever keyed right or wrong, and the child's own opinion is never picked from a list that is kept. Items key only structure: reason-to-opinion fit where the card's stated facts decide it, order where the events carry times, a linking word whose job the card states, a closing line that restates the opinion, and a purpose the card names. A taste choice is labeled "Either works" and never keyed. **Research rule:** the shared question uses supplied Willowbank cards and two facts already audited for pack 161, never the child's own data.

## The knowledge

Sourced in the [audit](../source-audits/258-town-noticeboard.md); unconfirmed claims were cut.

**Opinion pieces (CCSS W.K.1, W.1.1, W.2.1, W.3.1).** At kindergarten, children draw, dictate or write to name a topic and state an opinion or preference. In grade 1 they introduce the topic, state an opinion, supply a reason and give some sense of closure. In grade 2 they supply reasons and use linking words (*because*, *and*, *also*) to connect opinion and reasons, and end with a concluding statement. In grade 3 they create a structure that lists reasons and use linking words and phrases such as *because*, *therefore*, *since* and *for example*. England's curriculum asks pupils to articulate and justify opinions in speaking and to consider others' opinions. An opinion is not right or wrong; a reason supports it when it is relevant to it.

**Recounts and purposes (NC Year 2; CCSS W.1.3, W.2.3).** Year 2 develops stamina for writing by writing about real events, about personal experiences and those of others, and for different purposes. Grade 1 narratives recount two or more sequenced events and use temporal words to signal order. A recount follows time order; a notice tells everyone what someone thinks or wants; a letter addresses one named reader; instructions give steps.

**Saying it first (NC Year 1, Year 2, Years 3-4).** Year 1 pupils say out loud what they are going to write about and compose a sentence orally before writing it. Year 2 pupils plan or say out loud what they will write and encapsulate it sentence by sentence. Years 3-4 pupils compose and rehearse sentences orally, including dialogue, building a varied vocabulary and range of sentence structures. Appendix 2 (Year 4) names fronted adverbials and the comma after them; a short opener's comma is often optional in style guides, so the text says "writers often put a comma". Quotation marks (UK: inverted commas) enclose a speaker's exact words. EEF guidance for ages 5-7 puts oral language first among its recommendations, and Graham and Perin's *Writing Next* (2007) finds explicit teaching of planning strategies helps writing.

**Reading aloud and publishing (NC Year 1, Year 2, Years 3-4, Years 5-6; CCSS W.K.6-W.3.6).** Year 1: read aloud their writing clearly enough to be heard by peers and teacher; Year 2: with appropriate intonation to make the meaning clear; Years 3-4: to a group, controlling tone and volume; Years 5-6: perform their own compositions. The CCSS ask for digital tools to produce and publish writing with adult support, and keyboarding from grade 3. This pack uses no typing (owner question 6 default); "publish" means putting writing where its readers will see it.

**Shared research (CCSS W.K.7, W.K.8, W.1.7, W.1.8, W.2.7, W.2.8).** Children take part in shared research and writing projects, and with adult support recall information from experiences or gather information from provided sources to answer a question. The two real facts are from pack 161's audit: a hen's egg, kept warm, hatches after about 21 days; marmots in the Pyrenees sleep in burrows for about half the year (161's wording; used here as a true card that does not answer the question). Hob's lambs in spring and the orchard keeper's hens sitting in spring are Willowbank fiction.

## The lesson

## Saved work

```
noticeboard: {
  v: 1,
  items:   { [itemId]: { ver, shown: [choiceId | stripId], pick, outcome, support: 0..3, at } },
  benches: { [benchId]: { ver, tasks: { [taskId]: { shown: [id], pick, outcome } }, at } }
}
```

`pick` is one id, an ordered id list (an order, or a set of tapped tiles) or a map of placements `{ [id]: slotId | pileId }`, or null. `outcome` is one of `'fit' | 'not-yet' | 'shown' | 'skipped'`. Items and graded bench tasks share this one shape: a tile set, a strip order, a time-word placement, a purpose match, a pin-it-up placement and a card sort are each one bench task.

Codes only: ids, ordered id lists and fixed outcome codes. Each kept item and each graded bench task keeps its authored version id (the item's, or its bench's), the ids in the order shown, the selection and its kept outcome code, so it restores without today's wording or key; a pick is checked only against its own `shown`. Validate only what the save holds against structural bounds (three choices; at most six tiles, strips or cards in one `shown`; placement keys only from the task's own `shown`); never require today's bench sizes, item lists, wording or answer key. A missing item reads as absent. Never recompute a kept outcome on load. `support` records the highest stuck-ladder step used, kept apart from the independent attempt and never merged. **Never stored:** the child's opinions, the "your turn" topic card, A5, E4, the read-aloud and its listening guide, the Either works pick, and anything written on paper. An unknown `v` or unreadable entry stays exact and exportable, only that bench turns read-only, and one bad item never closes the board. Export, import, reset and save-off follow the shared store and `prepareProgressImport` limits.
