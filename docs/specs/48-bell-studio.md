# Bell Studio: make a call, compare, revise

## Purpose and bounded first slice

The café and Willow Post Office are open again after the storm. Their neighbors want short, recognisable calls for gatherings and deliveries. Add an original **Bell Studio**, `#studio/bells`, where a child chooses simple computer tones, puts them in order, plays a short call, saves a version and changes it for an audience. This introduces an auditory/visual making mode alongside the existing puppet composition and text/number games. A call's purpose is discussed with a human; no sound has an automatically inferred emotion or universal social meaning.

The first slice is three pure tones plus a rest, fixed short beats, up to eight slots and four open invitation cards. No recording, microphone, speech recognition, live AI, upload, audio download service, external samples, instrument simulation, phonics or hearing test. Original sequences use no supplied commercial melody. Music notation, timbre, harmony, acoustics of real bells, variable tempo/dynamics, light signals and distance experiments remain later work.

Use compact **choose → make → compare → keep/revise** pages. The selected audience/purpose, complete current sequence and immediate action stay visible on each task page. All pages/cards remain open; empty, unusual or unfinished work can be kept. Café orders/stock, Post Office deliveries/rivals, stories, letters and other drafts remain exact. Entry links after existing café and Post Office tasks can point to the same studio; no duplicated game state or automatic destination change is needed.

## The exact tone model

Model `bell-studio-v1` has three labelled choices: **Low** (220 Hz), **Middle** (330 Hz), **High** (440 Hz), plus **Rest**. These names describe relative pitch within this set, not the limits of hearing or a child's ability. Each audible choice is a Web Audio sine oscillator with frequency fixed for the note. All have the same digital gain envelope, waveform type and duration. Do not silently vary gain with pitch. Explain: **“These are simple computer tones for our bell calls, not recordings of a metal bell.”** Actual bells have a more complicated sound; no authentic bell timbre or model of a struck object is claimed.

Every slot lasts exactly 0.5 seconds in the authored schedule. A tone sounds for 0.35 seconds, with an onset ramp from zero to a fixed peak gain of 0.025 over 0.015 seconds and a final 0.035-second ramp back to zero; the rest of the slot is silence. Rest uses the entire silent slot. No overlapping notes, chords, endless repeat, background music or automatic replay. At most eight slots means at most four seconds per explicitly requested play. These are design constants, not evidence of an ideal tempo or a measured sound-pressure level.

There is no gain/volume slider in the first slice. Fixed low **digital** gain does not guarantee a physical listening level: speakers, headphones, device volume and listening conditions differ. A short cue by Play says **“Start with your device volume low. Stop whenever you want.”** Do not label gain as decibels, promise safe SPL, require headphones, or raise gain automatically if someone reports hearing nothing. A child may choose the visual route throughout.

The pitch explanation keeps frequency separate from loudness: **“In these computer tones, faster repeated changes make a higher pitch. Higher pitch does not mean louder.”** Details can name hertz as repetitions per second. A static same-time diagram shows two, three and four cycles of equal vertical height for 220/330/440 Hz, with one common horizontal window; label it a schematic of the source signal changing over time, not a measurement of the speaker or a drawing of air moving up and down. It is not a travelling-wave simulation. Fixed gain is a controlled signal setting, not proof different frequencies sound equally loud to every listener. Do not map this slice as a loudness/amplitude experiment.

## Four original invitations

The request cards give literal compositional constraints, not a correct tune or an aesthetic score. The same notes can serve many purposes; ask a companion whether the resulting call is useful.

| Stable ID | Audience and purpose | Optional literal arrangement check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `cafe-gather` | Purl wants a repeatable call to gather neighbors for dinner. Choose the pitches. | Exactly five slots: two matching tones, one rest, then two matching tones. The second pair may use the same or a different pitch. |
| `post-arrival` | A courier wants a short arrival call that finishes higher than it begins. | Exactly three sounding-note slots, no rests; final frequency exceeds first. Middle note is freely chosen. |
| `garden-return` | Garden helpers want a three-note phrase they can recognise when it comes again. | Exactly six sounding-note slots, no rests; the final three repeat the first three in order. The child invents the phrase. |
| `own-call` | Choose a call for any imaginary garden gathering, then tell someone its purpose. | Any one to eight slots with at least one sounding note; no emotion, suitability or melody judgement. |

All four cards start with an empty sequence. No hidden melody or required starter answer. “Another invitation” cycles these four, preserving each paused call; revisits are labelled familiar. The catalogue is finite, but the child's combinations are open within the bounded model. One working sequence, one optional before-version and one latest explicitly kept draft per card avoid an unbounded composition archive.

## Make and revise

Use labelled **Low / Middle / High / Rest** buttons to append a slot. Appending changes the composition silently; it does not unexpectedly play a tone. Separate **Hear Low / Hear Middle / Hear High** controls explicitly preview one short tone. In the eight-slot sequence, select a slot, replace its note/rest, move it left/right, remove it or clear the working call. These native controls must work by touch and keyboard without dragging, hover or timed responses. At the length limit explain how to replace/remove a slot; do not discard an earlier note.

Represent pitch with three vertically ordered positions and redundant names/shapes, and time with equal horizontal slots. Rest has an explicit pause symbol and text. The vertical ladder denotes relative pitch only, not physical height, sound level or musical-staff notation. Show the complete ordered text equivalent, such as **Low, Low, Rest, High, High**. A narrow screen may wrap slot buttons into ordered rows with clear positions; do not shrink controls below 44 px or require page-wide horizontal scrolling.

**Play my call** plays one snapshot of the current sequence. Editing during play first stops it, then applies the edit; queued audio must never continue with stale notes. **Stop sound** remains available during any preview/call/comparison, including when the selected page is unchanged. Optional manual **Inspect previous/next slot** provides the full visual sequence without audio or automatic timing. A subtle current-slot border during requested playback is acceptable; avoid flashing, pulsing or waveform animation. Reduced motion removes transitions and keeps the same manual inspection route.

**Save this version to compare** explicitly freezes the current sequence and source card. Editing the working call leaves that before-version unchanged. Saving a replacement before-version is explicit and does not silently delete the latest kept draft. In Compare, **Play before** and **Play current** are separate controls; never overlap or alternate them automatically. Visual order, slot count and changed positions are shown for both versions. Ask **“What changed? What stayed the same? Does this call help its listener?”** Human reflection is optional, unscored and not recorded as free text in this slice.

**Check this arrangement** is optional and checks only the literal card rule above. Distinguish length, required pause/tones, repetition or final-pitch relation as applicable; feedback describes the actual model and leaves the sequence editable. Empty/all-rest/other sequences are retained, not labelled bad music. A successful rule match does not prove a listener understood a signal, sound discrimination, creative quality or independent musical skill. No gate, inventory reward, play credit or success-dependent animation follows.

## Optional pitch comparison

Three finite authored pairs: `rise` = Low then High; `fall` = High then Low; `same` = Middle then Middle. Each has the same gain envelope, 0.35-second tone length and 0.5-second slots as the studio. The pair's source notes and a **Show the pitch ladder** control remain available. **Play this pair** is explicit; no autoplay on pair/page selection. The learner may choose **higher / lower / same / not sure**, or skip. Feedback compares the model's second frequency with its first, phrased **“In this computer pair, the second tone is set higher/lower/the same.”** Never say “You heard correctly,” diagnose hearing or treat an answer as independent because the ladder was not opened.

Keep audio-request support, visual ladder/example support, together-help and familiarity separate. The choice may have been made using source labels, replay, a companion, memory or listening; the app cannot infer which. All results are supported practice. An audio start/finish receipt does not prove the child heard, attended or perceived the scheduled pitches. A visual-only response stays valid practice and never becomes an audio-listening result. Opening the schematic, inspecting a slot or playing an example creates no answer on its own.

## Local audio contract and lifecycle

Create/resume an `AudioContext` only from an explicit trusted Play/Hear activation; never on render, import, profile restore or card selection. Use one small studio player, not a general audio engine, service worker, polling loop or persistent scheduler. Play one validated snapshot; a new Play first cancels the prior run. A monotonic in-memory request token is sufficient to prevent a delayed `resume()` promise from starting sound after Stop, navigation, another Play or explorer switch.

Every scheduled oscillator connects through its own envelope gain to the output and has a finite scheduled stop. Only one tone is audible at once. Stop cancels future as well as active nodes, drops gain to zero, clears visual callbacks and disconnects resources; ignore harmless already-ended-node errors. Do not rely on stopping only the current oscillator. Repeated tapping cannot accumulate nodes or increase output. A source scheduled to start in the future must remain stopped after cancellation.

Stop on route/page/card change, any composition edit, explorer chooser/switch, progress import/reset, film opening, `pagehide`, and `visibilitychange` to hidden. Opening/playing local speech stops studio audio; studio Play stops speech. Reopening/resuming a page never restarts sound. Root integration can add a narrow `stopBellStudio` hook beside existing `stopSpeech` boundaries; this is not a reason to redesign all app media. Playback UI updates may change status/highlight DOM locally without rerendering the app and accidentally stopping itself. Visual/interval callbacks are bounded by the four-second run and canceled on stop.

If Web Audio is absent, context creation/resume rejects, a browser remains suspended, or scheduling fails, stop/clean up and say **“Sound is unavailable here. You can still build and compare the picture call.”** Do not retry automatically, claim successful playback, ask for microphone permission or hide the composition. A later explicit Play may try again. All-rest sequences have no oscillator and may use manual visual inspection without creating an audio context; empty sequences simply invite adding a tone. No audio runtime object, promise, timer, sample buffer, output identifier or device fingerprint belongs in saved progress. Do not use analyser output, microphone calibration or offline-render fingerprints.

## Keep and local state

“Keep this call draft” accepts empty/unfinished work. Freeze its card/purpose/model ID, exact working sequence, before-version if present, latest literal arrangement check, optional comparison observations, support and familiarity. Later edits/playback/help cannot mutate it. A new keep replaces only that card's latest kept draft. The four-slot shelf can reopen original cards; notes explain that these are authored computer-tone activities, not recordings or attained levels.

One optional `bellStudio` version-1 root follows existing recovery/storage conventions. Missing means fresh; read/render/notes validation is nonmutating. Unknown versions/keys, invalid frequency IDs or unsupported source snapshots remain exact and unavailable; explicit fresh-start retains the complete old payload under `recovered`. Import/export, saving off, explorer switching and clearing include the root, with escaped display. No names, dates, audio files, typed child prose, device IDs or network learner data.

Suggested bounded fields: `version`, `currentCard` null/four IDs, `page` (`choose|make|compare|keep`), `cards` keyed only by those four IDs, optional `recovered`. Each card has an immutable clone of source/model constants, `sequence` of 0–8 IDs (`low|middle|high|rest`), selected index null/valid, optional frozen before-version, sticky support/familiarity, at most 24 total practice checks and one latest kept draft. The optional comparison holds one of three authored pair IDs and its response history within the same bounded checks. No open frequency, gain, duration or oscillator type is accepted from an import. Source snapshot IDs/constants must match the supported model; never score an old unsupported source against a new note mapping.

Check snapshots contain task kind, exact source/sequence or comparison pair, response, independently derived literal result, support/familiarity and `practice:true`; validate semantics and integer bounds rather than trusting a stored correctness flag. Later help cannot retroactively change older checks. Label `audioRequested` as a request, not proof of hearing. Visual/comparison help and together flags stay separate. Do not import composition/check success into café/Bramble/adaptive math results. Use the existing shared-fact exposure hook if the actual interface explicitly brackets or names equal groups (for example two groups of three note slots) or displays a multiplication/division relationship. Sequence length or repetition alone does not create a fact exposure. Whether a relation is scored does not decide exposure: an actual displayed relationship still counts as familiarity and never creates an arithmetic answer. A literal phrase check does not establish multiplication proficiency.

Suggested core actions: choose card/page; append/select/replace/move/remove/clear slot; freeze before-version; check arrangement; choose comparison pair/record response; mark support; keep; recover. Pure helpers validate/enumerate notes, derive a finite playback plan and describe pitch/order changes. A small local audio adapter consumes that plan and exposes play/stop only; browser objects stay outside core state. Use dependency injection for a fake AudioContext in targeted lifecycle tests, not a permanent telemetry framework.
