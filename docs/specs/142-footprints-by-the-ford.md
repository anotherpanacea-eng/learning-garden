## Purpose

The merged [Fossil Close-Look](106-fossil-close-look.md) (`#living/fossils`) gives one careful look at a body fossil and a trace fossil, and leaves out formation, deep time, extinction and fieldwork on purpose. In the [six-act strands](../six-act-strands.md) table, S8's Dinosaurs & Paleontology domain lists 29 source topics and none with current scoped references. S1 has fair tests and observation, but no case where a measurement becomes an *estimate* through a stated rule of thumb.

This spec adds (1) **a science pack**: real Mesozoic dinosaurs, story creatures and non-dinosaur neighbors, teeth as diet clues, fossil formation and rarity, deep time out to the pyramids, a gentle K-Pg extinction, birds as living dinosaurs, Mary Anning and changing ideas; and (2) **the Trackway Table**, where the explorer measures footprints and strides on a made-up muddy bank, estimates hip height, works out relative stride and judges walking or running, always as an estimate with a range.

A child comes to understand that dinosaurs were real and known from fossils, that a footprint records something an animal did, that a long stride means different things for a big animal and a small one, and that scientists label guesses and change their minds when new fossils turn up.

## The knowledge

Claims are sourced in the source audit (fact-check pass 2026-10-25); the only remaining `[check]` items are listed there.

- **Real, and long ago.** Dinosaurs were real animals known from fossil bones, teeth, footprints, eggs and droppings. They lived in the **Mesozoic Era**, about 252 to 66 million years ago: **Triassic** (about 252 to 201), **Jurassic** (about 201 to 145), **Cretaceous** (about 145 to 66). Boundaries carry uncertainties of a few million years; the Jurassic-Cretaceous boundary is still not formally fixed. Different dinosaurs lived at different times: more time separates Stegosaurus (about 150 million years ago) from Tyrannosaurus (about 68 to 66) than separates Tyrannosaurus from us (about 80 million years against about 66 to 68). No people lived with non-bird dinosaurs; people came tens of millions of years later.
- **Neighbors and stories.** Pterosaurs (flying reptiles) and plesiosaurs, ichthyosaurs and mosasaurs (sea reptiles) lived in the same era and were not dinosaurs. The pack's rule for its prehistoric cases: dinosaurs were land animals with legs straight under the body (birds are the stated exception). Dragons are stories; Ember is a story character. Films add people, drop feathers and exaggerate size; color and voice are mostly unknown from bones.
- **Teeth tell diet.** Sharp, pointed, often serrated teeth cut flesh; blunt, peg-like, leaf-shaped or flat ridged teeth strip or grind plants. The largest dinosaurs (sauropods) ate plants. Sharp teeth do not imply big prey; plant-eaters could carry horns, plates or spikes. Two categories only.
- **Fossil formation.** Death; quick burial in sediment, often under water, before rotting or scavenging; soft parts decay and hard parts remain; over very long times minerals fill and often replace the hard parts as sediment hardens to rock; erosion later exposes it. Burial is needed and guarantees nothing. Most living things leave no fossil.
- **Kinds of fossils** (revisit of 106). Body fossils (bone, tooth, shell); trace fossils (footprints, trackways, burrows, **coprolites**). A footprint is a mark, never the foot. Eggs stay out of scored sorts because eggshell can be read either way.
- **Trackways.** A **pace** runs from one print to the next; a **stride** from one print to the next print of the same foot (about two paces). Hind prints only, in a narrow line, suggest two legs; smaller front prints suggest four. Three slim toes suggest a theropod or some ornithopods; broad round prints suggest sauropods. No print names a species, and tracks are usually found without bones. Bigger prints usually mean a bigger animal. Gait is judged by stride for size, never by raw stride or depth (depth also reflects mud softness). Parallel trackways may mean a group, or a path used at different times.
- **The bench's rules of thumb.** R. McNeill Alexander (1976, *Nature* 261, "Estimates of speeds of dinosaurs") linked stride, hip height and speed using living animals and gave the widely used **hip height ≈ 4 × footprint length**. Thulborn (about 1989 to 1990) used group-specific multipliers (about 4.5 small theropods, 4.9 large theropods, 5.9 large ornithopods); a later model test (Henderson 2003) found the simple 4× rule fitted best overall, with large errors possible for some big animals. **Relative stride = stride ÷ hip height**; similar relative strides mean similar gaits across sizes. Cut-offs: **under about 2.0 walking, about 2.0 to 2.9 trotting or in between, over about 2.9 running**. Alexander (1976) put the walk-to-trot change near 2.0, from living mammals; Thulborn and Wade (1984) added the trot-to-run change near 2.9. Both come from living animals, so for dinosaurs they are assumptions. Trackway studies find most dinosaur trackways show walking (in one large theropod sample, 96% of strides; herbivore trackways rarely exceed a relative stride of 1.5); running trackways are scarce. A recent guineafowl study found firm-ground speed formulas overestimate speed from soft-mud tracks. The bench shows gait categories only and never a speed.
- **The Pyrenees.** At **Fumanya** (Berguedà, Catalonia), Maastrichtian rock (the last stage of the Cretaceous) holds nearly 3,000 footprints in more than 50 trackways attributed to **titanosaurs**, among the largest Late Cretaceous sauropod tracksites in Europe; erosion has destroyed some. The **Tremp Formation** has yielded bones, eggs, nesting sites and tracks close to the end of the Cretaceous, including hadrosaur tracks; the Museu de la Conca Dellà in Isona displays dinosaur fossils from the area, and Dinosfera at Coll de Nargó shows a large dinosaur nest. Willowbank, the ford and the slab are fictional.
- **K-Pg, told gently.** About 66 million years ago about three-quarters of plant and animal species died out, including all non-bird dinosaurs. Evidence: a worldwide iridium-rich clay layer (Alvarez team, 1980) and the buried **Chicxulub** crater under Mexico's Yucatán, about 180 km across in classic gravity studies and up to about 200 km in recent estimates, of matching age. Most life was lost to after-effects: soot and dust blocked sunlight, photosynthesis collapsed, and food chains failed. The impact is the most widely accepted main cause; huge Deccan Traps eruptions in India, which spanned the boundary, are still debated as a contributor. Birds survived. Child text: "most kinds of animals died out"; no spectacle.
- **Birds are dinosaurs.** Birds sit inside the theropods; shared features include a wishbone, hollow bones and three-toed feet. *Sinosauropteryx*, shown in 1996 from China, was the first non-bird dinosaur found with feather-like filaments. Hollis is, in a way, a dinosaur.
- **Mary Anning (1799 to 1847)**, Lyme Regis, working-class and largely self-taught: with her brother Joseph the first recognized ichthyosaur (skull 1811, skeleton 1812); a nearly complete plesiosaur (1823); England's first pterosaur, *Dimorphodon* (1828). Marine and flying reptiles, not dinosaurs; key evidence that extinction was real. She was not invited to the Geological Society meeting that debated her plesiosaur, and men often published her finds without naming her.
- **Changing knowledge.** Feathered fossils turned many scaly reconstructions feathered. Tail-dragging reconstructions gave way to tail-up animals, helped by trackways, which almost never show tail-drag marks; Gideon Mantell placed Iguanodon's thumb spike on its nose as a horn until the near-complete skeletons found at Bernissart, Belgium, in 1878 showed it belonged on the hand. Each change is paired with its evidence.

## The game or activity

### The Trackway Table (`#living/trackways`)

A tray of smooth mud, drawn from above, with a ruler on one edge. **Always visible:** *These are made-up trackways for practice. The rules give estimates, not exact answers.*

**Model `tt-v1`** (authored, fictional, centimeters; hip height from 4× and 5× footprint length; relative stride = stride ÷ hip height; zones: under 2.0 walking, 2.0 to 2.9 in between, over 2.9 running; assumptions: one steady gait along the trackway, flat ground, the multiplier range stands in for differences between kinds of trackmaker):

| ID | Prints | Footprint | Stride | Hip height (×4 to ×5) | Relative stride | Reading |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | three-toed, two legs, big | 40 | 280 | 160 to 200 | 1.75 to 1.40 | walking either way |
| B | three-toed, two legs, small | 10 | 160 | 40 to 50 | 4.00 to 3.20 | running either way |
| C | three-toed, two legs, middle | 20 | 180 | 80 to 100 | 2.25 to 1.80 | cannot tell: in between or walking |
| D | round hind prints, smaller front prints | 60 | 300 | 240 to 300 | 1.25 to 1.00 | walking either way |
| E1-E3 | three parallel small three-toed trackways | 12 | 90 | 48 to 60 | 1.875 to 1.50 | walking either way |

**Interaction** (native buttons, 44 px targets, no drag): choose a trackway card; tap a print and **Measure** (ruler snaps heel to toe, text reading); tap a print then another to **find a stride** (opposite foot gives a pace with `pace-stride` feedback; a farther same-foot print gives a double stride note); footprints-per-stride icons appear; optional **Estimate hip height** shows both multipliers and a range bar; **relative stride** for both ends is placed as a band on a 0 to 5 gait strip; the explorer chooses *walking*, *in between*, *running* or *can't tell from this range*, and the bench explains what the band covers, with no right/wrong mark. Any trackmaker figure is a gray outline labeled *a guess at the shape*. Every result card ends: *Estimate from a rule of thumb. Real trackmakers varied.* Optional **Keep this reading**.

**Busy bank:** a fixed picture of crossing trackways for counting items (Set 3).
