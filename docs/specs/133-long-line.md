## Purpose

The Travelers' Archive already puts three dated Paris events in order on an **ordinal** strip, and [spec 46](46-travelers-archive-first-visit.md) deliberately left calendar subtraction, centuries and BCE out of its first release. The Egyptian writing book ([spec 95](95-egypt-writing-book.md)) shows "about 2030 BCE" with no date arithmetic or year-zero lesson. The new archive packs ([134](134-nile-year.md) Egypt, [135](135-roads-and-water.md) Rome, [136](136-who-gets-a-voice.md) Athens, [137](137-mills-bridges-pilgrims.md) medieval Europe, [138](138-keepers-of-books.md) medieval worlds beyond Europe) will each bring dates that are thousands of years apart and on both sides of the BCE/CE line. Nothing in the game yet teaches how to read or place them.

This bench fills that gap in strand **H1** (Historical inquiry, H1-I "dates and event order", carried into H1-II/III through uncertain dates and how historians write them). Marble's History > Historical Thinking domain has 7 source topics, 3 with current scoped references ([six-act strands](../six-act-strands.md)); the chronology vocabulary (BCE/CE, century, decade, timeline) sits inside "Vocabulary: historical thinking" and is not yet taught anywhere. It also gives **S8** its first contrast case: the strand map says historical, geological and astronomical timescales "cannot be one undifferentiated timeline", and this bench shows why with a number.

A child comes to understand that:

1. Putting things **in order** and spacing them **by time** are different jobs. Evenly spaced cards say "before and after"; a scaled line says "how long".
2. BCE years count **down** toward the BCE/CE line and CE years count **up** from it. There is no year 0, so 1 BCE to 1 CE is one year.
3. The 1400s are the **15th century**, and a decade is ten years (the 1960s).
4. "About" and "c." are honest words. Historians write a range when the evidence gives one, and a date can change when new evidence turns up.
5. A story's time (Willowbank's) never goes on the real line, and a legend's date is a story people told, kept apart from the evidence.
6. Deep time is so long that it needs a **different scale**. On the history line's scale, the end of the dinosaurs would be kilometers away.

## The knowledge

All dates are checked in the [source audit](../source-audits/133-long-line.md). "About" is kept wherever the sources disagree or estimate.

### Order and scale

- An **ordinal** line keeps order only; equal spaces do not mean equal time. A **scaled** line keeps a fixed amount of time per unit of length (here, 1 box = 100 years), so distance means time.
- On a number line, the amount is the **space between** two marks, not the number of marks counted.

### BCE, CE and no year zero

- **CE** (Common Era) and **BCE** (Before Common Era) use the same numbers as AD and BC. 400 BCE and 400 BC are the same year.
- BCE numbers get **smaller** as time moves toward the BCE/CE line: 2550 BCE is earlier than 753 BCE.
- The calendar has **no year 0**: 1 BCE is followed directly by 1 CE. Years between a BCE date and a CE date = BCE number + CE number − 1. Between two BCE dates or two CE dates, subtract.
- Astronomers do use a year 0 (equal to 1 BCE) to make arithmetic easier, a numbering introduced by Jacques Cassini in 1740. Keeper's note only.
- This is the boundary with negative numbers: counting down through zero on a thermometer passes 0; counting down through the calendar does not.

### Decades and centuries

- A decade is ten years: 1969 is in the 1960s.
- The 1st century CE is years 1 to 100, so a century's number is one more than its "hundreds": 1455 is in the 15th century, 1086 in the 11th, 1969 in the 20th. BCE centuries work the same way going back: 447 BCE is in the 5th century BCE.
- Strictly, the 15th century runs 1401 to 1500, so 1500 belongs to it; in everyday use "the 1400s" means 1400 to 1499. The bench teaches the everyday rule first and gives the strict boundary at the Keeper's Desk. This is also why some people said the third millennium began on 1 January 2001.

### About, c. and ranges

- **c.** (circa) means "about". Historians write "c. 1455" when the evidence points near a year, and a range ("2589-2566 BCE") when it gives a span.
- Different chronologies can give different dates. Khufu's reign appears as about 2589-2566 BCE in some lists and 2551-2528 BCE in The Met's object records; the Great Pyramid is dated to **about 2550 BCE** (Harvard's Digital Giza, the date spec 134 uses). These chronologies disagree; about 2550 BCE is the chosen Digital Giza approximation, not a date inside both reign ranges. An earlier draft said 2560 BCE, which falls before The Met's start of the reign; it was aligned to spec 134 in review.
- Dates can change with evidence. The Pont du Gard was long said to date from about 19 BCE (credited to Agrippa); excavations now point to the **middle of the 1st century CE**, usually given as about 40-60 CE; the site itself says around 50 CE, which the card and items use.
- A **legend's date** is a date people gave to a story. Roman tradition put the founding of Rome by Romulus on 21 April 753 BCE, a date worked out by the scholar Varro in the 1st century BCE, centuries later. Archaeology shows people living in huts on the Palatine Hill from about the 10th century BCE, with the hut village lasting into the 7th century BCE. Both are on the bench, labeled differently.

### Deep time (S8 contrast)

- Non-avian dinosaurs died out about **66 million years ago**, at the end of the Mesozoic Era (about 252 to 66 million years ago).
- *Stegosaurus* lived in the Late Jurassic, **about 150 million years ago**. More time separates *Stegosaurus* from the end of the dinosaurs (about 84 million years) than separates the end of the dinosaurs from us (about 66 million years).
- Earth is about 4.5 billion years old (Keeper's note only).
- On the Long Line's scale (1 box = 100 years; on paper, 1 cm = 100 years), 66 million years ago would be 660,000 cm away: **6.6 km**. On a deep-time scale of 1 cm = 1 million years, all 5,068 years from Egypt's unification to the Moon landing take up about 0.05 mm.

**Top misconceptions designed against** (each is a tagged wrong choice somewhere in the sets):

1. Evenly spaced cards mean equal amounts of time.
2. A bigger BCE number is a later year.
3. There is a year 0, so 1 BCE to 1 CE is two years (or zero).
4. The century is the first two digits (1455 is the "14th century").
5. "About" or a range means the date is wrong or nobody knows anything.
6. A legend's date, or a story's event, belongs on the Long Line as history.
7. Deep time fits on the history line, or dinosaurs were only a little before the pyramids.
8. Counting marks instead of spaces on a scaled line.

## The game or activity

**Two strips, one wall.** Cards sit in a tray below the wall. Tap a card to pick it up, then tap a slot to place it (drag optional; keyboard: arrow keys move a picked card, Enter places it). Placed cards can always be moved. **Check** reads the current arrangement and gives per-card feedback; it never rearranges cards.

**Willowbank strip:** ordinal slots only, caption *Order only. A story from Willowbank.* No numbers, no scale, ever. A **Not sure when** tray holds cards with no known time. A real-history card dropped here slides back to the tray with the line *This is a true thing from history. It goes on the Long Line.* (and the reverse for story cards on the Long Line). The two strips never share a card, a slot or a scale.

**The Long Line** has two views, switched with one control:

- **In a row:** cards evenly spaced in order. Caption: *Order only. Spaces do not show time.*
- **Spaced by time:** the same cards slide to scaled positions (animated, with reduced-motion fallback of a fade). Scale: **1 box = 100 years**, ticks every 100 years, labels every 500. The line runs from 3500 BCE to 2100 CE (56 boxes) inside its own scroll area; the page itself never scrolls sideways. At narrow widths (320 px) the line turns vertical, long ago at the top. A marked boundary reads *1 BCE ends here. 1 CE begins. There is no year 0.* Range cards draw as a bar from start to end. "About" cards carry a soft fuzzy edge of plus or minus one box, explained in the card details.

**Placing tasks:** pick a card, tap a point on the scaled line. A readout shows the year under the finger, rounded to the nearest 10 years. Placement is accepted within plus or minus 50 years (half a box), stated in the task. **Simplified picture:** *Boxes stand for time. At this scale one year is too thin to see.*

**Counting tasks:** a jump arrow from one card to another with its length labeled. Across the boundary, the guided steps show the two parts (back to the boundary, then forward) and the "minus one" with the reason.

**Zoom-out lens:** a button, *Zoom out to deep time*. The Long Line shrinks to a bright sliver at the right end, and a new scale appears: **1 box = 1 million years**. Two deep-time cards (*Stegosaurus*, about 150 million years ago; the end of the dinosaurs, about 66 million years ago) are placed on this scale only. Caption: *A different scale. The whole Long Line fits inside this sliver.* Deep-time cards can never be dropped on the Long Line; trying shows *This one needs the deep-time scale.* The lens links to Fossil Close-Look and to [spec 142, Footprints by the Ford](142-footprints-by-the-ford.md), when built.

**Card details** (tap the card's small "i"): what the date means, why it carries "about" or a range, and a source in brief. Legend cards carry the label *A story people told* on the face.
