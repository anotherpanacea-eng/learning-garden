## Finite physical model

Three hypothetical constant-power winches: **Reed 20 W**, **Willow 40 W**, **Oak 60 W**. Each transfers energy mechanically from an unspecified external source to a raised load's gravitational store. This is a number model, not a simulation of a particular load/height/force. We ignore losses; power here is the rate of energy delivered to that store, NOT an appliance's input rating. No energy is created: source loses the same amount the load gains. No source reservoir or finite fuel supply is modelled. All quantities are model stipulations. No real winch, motor or appliance procedure.

One watt means one joule transferred per second. Each machine's chosen duration is independently 0…60 seconds in steps of 5; all start at zero. Fresh target is1200J for the current source; a generic fresh source starts at its last target. A tap **Add 5 seconds** advances MODEL time instantly, never wall time; **Remove 5 seconds** revises the hypothetical run, not a claim that physical energy flows backward. At 60/0 the relevant control is disabled. Energy in joules = watts × seconds. At zero seconds energy is zero; never divide by zero. No autoplay, timer or inferred speed from taps. Each tap fills or removes one five-second energy segment, and the three bars share a fixed 0…3600 J axis, not independently normalized percentages. Keep numeric W, seconds and J labels visible; color is redundant.

Two open turns, **Choose durations** and **Same energy job**, retain independent drafts. Choose durations: child controls all three durations; prompt first compare equal times, then invite different durations and compare both rates and totals. The current source supports Reed60s (1200J) versus Oak10s (600J), but reusable child prompts must not assume those names, durations or unequal powers in a historical sheet. No answer choices, correctness, score or mastery inference. Same job: choose supplied target600 or1200J; Add5seconds stops a machine at its own target time, and cannot exceed it.
