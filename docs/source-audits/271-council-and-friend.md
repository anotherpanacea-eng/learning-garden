Date: 2026-10-26. Writer's first pass for [spec 271](../specs/271-council-and-friend.md) and its [companion text](../content/271-council-and-friend-text.md).

Merriam-Webster's page returned 403. No research text is quoted at length.

## Cut or kept out of child text

- Any invented Willowbank dialect: cut, to avoid caricature; dialect comes only from S4.
- The book's "too common" and "untrained Yorkshire rustic" framing and the housekeeper's ban on Martha's Yorkshire: kept out of child text; named in the grown-up panel.
- Common Core L.6.1e's "own speaking" half: not taught (owner default); stated in the spec.
- Glosses "tha" as "said to a friend": cut; with the laugh line gone, no card uses tha.
- Dickon's "Tha'll make him laugh" and item C4 on it (reviewer, 2026-10-26): cut. It taught that Mary's Yorkshire is for laughs, and its distractor "Mary speaks it badly" was half-supported by the book (Dickon is amused by her attempts). Replaced by Dickon's springtime line and the narrator's "she loved his broad Yorkshire".
- The reply card's claim that the council's *les* is the formal "you": cut. *Ustedes* (object *les*) is the only plural "you" in Latin American Spanish, so it does not mark formality; *Atentamente* does.
- "Only when alone" for Mrs. Medlock: the grown-up panel quotes the book's one instance rather than saying she speaks Yorkshire "only" when alone.
- Any claim that one plan is best, or that standard forms are "needed" or "correct": none appears.
