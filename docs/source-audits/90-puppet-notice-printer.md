## Actual local ownership and selected gap

- `dist/instruction-ui.mjs:stripPage/runPanel` assembles supplied **complete sentences** and plays known action transitions. `wordsPage/humanPage` deliberately keeps optional person-read prose separate from that runner. Its underlying semantic actions never come from parsing original words. [Spec54](../specs/54-instructions-for-a-reader.md) owns that multi-sentence sequencing/reader-preparation contrast.
- `dist/letter-trail-ui.mjs:letterPage/keepControls` adapts the message UI using full revision cards, then separates human-read original messages and familiar replays. It does not reorder individual words or manipulate sentence boundaries.
- `dist/loft-ui.mjs` already exposes dialogue and scene-story work. Another unrestricted text field would duplicate that existing loop. The new printer links back to person-read Loft writing rather than introducing another prose store or grader.
- [E2-I in six-act-strands](../six-act-strands.md) names complete sentences, boundaries and questions; E4-I owns composing/revising for a reader. The proposed small controlled word/mark transformation is an E2 foothold with an E4 connection. It is neither the full sentence-building sequence nor a new language-level assessment.

The new action is explicitly **word order + visible first capital + ending mark**. Same fixed words can be a supplied telling line, supplied question or authored playful role reversal. Every other arrangement is unmodeled, not classified as bad language. This differs from rearranging already complete instructions or picking a complete revision. The printed-line display, source-owned scripts and native ticket moves are enough; no general parser, syntax tree, model service or universal lesson engine is needed.

## Primary convention guidance and limits

Independently opened official [CCSS Language Kindergarten](https://www.thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/L/K/) and [Language1](https://www.thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/L/1/) during this authoring pass. L.K.2a concerns the first-word capital; L.K.2b concerns recognizing/naming end punctuation; L.1.2b concerns using sentence-ending punctuation. L.1.1j includes constructing different sentence forms. These support the selected printed-convention contrast; the game does not establish full command of those standards or assign a learner grade. In particular, L.1.2a concerns dates/names, so it is not mislabeled as the first-word-capital standard.

Question marks, intonation and word order interact in real language. A statement-order echo question may be meaningful, and a surprising arrangement may work in a story. Therefore the app reports only exact model coverage, a known scripted purpose contrast and directly visible formatting. It does not generalize an unrecognized string into an ungrammatical sentence or judge dialect, oral phrasing, AAC, spelling or a person's interpretation. Puppet replies do not testify to real comprehension. Open person-read writing remains in the existing Loft/Instructions surfaces.
