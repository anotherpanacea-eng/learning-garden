## Review pass (independent fact-check, 2026-10-25)

Fifteen searches (excerpts only). Results:

- **Bears:** OFB *Ours infos 2024* gives at least 96 detected in 2024 (13 litters, 22 cubs), estimate 104 (97 to 123), 83 minimum for 2023; the 2025 release revises the 2024 minimum retained to 107 and gives at least 108 for 2025. Child text uses one dated figure (96, 2024 report); the grown-up panel names the revision as a teaching point. Consistent across spec and companion.
- **Condor:** 27 in April 1987; 561 (347 wild) at 31 December 2022 per USFWS. USFWS's 2024 status report revises 2022 to 564 (350 wild); the dated 2022 figures are kept as first published.
- **Red List:** desman Endangered globally (IUCN page; 2021 per S1); Alpine marmot and brown bear Least Concern (global species). Cleared.
- **Bucardo:** last one found dead in Ordesa, January 2000 (day and cause differ: 5 or 6 January; tree or avalanche). Text gives only the year and month. Cleared.
- **Bearded vulture:** last Alpine bird shot in the Aosta Valley, 1913 (VCF, Alpenzoo); first release 1986, Hohe Tauern; first wild chick 1997 (France). Cleared. The Pyrenean feeding-site claim was cut; the "What people think" panel now says persecution (shooting, poisoning) stopped, which VCF names as the cause of loss.
- **Gray squirrel:** 1876 Henbury release; squirrelpox carried without illness; most infected reds die; competition also named. Cleared.
- **Dodo:** last widely accepted sighting 1662 (Oxford museum); spec now says that instead of "within about 80 years".
- **Pigs from wild boar** (S24), **tawny owl tree hollows** (S22), **desman snout by touch** (S23), **goldfish harm** (S25): cleared.
- **Marmot:** introduced 1948 to 1988, about 500 animals (Conservation Evidence 7394), from Alpine stock; called both introduction and reintroduction. Grazing and burrowing impacts on Pyrenean grassland are suspected but little measured, so "no harm reported" became "any harm is not well measured yet". Consistent with draft 161 ("introduced ... from 1948").
- **Chamois:** studies of Alpine chamois find it cathemeral (active by day and night). Removed from the Night Watch "asleep" list; the barn swallow (draft 144) replaces it.
- **Patou:** a French word, so the guardian dog now comes from Les Saules (French-speaking, spec 120), not Vadoalto. Vadoalto is established in spec 120.
- Cleared as standard natural history without a search: tawny owls hoot to hold territory; foxes and badgers scent-mark; dipper nest beside its feeding stream.
- Pedagogy: shortened four right answers that were longest (A3, A7, B4, C6). Positions A 5, B 5, C 4. Math re-run in `check166r.py`.

## Rights notes for images

No images are embedded. For art reference prefer public-domain or open-access sources: USFWS images of the California condor (US government works, generally public domain; confirm each), museum open-access specimens (thylacine, dodo) under CC0 where the museum states it, and commissioned original drawings for the valley animals.
