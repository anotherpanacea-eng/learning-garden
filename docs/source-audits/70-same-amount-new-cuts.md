## Fresh primary mathematical references

Freshly opened the official [Common Core3.NF domain page](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/3/NF/). A.1 defines fractions through equal parts of a whole; A.2a/b define the0–1 whole and iterated unit lengths from zero. A.3a/b support equivalence as equal size/same point and examples including1/2=2/4 and4/6=2/3. A.3d explicitly conditions fraction-size comparisons on the same whole. These facts support aligned same-whole ribbons; no worksheet, diagram, prose or standard text is copied into the product.

Also freshly opened [Common Core4.NF.A.1](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/4/NF/A/1/): visual models can explain how number/size of parts change while the represented fraction stays the same. This is an authoritative content reference, not an additional mapped attainment claim or justification for a general multiplication algorithm. The standalone3.NF.A.3 page was opened too but gives only the high-level equivalence/comparison statement; the parent domain page supplies its detailed subparts.

A longer whole could have the same fractional label and a different absolute selected length. Equal ribbon widths keep the length and shaded-area views consistent; do not compare shaded areas of differently sized rectangles as if their wholes were identical. Count intervals rather than cut/boundary marks. More smaller equal parts can cover the same selected length; adding cuts does not add ribbon. Reject the generalisation that equal numerators or visually equal shaded areas on arbitrary different wholes prove equivalence. These references describe mathematical content, not a measured learning effect of this interface.
