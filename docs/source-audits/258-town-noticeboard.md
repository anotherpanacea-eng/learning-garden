# Source audit: spec 258, The Town Noticeboard

Date: 2026-10-26. Writer's first pass for [spec 258](../specs/258-town-noticeboard.md) and its [companion text](../content/258-town-noticeboard-text.md).

**Method and limits.** The English national curriculum was read from the DfE programme of study text held in the writer's scratch (`nc-english.txt`, the 2013 framework, Open Government Licence). The Common Core writing standards were read from thecorestandards.org pages for kindergarten and grades 1-3 (four page fetches). Two searches checked the EEF Key Stage 1 guidance and the Chicago Manual of Style rule on introductory commas. The two science facts are not re-sourced: they are reused from pack 161's audit, which is on `main`. Nothing was quoted at length; standards are paraphrased in the spec.

## Fiction and models (no source needed; labeled in the text)

Willowbank, the town noticeboard outside Willow Post Office, the weekly market and its stalls, Pip's soup notice and Bramble's no-soup notice, Dot's roof notice and her hat poster, the baker's sign, Dot's stall list (seven, nine, noon, five), Pip's recount and his letter to Ember, Wren Halloway's spring question, Hob's lambs in spring, the orchard keeper's hens sitting in spring, the miller's cat by the oven, and Pip's unchecked guess about ducklings. All are labeled "A story from Willowbank". Pip's duckling card is never keyed true or false, only "still to check".
