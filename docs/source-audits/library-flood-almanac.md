## Historical sources checked

1. [Archives de Paris, *Repères chronologiques : la crue de janvier 1910*](https://archives.paris.fr/image/303449/11801454?crop=centre&download=1&force-inline=&format=pdf&realHeight=1754&realWidth=1240&region=full&size=!800,800), reached from its [official teaching page](https://archives.paris.fr/offre-pedagogique/ressources-pedagogiques/la-crue-de-1910-une-societe-face-aux-risques). This is a modern educational chronology, not an eyewitness account; its exact publication date is not stated in the PDF. It records the highest Seine level at the Austerlitz gauge on **28 January 1910**, formation of the government flood commission on **9 February 1910**, and the Seine's return to its riverbed on **15 March 1910**. The PDF also notes rail and metro services resuming then, but the draft does not use that extra claim. It does not date the drying of every street.
2. [Archives de Paris, after the flood](https://archives.paris.fr/offre-pedagogique/ressources-pedagogiques/la-crue-de-1910-une-societe-face-aux-risques/lapres-crue-de-la-revision-du-systeme-de-protection-a-la-memoire-de-la-catastrophe) says the commission was charged with studying causes and means to prevent or lessen similar disasters. The draft's shorter commission sentence is an original paraphrase.
3. [Musée Carnavalet / Paris Musées, PH8646](https://www.parismuseescollections.paris.fr/fr/musee-carnavalet/oeuvres/crue-de-la-seine-janvier-1910-quai-des-grands-augustins) catalogues a photograph of the January 1910 flood at Quai des Grands-Augustins, attributes it to **Identité judiciaire**, gives a production date of **29 January 1910**, and describes a boat. The exact moment photographed is not established by that production date. I inspected the project's already credited full-frame local reproduction, `dist/assets/archive-paris-1910-ph8646.jpg`: water, boats and buildings are visible. [Spec 46](../specs/46-travelers-archive-first-visit.md) documents the exact museum hero visual and its separate **CC0** label and credit. This new pack uses no photograph or other asset, so it needs no image field or new rights grant.

| Draft wording or implication | Basis and limit |
| --- | --- |
| The three fixed dated sentences and chronological order | Archives chronology, dates and events above. “Highest mark” is specifically the Austerlitz measurement, not a depth everywhere. |
| “The commission was asked to study causes and ways to lessen future floods” | Official after-flood page. This describes the commission's remit, not a claim that future floods were prevented. |
| One photograph showing floodwater, a boat and buildings at a Paris quay | Museum record plus visual inspection of the exact credited PH8646 reproduction. The photograph is described in text only in this pack. |
| One photograph cannot show every street or anyone's thoughts | Limit of one image, not a new claim about unseen streets or people. The talk prompt's “might” is conjecture, and no answer is scored. |
| “A highest mark is one point in a longer event”; photograph and chronology tell different parts | Inference from the dated chronology and the single-scene photograph, labelled as explanation rather than a new historical fact. |
| The flood happened in real Paris while the Learning Garden town is imagined | Official Paris chronology; the town, Lucie, and the almanac framing are project fiction. No fictional character is presented as a 1910 witness. |

The six shared passage sentences are original modern prose. The source-credit sentence names both institutions in every length; it is not a quotation, a claim that the chronology is contemporary, or an assertion that the two records are independent witnesses. No rainfall amount, street depth, citywide dry date, photographed person's motive or feeling, casualty, safety instruction, historical voice, or source image is included.
