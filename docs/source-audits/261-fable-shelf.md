# Source audit: spec 261, The Fable Shelf

Date: 2026-10-25. Writer's first pass for [spec 261](../specs/261-fable-shelf.md) and its [companion text](../content/261-fable-shelf-text.md).

**Method and limits.** The public-domain texts were read in full from Project Gutenberg plain-text files (Townsend, Jacobs, Grimm/Hunt, Mooney, Carroll) and Watts from the University of Toronto's Representative Poetry Online, saved to the writer's scratch and searched by script. Other claims were checked by web search (13 searches) and page fetches (8; Merriam-Webster, Wikisource and Pitt pages refused fetches, so their claims rest on search excerpts). Nothing here quotes research text; quoted lines are from public-domain works. Claims not confirmed are marked `[check]` and kept out of child text.

## Open checks

- Townsend's first-edition year: 1865 or 1867 (S17). Child text says only "the 1800s", true either way.
- Resolved in review: "La Tortue et les deux Canards" is X, 2, printed 1679; moral line confirmed (S16). `[check]` which telling La Fontaine actually borrowed from (child text says only that he read Indian fables in French).
- `[check]` La Fontaine's "Le Chat et le Renard" moral ("on perd du temps au choix" is recalled, not confirmed). Not used anywhere.
- `[check]` *Calila e Dimna* plot points (pond with more water, the telling sandpiper, good counsel) rest on one encyclopedia summary; confirm against a critical edition before build.
- `[check]` Origin of "He who hesitates is lost" (often traced to Addison's *Cato*, 1713, whose line is "The woman that deliberates is lost"). Child text gives it only as "an old saying", with no source.
- **Cultural review:** the Cherokee retelling, and whether it may be shared year-round, needs a Cherokee reviewer before build (spec review ask).
- Spanish and French lines need fluent readers.
