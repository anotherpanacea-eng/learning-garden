## Review pass (2026-10-25, independent reviewer)

Eleven WebSearch calls (search excerpts only; WebFetch blocked). Every writer `[check]` is now settled or softened and the markers are removed.

- **Story:** spec 120 keeps Hollis offstage until Act VI, and in Act I nobody remembers the heron's name. "Hollis's stone drawer" (Act I) and "Hollis's old quarry" would leak the name, so the drawer is now the librarian's own, everywhere.
- **Record before lifting:** confirmed as standard practice (La Brea Tar Pits excavations page, tarpits.org; BYU Geology "Collecting fossils"; Canadian Fossil Discovery Centre).
- **Anning and Buckland:** confirmed (Geological Society page, row 1; Biodiversity Heritage Library blog, 2015). Buckland read the paper in 1829 and named her.
- **Abditosaurus:** Kühne 1954 near Orcau, named 2022 (Vila et al.). Some bones were dug in 1986 before work stopped again, so item 1.6 no longer says it "waited, half dug, for 68 years". Wording now "most complete titanosaur skeleton yet found in Europe".
- **Pangaea:** began to break up about 200 million years ago, around the Triassic-Jurassic boundary (Britannica). The 200 to 175 range was dropped.
- **Glossopteris and Lystrosaurus:** confirmed as classic matching-fossil cases (Geological Society plate tectonics pages; University of Hawaii table). Lystrosaurus: Africa, India, Antarctica. Spec 155 uses Glossopteris and Mesosaurus; consistent.
- **Map pins:** Spinosaurus, Bahariya (Egypt) and Kem Kem (Morocco); Australovenator, Winton Formation, Queensland. Confirmed.
- **First land plants:** spores from about 475 to 470 million years ago (Wellman et al. 2003, *Nature*). Now "roughly 470 million years ago".
- **First dinosaurs:** securely dated by about 233 million years ago (Santa Maria Formation, Brazil); Ischigualasto about 231.7; Nyasasaurus about 243 is disputed. Now "by about 233 million years ago, perhaps earlier". Consistent with spec 142's Triassic 252 to 201.
- **Microraptor:** Jiufotang Formation, Aptian, about 120 to 125 million years (He et al. 2004 tuff, 120.3 million years). Kept.
- **End-Ordovician:** about 445 million years ago; glaciation, sea-level fall and low oxygen all proposed. Now "linked to glaciation and falling seas; details debated".
- **End-Permian:** older figures (90 to 96 percent) partly mixed in an earlier crisis; Stanley 2016 (*PNAS*) estimates about 81 percent. Now "probably more than 80 percent of sea species"; child text "about 8 in 10 kinds of sea life, maybe more".
- **K-Pg 66 million years:** matches spec 142. Mammals and birds diversifying afterward and a warmer Antarctica in a different place are textbook facts; kept without a marker.
- **Math** recomputed (`review-173/math.py`): Life Rope 35 m, 4.7 m, 2.52 m, 0.66 m, 0.55 m, 3 mm, written history 0.05 mm (caption now "about a hair's width"); 68; 10 and 11.7 carts; 2.1 to 3.5 kg; 5 to 10 million years. The 1.6 wrong-answer examples (132, 1068) were not plausible slips; now 78 (borrow missed) and 1932.
- **Pedagogy:** right answers 5/5/5 across a, b, c. Items 2.4 and 3.5 had the right answer as the longest or the only hedged choice; reworded.

## Notes on facts handled with care

- **Coprolite mass:** about 7 kg is from the Geological Survey of Canada page; item 2.6 frames it as "about" and gives a range of bone mass.
- **Compsognathus:** AMNH (70 cm, 3 kg) and USGS (1 m, 2.5 kg) differ; the pack gives the range.
- **Argentinosaurus:** estimates range widely (30 to about 40 m); the pack uses 30 to 35 m and says why estimates vary.
- **End-Permian severity:** search excerpts gave 80 to 90 percent and 93 to 97 percent of marine species; the review pass changed this to "probably more than 80 percent", Keeper's Desk only.
- **Anchiornis:** reported as 155 and 160 million years; item 4.4 answers with a 5 to 10 million year range.
- **Horse sequence:** taught as a bush, avoiding the old straight-line display.
- **Abditosaurus arrival:** the authors' suggestion (lower sea levels about 70.6 million years ago) is taught as "probably," and item 3.5's answer is phrased as routes between lands, not drift alone.
