## Primary science: exact bounded claims

- [USGS Water Science School, “Condensation and the Water Cycle”](https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/condensation-and-water-cycle), primary indexed page checked 2026-10-22: condensation changes water vapor in air into liquid water; its cold-glass example locates the resulting water on the outside of a cold surface. The page also says water vapor is usually unseen. This supports already-present air water → liquid drops, not a leak through pane. The original pane is an analogy; it is not a USGS diagram or prescribed experiment. The page's full body timed out in the browsing tool on this check, so the bounded claims here rely on its indexed USGS lead and the corroborating full NOAA/USGS pages below.
- [USGS Water Science School, “Evaporation and the Water Cycle”](https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/evaporation-and-water-cycle), full page opened 2026-10-22: evaporation changes liquid into gaseous water, and condensation is the reverse process; its cold-glass example concerns air cooling at a surface. The optional warm-again frame is a teaching reversal under made-up model conditions, not a claim that real drops vanish at a fixed time or that every warm pane becomes dry.
- [NOAA Education, “The water cycle”](https://www.noaa.gov/education/resource-collections/freshwater/water-cycle), full page opened 2026-10-22: water vapor can condense into liquid; conditions depend on how much vapor air can hold at its temperature. It explicitly says the common simple circle understates the real cycle. Hence the model states “cool enough in this made-up air” instead of “cold always makes water,” and claims neither precipitation nor a completed water cycle. Its wider flux material is context only.
- [Marble's external Water Lab](https://withmarble.com/water-lab/), live page opened 2026-10-22: Glass/Atoms views and its original copy focus on liquid molecular movement and surface evaporation. Our local existing link continues to point there. No Marble view, illustration, sentence, controller, audio or code is used to portray condensation.

The pane/water-molecule image is an explanatory schematic. The gas is **not** shown as visible mist, blue clouds or floating droplets in the macro panel. Drops remain on the air-facing surface of a continuous pane; the far side is dry. Molecule dots appear only under an “imagined tiny view” heading, stay the same size, and are not to scale or a measured particle count. The cool frame has one close-packed liquid sample of three same-size dots near the pane and two visible macro drops; the deliberately different counts and explicit caption reject one molecule per drop. The model omits surface energy, humidity thresholds, airflow and exact molecular kinetics. There is no real cooling/heating instruction or safety test.

## Rights and review boundary

Original local SVG frames and responsive mock contain only authored shapes and text. Primary pages are linked for attribution and factual checking, not copied as art or licensed into this repo. The mock must be rendered and reviewed at 320 and 820 CSS pixels before build, especially the dry far side, continuity of the pane, visible surface-only drops and identical-size imagined dots.
