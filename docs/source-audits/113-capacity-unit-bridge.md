## Unit and science references

[NIST SI units: volume](https://www.nist.gov/pml/owm/si-units-volume) confirms 1000 mL=1 L and uppercase L to avoid confusion with 1. [Oak capacity and volume](https://www.thenational.academy/teachers/programmes/maths-primary-ks2/units/measures-mass-and-capacity/lessons/understanding-capacity-and-volume) supports separating maximum capacity from currently contained volume and the importance of practical experience. [Official Common Core 5.MD](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/5/MD/) describes a wider unit-conversion and real-problem direction. This tiny supplied screen contrast does not complete that standard, replace physical measuring or observe independent human prediction/conversion. Oak content carries OGL 3.0 except noted material; no slides/text/images are copied.

L names volume, mL is a smaller volume unit, and a fixed amount needs 1000 times as many mL as L. We do not equate mL with grams, unspecified cups, arbitrary rain tokens, container height generally or ingredient-mixture totals. Constant interior cross-section is an explicit mathematical assumption. The model does not purport to measure a real object or model surface tension, density, wet surfaces or experimental uncertainty.
