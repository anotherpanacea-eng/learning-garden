## Evidence for the exact named relations

| Relation or convention in proposed original copy | Directly consulted source and narrow use | Excluded inference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Broad bean (*Vicia faba*) supplies sap to black bean aphid (*Aphis fabae*) | [RHS Black bean aphid / blackfly](https://www.rhs.org.uk/biodiversity/blackfly) describes sap feeding on broad beans. [Shannag & Obeidat 2006 full paper](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/aez/41/3/41_3_521/_pdf) rears the named aphid on faba bean in its experiment. | An aphid chewing a loose bean/whole plant; identifying a species by black colour alone. |
| Black bean aphid can feed seven-spot ladybird **larva** (*Coccinella septempunctata*) | [Shannag & Obeidat 2006](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/aez/41/3/41_3_521/_pdf), *Applied Entomology and Zoology* 41:521–527, DOI 10.1303/aez.2006.521, studies aphid consumption by this predator's larval stage. Existing audit66 records morphology support from RHS ladybirds. | An adult in place of the larva, a wild-garden encounter, all possible larval foods, or a pest-control rate. |
| Some hoverfly larvae consume aphids; general note alone does not name our precise pair | [RHS Hoverflies](https://www.rhs.org.uk/biodiversity/hoverflies) distinguishes aphid-eating larvae from adults, describes larvae as legless, and separately names the marmalade hoverfly among aphid predators. It does **not** name *Aphis fabae* as that species' prey. The initial slip deliberately presents only its general note. | “All hoverflies eat aphids” or the exact *E. balteatus*–*A. fabae* pair from this RHS guide. |
| Black bean aphid can feed marmalade hoverfly **larva** (*Episyrphus balteatus*) on broad bean | [Wojciechowicz-Żytko 2000 full paper](https://journals.pan.pl/Content/131235/PDF/11_PLANT_40_2_2000_Wojciechowicz_Zytko_The_Effectiveness.pdf), *Journal of Plant Protection Research* 40(2), studies the named aphid, hoverfly larval predator and broad bean under laboratory/greenhouse conditions. This is the new relation's specific *closer* slip. | Every larva eating this aphid outdoors, a particular child's garden observation, a feeding rate, effect on population, or successful biological control. |
| Arrow goes food → eater and represents energy transfer | [Singapore NParks food-chain basics](https://biodiversitysg.nparks.gov.sg/our-ecosystems/food-webs/food-chains-and-food-webs-basics/) explains the arrow direction. | A life-stage arrow, animal-travel arrow, complete diet or forecast. |

The proposed four card passages, three short evidence slips, feedback, legend and help in spec88 are original paraphrases of these bounded facts. The earlier slip is intentionally **insufficient** for the exact hoverfly pair; the closer paper changes what the displayed evidence can support. Checking a proposed edge against one displayed slip is a source-reading action, not a claim that an unsupported pair is biologically impossible. The science reviewer must verify this distinction and every named/staged card before build. No numerical results, figures, paper tables or experiment directions are transferred.

## Art and rights

The existing three local SVGs are original project schematics. The proposed fourth is another original static vector: tapered and legless *hoverfly larva*, explicitly labelled and supplied with a text equivalent. Do not use the source page's adult hoverfly photo; do not copy any institutional or paper image, layout or identification plate. The picture is not an identification key, exact relative-size diagram or field guide. Independent rendered-art review must check that no adult wings or six walking legs were added, and that bean, aphid and larval predator labels remain unambiguous. No image generation is needed.

Jane's exact-commit licensing file identifies code as MIT and lessons/docs as Creative Commons content; Marble's database and authored text have separate ODbL/CC BY-SA terms. Here those materials are studied and attributed for meanings, not copied into app text or runtime. Primary biology pages and papers supply facts and citations, not asset reuse rights. Bibliographic links can appear in the source fold and frozen source metadata as user-activated links. All learner copy and proposed SVGs are new local work, subject to the independent pack review and actual implementation check. No research paper provides evidence that this UI improves learning or that a constructed link predicts an ecosystem outcome.
