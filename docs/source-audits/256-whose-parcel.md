## Language facts used without a separate search (low risk; reviewer to confirm)

*Can't* is the contraction of the single word *cannot*; *won't* means *will not*; *ill*, *well*, *shell* and *were* are ordinary words distinct from *I'll*, *we'll*, *she'll* and *we're*; *sheep* has the same singular and plural form, so *sheep's* serves both; *mice* is the plural of *mouse*; *children* of *child*. Spanish *la carta de Nuria* ("Nuria's letter") and French *la lettre de Léon* express possession with *de*, and neither language uses a possessive 's; French elides *je* before a vowel (*j'arrive*, "I'm coming"). The Spanish and French lines need a fluent reader before build, as spec 120 requires.

## Checks on the keyed items

- **A3, A5, A6, A4:** each keyed spelling is the standard form; the wrong ones (*Il'l*, *do'nt*, *did'nt*) are the mark-at-the-join error or a misplaced mark.
- **B1, D4:** Willowbank has one baker; D4 states "one cat", so *cat's* is the only fit.
- **C1:** *hens'* is unambiguous in print: one hen would be *hen's*. The "can't tell" choice is wrong for an evidence-specific reason.
- **C5:** with only the words given, *sheep's* fits one or many; the bench picture shows a flock, and the item says "from these words alone".
- **C6:** *Ways* is a plain plural, so its possessive is *Ways'*. The original "of" form is not offered as a wrong choice. *Ways's* is the doubled-s error. **Reviewer:** the writer rested the plural on spec 120's page 3, which no child reads before Act III ends; the prompt now states "Ways means many ways" (also taught on card 7 and in D1), so the key rests on the card. Even read as a name, *the Ways* is plural in form, and Chicago ¶7.20 (18th ed., via Chicago's Q&A) gives such names the apostrophe alone (*the United States'*), so *Ways's* is not a style-choice answer. The possessive expresses the keeper's relation to the Ways, not ownership, as the DfE glossary allows (*The boys' arrival*). It reads naturally as a label rewrite (compare *the city's mayor*).
- **D1:** in the label *Ways* is a plural with nothing owned, so no mark is right; both marked forms are wrong.
- **E2, E3:** the gaps allow only one form each: *___ raining* needs a subject and verb (*it's*), *lost ___ string* needs a possessive determiner (*its*).
- **E4:** *___ parcel is this?* needs a possessive interrogative (*whose*); *who's* would read "who is parcel".
- **E5:** "All six hens" fixes the count, so *hens'* is the only possessive that fits; the script confirms the note and answer differ in exactly three words.
