# Willow Path: paper and upstream audit

Author audit for [spec 76](../specs/76-willow-path.md), 2026-10-22. This is a design and source check, not evidence that the Learning Garden activity improves learning. No paper figure, worksheet, game name, token art, upstream code, or lesson prose is incorporated.

## Primary studies and the design boundary

The author's full papers were read: [Siegler and Ramani (2008), *Developmental Science* 11, 655–661](https://siegler.tc.columbia.edu/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/sieg-ram08.pdf), especially pp. 657–658, and [Siegler and Ramani (2009), *Journal of Educational Psychology* 101, 545–560](https://siegler.tc.columbia.edu/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/sieg-ram09.pdf), especially pp. 548–549. [Ramani and Siegler (2008), *Child Development* 79, 375–394](https://siegler.tc.columbia.edu/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/Ram-Sieg2008.pdf) independently describes the same apparatus and counting-on procedure. The 2008 *Developmental Science* study compared a number board with an otherwise similar color board; the 2009 study compared linear, circular and other numerical activity conditions. Their tested outcomes belong to those researcher-led sessions with their participants, not to our digital path.

| Detail checked in the papers | Consequence for this original activity |
| --- | --- |
| The 2008 board had **11** equal squares in one horizontal row: a leftmost **Start** square followed by numbered **1–10**. The 2009 description has **10** equal numbered squares in one horizontal row, with “Start” just left of 1 and “End” just right of 10. | Draw exactly ten equal numbered stones in one left-to-right row, plus a distinct unnumbered gate at position 0. The gate is not an eleventh numbered stone. The two paper descriptions differ in whether Start itself is described as a square; both establish a separate start before 1. |
| Both number boards used a spinner with one half labeled **1** and the other **2**. | One fair digital spin chooses 1 or 2. This is the only authored spinner for v1. |
| A token on 3 with a spin of 2 moved through and named **4, 5**, rather than counting the step indices **1, 2**. The adult experimenter modeled the square numbers when needed. | One tap moves one square and labels its *destination* numeral. Adult co-naming is invited. No microphone or assertion that the child said or understood a number. |
| The experiments used an adult and child taking turns, bear/rabbit tokens, first to the end winning, repeated short games and experimenter correction. | Leaf/Lantern art and copy, solo option, both-token cooperative ending, optional help, no correction and no race record are **adaptations**, not experimentally tested features. Their value here is an open, parent-guided interaction, not a transferred efficacy claim. |
| The 2009 linear board produced larger gains than the circular condition on tasks assessing numerical magnitude. Other outcomes and comparisons varied. | Preserve the spatially linear, equally spaced board. Do not claim this app teaches magnitude, counting, social behavior or any full taxonomy topic without separate evidence. |
