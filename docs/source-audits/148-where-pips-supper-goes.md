# Source audit: 148, Where Pip's Supper Goes

Date: 2026-10-25. Writer: Claude (writing only). Covers [spec 148](../specs/148-where-pips-supper-goes.md) and [its companion text](../content/148-where-pips-supper-goes-text.md).

## How the facts were checked

The first draft was written without web access, with every specific fact marked `[check]`. A fact-check pass later the same day used about 25 web searches. **Source pages could not be opened directly in this session (page fetching was blocked), so each claim below was confirmed from search-result excerpts of the named pages.** Where an excerpt came from a weaker site, the table says so. A subject reviewer should open the primary pages before build. Marble's teaching-research records (pinned `7bd8b6794cb78d9ca18216adb935f3cfd59cf5ac`) were also used, and two of their sources are marked verified there: the NHS Eatwell Guide and the UK national curriculum placements.

All `[check]` markers have been cleared or the text rewritten. Two changes came out of the pass: (1) small-intestine length is now "several meters long", with 6 m used only as a labeled round example, because sources range from about 3 to 7 m; (2) Ibn al-Nafis is described as studying in Damascus and working in Cairo, with no exact year for his commentary. One unsourced line was cut from the spec ("children's hearts usually beat faster than adults'"), and the stomach line now says "most absorption comes later, in the small intestine" instead of claiming how little the stomach absorbs.

## Review pass (independent reviewer, 2026-10-25)

- **Searches (2 of 15 used):** (1) Harvey's quantitative argument: a PMC article and *Physiology by Numbers* (Cambridge, https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/physiology-by-numbers/preface-to-the-first-edition/53BF070B323C5D34B87DFECB00BF6BF8 ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2776239) give it as "more blood passes through the heart in half an hour than the whole body contains". The draft's "in an hour, more than the body weighs" was changed to that wording in the spec and source card 2. (2) Vein valves: Fabricius described them (*De Venarum Ostiolis*, usually dated 1603) but misread their function; Harvey's arm-ligature experiments showed it (https://oajournals.fupress.net/index.php/ijae/article/view/3844). Card 2 now says Harvey "showed" what the valves do, not that he "found" them.
- **Math:** every Set C answer and wrong-answer value recomputed (`scratchpad/review-148/math148.py`, `python3 -I`). Answers were right, but two explanations were wrong: C2 said the 12 extra adult teeth were premolars and molars "that baby sets don't have" (baby sets have 8 molars; the 12 are 8 premolars and 4 more molars), and C5 said a fox's 10 extra teeth were "extra premolars and molars" (a fox has 4 more incisors and 8 more premolars but 2 fewer molars than a person with 32). Both fixed.
- **Crosswalk:** every hard prerequisite re-read from the pinned `dependencies.json` by script; names and ids match, including the non-target `mt_DbXWivdJFB` and its hard prerequisite `mt_Ruk2-lyGPZ`.
- **Body safety and privacy:** the saved-work sketch had a free-text `sayWhy` field (2,000 characters). It was removed: say-why answers are spoken to a grown-up and never stored, so no field can hold personal details. Teeth card 3 now says "a person grows 20 baby teeth" in place of "a child", per the pack's own rule. The pulse card now says it is not a health check. No screen asks about the explorer's body, food, health or family (scanned for "your heart/teeth/body", "you ate" and similar: no hits).
- **Pedagogy:** guided items had the right answer first in every activity; the supper and pump guided items were reordered. Two choices were lengthened so the right answer is not the longest (picture prompt, D3). One confusing tag note was cut from B6.
