## Eight directed chart-neighbour tasks and biological interpretation

The proposed four-stage chart has egg, larva/caterpillar, pupa/chrysalis and adult. Four forward-neighbour selections and four backward-neighbour selections produce eight supplied tasks. Forward/backward refers to **reading the directed chart**, not making an animal grow in either direction. Label the arrow direction and whether the task asks for the next or preceding chart position. A backward lookup must not suggest biological reversal (adult becoming pupa, or pupa becoming larva).

Keep two response meanings separate: (1) which stage is the requested chart neighbour, and (2) whether the linked depictions concern one developing individual or a parent/offspring generation boundary. Egg → larva → pupa → adult depicts one individual’s developmental sequence. Adult female → offspring egg crosses to a new generation; looking backward from that egg reaches its parent-generation adult, not an earlier adult phase of the egg’s own individual. Both lookup directions across this boundary must retain the new-generation interpretation. A cyclic layout is not evidence of an immortal individual or reverse development.

A correct directed-order choice can coexist with an incorrect continuity explanation, and vice versa. Do not collapse the two into one “life cycle mastered” outcome. The chart, factual passage and optional adult narration remain available; a supported response is welcome. If the continuity prompt depends on an authored link between displayed individuals, make that link explicit in the case rather than asking learners to infer biological identity from colour/shape resemblance. Repeated exposure to this same four-stage case stays familiar, including when a question reverses its direction.
