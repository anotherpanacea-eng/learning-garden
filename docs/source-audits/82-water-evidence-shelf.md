# Water Evidence Shelf: source and overlap audit

Supports proposed [spec 82](../specs/82-water-evidence-shelf.md). Checked 2026-10-22 against the Learning Garden base `68777edc7e976d41e1d7e407ca1cc40b9a780a9f`. This audit concerns three original illustrated before/after cases, not a physical experiment or a full water-cycle lesson.

## Existing work and disposition

`dist/science-content.mjs` already has melt, warm, and evaporate molecule visits. The evaporation visit says a shrinking puddle may reflect flow, soaking, or evaporation, and its pictures follow one molecule into the air. `dist/science-ui.mjs` carries the hub caveat that a smaller puddle alone does not locate all its water. `dist/science-core.mjs` saves up to 24 version-1 molecule visits with stage, prediction, notice, reading report, and picture frame. Adding a fourth mission would incorrectly imply another simulator/molecule visit and create new persisted visit semantics. The proposed shelf instead uses three supplied surface-level picture comparisons and transient choices within the hub. It does not mutate or infer any existing result.

`dist/rain-paths-content.mjs` and its runtime are an authored storage/runoff token machine with explicit omitted evaporation and deep-drainage processes. The Water Evidence Shelf will not borrow token amounts as observed hydrology. Fair Test Bench and Apprentice distinguish model readings from explanations, but their ramp data and response records do not answer this water-path clue question. Field Kit's weather/dryness invitations do not verify a real child's observations. No existing activity should be treated as a measured soil, dish, or puddle experiment.

## Primary science and teaching sources

- [NOAA, “The water cycle”](https://www.noaa.gov/education/resource-collections/freshwater/water-cycle), checked 2026-10-22: describes distinct evaporation, surface runoff, and infiltration routes and notes that the whole cycle is more complex than a simple circular diagram. The inference that one smaller puddle cannot identify a unique route is our limited application of those alternatives, not a NOAA quotation or a measured finding about the fictional picture.
- [USGS, “Infiltration and the Water Cycle”](https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/infiltration-and-water-cycle), checked 2026-10-22: water can enter soil and remain shallow or move deeper, depending on conditions. A darker *drawn* soil patch is only a clue compatible with wetness. It cannot measure infiltration, prove groundwater recharge, or exclude other routes.
- [USGS, “Evaporation and the Water Cycle”](https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/evaporation-and-water-cycle), checked 2026-10-22: liquid water can become water vapor without boiling. The open-dish card's no-pour/no-spill/no-drain setup is authored, not a USGS experiment; the lower *drawn* waterline makes evaporation a reasonable explanation under those supplied conditions, not proof, a vapor sighting, or a rate/quantity measurement.
- [Department for Education, National curriculum in England: science programmes of study](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/national-curriculum-in-england-science-programmes-of-study/national-curriculum-in-england-science-programmes-of-study), checked 2026-10-22: early scientific work uses observations and ideas to suggest answers, while the year-4 states-of-matter program extends to evaporation/condensation and temperature-rate association. The non-statutory guidance suggests observing evaporation over time. This illustrated screen practice is narrower and is not practical-work completion.
- [Marble, Water Lab](https://withmarble.com/water-lab/), checked 2026-10-22: its glass/atoms view explains motion and molecules escaping at a liquid surface. The proposed shelf complements the local guided simulator visit with different surface-level evidence claims; no simulator code, screenshot, illustration, or text is used.

All three cases, their option wording, and any SVG/CSS drawings are original Learning Garden content. The pictures do not supply recorded weather, duration, water amounts, rates, measured moisture, or actual household observations. This source audit permits a later reviewed finite pack extension, but does not assert that these three cards cover the whole cycle or train transferable real-world diagnosis. No source requires collecting family or child observations, and none will be stored by this proposal.
