## Primary measurement references and limited use

[NIST IR7383 (2019), GMP3](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ir/2019/NIST.IR.7383-2019.pdf), particularly PDF pages20–26, discusses meniscus reading in volumetric calibration and the importance of eye position/scale geometry. It supports asking an adult how to read a particular water surface at its level. It does not establish a household jug's calibration, require importing laboratory adjustment/chemical procedures, or justify assigning a true volume from two user entries. No NIST diagram/text is copied.

The [NIST measurement-process variability discussion](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/mpc/section1/mpc114.htm) describes variation between repeated measurements and conditions. It supports retaining discrepant reports without declaring a cause. This two-reading notebook estimates no uncertainty, standard deviation, reliability or population property; agreement is not accuracy evidence.

The [University of Wisconsin graduated-cylinder guide](https://www2.chem.wisc.edu/deptfiles/genchem/lab/labdocs/modules/gradcyl/grad100mL.htm) explicitly distinguishes adjacent scale intervals, their numeric increment and a reading estimated between them. It was inspected to avoid confusing a count of marks with their spacing and to avoid falsely forcing household reports to whole mL. Its particular cylinder picture,52.9mL example and laboratory precision convention are not reused.

The original method keeps the adult's household judgement, cold water, steady surface, glass/heavy-jug help and spill precautions already reviewed for Field Kit. Separate content review must still judge the exact new directions and screen-away-from-water addition before build. These references establish design limits, not educational effectiveness, verified offscreen action or a physical result.
