## Grammar judgment calls for the subject reviewer

| Item or card | Call made | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Log Desk Sprout, "These days, it ___ a lot every spring." | *rains* (habit) | The plain present for a habit that still holds (S2). Reviewer: the writer's "Every spring it ___" also allowed *rained* (a past habit), so *these days* now pins the present; card 7's example and Hold It Steady entry 3 and E2 got the same anchor. |
| A5, Log Desk Seedling, "So far this week it ___ twice." | *has rained* only | *So far* reaches up to now and is the usual perfect signal. A5 leaves *rained* out of the choices; the Log Desk page offers it, and the subject reviewer should confirm US readers would not accept "so far this week it rained twice" (reviewer: rare in US writing, heard in speech). |
| Clue trays | *this week, and still wet* replaced by *up to now* | Reviewer: "it rained this week" is standard US English, so that card had two right trays. |
| Card 2 and A4 | *in March* and *today* replaced by *last week* and *at the moment* | Reviewer: "in March" and "today" can point to the past or the future, so they did not settle a tense. Seedling "on Saturday" became "next Saturday" for the same reason. |
| Cards 9, 14, glossary | "has/had plus a has form (gone)", not "plus a past form" | Reviewer: "plus a past form" followed literally gives *has went*, which D3 marks wrong. |
| Card 5 | Families are memory aids; *glow, glowed* | Reviewer: copying a look-alike gives *glew, flew (flow), shew*. |
| D1, "The sky is clear now, but the paths are still wet from rain. It ___." | *has rained* | *Rained* would also be heard in US speech, so it is **not** offered as a choice; the distractors are *will rain* and *is raining*. |
| D4, "has gone": still away | Withholding choice wrong | S2's own gloss: *has gone* implies the person is still away. |
| Hold It Steady entry 2 | No slips | Three times (last spring, this week, tomorrow), three tenses; CCSS L.5.1d targets only *inappropriate* shifts. |
| Letter line "The old Keeper's ledger lists the floods" | Present kept | The ledger exists and lists them now; only the floods themselves are past (*came*). Reviewer: the slip line now reads "Back then, the floods come closer together"; the writer's "In the later years, the floods come..." could be read as describing the record in the present (spec 120 itself words page 2 that way), so it was not clearly a slip. |
| E5, "If it rained tomorrow, we will check the post." | Wrong | Past for an imagined future pairs with *would*, not *will*; unreal conditions are pack 274's, so the feedback says only "for a plan, say if it rains". |
| E4, "have gone in before the water came" | Wrong | A present perfect cannot sit with a finished past reference point; the past perfect marks the earlier event (S2). |
