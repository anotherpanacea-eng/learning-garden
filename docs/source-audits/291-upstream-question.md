## Recommendation

The useful next story move is one purposeful English request for missing information, followed by an explicitly opened fictional reply and a marked-post drawing. Begin from the actual first letter, which invites comparing notes but supplies no number, unit, reference line or method. Show the letter and question work together. Keep supplied wording, a supported question and original words equally welcome as story participation. Do not infer independent writing or measurement competence from selecting or sending a supplied message.

A suitable question asks how high the brook reached **at Vadoalto's post, in the same story storm**, what the height was measured **from**, and which **units** were used. A method/estimate question is a useful optional further question. Do not presuppose that Nuria uses Willowbank's ordinary-level line: the reply should supply her own local reference. Model specificity as helping the reader know what to answer; remove the draft's promise that clear wording produces an exact measurement.

The reply may report an estimate of **about 240 cm above Vadoalto's own stated ordinary level**, with post marks at 50, 100, 150, 200 and 250 cm and an explicit 0/reference line. Phrase that reference as a fixed line used in this fictional record, labelled ordinary level, rather than today's changing water surface or a universal hydrological normal. Distinguish the supplied estimate from what the drawing alone licenses: a line between 200 and 250 does not uniquely yield 240. Label the storm line as Nuria's reported approximate estimate, rather than asking the child to read exactly 240 from coarse marks. A matching prose equivalent should name the marks, local reference and approximate storm height.

The resulting insight is narrow and worthwhile: a useful numerical answer needs its unit and reference; a specific question can receive an approximate answer; a correspondent's reported observation is evidence from that correspondent, not an observation the learner personally made. Comparing the two local departures is possible once defined, but the scene does not establish absolute water-surface elevation, water depth, discharge, flood severity, danger or cause.

## Primary science/metrology check

- [USGS, Why we use gage height](https://waterdata.usgs.gov/blog/gage_height/), updated2026-08-20, read live: gage height is relative to a site-specific datum; datum is chosen as a stable reference. This supports naming the reference and avoiding direct absolute elevation or depth claims. The game's ordinary-level reference is an invented convention, not a claim that real USGS datums normally sit at ordinary water level.
- [USGS, Floods: Things to Know](https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/floods-things-know), read live: rainfall intensity/duration, topography, soil, cover, channel/floodplain and upstream/downstream conditions all affect flooding; stage-to-discharge requires a site-specific relation. A narrow valley can motivate a later question, but these two fictional heights cannot establish its causal contribution. Omit the draft's so-the-water-climbs-fast explanation here.

This supports replacing exact-answer language with an openly approximate report. It does not establish that this fictional 50cm post can resolve a value to10cm, nor require bringing uncertainty calculus into a child-facing scene. Do not invent a numerical uncertainty interval or a measured calibration.
