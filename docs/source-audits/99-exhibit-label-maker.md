## Primary object records, photographs and historical limits

The two current Met object pages were opened again for this draft. The exact same local photographs were inspected visually. They are the [Writing board of an apprentice scribe, 28.9.5](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/545112) and [Scribe's Palette, 10.176.62](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/560805). The pages mark each **Public Domain** and provide the object details below; [The Met Open Access policy](https://www.metmuseum.org/hubs/open-access) releases public-domain work images and basic collection data under CC0. Existing audit95 records API `isPublicDomain:true` and original web-large photo URLs. Reusing the checked-in JPGs adds no new image download or license assumption. Keep exact object/Met credit and links next to the images.

| Record | What supports the exact label | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Board `28.9.5`: Egypt, wood/whitewash/ink, ca.2030 BC. | The museum interprets unevenly formed and spaced writing as apprentice practice. It describes removing ink and adding whitewash to reuse the board. The local full-object photo shows the pale board and uneven marks. | The photographed marks alone do not identify their writer, age or gender; this one object says nothing about every Egyptian child's education. “Apprentice” is museum interpretation, not a personal biography. |
| Palette `10.176.62`: Egypt, wood/pigment, ca.2030–1550 BC. | The Met explains scribes' palettes held ink and brushes. It reports substantial black ink remaining, red almost gone, and reed brushes with frayed ends. The local full-object photo shows a long wooden palette with slot/recesses. | It does not identify an owner, establish use of only black, show an original brush in this photograph or tell the complete color history of one person. The approximate broad date cannot be placed strictly after the board. |

The app's exact proposed sentences are modern original summaries. Do not copy the Met's explanatory paragraph/captions wholesale. The title “Writing board of an apprentice scribe” is the museum's object title, attributed as such; in the learner's narrative, **The Met interprets** the uneven signs. A photograph of either object today is not a photo of an ancient person using it. The design mock uses the existing checked-in images at their original aspect ratio; it adds original paper/ticket layout, not a new depiction of ancient Egyptian people or script. All fictional visitor speech and teaching frames are original. Before build, independent reviewer should compare the two exact `claim` alternatives and source cards against these pages, view both actual JPGs and the rendered mock, and verify that overclaim text is visibly marked a draft rather than museum fact.

The source references and rights allow this **narrow** reuse of images and basic data. They do not clear new ancient-world content, copied museum prose, an invented hieroglyph translation or a claim about how common literacy was. A structurally readable imported source can be preserved without today's catalog match, but its historical claims cannot be independently verified by the local validator; only this exact authored pack receives the content review described here.
