## Claims and their status

- Diffusion as net random movement; passive; rate factors: standard science, supported by the Marble record's framing and the 3M demo (temperature). Cleared.
- Air-sac count about 480 million (range 274 to 790 million): Ochs et al. abstract. Cleared.
- Surface: varies by method, about 50 to 100 m²; 70 m² used as an example. Settled in the review pass.
- Right lung larger, three lobes vs two: cleared in the review pass.
- Insects: spiracles and tracheae; blood does little oxygen carrying. Cleared (educational sources).
- Earthworm: moist skin; five pairs of aortic arches; count varies. Cleared, low-authority sources; textbook confirmation welcome.
- Heart chambers by group (fish 2, amphibian 3, birds and mammals 4, crocodiles 4 with a gap): cleared.
- Four valves and route: cleared.
- Left ventricle thicker for higher pressure: cleared. Millimeter figures: cut in the review pass.
- Capillary 5 to 10 µm, one cell thick; red cells about 7.5 µm: cleared.
- Plasma about 55%: cleared (Red Cross). Plasma about 92% water, carries dissolved glucose: cleared in the review pass.
- Cardiac output about 5 to 6 L/min at rest; about equal to blood volume; 20 to 25 L/min at maximal effort: cleared. 5 L and 20 L are labeled example values.
- Word equation, mitochondria, plants respire: cleared.
- Breath clouds are condensed water vapor: cleared. "Partly from respiration": removed in the review pass (water comes mostly from the airway lining).
- Lavoisier and Laplace, winter 1782-83 (reported 1783), guinea pig, ice melted, charcoal comparison, slow combustion: cleared. Mild winter detail: one Spanish-language source. The animal's death in one report is deliberately not told.
- Wunderlich 37 °C in 1868 from armpit readings; Obermeyer 2017 mean 36.6 °C from 35,488 people: cleared from secondary reports; one source dates Wunderlich's work to 1852 (1868 is the usual publication date).
- Thermoregulation responses and hypothalamus: cleared (revision-level sources).
- Dogs cool mainly by panting: cleared. Foxes: softened in the review pass to "pant too, and lose heat through ears and legs".
- Lizard basking; "cold-blooded" misleading: cleared.

## Review pass (independent review and fact-check, 2026-10-25)

Seven web searches (excerpts only; WebFetch blocked). Settled or softened:

- **Alveolar surface:** estimates vary with method, about 40-80 m² in one morphometric study and up to about 100 m² or more elsewhere (PMC review table, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8863270/table/TAB1). Spec now says "roughly 50 to 100 m²"; A8 keeps 70 m² as a labeled example.
- **Right lung larger, three lobes vs two:** confirmed (LibreTexts anatomy, https://med.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Anatomy_and_Physiology/Anatomy_and_Physiology_(Boundless)/21:_Respiratory_System/21.4:_The_Lungs_and_Pleurae/21.4B:_Lobes_Fissures_and_Lobules). A6 now says it plainly and the prompt says "shared equally".
- **Ventricle wall millimeters:** low-authority only; cut.
- **Plasma:** about 92% water, carries dissolved glucose and other nutrients (BYU-Idaho *Composition of Blood*, https://books.byui.edu/docx/6036). Card 10 reworded ("mostly water, carries food like glucose").
- **Breath-cloud water:** the airways humidify inhaled air from their moist lining (Deranged Physiology, https://derangedphysiology.com/main/cicm-primary-exam/respiratory-system/Chapter-006/heating-and-humidification-normal-respiratory-tract); no source supports "partly from respiration", so the claim is removed.
- **Foxes:** no source says red foxes cool mainly by panting; a red-fox study stresses heat loss from head, ears, legs and paws (Univ. of Illinois IDEALS, https://ideals.illinois.edu/items/22351). Text now says foxes pant too and lose heat through ears and legs.
- **Lavoisier and Laplace:** experiments over winter 1782-83, read to the Académie 28 June 1783, printed 1784 (IUPAC history, https://publications.iupac.org/publications/pac/1987/pdf/5901x0091.pdf). Dates changed to "winter of 1782 to 1783"; the single-source mild-winter detail cut; the grown-up panel no longer hints at the animal's fate.
- **Wunderlich and the 2017 study:** 1868 publication, about 25,000 patients and over a million mostly armpit readings; Obermeyer et al., BMJ 2017, 35,488 patients, mean 36.6 °C, mostly oral readings (DocCheck, https://www.doccheck.com/de/detail/articles/418-das-37-grad-dogma). One source dates his averaging to 1852. The downward drift is from later work (2020), so the spec no longer pins it on the 2017 study.
- **Not searched again:** Ibn al-Nafis (13th century, died 1288) and Harvey 1628 match spec 148's fact-checked audit.

**Privacy change:** Card 9 and C8 now frame 37 °C and 36.6 °C as typical values scientists measured across many people, with no invitation to check anyone; the grown-up panel says nothing asks anyone to take a temperature. Owner default stays "keep at Keeper's Desk"; cutting C8 is the fallback.

**Consistency:** checked against spec 148 (Ibn al-Nafis, Harvey, red-not-blue blood, the single pulse card, white cells out of scope) and spec 159 (diaphragm and bell jar owned there); no conflicts. Arithmetic re-run in the reviewer's scratch; all answers hold.
