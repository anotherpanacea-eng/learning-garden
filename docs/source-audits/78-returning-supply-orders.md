## Exact arithmetic and review boundary

`42 = 4×10 + 2`. Returning one ten and two ones leaves `30`. For seven loose ones, opening a table ten yields three tens and twelve loose ones without changing `42`; returning one ten and seven ones leaves two tens and five ones, or `25`. The distinct help example is `31 − 16 = 15`: open a ten, return one ten and six ones. The model conservation equations in the spec ensure all accepted moves keep 42 across the two trays, while the table amount decreases only on a return. A matching typed number before moving counters is solely a number response; picture completion is separate. Bundle Exchange's existing exact-picture exposure bridge also matters here: showing a full conventional four-tens/two-ones/42 picture can make a later matching original Number Garden bundle question familiar, without creating an answer or mastery claim. The spec therefore records only actually displayed conventional signatures and conservatively marks unknown recovered history. This packet includes no implementation, browser observation or learning result.

Independent reviewers should inspect whether the separate tray makes subtraction's change unmistakable, whether a wrapper can accidentally look like an extra counter, whether the optional example supplies too much, whether counts remain readable on a phone, and whether the saved-source and recovery limits preserve old drafts. A release should never call a supplied equation or successful picture move independent strategy evidence.
