## Method and evidence limits

Read the supplied original 232 spec, copy and audit from the dated proposal folder, plus repository `AGENTS.md` and `CONTRIBUTING.md`. The original audit explicitly relied mostly on search excerpts and left the omnivore exemplars and heron plate open. This pass opened the sources below and inspected their relevant diet sections. Search snippets alone were not accepted as clearance. Descriptions of diet on agency, zoo and conservation-organization pages are institutional syntheses, not claims that each institution performed a new feeding experiment. The two ladybird studies are original research; their accessible abstracts were read, not their full papers.

All successful sources below were opened on **2026-10-28**. The URL is the actual opened destination; relevant headings and exact support are given to make the check repeatable. No source photos, illustrations, tables or lengthy prose are incorporated. Supplied names identify the authored examples; drawings are original stylized schematics, not field-identification keys.

A food picture is an illustration of the supplied species information. It is neither one observed meal nor enough evidence by itself to establish a species' entire usual diet. The teaching must say that these are examples of foods, and that the groups describe diet. They are not universal rules that an individual never eats anything else. An omnivore can eat plant and animal foods without eating both at every meal, in equal amounts, or eating literally anything. Size, teeth, appearance, home and hunting behaviour do not determine a basket.

## Teaching corrections to carry into finite copy

1. **Ladybird answer changes.** An aphid-only picture can illustrate predation, as in existing food-link material, without establishing exclusive carnivory. This board asks the diet-group question, so the pollen evidence must be included and the keyed basket must change to omnivore. Existing66's possible aphid→ladybird feeding relation remains compatible with this correction.
2. **Plate evidence must be supplied as examples of species diet.** Replace replies that imply a depicted meal establishes the entire diet, such as the original “That's what its plate shows” or “The shrew's plate has none [plants]” as an exclusive classification proof. Ask learners to use the card's supplied food information. A blank/uncertain response is not evidence that the learner thinks an animal eats nothing.
3. **Use precise organism names.** Child-readable short labels may remain where the full supplied label and source fold unambiguously name the animal. Art must match the selected species and life stage. Latin names are attribution/context, not a decoding or identification task.
4. **Appearance contrasts need actual reasons.** Elephant/shrew/beaver prompts may contrast appearance with the sourced food examples, but the explanation must point to plants or animal material. The vulture case targets scavenging versus hunting: bones are animal remains, so eating them does not make it a plant eater.
5. **Keep human food and animal-care claims out.** Do not import proposal232's human-omnivore panel, personal plate questions, nutrition advice, disease material, habitat changes, sunlight, DDT, food-chain tracing or tooth mechanics into this bounded board. The fictional plate is a diagram, not permission to feed wildlife.
