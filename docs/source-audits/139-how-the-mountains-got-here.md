## Open checks

None remain in the text. Fact-check pass 2 (2026-10-25, 13 further searches) cleared all five earlier items:

1. **Marine fossils high in the Pyrenees.** No source named nummulites at a particular summit, so the claim was changed rather than kept: the coin stone card now says nummulitic limestone is found across the Pyrenees (sources 6, 7) and cites Eocene marine rock high in the range: Monte Perdido's Cretaceous and Eocene limestone (source 8) and the 2024 Ordesa fossil starfish (source 26).
2. **Garrotxa "dormant".** Kept, with the grown-up note explaining that Spanish hazard planning treats the field as active (sources 14, 25).
3. **Cross-cutting principle.** Confirmed (source 23).
4. **Monte Perdido height.** 3,355 m, third-highest in the Pyrenees (source 24); UNESCO's 3,352 m is noted as a rounding or survey difference.
5. **Spanish and French earthquake guidance.** Same core actions in their own wording (source 27); the grown-up note now says so.

Collision dates spot-checked (source 28): convergence from the Late Cretaceous, full collision in the Eocene, shortening into the Oligocene. "Mostly about 55 to 25 million years ago" stands; the spec's onset wording was changed to "Late Cretaceous, roughly 80 to 70 million years ago."
