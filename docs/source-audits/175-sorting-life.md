## Review pass (independent review and fact check, 2026-10-25)

Eight WebSearch calls (excerpts only; WebFetch blocked). Checked and changed:

- **Guadarrama 293 m:** confirmed in the abstract of Wilson, Gutiérrez, Gutiérrez and Monserrat 2007, *Global Change Biology* 13: 1873-1887 (DOI 10.1111/j.1365-2486.2007.01418.x): communities shifted uphill 293 m (SE 26) between 1967-73 and 2004-05, against about 225 m for isotherms. The review brief named "Wilson et al. 2005"; that *Ecology Letters* paper is the source of the separate 212 m lower-limit figure, so the spec keeps 2007.
- **1977 drought:** Boag and Grant 1981, *Science*, "Intense natural selection in a population of Darwin's finches": nonrandom survival favoring large, deep-beaked birds that could crack large hard seeds. Secondary pages give 9.42 mm before and 9.96 mm in survivors: that half millimeter compares survivors with the population before the drought. Offspring figures conflict across secondary pages (4 to about 10 percent), so the "next generation half a millimeter, 4 percent" line was replaced with survivors' half millimeter plus "their young had deeper beaks on average." Survival share and population numbers are not used (sources disagree; death figures kept out of child text).
- **Finch count:** Linnean Society (2024) says 18; older sources 13 (some count the Cocos Island finch separately). "At least 13" stands.
- **Pyrenees birds:** Couet et al. 2025, *Global Ecology and Biogeography* (BTO; University of Helsinki): 177 species, Alps, Pyrenees, Scandinavian mountains, UK uplands, 2001-2021; 63 percent moved uphill; no uphill shift in the UK or the Pyrenees. Text unchanged.
- **Linnaeus:** ICZN Code Article 3 (https://code.iczn.org/zoological-nomenclature/article-3-starting-point/) fixes 1 January 1758 for the tenth edition (with Clerck's *Aranei Svecici*); *Species Plantarum* 1753 for plants (secondary). Text unchanged.
- **Whales and hippos:** molecular studies place hippos as whales' closest living relatives; *Indohyus* (about 48 million years, Thewissen et al. 2007, *Nature*) is the closest known fossil relative (ScienceDaily 2009; National Geographic 2007). Text unchanged.
- **Photo 51:** King's College London ("The story behind Photograph 51", https://www.kcl.ac.uk/the-story-behind-photograph-51): exposure set 2 May 1952, developed 6 May, Gosling under Franklin; Wilkins showed it to Watson in early 1953 without Franklin's knowledge; published by Franklin and Gosling in *Nature*, 25 April 1953. Child file now names the Franklin and Gosling paper.
- **Sensitivity:** Franklin's death cut from child text; one neutral grown-up-panel line explains the Nobel rule.
- **Classification wording:** Big Groups gains a "one useful way to sort" line; the grown-up panel notes birds sit inside the reptile branch in family-tree classification (consistent with draft 173's dinosaur-to-bird).
- **Pedagogy and contract:** mismatched misconception tags removed from A2 C and A3 B; `water-means-group` added to the spec list. Positions A 3 / B 4 / C 3; no right answer is the single longest.
