# Six-act longitudinal teaching pathways

**Durable teaching-pathway proposal, not the current queue.** See the [current roadmap](roadmap.md) for merged-versus-live status, present scoped atlas counts and selected next increments. The 26 pathways below identify candidate unit families and missing transitions; their older status and reference-count rows are planning snapshots, not claims that lessons or whole acts are complete.

Publication update, 2026-10-21: Bell Studio subsequently cleared and was published in [PR 27](https://github.com/anotherpanacea-eng/learning-garden/pull/27), with the stack now on main. The “unpublished foothold” wording in the earlier snapshot below is historical. This changes no phonics, musical-proficiency or physical-audibility claim.

Companion to [spec49](specs/49-six-act-curriculum.md). These 26 proposed pathways own all54 pinned domains. The six cells per pathway are **unit-family candidates**, not complete lesson specifications, not age placements and not a claim every listed topic has been taught. Topic-level introduction/contrast/application placements remain explicit gaps in the [full ledger](../curriculum/six-act-topic-plan.csv).

The acts give useful reasons to return. Their columns suggest revisits; a child can use a foundation or a supported advanced activity anywhere. A topic routes to its strand, not automatically to all six cells. Subject review must resolve the actual concepts and prerequisites before a unit is implemented.

## Proposed unit families by act

### M1: Whole-number structure and additive reasoning

Owns 118 source topics; descriptions and exact lesson placements require review. Each `M1-<act>` below is an authoring family, not an activity ID.

- **M1-I:** Count and compare pictured sets; parts and whole.
- **M1-II:** Place-value bundles; number-line addition; regrouping.
- **M1-III:** Whole-number and decimal computation; estimation; inverse checks.
- **M1-IV:** Signed quantities; number-line distance; choosing useful accuracy.
- **M1-V:** Explain algorithms; number sets and powers as needed.
- **M1-VI:** Use number reasoning in journey plans; specialist infinity optional.

### M2: Multiplicative structure

Owns 57 source topics; descriptions and exact lesson placements require review. Each `M2-<act>` below is an authoring family, not an activity ID.

- **M2-I:** Equal groups and both division meanings; factor roles.
- **M2-II:** Full12x12 practice; remainders with context; arrays to distributivity; additive versus multiplicative comparison with complete batch copies.
- **M2-III:** Factors, multiples, primes; written multi-digit operations.
- **M2-IV:** Decimal multiplication/division; signs; order of operations.
- **M2-V:** Choose efficient methods; inverse reasoning in constraints.
- **M2-VI:** Use linked quantities in planning; no fact-recall finale gate.

### M3: Fractions, decimals, percentages and proportion

Owns 85 source topics; descriptions and exact lesson placements require review. Each `M3-<act>` below is an authoring family, not an activity ID.

- **M3-I:** Equal shares, same whole, unit fractions of shape/set.
- **M3-II:** Number-line fractions; equivalence; compare; tenths/hundredths.
- **M3-III:** Mixed/improper; unlike-denominator add/subtract; decimal percent links; introduce ratio versus difference and unit rate before journey tools rely on them.
- **M3-IV:** Fraction multiplication/division and scaling; revisit ratio versus difference and unit rate; introduce noninteger scale factors with fraction-as-scaling lessons.
- **M3-V:** Direct/inverse proportion; similarity; percentage change; compound units.
- **M3-VI:** Defend scaling and allocation models; retain unit and whole.

### M4: Patterns, algebra and functions

Owns 25 source topics; descriptions and exact lesson placements require review. Each `M4-<act>` below is an authoring family, not an activity ID.

- **M4-I:** Repeated patterns and missing quantities.
- **M4-II:** Generalise arithmetic patterns; balance/equality; systematic listing.
- **M4-III:** Words to expressions and formulae; tables and ordered pairs.
- **M4-IV:** Substitution, like terms, distributivity; linear equations and inequalities.
- **M4-V:** Sequences, nth term, linear graphs/gradient, rearranging formulae.
- **M4-VI:** Two constraints and graph intersections; quadratics optional stretch.

### M5: Space, geometry and measurement

Owns 137 source topics; descriptions and exact lesson placements require review. Each `M5-<act>` below is an authoring family, not an activity ID.

- **M5-I:** Position/turns; compare and measure lengths/capacity; shapes.
- **M5-II:** Time/money; units; perimeter/area; symmetry and properties.
- **M5-III:** Coordinates, transformations, angles; conversion; compound area; teach simple map scale with whole-number unit relationships before route planning.
- **M5-IV:** Nets and volume; constructions; similarity and scale maps.
- **M5-V:** Circle measures; Pythagoras where supported; surveying.
- **M5-VI:** Navigation/engineering plans; trigonometry optional specialist extension.

### M6: Data, statistics and chance

Owns 33 source topics; descriptions and exact lesson placements require review. Each `M6-<act>` below is an authoring family, not an activity ID.

- **M6-I:** Sort/record; read honest simple tables and bars.
- **M6-II:** Choose relevant data; construct scaled bars; blank vs zero.
- **M6-III:** Time graphs; means and distributions; likelihood and simple trials.
- **M6-IV:** Choose representation; sampling; theoretical vs experimental chance.
- **M6-V:** Scatter graphs, correlation vs causation; uncertainty; trees/Venn as needed.
- **M6-VI:** Qualified predictions and evidence claims; no certainty from model outputs.

## Explicit small lessons and difficult bridges

The [math/literacy audit](six-act-math-literacy.md) breaks these broad families into smaller transitions. The following non-maths examples are equally necessary; theme names or projects do not substitute for them:

- **S1 inquiry:** observation/inference → a question that can be investigated → selecting a measure/unit/instrument → independent/dependent/control variables → repeated trials and honest variability → accuracy/precision/resolution → representations and alternative explanations → qualified conclusion → report/replication. Keep model data distinct from real observations.
- **S2 life/body:** organism needs and life cycles → organs and systems → cells and organization → reproduction/inheritance and variation → nutrition, transport, respiration and interacting systems → evidence-based explanations. Exact specialist topic scope, scientific vocabulary and human/offscreen observation require separate reviewed packs; never collect personal health disclosures.
- **S3 ecology:** identify described features → classification keys → organism/habitat conditions → food chains and arrow meaning → producers/photosynthesis and food webs → decomposers/matter cycles → population interactions/change → sampling/evidence and conservation trade-offs. Toy food webs require explicit assumptions and do not forecast a real habitat.
- **S4 biomes/ocean:** compare local conditions → locate and compare environments → adaptations versus individual choice → interacting food webs → environmental change and evidence → conservation proposals and trade-offs. Ocean, polar and rainforest knowledge each needs substantive source-reviewed content, not a reskinned local garden.
- **S5 matter:** observable properties and material selection → reversible/irreversible change → states and particle models → mixtures/separation → physical versus chemical change → elements/compounds and reaction explanations → specialist polymers/composites. Model representations and measurement conventions need explicit teaching.
- **S6 physical systems:** push/pull and motion → controlled comparisons and force effects → energy transfers/stores → friction/losses and efficiency → circuits/current/voltage/resistance → energy/power and quantitative relationships → vibrations, pitch/amplitude and waves → light/ray diagrams. Match each formula to a defined model, units, controlled quantities and a reason to use it.
- **S7 Earth/weather:** water pathways and weather observations → weather versus climate → rocks, soils and changing land → water/carbon/energy systems → plate processes and hazards → measured trends and uncertainty → resource/energy choices. Climate and hazard facts require reviewed sources; no flood-safety advice from arbitrary rain tokens.
- **S8 deep time/space:** order events and use relative duration → fossils/evidence and changing Earth → geological timescales/extinction explanations → Earth/Sun/Moon and motion → observational evidence about space → exploration tools and mission constraints → specialist stars/Mars questions. Historical/geological/astronomical timescales cannot be one undifferentiated timeline.
- **H1/H2 history:** personal/fictional sequence versus sourced past → chronology/period/context → artefact/source/provenance → claim/evidence and gaps → compare accounts and absent voices → change/continuity/cause → qualified historical interpretation. Ancient Egypt, Greece/Rome and medieval knowledge get their own substantial reviewed encounters; people are not mere costumes for a generic town story.
- **C1 computing/AI:** explicit instruction versus intention → sequence/choice/repetition → trace/debug a finite procedure → data/features/categories → train versus test and model error → automated recommendation/credibility/fairness → human responsibility and limitations. Source AI examples need factual review; automatic sensing is not by itself AI. Programming beyond this source’s narrow AI domain is an explicit extension.
- **L1 money/enterprise:** needs/wants and exchange → currency/place value/change → costs/revenue/profit → unit price and budget → saving/borrowing with stated assumptions → shared services/tax and opportunity cost → a justified enterprise plan. Fictional prices and accessible representations; no personal finances collected.
- **L2 learning:** notice a gap → choose a manageable goal → select a strategy and request help → check a result → use feedback/revise → compare strategies and evaluate a goal. Self-reflection is voluntary, not a global diligence score.
- **L3 self/community:** name fictional feelings/needs → distinguish mixed feelings and reasons → listen/clarify/perspective-taking → boundaries/cooperation/conflict repair → recognize consequences and competing values → represent objections fairly → inclusive shared planning. Each of the six source domains retains its own lesson targets; do not equate a selected kind response with empathy, emotional health or virtue. Personal reflection stays with a human and need not be recorded.

## Knowledge, practice and human observation

A substantive history/science knowledge pack contains actual reviewed information and sources, contrasts/explanations, meaningful tasks and later uses. Cross-cutting inquiry/comprehension practice is not a substitute for that knowledge. Oral language, handwriting, independent whole-book reading, real experimentation and community participation require explicit human/offscreen opportunities; screen completion does not certify them.

Every implemented unit must list its source-topic targets and exact teaching, contrast, practice, application and revisit tasks. Until then, the ledger continues to say topic design required. No arbitrary number of games, questions or years establishes complete schooling.
